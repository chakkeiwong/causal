\chapter{Channels, doses and sensitivity to assumptions}




% SOURCE docs/release/2026-10-29/source/docs/writing-repair/2026-10-29/src/108eac4b7183ec31.tex CHAR 0
A bank may know that a policy works on average and still need to choose
which mechanism to change, how large an intervention to offer and how much
confidence to place in the causal assumptions. These are related decisions,
but they require different targets. We begin with channel attribution and
feasible mediator interventions, then compare natural-effect estimation and
sensitivity methods across implementations. The final dose analysis asks how
to choose an amount rather than merely fit a response curve. Throughout,
changes in assumptions and changes in sampling uncertainty remain distinct.

\section{Commercial mediation software and the meaning of a channel}
\label{exec20:med-scope}
A credit team may know that a servicing offer improves repayment while still not knowing whether the improvement travels through customer understanding, available liquidity or a change in staff behavior. That distinction matters when the proposed investment changes only one channel. An effect attributed to a mediator is not automatically the effect of a feasible intervention on that mediator. Commercial mediation software supplies developed estimation and reporting workflows, but the comparison must begin with the intervention, counterfactual target and observation clock.

The selected Stata manuals and Mplus chapter document concrete capabilities. The StataNow two-mediator manual is a separate product feature from the one-mediator command. The selected Mplus chapter has March 2017 PDF metadata and supports a historical technical profile; it does not establish current feature absence or exact licensed entitlement. Sources acquired after the literature cutoff cannot, through an internal date alone, authenticate their exact bytes on that cutoff date. The available comparison therefore supports scoped design choices while retaining unresolved versions and commercial alternatives.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_one,exec20_med_stata_multiple,exec20_med_mplus_ch17}

\subsection{Four nested means and two valid decompositions}
\label{exec20:med-targets}
Let $A\in\{0,1\}$ indicate an offer, $M(a)$ the mediator that would follow offer $a$, and $Y(a,m)$ the outcome under offer $a$ and mediator setting $m$. For example, $M$ might describe a measured comprehension score and $Y$ a net recovery amount over a fixed subsequent horizon. The intervention on a score needs its own operational interpretation: administering a quiz, improving understanding and changing a recorded answer are distinct actions. Define
\begin{equation}
 \mu_{ab}=E\{Y(a,M(b))\},\qquad a,b\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{exec20:med-means}
\end{equation}
The means with $a\ne b$ combine an outcome under one offer with a mediator under another. Observing the offer, mediator and outcome on many customers does not directly reveal these nested outcomes.

Using labels that state which component is held fixed, the additive total effect has two decompositions:
\begin{align}
 \mu_{11}-\mu_{00}
  &=(\mu_{10}-\mu_{00})+(\mu_{11}-\mu_{10}),\nonumber\\
  &=(\mu_{11}-\mu_{01})+(\mu_{01}-\mu_{00}).
 \label{exec20:med-decompositions}
\end{align}
The first pairs a pure natural direct effect with a total natural indirect effect. The second pairs a total natural direct effect with a pure natural indirect effect. Each identity follows by adding and subtracting its intermediate mean. Mixing the direct term from one line with the indirect term from the other generally fails to recover the total. Stata's one-mediator manual documents these alternatives and includes treatment--mediator interaction by default. Its supported model combinations also matter: a logit outcome cannot be combined with the listed linear or exponential-mean mediator model in this interface.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_one}

Under the manual's stated identification assumptions, a nested mean is represented by averaging an outcome regression under $A=a$ over the mediator distribution under $A=b$ and the baseline population:
\begin{equation}
 \mu_{ab}=\int\!\!\int
 E(Y\mid A=a,M=m,X=x)\,dF(m\mid A=b,X=x)\,dF(x).
 \label{exec20:med-integral}
\end{equation}
Here $X$ contains permitted pretreatment covariates. The assumptions include treatment and mediator ignorability, appropriate support and the absence of a treatment-induced mediator--outcome confounder for this standard formulation. Regression software can calculate the integral under its models; it cannot infer those scientific conditions from a converged fit. Stata's introductory workflow explicitly places question, decomposition and assumptions before estimation and interpretation.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_one,exec20_med_stata_intro}

\subsection{Interaction changes the channel attribution}
\label{exec20:med-interaction}
An authored continuous example makes the labels consequential. Suppose
\begin{align}
 M(a)&=2+3a+U,\nonumber\\
 Y(a,m)&=5+4a+2m+am+V,
 \label{exec20:med-law}
\end{align}
where $U$ and $V$ have mean zero and are independent under the stipulated causal model. The four means are $\mu_{00}=9$, $\mu_{10}=15$, $\mu_{01}=15$ and $\mu_{11}=24$. The total effect is 15. Its pure-direct/total-indirect decomposition is $6+9$, while its total-direct/pure-indirect decomposition is $9+6$. Multiplying the mediator coefficient 3 by the untreated outcome slope 2 gives 6; that is the pure indirect component, not the total indirect component 9. Dropping the interaction changes the scientific model instead of merely simplifying a report.~\citep{exec20_med_reference}

The controlled direct effect at a fixed mediator value $m$ is
\begin{equation}
 E\{Y(1,m)-Y(0,m)\}=4+m.
 \label{exec20:med-controlled}
\end{equation}
It is 4, 6 or 9 at $m=0,2,5$, respectively. A bank specifying an operational mediator setting should request that setting explicitly. If $Y$ is measured in dollars and the full offer costs an incremental USD 10 per customer, the stipulated total-effect margin is USD 5. Charging that cost against one natural component would answer a different question unless a feasible component intervention and its cost were separately justified. This example supplies a finite analytical reference, not a commercial engine benchmark or a measured banking return.

The inspected Mplus chapter distinguishes conventional coefficient-product effects from counterfactually defined effects. Its conventional branch allows multiple mediators and outcomes; categorical mediator or outcome paths can use underlying latent response variables. A product on a latent-response scale is not thereby a difference in repayment probabilities. The chapter separately documents total natural indirect, pure natural direct and total effects for a single mediator, with \texttt{IND} and \texttt{MOD} syntax and explicit controlled-effect mediator values. The appropriate branch and scale should be part of the requested target, not inferred from the word indirect in an output table.~\citep{exec20_med_mplus_ch17}

The selected \texttt{MODEL INDIRECT} command uses delta-method standard errors by default, with documented bootstrap and interval alternatives. The chapter excludes this command for \texttt{TYPE=RANDOM}, the \texttt{CONSTRAINT} option of the \texttt{VARIABLE} command and \texttt{TYPE=EFA}. Those are statements about this inspected command and version profile. They do not prove that every other Mplus modeling route lacks a desired effect. Likewise, a documented interval option does not establish its calibration under shared bank or calendar shocks.

\subsection{Randomizing an offer does not identify every channel}
\label{exec20:med-nonidentification}
Consider two authored worlds with independent fair binary variables $A$ and $U$. The offer $A$ is randomized, and the mediator is
\begin{equation}
 M(a)=a\mathbin{\oplus}U,
 \label{exec20:med-xor}
\end{equation}
where $\oplus$ denotes exclusive OR. In the direct world, $Y(a,m)=a$. In the mediated world, $Y(a,m)=m\mathbin{\oplus}U$. In both worlds the observed mediator is equally likely to be zero or one within each offer arm, and the observed outcome is exactly $Y=A$. The complete observed joint distribution of $(A,M,Y)$ is identical. There is observed mediator overlap, and the offer's total effect is 1 in both worlds.~\citep{exec20_med_reference}

The nested means differ. In the direct world, $Y(1,M(0))=1$, so the pure natural direct effect is 1 and the total natural indirect effect is 0. In the mediated world,
$Y(1,M(0))=U\oplus U=0$, so those components are 0 and 1. The latter world contains unobserved mediator--outcome confounding through $U$, violating the assumptions needed for the ordinary mediation integral. The construction therefore does not contradict software operating under qualified assumptions. It shows why randomizing $A$ and checking the observed support of $M$ are insufficient to justify the natural-effect decomposition.

Even the mediated world's natural indirect effect of 1 is not the effect of setting everyone's mediator to one rather than zero. Under either constant mediator setting, $m\oplus U$ has mean $1/2$, so this controlled mediator-only contrast is zero. At an assumed USD 100 per beneficial outcome and USD 20 intervention cost, offering $A$ has a stipulated incremental margin of USD 80, whereas the mediator-only contrast gives USD $-20$. These refer to different proposed actions. A channel attribution cannot be sold as the return from changing a dashboard score.

An interventional mediator target would instead specify a feasible distributional intervention, for example a draw from a declared reference mediator distribution conditional on eligible baseline information. That differs from retaining each person's own unobserved $M(b)$ inside a nested counterfactual. Its identification conditions and practical implementation need their own derivation, especially when treatment changes mediator--outcome confounders. Section~\ref{sec:exec25-rmed} develops one such interventional mean and the associated restrictions using the retained primary text. The selected commercial documents do not establish that every such target is available. The proposal should preserve unsupported targets rather than relabel a natural effect as interventional.

\subsection{Population averaging, signs and multiple mediators}
\label{exec20:med-population}
Stata's controlled-effect postestimation averages counterfactual predictions over covariates by default; its \texttt{atmeans} option evaluates them at covariate means. In the inspected Mplus moderation syntax, continuous control variables evaluated at zero can instead be evaluated at their means by centering them. A comparison must therefore specify the target population and the reference point before interpreting numerical disagreement.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_post,exec20_med_mplus_ch17}

For an exact nonlinear illustration, let $X$ be equally likely to equal $-1$ or 1 and stipulate
\begin{equation}
 P\{Y=1\mid do(A=a),do(M=m),X=x\}
   =\tfrac15+a\left(\tfrac1{10}+\tfrac{x^2}{10}\right).
 \label{exec20:med-population-law}
\end{equation}
The mediator has no role in this law. The population-average controlled effect is $1/5$, while evaluation at the mean covariate value $E(X)=0$ gives $1/10$. At an assumed value of USD 100 and cost of USD 15, those two targets imply margins of USD 5 and USD $-5$. The example isolates the averaging operation and does not claim that a selected vendor's available parametric family reproduces this law automatically.~\citep{exec20_med_reference}

The proportion mediated is also a derived quantity whose denominator and signs must remain visible. Under the simple stipulated law $M(a)=a$, $Y(a,m)=3a-2m$, the total effect is 1, the pure direct component is 3 and the total indirect component is $-2$. The ratio of indirect to total is $-2$, not a share between zero and one. With total effect approaching zero, such a ratio can become arbitrarily unstable even when the components are meaningful. Stata's postestimation manual normally refuses conflicting effect signs and permits an explicit \texttt{force} override. That override authorizes a computation, not an interpretation as a percentage of benefit.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_post,exec20_med_reference}

The separate StataNow two-mediator manual documents parallel and causally ordered sequential mediators. Its selected syntax supports continuous outcome and mediator variables, with binary or continuous treatment. Interaction switches distinguish treatment--mediator, mediator--mediator and first-mediator/treatment interactions in the second mediator equation. The stated model requires no unmeasured confounding across the treatment, mediator and outcome system, with independent zero-mean errors in the displayed parametric model. More path components create more scientific assumptions and more possible decompositions; they do not reveal a causal ordering from the data alone.~\citep{exec20_med_stata_multiple}

For a bank, understanding and liquidity may be parallel channels, understanding may change liquidity management, or an unobserved hardship shock may affect both. The business description does not select among these graphs. An assistant can draft alternatives, retrieve source-supported mechanisms and request the relevant measurement clocks. It should retain unresolved arrows and refuse to convert an expert's preferred story into verified mediator independence. The resulting graph and estimand should be reviewed before a vendor command is constructed.

\subsection{Sensitivity parameters do not define probability intervals}
\label{exec20:med-sensitivity}
The absence of a measured confounder can motivate a sensitivity analysis, but the parameter and its interpretation must be explicit. Consider the authored structural model
\begin{equation}
 M=aA+u,\qquad Y=cA+bM+e,
 \label{exec20:med-residual-law}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is independent of the errors, $\operatorname{Var}(u)>0$ and
$\rho=\operatorname{Corr}(u,e)$. After removing the linear contribution of $A$, the coefficient of $M$ in an ordinary outcome regression satisfies
\begin{equation}
 b_{\rm OLS}
   =b+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(u,e)}{\operatorname{Var}(u)}
   =b+\rho\frac{\sigma_e}{\sigma_u}.
 \label{exec20:med-bias}
\end{equation}
This follows directly by projecting $bu+e$ onto $u$. Under the stated additive structural intervention, the indirect effect is $ab$. If $a=2$, $b_{\rm OLS}=3$ and the structural scale ratio $\sigma_e/\sigma_u=2$ is assumed, varying $\rho$ over $[-1/2,1/2]$ produces effects from 4 to 8, with value 6 at $\rho=0$. At an assumed USD 100 per effect unit and USD 500 cost for a separately justified intervention, the corresponding arithmetic margins range from USD $-100$ to USD 300.~\citep{exec20_med_reference}

This is a set of declared sensitivity scenarios, not a 95\% confidence interval or a posterior credible interval. The structural scale ratio is an assumption; it must not be substituted silently by the residual standard deviation from a regression that has already projected part of $e$ onto $u$. Sampling uncertainty within each scenario is a separate calculation. The scenarios have not been shown to preserve every feature of a common observed law, so the displayed range is not claimed as a sharp identified set either.

Negative controls can provide additional falsification information only under their own exclusion and confounding structure. A variable called a negative control by an assistant or a user does not gain those properties by declaration. The present commercial comparison has not completed a product-specific census of negative-control or sensitivity procedures. No failure to locate an option in these selected manuals establishes that a vendor or an extension lacks it. Completing that survey requires matched originals and explicit parameter mappings, not broader interpretation of an existing bootstrap confidence interval.

\subsection{Commercial alternatives and a proposed integration contract}
\label{exec20:med-integration}
Stata supplies a documented parametric causal-mediation workflow, including potential-outcome means, interaction, controlled effects, multiple output scales and several variance options. Mplus supplies a different modeling environment with latent-variable and conventional path capabilities alongside a counterfactual branch. These are substantive strengths against a proposal whose current executable evidence in this comparison consists of exact authored references. There is no measured accuracy, speed or integration advantage over either vendor. A future adapter should preserve their documented scientific conventions before comparing matched targets.

A complete edition-specific comparison remains open for three further families. These are SAS/STAT CAUSALMED, IBM/AMOS and PROCESS; each needs matched technical originals. The PROCESS author website establishes an independent extension for SPSS, SAS and R, and explains that its documentation is version sensitive. This extension must not be counted as a native IBM or SAS capability without verifying the concrete host, version, terms and implementation. The author's book page is a discovery source; the book and linked scholarly papers are not represented as inspected originals here. Missing technical evidence leaves a comparison unresolved and does not establish product absence.~\citep{exec20_med_process_lead}

The proposed library request should identify treatment and mediator intervention values, the permitted information clock, graph assumptions, natural/controlled/interventional/path-specific target, effect scale, interaction structure and target population. It should also identify resampling units, covariance options, sensitivity parameters and source versions. Outputs should preserve the four nested means when available, compatible decompositions, denominator/sign diagnostics, uncertainty type and unsupported status. A client must not receive a sensitivity range under a confidence-interval field or a latent-response coefficient product under a repayment-risk field.

CLI and MCP transport should preserve that same typed object, including failure and refusal. An agent can compare requested semantics with a vendor command and expose mismatches. It cannot supply missing ignorability, historical entitlement or calibration through a fluent explanation. The first public prototype experiments should check the exact interaction means, the two indistinguishable observed worlds, covariate averaging, conflicting effect signs and sensitivity arithmetic. Subsequent licensed numerical comparisons should measure fitting, conversion, review time and failed requests under frozen versions and generated mechanisms. Bank data and measured commercial value become relevant after those public experiments establish why a prototype deserves a costly institutional trial.

\section{R mediation: mechanism questions require an explicit intervention contract}
\label{sec:exec24-mediation-reuse}

A servicing intervention may improve repayment partly through customer contact,
understanding or a revised payment plan. A sponsor may ask how much of the total
benefit follows the proposed mechanism and whether investing in that mechanism
alone would reproduce the benefit. These are distinct questions. R
\texttt{mediation} 4.5.1 is a useful reference for model-based, design-based,
sensitivity and selected noncompliance analyses, but its fitted effects cannot
supply the scientific meaning of an intervention on understanding or contact.
The selected source is the cutoff-pinned CRAN distribution at commit dated
8 June 2025; its declared publication is 7 June. We distinguish that mirror
identity from the author repository and from the installed environment.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources}

The authentic bundled author paper has 40 pages and matches the title and five
authors in the package's 2014 citation. Its PDF was generated with the selected
2025 distribution; it is not asserted to be the final 38-page publisher layout.
It separates the general model-based approach from designs with different
identification assumptions, and explicitly states that treatment randomization
alone does not identify causal mediation effects. Its historical interface and
examples are read alongside the current pinned source.
\citep{exec24_mediation_original}

\subsection{Natural components, controlled interventions and a banking decision}
Write $M(t)$ for the mediator under treatment $t$ and $Y(t,m)$ for the outcome
under a joint treatment and mediator intervention. Natural indirect and direct
effects are
\[
 \delta(t)=E\{Y(t,M(1))-Y(t,M(0))\},\qquad
 \zeta(t)=E\{Y(1,M(t))-Y(0,M(t))\}.
\]
The total effect satisfies $\tau=\delta(0)+\zeta(1)=\delta(1)+\zeta(0)$.
The indices pair across treatment conditions; summing arbitrary same-index
components need not recover the total. The original's sequential ignorability
assumption adds mediator--outcome restrictions and support conditions beyond
treatment randomization. In particular, the standard identification route
excludes the relevant post-treatment confounding, even when a confounder is
measured. A model fit cannot verify this premise.
\citep{exec24_mediation_original}

In our authored model, let $M(t)=2t+U$ with $E(U)=1$ and
$Y(t,m)=t+3m+4tm$. The natural indirect effects are $(6,14)$ and the natural
direct effects $(5,13)$, giving total effect 19 through either correct pairing.
The same-index sums are 11 and 27. At a stipulated 100 USD per outcome unit,
the indirect component is 600 USD under one treatment setting and 1,400 USD
under the other. Neither is automatically the value of a stand-alone mediator
service. A controlled contrast sets $m$ to specified values; a natural effect
couples the mediator that would arise under another treatment to the outcome
under the selected treatment. A stochastic mediator intervention is another
target with its own intervention distribution and identification conditions.
\citep{exec24_mediation_contract}

The earlier project reference supplies a stronger negative example that we
preserve unchanged. With randomized fair binary $A$, independent fair $U$ and
$M(a)=a\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}U$, the worlds $Y(a,m)=a$ and
$Y(a,m)=m\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}U$ have the same observed distribution and
mediator overlap. Their natural decomposition differs. Thus even perfect
measurement of this observed law does not settle the mechanism without more
restrictions or information. The earlier controlled-intervention, negative
mediated-proportion and residual-sensitivity constructions remain separate
from any claim about a package's native execution.
\citep{exec20_med_reference}

\subsection{Preserve target labels, population and model-object semantics}
The inspected \texttt{mediate} frontend accepts fitted mediator and outcome
models, explicit treatment and control values, simulation count, bootstrap
choice and uncertainty options. If supplied categorical treatment values do
not match factor levels, it warns and falls back to the first two levels.
That is an observable source contract, not an acceptable silent conversion
for a banking API. A request for labels \texttt{none} and \texttt{offer}
cannot be treated as a request for \texttt{declined} and \texttt{offered}
without a verified mapping. The proposed adapter refuses unmapped labels and
returns the exact values used when a mapping is declared.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

When \texttt{dropobs} is enabled, the source merges the model frames and refits
on shared rows. It warns about possible cluster alignment problems with
missing observations. The observation population therefore belongs in the
result. Mediator identifiers $\{a,b,c\}$ and outcome identifiers
$\{b,c,d\}$ intersect at only $\{b,c\}$. The common sample represents half
the union, not an unchanged four-customer target. An adapter must retain the
original row identities, model-frame membership, missingness rule and refitted
sample, and must not use row counts as a substitute for matching identities.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

The source treats prior model weights as sampling weights, warns when a
binomial mediator model could invite a trial-count interpretation, and checks
matching weights between the relevant model frames. Effects $(0,10)$ with
weights $(1,9)$ average to 9, while equal weights give 5. Importing binomial
trial totals as population weights can therefore change the target, not just
its standard error. Dependency objects and their classes must retain this
meaning through CLI or MCP serialization.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

\subsection{Simulation, sampling uncertainty and sensitivity are different objects}
The frontend warns that its \texttt{cluster} and \texttt{robustSE} options are
ignored for the ordinary nonparametric bootstrap path; the inspected call uses
\texttt{boot::boot} with an ordinary simulation setting. Some mixed-model
combinations instead require the non-bootstrap path. A cluster option in a
request cannot be assumed to qualify the returned interval. The adapter must
record the executed uncertainty route, warnings and sampling unit and refuse
an unsupported dependent-data claim. In an authored exchangeable example with
20 independent customers, 25 unit-variance visits each and within-customer
correlation 0.6, mean variance is 0.0308 rather than the independent-visit
0.002. This is an analytical warning example, not a native coverage experiment.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

The inspected quasi-Bayesian branch summarizes the proportion mediated using
the median of simulated effect ratios, while the bootstrap branch forms a
ratio from point effects. These are distinct summaries. Indirect draws
$(1,2,9)$ and total draws $(1,4,10)$ have median ratio 0.9 and ratio of means
0.8. The bridge should preserve both the field definition and the underlying
draws when needed, not compare unlike summaries as a numerical defect. If an
indirect effect is 1 and a direct effect minus 1, total effect is zero and the
proportion is undefined. Negative or greater-than-one proportions can also
have a valid algebraic origin; coercing all outputs to a percentage in
$[0,1]$ destroys their meaning.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract,exec20_med_reference}

The selected \texttt{medsens} documentation and branches support specific
linear and binary-probit combinations; binary-outcome interactions and a
logit outcome are excluded in the inspected route. The sensitivity parameter
$\rho$ represents a hypothetical association of model errors. Varying it does
not estimate actual hidden confounding or create a confidence interval over
all possible causal worlds. The linear branch solves stacked equations at
successive $\rho$ values using a coefficient-change stopping condition; its
inspected loop has no explicit iteration budget. A prototype must bound
process time, preserve nonconvergence and singular-matrix failures, and test
the requested grid and tolerance. A smaller numerical error cannot cure an
unsupported causal restriction.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_original,exec20_med_reference}

\subsection{Design and noncompliance routes retain their own assumptions}
The single-experiment interface distinguishes a sequential-ignorability point
analysis from a bound calculation. The parallel-design interface distinguishes
a no-interaction point route from its alternative bound route. Binary coding,
missing-row removal, experimental assignment and mediator manipulation must be
retained; a bound on a natural effect is not a sampling confidence interval
or proof of a feasible mediator intervention. The original emphasizes that
mediator manipulation needs an interpretation with no unintended direct effect.
Its selected parallel-design page contains differing prose and displayed
numerical values; we do not treat those numbers as reproduced evidence.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_original}

The multiple-mediator documentation describes a varying-coefficient linear
structural model, point effects under homogeneous treatment--mediator
interaction, and sensitivity to interaction heterogeneity. This is a specific
route for selected post-treatment confounding, not a general permission to
adjust for any downstream variable. The noncompliance interface targets
complier-local mediation and direct effects, distinct from a full-population
effect. Its source documentation limits model families and notes potentially
expensive numerical integration. The present reference decision does not
claim a complete proof or implementation audit of those delegates.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources}

Numerical failure can change the averaging population. In the inspected
continuous-mediator path with covariates, non-finite integration values are
excluded from each component's mean and issue counts are returned. The
no-covariate path extracts integration values without the same per-observation
issue accounting. These are branch-specific static observations. If stipulated
complete contributions are $(1,3,100)$ but the last is unresolved, averaging
the finite values gives 2 instead of the complete mean $104/3$. We do not
impute the missing contribution or call the selected mean the original target.
The worker must return counts, integration status, component-specific retained
sets and a declared failure policy. Different retained sets can also undermine
an apparent decomposition identity, even when each scalar looks finite.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

\subsection{A bounded reuse decision and public qualification route}
We retain mediation 4.5.1 as a reference and comparison candidate. A conditional
original R worker can preserve fitted classes, uncertainty choices and design
objects better than an unmotivated complete port. Direct admission is deferred
until the chosen scope has rights, dependency, scientific and numerical
qualification. We reject automatic interpretation of natural components as
stand-alone banking interventions and automatic promotion of sensitivity
scenarios to identified uncertainty. The declared GPL at least version 2,
R at least 2.15.1 and dependencies including MASS, Matrix, mvtnorm, sandwich,
lpSolve, Hmisc and lme4 identify requirements; they do not clear a complete
commercial distribution or establish continuing maintenance support.
\citep{exec24_mediation_sources,exec24_mediation_contract}

A public/generated study can first replay independent natural-effect
identities, observationally equivalent worlds, treatment interactions,
weighted populations, model-frame mismatches, categorical mappings, zero total
effects, clustered histories and integration failures. Freeze models, grids,
versions, seeds, warning/refusal policies, all denominators and full workflow
costs before final evaluation. Compare the exact target and the actual
uncertainty method, not merely returned field names. The scientific owner
justifies the mechanism and design; the adapter owner preserves semantics;
the rights/platform owner qualifies the environment; and the evaluator
maintains independent oracles and failed-request accounting. Illustrative
cost of 1,800 USD across 100 requests, 75 conformant outputs and 60 useful
outputs gives 18, 24 and 30 USD per denominator. These are not measured prices.
Unrestricted cross-world identification, hidden confounding, arbitrary
persistence and institutional acceptance remain unresolved by this reference
decision.~\citep{exec24_mediation_contract}

\section{Natural-effect models need an expansion and uncertainty contract}
\label{exec23:medflex-scope}
In a servicing proposal, a repayment holiday may change both immediate cash
availability and later contact engagement. An analyst may want to separate
those pathways. The medflex package offers natural-effect models using either
predictions from an outcome working model or ratio-of-mediator probability
weights. Its inspected 0.6-11 sources provide a concrete R implementation
reference. The proposed boundary must retain the exposure, mediator, baseline
covariates, original subject identity and nested-outcome scale. Expanding a
dataset creates computational rows; it supplies neither new independent
customers nor the scientific assumptions needed to interpret a mediation
contrast.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

\subsection{A decomposition whose components depend on their definition}
\label{exec23:medflex-target}
Write $M(x)$ for a mediator under exposure $x$ and $Y(x,m)$ for an outcome
under jointly specified exposure and mediator. Define
$\mu(x,a)=E\{Y(x,M(a))\}$. A pure direct contrast is
$\mu(1,0)-\mu(0,0)$, and its complementary total indirect contrast is
$\mu(1,1)-\mu(1,0)$. Another valid algebraic decomposition uses
$\mu(0,1)-\mu(0,0)$ and $\mu(1,1)-\mu(0,1)$. Both sum to
$\mu(1,1)-\mu(0,0)$, but their individual terms need not coincide.
These definitions are project notation; interpreting them through observed
data requires separate justification for the nested counterfactual law.

For an explicit constructed example, let $M(x)$ be binary with probabilities
$p_0=1/5$ and $p_1=3/5$, and stipulate
$Y(x,m)=10+2x+5m+4xm$. Direct enumeration gives
$\mu(0,0)=11$, $\mu(1,0)=69/5$, $\mu(0,1)=13$ and
$\mu(1,1)=87/5$. The first decomposition is $14/5+18/5$;
the second is $2+22/5$. Their common total is $32/5$. With
stipulated value USD 10 per outcome unit and total-policy cost USD 50,
the total net is USD 14. Substituting the pure direct component gives
USD $-22$ and answers a different question. A natural-effect component
is not automatically a feasible intervention with the same implementation
cost. The example illustrates target discipline, not measured bank economics
or an identification theorem.
\citep{exec23_medflex_reference}

\subsection{What the inspected expansion actually preserves}
\label{exec23:medflex-expansion}
The imputation interface expects exposure and mediator variables in a
prespecified formula order; its second predictor is the first mediator.
The weighting interface similarly interprets the first predictor as the
exposure. A wrapper must inspect the resulting variable-type pointers, factor
levels and contrasts instead of relying on a reordered display formula.
The expansion implementation creates an identifier from original row names
and joins hypothetical exposure values to that identifier. Categorical
exposures expand over their factor levels. Continuous exposures use a
specified replication count and either sampled values or conditional normal
quantiles, by default between the fifth and ninety-fifth percentiles. Those
finite nodes do not cover an unbounded distribution's entire support.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

The two paths are semantically distinct. Imputation predicts nested outcomes
and assigns the \texttt{impData} class. Weighting forms mediator probability
or density ratios and assigns \texttt{weightData}. The inspected
\texttt{expData} help text reverses those class descriptions and describes
row count using \texttt{length(data)}. The constructor paths and explicit
subject-count checks should govern a prospective adapter; the textual
discrepancies do not establish an observed numerical failure. The weighting
path restricts the joint mediator request to one mediator with a warning,
while imputation has a distinct multiple-mediator path. One generic method
label must not erase that difference.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

An exact probability example makes the denominator contract visible. If
the binary mediator probabilities are $1/5$ and $3/5$, the event ratio is
3 and the nonevent ratio is $1/2$. Reversing the ratio changes the weighting
target. At zero denominator probability the ratio is undefined; a production
profile must specify overlap diagnostics and refusal rather than silently
changing the estimand through clipping. Optional exposure weights introduce
another denominator and another model. The inspected helper verifies exposure
and covariate pointers, and suppresses warnings around its final GLM call.
Consequently, proposed validation must inspect convergence, finite outputs,
weights and model objects; the absence of a displayed warning is insufficient.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources,exec23_medflex_reference}

\subsection{Full refitting and original-subject covariance}
\label{exec23:medflex-uncertainty}
The default natural-effect fit requests 1,000 bootstrap replicates. Its
bootstrap callback resamples original data indices, refits the working model,
maps each original index to its expanded rows, refreshes the expansion's
predictions or weights, refits an optional exposure model and refits the
natural-effect model. This source path supports a full-refitting design;
it does not authorize freezing all first-stage fitted values in a wrapper.
Its index arithmetic also makes original ordering and constant replication
count a contract to verify, especially after missing-value handling, slicing
or serialization. The helper recovers data by evaluating the stored model
call in the global environment, so a portable job needs explicit data custody
and reconstructed call bindings.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

The robust branch accepts only GLM working models and restricts selected
weighting/exposure families to Gaussian, binomial or Poisson. It aggregates
the expanded natural-effect scores by original identifier, combines them
with nuisance scores and includes off-diagonal derivative blocks in the
sandwich calculation. This is not the covariance from treating every expanded
row as an independent borrower. It also does not, by itself, justify arbitrary
branch, household or calendar dependence beyond the original-subject unit.
The original mediator is excluded from the final natural-effect formula;
an imputation model that does not reflect the final model structure generates
an explicit warning.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

Two independent score examples show why these distinctions matter. Original
subject scores $(-1,1)$ give the empirical mean-score variance
$\sum_i s_i^2/n^2=1/2$. Duplicating each subject twice and treating four
rows as independent gives $1/4$. Separately let
$A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
and $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The first diagonal element of $A^{-1}BA^{-T}$ is 7, versus 1 if
the nuisance contribution is frozen. These are exact stipulated score laws,
not package outputs or measured finite-sample coverage. The package's
bootstrap interval helper defaults to a normal interval; requesting a
different interval type is an explicit inference choice.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources,exec23_medflex_reference}

\subsection{Reference decision and a bounded integration route}
\label{exec23:medflex-decision}
Retain the inspected package as a reference for natural-effect semantics,
subject expansion and full uncertainty propagation. An isolated R worker
is a plausible future prototype route once the complete artifact, environment,
dependency notices and actual delivery rights are bound. The GPL-2 declaration
and a separately identified local license text do not clear transitive rights.
Direct adoption of every supported learner and mediator family is premature;
a port to Python, Java or C++ would also inherit dispatch, formula, missingness,
bootstrap and covariance maintenance obligations without a measured advantage.
\citep{exec23_medflex_sources}

The initial qualification matrix should use binary exposure and one mediator,
compare weighting and imputation on an independently specified law, preserve
the nested target and link scale, and test subject permutation, duplicated
rows, missingness and fresh-process data binding. Compare bootstrap refitting
against the robust branch only within their shared model restrictions,
retain seed and backend choices, and reject unsupported or nonfinite results
with complete diagnostics. More general mediation identification, clustered
or longitudinal sampling and empirical utility require separate evidence.
This reference decision approves no native adapter and establishes no
implemented banking capability.

\section{Two complementary sensitivity references for a banking decision}
\label{exec23:sensitivity-reuse-scope}
A lending or servicing analysis can return a precise estimate while remaining
vulnerable to omitted borrower information. A useful product proposal must
explain which perturbation a sensitivity tool studies, which sampling
uncertainty it preserves, and what changes in the decision if the perturbation
is credible. Two concrete R candidates illustrate why one generic robustness
field would be inadequate. The inspected CRAN mirror revisions are
sensemakr 0.1.6, dated 23 July 2024, and EValue 4.1.4, dated 29 August 2025.
Their immutable trees authenticate thirteen selected source and documentation
blobs. They are different engines: the former develops regression sensitivity
in partial-$R^2$ coordinates; the latter includes risk-ratio sensitivity
thresholds and several effect-scale conversions.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr,exec23_sensitivity_evalue}

The package declarations are GPL-3 and GPL-2 respectively. The audit retains
those declarations and separately identifies local operating-system copies of
the license texts; it does not represent the latter as newly acquired package
release files or a review of every dependency. sensemakr declares R 3.1.0 or
later and suggests additional packages including fixest. EValue imports
several plotting, meta-analysis and data-processing dependencies. A named
maintainer and dated CRAN snapshot establish concrete provenance, but do not
promise future support or a complete reproducible environment. The reference
route below is therefore distinct from permission to distribute an adapter.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr,exec23_sensitivity_evalue}

\subsection{Regression sensitivity preserves its covariance convention}
\label{exec23:sensitivity-reuse-regression}
Let $\widehat\beta$ be the short-regression treatment coefficient, $s$ its
standard error and $\nu$ the residual degrees of freedom. Write
$a=R^2_{D\sim U\mid X}$ for omitted information $U$'s partial explanatory
strength for treatment $D$, conditional on recorded covariates $X$, and
$b=R^2_{Y\sim U\mid D,X}$ for its partial strength for outcome $Y$.
These are sensitivity restrictions, not estimates of an observed confounder.
The selected numeric helpers implement bias magnitude and adjusted standard
error
\[
 B=s\sqrt{\frac{\nu ab}{1-a}},\qquad
 s_{\mathrm{adj}}=s\sqrt{\frac{1-b}{1-a}\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}.
\]
The sign and direction of adjustment are separate arguments: reducing a
positive coefficient gives $\widehat\beta-B$, while increasing its magnitude
is a different request. A credible banking report must identify the actual
regression, outcome units, adjustment set and sensitivity direction before
interpreting either number.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr}

For an entirely stipulated exact example, take $\widehat\beta=3$, $s=1$,
$\nu=8$, $a=1/5$ and $b=1/2$. Substitution gives $B=1$, adjusted coefficient
2 and adjusted variance $s_{\mathrm{adj}}^2=5/7$. Suppose one coefficient
unit is valued at USD 10 and the intervention costs USD 25. The original
point-based net is USD 5, whereas the sensitivity-adjusted point-based net
is USD $-5$. The sign change explains why plausible omitted-information
restrictions matter; it is not a measured treatment benefit, a confidence
statement or an empirical finding from either package.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_reference}

In the inspected fixest path, model extraction explicitly requests iid
covariance. A warning in the corresponding adjustment path identifies this
restriction. Consequently, accepting a clustered model object does not justify
claiming that the original clustered uncertainty survives the sensitivity
calculation. The numeric helper's degrees-of-freedom guard admits zero and
one, while its adjusted-variance expression contains $\nu-1$ in the denominator.
The partial-$R^2$ validator admits the endpoint $a=1$, while the bias expression
contains $1-a$. These are static domain observations. The proposed regular
profile requires finite inputs, $s>0$, $\nu>1$, $0\leq a<1$ and $0\leq b<1$;
degenerate boundaries receive an explicit separate disposition instead of
being presented as a finite scientific result. No native failure is inferred
from source inspection alone.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr}

Two further details belong in the qualification protocol. The direct
\texttt{adjusted\_ci.numeric} helper uses $\nu-1$ in its critical-value
calculation; the inspected numeric wrapper constructs intervals with $\nu$.
Their uncertainty outputs cannot be promised identical without resolving the
intended convention. The wrapper also passes an estimate to an adjusted-SE
helper that accepts an ellipsis. That extra argument is accepted by the
signature; it is not evidence of an unused-argument error. Observed-covariate
benchmarking introduces additional assumptions through separate treatment and
outcome multipliers. Its implementation rejects impossible treatment bounds
and warns before capping an implied outcome bound above one. The adapter must
retain those warnings, raw restrictions and benchmark choices, rather than
turning a benchmark label into evidence that the omitted variable exists or
is no stronger than the chosen observed variable.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr}

\subsection{Risk-ratio sensitivity requires an explicit effect scale}
\label{exec23:sensitivity-reuse-ratio}
For the null target and a strictly positive observed risk ratio $r$, orient
its magnitude as $q=\max(r,1/r)$. The selected threshold calculation reduces to
\[
 E=q+\sqrt{q(q-1)}.
\]
The package describes this as confounding strength required to shift the
observed association to the specified target. It is not an estimated
confounder strength, a corrected treatment effect or a probability that the
causal claim is true. The interface also permits a non-null target, which must
remain an explicit field rather than silently inheriting the null formula.
It reports the relevant near-target confidence limit separately; a supplied
interval crossing the target receives threshold one. That result does not
establish that the effect is zero, and the original interval's sampling
justification remains the caller's responsibility.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_evalue}

The reciprocal ratios $9/5$ and $5/9$ both give threshold 3, while the null
ratio 1 gives threshold 1. These exact arithmetic symmetries are useful
conversion oracles. They do not test finite-sample interval coverage. The
source rejects negative point ratios and inconsistent interval ordering, but
its zero-inclusive checks and reciprocal operations leave singular boundary
behavior for native qualification. The proposed worker instead declares a
strictly positive finite ratio and target, compatible bounds, event coding,
population and horizon; an infinite or missing result is not renamed strong
causal evidence.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_evalue,exec23_sensitivity_reference}

The broader package offers different conversions for odds ratios, hazard
ratios, standardized mean differences and linear-regression coefficients.
For example, its nonrare-outcome odds-ratio conversion uses a square root.
This is an approximation, not a universal algebraic identity. For odds ratio
$\mathrm{OR}=81/25$, that conversion is $9/5$. Under a stipulated baseline
risk $p_0=1/5$, the exact risk-ratio identity for those two risks is instead
\[
 \mathrm{RR}=\frac{\mathrm{OR}}{1-p_0+p_0\mathrm{OR}}
             =\frac{405}{181},
\]
which differs from $9/5$. A banking interface that discards event prevalence
or the rare-outcome flag would conceal this distinction. The OLS route needs
an exposure contrast and a standard deviation; its documentation also states
that interval conversion treats the supplied standard deviation as known.
The conversion history and its additional assumptions must accompany the
returned sensitivity number.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_evalue,exec23_sensitivity_reference}

\subsection{Reference decisions and the bounded prototype route}
\label{exec23:sensitivity-reuse-decision}
Retain both packages as method-specific proposal references. A future isolated
R worker is a conditional route for a declared regression-sensitivity or
risk-ratio profile, after complete artifact and dependency custody, applicable
rights review and native conformance. Direct adoption of every package family
would overextend this inspection. A new Python, Java or C++ port is not
justified without a measured platform or maintenance advantage sufficient to
cover dispatch, numerical, uncertainty and upgrade obligations. Independent
arithmetic remains a matched oracle, not a substitute calibrated inference
service. Neither package replaces graph identification, trend sensitivity,
selection-bias analysis or a different confounding model merely because all
are called sensitivity analysis.
\citep{exec23_sensitivity_sensemakr,exec23_sensitivity_evalue,exec23_sensitivity_reference}

The scientific owner must specify the causal question, adjustment clock,
sampling law and plausibility of sensitivity restrictions. The maintainer
must preserve model and row identity, covariance type, full conversion
history, warnings and failures; compare direct helpers with numeric and model
dispatch; and register singular-input and incompatible-interval cases. The
operator must bind an actual delivery, dependencies and resource budget.
Qualification then compares the selected outputs with independent references
on public or generated data before expensive bank experiments. Changes in
source, target, covariance law or delivery trigger re-review. The two reference
decisions settle the proposal's present reuse choice while granting no native
integration capability or general sensitivity guarantee.

\section[R mediation and sensitivity contracts]{R mediation and sensitivity: preserving the intervention, the mixture and the unit of uncertainty}
\label{sec:exec25-rmed}

A bank considering a servicing intervention needs to distinguish an improvement
caused by the intervention from an improvement transmitted through a particular
mechanism. Let $A$ denote an offer of restructuring, $M$ subsequent payment
engagement, and $Y$ loss over a fixed horizon. Baseline information $W$ precedes
the offer. A later liquidity assessment $Z$ may be changed by the offer and
affect both engagement and loss. The question therefore requires a declared
intervention on engagement, a target population, an information clock and
assumptions linking the intervention to observed data. R packages supply useful
approaches, but their targets and uncertainty calculations are not interchangeable.
This comparison extends the preceding mediation, medflex, sensemakr and EValue
discussions with CMAverse, medoutcon, regmedint, causalsens, tipr and BayesGmed.
Inspected sources and authored examples support conditional reuse decisions;
they do not establish native qualification. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{A causal target before a software method}

Write $Y_{a,m}$ for the outcome after setting offer and mediator to $(a,m)$,
and $M_a$ for the mediator under offer $a$. Two complementary natural
decompositions on an additive scale are
\[
 \operatorname{TE}=E(Y_{1,M_1}-Y_{0,M_0})
 =\operatorname{PNDE}+\operatorname{TNIE}
 =\operatorname{TNDE}+\operatorname{PNIE}.
\]
Here $\operatorname{PNDE}=E(Y_{1,M_0}-Y_{0,M_0})$ and
$\operatorname{TNIE}=E(Y_{1,M_1}-Y_{1,M_0})$; the other pair interchanges the
reference treatment. The controlled direct effect $E(Y_{1,m}-Y_{0,m})$ instead
holds engagement at the same specified level. It need not equal a natural
effect, and subtracting it from TE need not yield a natural indirect effect.
The selected BayesGmed primary text develops these distinctions under consistency,
positivity, no unmeasured confounding and a cross-world condition. Randomizing
the offer alone does not supply every mediation assumption; a posterior interval
does not establish them either. \citep{exec25_rmed_yimer_paper}

When offer-induced liquidity confounds engagement and loss, an alternative
draws a new mediator $G_b$ from the distribution of $M_b$ conditional on $W$,
independently of the realized intermediate confounder under the declared
intervention law. Let $\mu(a,z,m,w)=E(Y\mid A=a,Z=z,M=m,W=w)$. The selected
D\'iaz and colleagues paper identifies the corresponding mean as
\[
 \theta(a,b)=\int \mu(a,z,m,w)\,p(z\mid a,w)\,
                  p(m\mid b,w)\,p(w)\,d(z,m,w).
\]
The identifying requirements include the relevant treatment and mediator
exchangeabilities, consistency and conditional support. This intervention
differs from drawing a mediator conditional on the intermediate confounder as
well as baseline information. Changing the conditioning set changes the
pathways assigned to direct and indirect components.
\citep{exec25_rmed_diaz_paper}

Define $\operatorname{IDE}=\theta(1,0)-\theta(0,0)$ and
$\operatorname{IIE}=\theta(1,1)-\theta(1,0)$. Their sum is the total contrast
under the randomized mediator intervention, which need not equal the ordinary
treatment effect. An authored example takes
$P(Z=1\mid A=0)=1/4$, $P(Z=1\mid A=1)=1/2$,
$P(M=1\mid Z)=1/4+Z/2$, and $Y=ZM$. The ordinary treatment effect is $3/16$,
whereas IDE is $3/32$ and IIE is $1/16$, summing to $5/32$. All mediator
probabilities are positive. The difference arises from changing dependence in
the intervention, not estimation error. The product must label the two totals
explicitly. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{CMAverse: a broad interface with a consequential draw contract}

CMAverse version 0.1.0 documents regression, weighting, inverse odds weighting,
natural-effect, marginal structural and g-formula approaches. Its interface
restricts methods when post-treatment confounders are supplied and changes
natural-effect labels to randomized analogues. Parameter-function and imputation
estimators have different applicability; the documented logistic and Cox
parameter-function cases require a rare outcome. Its sensitivity interface
distinguishes unmeasured confounding from measurement error and documents a
single-variable measurement-error scope. These are useful capabilities to
compare, provided the proposed product exposes method, scale and restrictions.
\citep{exec25_rmed_cmaverse}

The selected g-formula delegate simulates intermediate confounders from
exposure and baseline covariates, then mediators using those variables and the
simulated intermediate values. In its post-treatment-confounder branch, it
subsequently permutes each mediator column across eligible rows. Outcome
prediction retains the row's baseline and intermediate variables. Global
permutation requires a different justification from a draw conditional on that
row's baseline information; separate column permutations also require
justification when a joint mediator law is intended. \citep{exec25_rmed_cmaverse}

For $W\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, mediator probabilities $1/4$ and
$3/4$ in the two baseline groups, and outcome regression $WM$, the
baseline-conditioned stochastic mean is $3/8$. A global shuffle pairs each
row with the marginal mediator probability $1/2$, yielding $1/4$ in expectation.
For two identical balanced binary mediators, $E(M_1M_2)=1/2$; independently
permuting columns yields $1/4$ in expectation. These exact witnesses specify
invariants for an adapter. They are neither native failure frequencies nor a
rejection of g-computation. The route is to declare the conditional joint draw,
reconcile the delegate, and retain an explicitly different target or a separately
reviewed repair. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

Conditional R invocation is preferable to porting a large method collection
before resolving its target. Requests should carry mediator order, conditioning
set, treatment contrast, scale, simulation budget and random state. Resampling
units must match the data law: a row bootstrap is not automatically branch-cluster
or shared-calendar inference. Imputation, simulation integration and bootstrap
variation require separate accounting. The GPL declaration and full dependency
inventory also accompany contemplated redistribution; this source review does
not clear every artifact. \citep{exec25_rmed_cmaverse,exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{medoutcon: efficient scores and the sampling contract}

medoutcon targets natural and interventional effects using one-step and targeted
minimum loss estimators. The primary paper assumes independent, identically
distributed observations, derives an efficient influence function, and requires
positivity and products of nuisance-estimation errors vanishing faster than
$n^{-1/2}$. Cross-fitting relaxes empirical-process restrictions; it does not
prove those rates for arbitrary learners or remove hidden confounding and
dependence. A low-dimensional intermediate-confounder representation avoids
directly estimating a high-dimensional mediator density, a useful advantage
for multivariate engagement measurements within the stated conditions.
\citep{exec25_rmed_diaz_paper}

The selected source switches to natural effects when $Z$ is explicitly absent,
constructs binary contrasts, and rescales outcomes by their observed range.
A constant outcome makes that scaling denominator zero. Contrasts subtract
influence scores before variance calculation; propensity clipping must be
recorded as a numerical choice rather than proof of population support. For
two-phase mediator measurement, the source uses
\[
 D_{\mathrm{obs}}=\frac{R}{\pi}D
                 -\left(\frac{R}{\pi}-1\right)m_D,
\]
where $R$ indicates observation, $\pi$ is inclusion probability and $m_D$
predicts the complete-data influence quantity. With $D=3$, $m_D=1$ and
$\pi=1/2$, the observed and missing cases give 5 and 1, averaging to 3 under
that law. False inclusion probabilities do not become valid through calculation.
\citep{exec25_rmed_medoutcon,exec25_rmed_choice}

In the inspected one-step survey-weight branch the estimate is a weighted
mean, but the variance expression multiplies the influence quantity by supplied
weights without corresponding normalization. For scores $(1,3)$, changing
weights from $(1,1)$ to $(2,2)$ retains estimate 2 while changing the displayed
variance-of-the-mean expression from 1 to 4. Requiring normalized weights may
reconcile that convention; an adapter accepting arbitrary relative weights
cannot silently assume it. The proportion-mediated option is a log-ratio
functional, distinct from an additive proportion. These observations motivate
qualification tests rather than establish installed-estimator coverage.
\citep{exec25_rmed_medoutcon,exec25_rmed_choice}

The conditional adoption route retains the R learner stack until independent
fixtures establish target, score, contrast, rescaling and uncertainty parity.
Public trials should include weak support, constants, weight rescaling,
incomplete mediator measurement and deliberately invalid dependent sampling.
A port needs a deployment benefit sufficient to maintain these scientific
invariants. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{regmedint: interactions and conditional effects}

regmedint supplies a more explicit parametric alternative, carrying baseline
evaluation values, mediator and outcome model families, treatment--mediator
interaction and effect modification. Its case-control branch fits the mediator
in controls and states a rare-outcome requirement. The selected delta
calculation constructs separate regression covariance blocks. Evaluation at
mean covariates is not automatically population standardization, and a block
covariance is not automatically appropriate for clustered servicing data.
\citep{exec25_rmed_regmedint}

Consider $M=1+2A+\varepsilon_M$ and
$E(Y\mid A,M)=3A+4M+5AM$, with mean-zero disturbance and the necessary causal
assumptions. PNDE and TNIE are 8 and 18; TNDE and PNIE are 18 and 8; TE is
26. The controlled direct effect at zero is 3, and the coefficient product
$2\times4=8$ gives only one indirect component. Output must retain interaction,
reference treatment and mediator setting. Proportion mediated also needs a
nonzero interpretable total and need not lie between zero and one.
\citep{exec25_rmed_regmedint,exec25_rmed_choice}

Use this package conditionally as a parametric comparator and fixture source.
When a marginal effect is needed, define the standardization explicitly.
Logistic formulas, log effects and rare-outcome approximations should be checked
against exact binary enumeration at both small and substantial baseline risks.
A port should preserve the chosen approximation rather than present an exact
general risk decomposition. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{Sensitivity measures have units, directions and domains}

causalsens asks how a linear outcome analysis changes under a specified
selection-bias function. It adjusts outcomes and refits the linear model using
model-based uncertainty. Its one-sided adjustment is $\alpha(A-e)$ for
propensity $e$, whereas alignment uses
$\alpha\{(1-e)A+e(1-A)\}$. With $e=1/2$ and $\alpha=4$, the first reduces a
treated-minus-control mean gap by 4; the second subtracts 2 from both groups
and leaves the gap unchanged. Treated-population functions differ again.
Thus $\alpha$ carries outcome units and a confounding direction. A sensitivity
curve does not identify a mediation mechanism. \citep{exec25_rmed_causalsens,exec25_rmed_choice}

tipr provides coefficient adjustment and tipping-point interfaces. Its selected
partial-$R^2$ and robustness functions delegate to sensemakr. A simple coefficient
adjustment subtracts a declared bias product. Its numeric E-value helper applies
$r+\sqrt{r(r-1)}$, after reciprocating an input at or below one, without a
baseline-risk or effect-scale argument. An odds ratio of 2 at baseline risk
$1/2$ corresponds to risk ratio $4/3$. Applying the formula to 2 gives about
3.414, whereas applying it to $4/3$ gives 2. This exact example rejects silent
scale substitution; it does not supply a universal hazard-ratio conversion.
Nonpositive inputs and intervals crossing the null need explicit handling.
\citep{exec25_rmed_tipr,exec25_rmed_choice}

The inspected boundary helper contains a fallback that resets outcome
partial-$R^2$ when an interval misses its zero-boundary check. An adapter must
preserve the requested scenario and verify its endpoint, reporting a failed
boundary calculation when appropriate. Conditional interface reuse can build
on the previously reviewed underlying methods while keeping their scientific
and numerical contracts visible. \citep{exec25_rmed_tipr,exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{BayesGmed: verify the latent-state likelihood before sampling}

BayesGmed offers Bayesian parametric g-computation for continuous or binary
mediators and outcomes. Its selected primary text describes independent
observations, identifying assumptions, correctly specified conditional models,
posterior simulation and bootstrap integration over baseline covariates.
The sensitivity discussion introduces a binary unobserved confounder with a
prevalence model and bias parameters. The paper describes uniform bias priors;
selected version 0.0.3 instead supplies a multivariate Gaussian prior through a
covariance matrix. These are different analyses requiring explicit justification.
The wrapper's link arguments also do not alter its fixed binary/continuous
dispatch. Unsupported link requests should be rejected rather than reported
as model changes. \citep{exec25_rmed_yimer_paper,exec25_rmed_bayesgmed}

More fundamentally, if $p=P(U=1\mid A)$ and $f_u(m,y)$ is the conditional
mediator--outcome density in state $u$, the normalized mixture is
\[
 L(m,y)=(1-p)f_0(m,y)+p f_1(m,y).
\]
All four inspected Stan sensitivity files attach $\log(1-p)$ to both latent
states before log-sum-exp. The two inspected generated C++ counterparts retain
the same expression. It therefore becomes
\[
 L_{\mathrm{src}}(m,y)=(1-p)\{f_0(m,y)+f_1(m,y)\},
 \qquad \int L_{\mathrm{src}}=2(1-p).
\]
This is a static normalization discrepancy at the selected revision, not a
measured sampler failure rate. It concerns the inspected sensitivity paths
and does not by itself reject ordinary models or Bayesian mediation generally.
\citep{exec25_rmed_bayesgmed}

An exact binary witness takes $p=4/5$, mediator probabilities $(1/4,3/4)$ and
outcome probabilities $(1/5,4/5)$ in states zero and one, conditionally
independent within each state. The intended probability of $(M,Y)=(1,1)$ is
$49/100$. The source expression gives $13/100$ and sums to $2/5$ over the
four observations. Renormalization gives $13/40$, still different because it
substitutes equal mixing weights. When the components coincide, the intended
likelihood loses dependence on $p$, but the source expression retains it.
These checks should precede expensive sampling. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

Defer the selected sensitivity implementation until mixture, prior, link and
artifact semantics are reconciled. A repair needs a versioned successor,
independent likelihood enumeration, posterior and predictive checks,
integration diagnostics and public known-law comparisons. Ordinary-model
review can proceed independently. Rights also need clarification: package
metadata declares MIT while a retained separate Stan license include states
GPL3 or later. Its applicability to a redistributed artifact is unresolved;
neither an installed binary nor all dependency rights is qualified here.
\citep{exec25_rmed_bayesgmed,exec25_rmed_choice}

\subsection{A common contract and public qualification route}

A CLI or MCP request should name baseline and post-treatment variables,
mediator ordering and joint law, intervention conditioning, treatment values,
target population, outcome scale and horizon, sampling units, measurement
inclusion law and sensitivity parameters with their units. Responses should
distinguish unsupported identification, support failure, undefined ratios,
numerical failure and an admissible estimate. They should retain exact source
revision, method, prior, folds, resampling unit, integration budget and
uncertainty definition. An LLM can help elicit declarations and present
alternatives, but cannot infer scientific truth or choose a conditioning set
merely to make a backend run. \citep{exec25_rmed_choice}

Public qualification starts with finite known-law examples, followed by frozen
simulations with supported and intentionally unsupported structures. Parametric
formulas, semiparametric scores, sensitivity wrappers and Bayesian likelihoods
can be tested independently. Complete request counts, refusals, target errors,
interval behavior and end-to-end costs are needed. For hypothetical USD 30,000
spent on 1,000 requests, 600 returned results and 400 scientifically admissible
results, costs are USD 30, USD 50 and USD 75 per respective denominator. Runtime

\section[Python mediation and sensitivity contracts]{Python mediation and sensitivity: effects, resampling, and the limits of computed bounds}
\label{sec:exec25-pymed}

A bank evaluating a digital servicing channel needs more than a coefficient labelled indirect effect. Let $A$ denote the assigned channel, $M$ a subsequent customer response, $X$ information fixed before assignment, and $Y$ the net outcome over a specified horizon. A response measured after a collection decision cannot silently become a baseline adjustment variable. The proposal therefore treats the observation clock, intervention, population and outcome scale as part of the request. A package comparison is useful only after these quantities have been fixed. The Python ecosystem supplies several different services: regression path summaries, model-based potential-outcome integration, graph-guided orchestration, and partial-identification calculations. They should be compared at the level of their estimands and failure conditions rather than placed behind one interchangeable mediation command. \cite{exec25_pymed_statsmodels,exec25_pymed_pingouin,exec25_pymed_dowhy,exec25_pymed_process,exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{An estimand contract before a package choice}
Write $M(a)$ for the mediator under assignment $a$, and $Y(a,m)$ for the outcome under joint interventions. A useful nested mean is
\[
 \theta(a,a')=E\{Y(a,M(a'))\}.
\]
For a binary contrast, $\theta(1,1)-\theta(1,0)$ is one natural indirect effect and $\theta(1,0)-\theta(0,0)$ its complementary direct effect. Their sum is the total effect, provided these nested quantities are justified. The corresponding definitions with the outcome exposure fixed at zero need not give the same indirect effect when there is interaction. Identification additionally requires a defensible causal model, consistency, overlap, and the relevant treatment--mediator and mediator--outcome exchangeability or stronger cross-world restrictions. Neither a fitted path nor a sensitivity plot certifies those assumptions. The preceding R comparison develops alternative interventional targets; the Python interface must retain that distinction. \cite{exec25_pymed_choice,exec25_pymed_sharp_paper}

For a concrete scale check, suppose $P\{M(0)=1\}=1/4$, $P\{M(1)=1\}=3/4$, and $E\{Y(a,m)\}=3a+4m$. Under an otherwise identified law, either natural indirect effect is $4(3/4-1/4)=2$. A logistic regression of the binary mediator on $A$ instead has slope $\log 9$; multiplying this slope by the outcome coefficient gives $4\log9\simeq8.789$. This is a coefficient product on mixed scales, not the same outcome contrast. Reporting the latter number as the former would change the business quantity before any uncertainty calculation begins. This is an authored population example, not an observed error rate from a fitted package. \cite{exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{Potential-outcome simulation versus regression paths}
The inspected statsmodels mediation implementation is a useful reference for explicit counterfactual construction. Its formula route rebuilds the design matrix after exposure and mediator interventions; this matters for interaction terms. Its matrix route directly replaces columns and cannot be assumed to rebuild derived interaction columns. It simulates mediator draws, uses the same mediator realization for both outcome-exposure levels within a given mediator assignment, and computes direct and indirect differences. The selected loops fix assignments to zero and one, so another treatment contrast requires an explicit transformation or an extended implementation. \cite{exec25_pymed_statsmodels}

Uncertainty requires a separate review. The parametric route draws mediator and outcome parameters in separate calls. The bootstrap route calls a fitting helper twice; each call generates its own row indices. These operations do not preserve arbitrary cross-equation dependence merely because both equations originated in one customer table. An exact toy calculation makes the distinction visible. Draw two values with replacement from $\{-1,1\}$, and let $Z^*$ be their mean. Its distribution is $(-1,0,1)$ with probabilities $(1/4,1/2,1/4)$. If two fitted components share this perturbation, $E[(Z^*)^2]=1/2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(2Z^*)=2$. Independently drawing the components gives product expectation zero and sum variance one. This does not prove that every statsmodels mediation interval is invalid: joint covariance can vanish under additional model conditions. It proves that a shared-resample contract and a separate-resample contract need a stated justification. Customer, household and calendar dependence introduce further choices that an ordinary row bootstrap does not settle. \cite{exec25_pymed_statsmodels,exec25_pymed_choice}

Two smaller boundaries also deserve tests before exposure through a product interface. Initial method validation accepts a prefix beginning with \texttt{para}, whereas the iteration dispatch selects parameter simulation only for the exact string \texttt{parametric}; accepting a prefix can consequently change the computational route. The sign-tail expression for reported significance counts strict positive and strict negative draws. With four exact zeros its arithmetic returns zero, which is not evidence against the null. The proportion-mediated calculation also divides by the total effect without defining an economic interpretation when that total is zero or changes sign. We would wrap a narrowly qualified version with exact enumerations, typed zero-total results and an explicit sampling contract, rather than infer qualification from the API name. \cite{exec25_pymed_statsmodels,exec25_pymed_choice}

Pingouin exposes a compact parallel-mediation workflow. The inspected implementation drops incomplete rows, detects binary mediators and uses logistic mediator regressions for them, fits a linear outcome model, and returns products of the mediator and outcome coefficients. The source therefore directly motivates the mixed-scale example above. Its bootstrap applies one row-index array to the mediator and outcome inputs, preserving the sampled row pairing. Its bias-corrected interval is explicitly not an accelerated BCa interval, and its documentation warns that bootstrap sign tails are not a null-conditioned permutation test. The zero-point-estimate branch returns a significance value of one, unlike the strict-sign expression just discussed. Moreover, the displayed $Y\sim M+X$ path omits treatment while the coefficient used for the indirect product comes from the full treatment-and-mediator outcome model; the product should not be reconstructed by multiplying arbitrary displayed rows. \cite{exec25_pymed_pingouin}

For continuous linear models with appropriately justified assumptions, Pingouin can be a conditional comparator. For binary mediators it should be presented as a regression-path summary unless a separate target derivation is supplied. Complete-case selection changes the analyzed population and requires its own missingness argument. Multiple mediator coefficients do not establish a causal ordering, joint intervention or absence of mediator--outcome confounding. These distinctions justify retaining the package as a candidate while rejecting a universal causal-effect interpretation. \cite{exec25_pymed_pingouin,exec25_pymed_choice}

The selected PyProcessMacro revision extends this regression-path family with moderated and serial constructions, categorical coding and outcome-family choices. Its serial path calculation multiplies coefficients along each ordered path. The bootstrap code fits the equations on the same resampled rows and retains a count of failed fits. It continues until the requested number of successful resamples is obtained, with an explicit failure cap. This is useful instrumentation, but the retained draws are conditional on successful fitting. A later public evaluation must report attempted, failed and accepted draws, separation and rank failures, and the effect of those failures on the reported interval. A request that produces enough successful draws has not thereby shown that discarded draws are ignorable. We would adopt the failure-accounting and shared-resample interface ideas, compare serial linear paths under an exact graph, and require a separate causal derivation for nonlinear coefficient products. \cite{exec25_pymed_process,exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{Graph orchestration does not make all two-stage estimates equivalent}
DoWhy supplies graph-oriented identification and estimator orchestration. In the inspected mediation branch, variables on directed paths are collected and separate adjustment searches are performed for the treatment--mediator and mediator--outcome stages. These are valuable, inspectable steps. The selected two-stage estimator nevertheless restricts treatment and mediator dimensions, defaults to linear regression stages, multiplies the two estimated effects, and obtains a direct effect by subtracting that product from an estimated total. The request must therefore bind the graph assumptions to this estimator's actual algebra. An identifier returning a mediation expression is not certification of arbitrary nonlinear mediation. \cite{exec25_pymed_dowhy}

A nonunit contrast exposes an especially concrete interface issue. Suppose $M=2A+\varepsilon_M$ and $Y=3A+4M+\varepsilon_Y$, with suitable independent, mean-zero errors and an identified continuous treatment. Changing $A$ from zero to two gives an indirect effect $2\cdot4\cdot2=16$ and direct effect six. The selected source forwards the same control and treatment values to both stages. Their regression intervention contrasts are therefore four and eight; multiplying gives 32. Subtracting that from the total contrast 22 gives $-10$. The intended unit mediator slope has been replaced by a second nonunit contrast. The source route agrees with the product formula for a unit contrast under its linear restrictions; other contrasts need correction or explicit refusal. This is exact algebra for the inspected calls, not a native execution result. \cite{exec25_pymed_dowhy,exec25_pymed_choice}

DoWhy also offers distinct sensitivity analyzers rather than one universal sensitivity parameter. The inspected linear analyzer uses partial $R^2$ values and a bias factor proportional to
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{R^2_{Y\sim U\mid A,X}R^2_{A\sim U\mid X}}
 {1-R^2_{A\sim U\mid X}}}.
\]
The partially linear and nonparametric analyzers involve different scores, variance quantities and learner conditions. Its E-value path dispatches by outcome family and link; the logistic route chooses a rare-outcome approximation using an observed outcome-frequency threshold of 0.15. These contracts must remain separately named, with domains, scales, estimands and nuisance assumptions recorded. A generic refutation score should not be translated into an identified mediation effect or probability that the graph is true. Remaining learner delegates and validity arguments require their own inspection and public qualification. \cite{exec25_pymed_dowhy,exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{Sensitivity bounds with explicit mathematical scope}
The generalized marginal sensitivity model of Frauen, Melnychuk and Feuerriegel is particularly relevant to channel pathways. It bounds the change in the latent-confounder distribution between conditioning on treatment and intervening. For each relevant mediator or outcome node $W$, the selected paper uses bounds $s_W^-\leq1\leq s_W^+$ on a density ratio. Its framework allows a sequence of discrete mediators, but excludes additional unobserved confounding between mediators and the outcome for the stated path-effect interpretation. For a weighted construction, a sensitivity parameter $\Gamma_W\geq1$ and weight $q_W(a,x)\in[0,1]$ give
\[
 s_W^-=\frac{1}{(1-\Gamma_W)q_W+\Gamma_W},\qquad
 s_W^+=\frac{1}{(1-\Gamma_W^{-1})q_W+\Gamma_W^{-1}}.
\]
The binary-treatment specialization uses the observed treatment probability; the continuous specialization has $q_W=0$. These are restrictions on a possible data-generating law, not fitted probabilities that confounding is small. \cite{exec25_pymed_sharp_paper}

The paper derives extremal distribution shifts and a backward recursion over ordered mediator states. Sharpness has additional conditions: the intervention must leave the relevant latent distribution equal to its baseline conditional distribution, with the specified discrete-treatment feasibility condition or continuous treatment. The paper explicitly excludes its time-varying extension from that sharpness conclusion. Its inclusion of a longitudinal sensitivity model must therefore not become a claim of generally sharp longitudinal bounds. Nor does a weighted average of stratum-specific quantiles automatically equal the quantile of the mixed outcome distribution; the functional in the request must match the paper's definition. \cite{exec25_pymed_sharp_paper}

The selected SharpCausalSensitivity source implements binary, continuous and weighted variants, mediator-state enumeration, Monte Carlo outcome calculations and bootstrap ensembles. It also contains important boundaries for any port. The discrete mass-shift branch uses strict comparisons around a cumulative-probability threshold, with separate handling of the lagged cumulative probability. Take $\Gamma=3$ in the continuous-treatment sensitivity specialization, so $s^-=1/3$, $s^+=3$, and $c^+=3/4$. For an ordered binary mediator with probabilities $(3/4,1/4)$, the first category lands exactly on the threshold and satisfies none of the selected positive-mass conditions. The expressions then produce masses $(0,3/4)$, and the source's normalization assertion should reject them. The mathematical split at this boundary gives $(1/4,3/4)$. The assertion is protective; the missing equality case still needs reconciliation. \cite{exec25_pymed_sharp,exec25_pymed_choice}

The finite-sample continuous-mean routine also merits an explicit quadrature check. With four sample values, $\Gamma=2$, and the branch without a fitted outcome-mean regression, the cumulative sums include the index obtained by flooring the quantile position. For four unit values, the selected upper expression gives $7/8$ and the lower expression $5/4$, although a normalized weighted constant must remain one. This fixed-array diagnostic identifies a normalization and fractional-boundary requirement; it does not estimate how often trained neural distributions encounter such a configuration. Continuous integration should be checked against exact finite-support or analytic oracles, including translation of the outcome, small sample counts, ties and $\Gamma=1$. Selected binary-outcome paths explicitly raise \texttt{NotImplementedError}. We would retain the mathematical recursion as a design reference and defer this implementation's automatic admission pending these repairs and version-specific tests. \cite{exec25_pymed_sharp,exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{Neural bounds need both a theorem and numerical certificates}
NeuralCSA generalizes the sensitivity family using two conditional normalizing flows. The retained primary paper assumes independent observations, consistency, positivity and latent unconfoundedness. Its key sufficiency theorem concerns a transformation-invariant generalized treatment sensitivity model, an invertible map that exactly reproduces the observational outcome distribution, and optimization over the full admissible distribution class. That result motivates the two-stage construction. It does not say that any fitted flow, finite Monte Carlo sample and stopped optimizer produces certified extrema. \cite{exec25_pymed_neural_paper}

The selected implementation samples latent variables, evaluates sensitivity constraints and updates a constrained objective using multipliers and a penalty. For supremum-type restrictions, sampled extrema can miss a small violating set. If a violation occupies probability 0.001 and 100 independent probes are drawn, the probability of seeing none is $0.999^{100}\simeq0.905$. This is an authored diagnostic, not an assessment of the paper's experiments. A feasible maximization iterate is generally a lower bound on the optimal maximum, not by itself a valid upper bound on the true causal query. A proposal for certified output therefore needs a feasibility assessment, a justified optimization-gap bound or analytic outer envelope, and a separate account of density-estimation and sampling uncertainty. \cite{exec25_pymed_neuralcsa,exec25_pymed_neural_paper,exec25_pymed_choice}

The source inspection also finds that a public sensitivity-check helper calls a three-argument sensitivity method with only the training batch and latent sample; both selected subclass definitions require the additional mixture sample. That static signature mismatch is a focused candidate for a native regression test, not evidence about every training route. Continuous Rosenbaum sensitivity is explicitly unimplemented. No license-named file was found in the complete selected NeuralCSA and SharpCausalSensitivity trees; publication of source does not settle permission to copy or redistribute it. We would compare their methods and seek artifact-specific rights and delegate completion before code adoption or a port. \cite{exec25_pymed_neuralcsa,exec25_pymed_sharp,exec25_pymed_choice}

Overcast supplies a contrasting continuous-treatment workflow. The retained Jesson and colleagues preprint bounds a treatment density ratio rather than inserting a density into the binary-treatment odds-ratio formula. Its consistency theorem requires convergence of the fitted outcome density and conditional mean, increasing Monte Carlo effort and a positivity condition; its uncertainty section proposes bootstrap intervals for the bound endpoints. The retained version is the April 2022 author original. A later title and author list in the repository README are not substituted for that original. \cite{exec25_pymed_overcast_paper,exec25_pymed_overcast}

In the selected continuous-treatment source, the sensitivity input is logarithmic, the code exponentiates it, and an epsilon regularizes the expression near the no-confounding boundary. It forms pairwise differences between outcome draws to evaluate threshold candidates. The intermediate array has order $m^2bd$ values for $m$ draws, batch size $b$ and $d$ outcomes. For $m=1000$, $b=256$, $d=1$ and four-byte values, one such array alone is 1,024,000,000 bytes, before masks and other working arrays. This is a storage calculation, not a measured platform limit. A sorted cumulative-sum alternative is a possible port only after tie handling, centering, endpoint semantics and numerical parity have been demonstrated. The method is a conditional dose-response candidate, not a substitute for a longitudinal policy or mediation model. \cite{exec25_pymed_overcast,exec25_pymed_choice}

\subsection{A public qualification sequence with economic consequences}
The package decisions imply independent, executable prototype questions. First, freeze a graph, target, contrast, units, missingness policy and observation clock. Use public synthetic laws with known nested means, including binary mediators, interaction, nonunit treatments and exposure-induced confounding. Compare a regression-path service and a potential-outcome service only where their assumptions imply the same target. Next, enumerate small resampling distributions and finite-support sensitivity sets to check shared-row behavior, failed-fit denominators, probability normalization, ties, contrast scaling and explicit refusal. Neural candidates additionally require held-out density diagnostics, sensitivity feasibility, increasing integration effort, repeated optimization and an external bound oracle. Dependent customer and calendar experiments form a distinct qualification branch. \cite{exec25_pymed_choice}

The same interface must preserve the economics of uncertainty. Suppose, purely for illustration, that a proposed intervention serves 10,000 customers, its benefit lies between USD 2 and USD 8 per customer under the declared sensitivity model, and delivery costs USD 5 per customer. The resulting net interval is USD $[-30{,}000,30{,}000]$. Neither the positive endpoint nor a narrower optimizer output establishes a profitable decision. Further information is valuable only if its expected decision benefit justifies acquisition, computation and review costs. Public known-law fixtures can establish whether the proposed interface preserves this reasoning before expensive bank data work is commissioned. \cite{exec25_pymed_choice}

Accordingly, statsmodels and DoWhy are conditional integration candidates with explicit counterfactual and contrast wrappers; Pingouin and PyProcessMacro are useful regression-path comparators with restricted causal interpretations; SharpCausalSensitivity and NeuralCSA require the stated mathematical, numerical and rights conditions before implementation admission; and Overcast is a continuous-dose sensitivity candidate with a measurable integration-cost question. Selected BSD-style, MIT, GPL and Apache declarations must be assessed together with the actual artifacts and dependency chain; no umbrella rights clearance is inferred. Search tails, negative-control and path-specific alternatives, installed-artifact validation and public native trials remain open. The proposed product should expose an informative refusal whenever a request exceeds the admitted target and sensitivity qualification. \cite{exec25_pymed_statsmodels,exec25_pymed_pingouin,exec25_pymed_dowhy,exec25_pymed_process,exec25_pymed_sharp,exec25_pymed_neuralcsa,exec25_pymed_overcast,exec25_pymed_choice}

\section[Java mediation and structural contracts]{Java mediation: structural equations, intervention semantics, and portable diagrams}
\label{sec:exec25-jmed}

A bank drawing a channel-to-response-to-repayment diagram has two distinct requirements: estimating a statistical structural equation model and evaluating an intervention justified by that model. Java software offers substantial graph and structural-equation components, but the existence of an arrow in a graphical editor does not establish the assignment mechanism, mediator timing or absence of hidden common causes. The selected Tetrad and Onyx sources support a concrete comparison of these components. The resulting proposal is to reuse narrowly specified graph, estimation and export services, with separate effect and uncertainty contracts. A bounded repository search that finds few dedicated mediation projects is not evidence that the language lacks the capability. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_onyx,exec25_jmed_choice}

\subsection{What a linear path product means}
Let $A$ be a servicing assignment, $M$ an intermediate response, and $Y$ the outcome over a fixed horizon. A recursive linear structural model can be written as
\[
 A=\varepsilon_A,\qquad M=aA+\varepsilon_M,\qquad
 Y=cA+bM+\varepsilon_Y.
\]
If these equations are valid structural assignments, intervening on $A$ changes the conditional mean of $Y$ with slope $c+ab$. The direct path has coefficient $c$, while the path through $M$ has product $ab$. This algebra does not itself identify the coefficients from observed data. Interpreting regression estimates as structural coefficients needs restrictions on disturbances, measured covariates, timing, selection and measurement. In particular, a model that fits an observed covariance matrix is not automatically an identified intervention model. \cite{exec25_jmed_choice}

For an authored example, set $(a,b,c)=(2,4,3)$ and disturbance variances to one. With independent disturbances the indirect effect per unit of assignment is eight and the total is eleven. If instead $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_M,\varepsilon_Y)=1/2$, while assignment remains independent of these disturbances, regression of $Y$ on $A$ and $M$ yields mediator slope $4.5$ and direct coefficient two. The regression path product is then nine, although the structural indirect effect remains eight. The total remains eleven: a correct total can conceal an incorrect decomposition. A proposed Java adapter must distinguish a supplied structural parameter from a regression estimate and retain any correlated-error assumptions. \cite{exec25_jmed_choice}

\subsection{Tetrad total effects and the covariance carried through an intervention}
The selected Tetrad \texttt{SemIm.getTotalEffect} method temporarily zeros coefficient parameters for directed edges entering the treatment node, recomputes an implied covariance matrix, and returns the treatment--outcome covariance divided by treatment variance. Its helper computes the usual linear-SEM expression
\[
 \Sigma=(I-B)^{-1}\Omega(I-B)^{-T},
\]
where $B$ contains structural coefficients and $\Omega$ is the disturbance covariance. The inspected method changes entering directed coefficients, but retains $\Omega$. That distinction matters when treatment shares a disturbance with another variable. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad}

Keep the same coefficients $(2,4,3)$ and set $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_A,\varepsilon_M)=1/4$, $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_A,\varepsilon_Y)=1/2$, and $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_M,\varepsilon_Y)=0$. The unit-diagonal disturbance covariance is positive definite: its determinant is $11/16$. The structural intervention derivative is still eleven, but the selected covariance-ratio expression is
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(A)}
 = 11+4(1/4)+1/2=12.5.
\]
The treatment has no entering directed edge to remove in this example, so retaining its error correlations retains the observational dependence that an exogenous intervention should break. This is a precise condition on this source route, not a claim that all Tetrad effect methods are invalid. A conditional integration could restrict this route to justified independent treatment disturbances or derive the appropriate graph surgery and propagate its consequences. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_choice}

State restoration is also part of the scientific contract. The selected method restores changed parameters in a \texttt{finally} block around covariance evaluation. However, the initial parameter-zeroing loop precedes that protected block. Its setter accepts free parameters and means, while rejecting other parameters. A fixed entering coefficient can therefore cause a refusal before the protected evaluation begins; if other coefficients were already changed, the wrapper must establish that state is restored. The method additionally returns zero when treatment variance is exactly zero, although a structural intervention derivative can remain nonzero in a supplied model with degenerate observational assignment. That boundary should be a typed qualification condition, not an automatic finding of no effect. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad}

Estimation paths must be kept separate. The inspected default estimator selects different optimizers for models with fixed parameters, cycles, correlated errors or latent nodes; its local regression optimizer is used for a more restricted case. Parameter standard errors may use parent regression or a different covariance calculation, and missing or unsupported cases can return undefined values. Neither a parameter standard error nor two marginal standard errors automatically supply uncertainty for the indirect product. For estimated coefficients $\hat a,\hat b$, the first-order variance is
\[
 b^2\operatorname{Var}(\hat a)+a^2\operatorname{Var}(\hat b)
 +2ab\operatorname{Cov}(\hat a,\hat b).
\]
At $a=2$, $b=4$, variances $0.04$ and $0.09$, and covariance $0.02$, the expression is $1.32$ rather than one when the covariance term is omitted. This is an authored delta-method illustration; nonregularity near zero products, selection of a graph and dependent observations need separate uncertainty analysis. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_choice}

The earlier Tetrad comparison considered effect sets across alternative parent configurations. The inspected IDA routine enumerates candidate sets and catches exceptions while computing effects. Consequently, a returned effect list should be accompanied by attempted and failed configurations; a shortened list is not automatically the full set implied by the graph class. This is graph uncertainty, distinct from a probability distribution over mediation effects. The proposal would preserve both the graph-class semantics and the computation denominator. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_choice}

A related source helper demonstrates why computational limits must not become causal zeros. The total-effect eligibility helper searches potentially directed paths up to a supplied maximum length and labels an empty bounded search as no potentially directed paths. Its documentation instructs callers to force a zero effect in that case. The path delegate explicitly stops when the edge count exceeds the cutoff. In the chain $A\to M_1\to M_2\to Y$ with coefficients two, three and four, a two-edge search returns no complete path even though the structural effect is 24. An unbounded search or a separately proven reachability result is needed before turning absence into zero. Otherwise the request should return search-incomplete. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_choice}

\subsection{Onyx covariance fitting and failure propagation}
Onyx offers a graphical structural-equation environment, a Java estimation backend and export paths to several other systems. Its selected RAM representation uses an asymmetric path matrix $B$, symmetric disturbance covariance $\Omega$, mean vector $m$ and observed-variable selection matrix $F$. The ordinary branch evaluates
\[
 \mu=F(I-B)^{-1}m,\qquad
 \Sigma=F(I-B)^{-1}\Omega(I-B)^{-T}F^T.
\]
This is an appropriate statistical representation for a declared model. Its inversion and covariance calculations do not establish whether an arrow is manipulable or whether correlated disturbances permit a particular causal decomposition. The name \texttt{isIndirectData} in the backend concerns covariance/summary-data input and must not be advertised as an indirect causal-effect service. \cite{exec25_jmed_onyx,exec25_jmed_choice}

The selected covariance-evaluation branch catches an inversion exception, prints an accelerating-cycle warning, and then continues matrix multiplication using the inverse buffer. The inspected inversion delegate initializes that buffer to the identity before pivoting and throws on an exactly singular pivot. Consider the scalar case $B=1$, $\Omega=1$, $m=2$: $I-B=0$ has no inverse and the structural equilibrium is not defined. Following the selected expressions leaves a unit inverse buffer, after which the continued covariance and mean expressions are one and two. These are not valid solutions to the singular model. This is a static control-flow witness rather than an observed GUI output. A reusable backend must propagate the invalid state, invalidate prior results and prevent a warning-only path from supplying accepted estimates. Near-singular cases additionally need conditioning and residual checks. \cite{exec25_jmed_onyx,exec25_jmed_choice}

The presence of a cycle must be distinguished from singularity and from instability. Invertibility of $I-B$ supplies an algebraic equilibrium under the stated linear model; convergence of an iterative dynamic interpretation requires further conditions. A warning that names an accelerating cycle does not determine which condition failed. Conversely, a stable-looking diagram is not evidence of causal identification. This distinction supports a proposed result schema containing model validity, numerical status, structural assumptions and target identity as separate fields. \cite{exec25_jmed_choice}

\subsection{A diagram export is a scientific transformation}
Cross-language reuse is valuable if it preserves variables, direction, parameters, constraints and observation semantics. The inspected generic Onyx exporter dispatches an observed-to-observed directed edge through an \texttt{ON} branch. For an internal edge $A\to M$, its lavaan formatter emits the regression orientation $M\sim A$. The selected Julia formatter emits a right arrow from target to source for the same branch, while its latent-to-observed branch emits the opposite source-to-target orientation. The literal output for the first case is therefore $M\to A$. This source inconsistency requires a target-parser and semantic round-trip test before claiming equivalence to a Julia model. No Julia runtime test is claimed here. \cite{exec25_jmed_onyx,exec25_jmed_choice}

The lavaan exporter also chooses concrete defaults, including estimation with \texttt{fixed.x=FALSE} and full-information handling of missing data, and rejects definition-edge models through its validity predicate. These choices need to be visible when moving a bank analyst's diagram to another runtime. The shared name mapper does attempt collision handling after sanitization, so a superficial inspection of character replacement alone would be insufficient to conclude that labels necessarily collide. An adequate round-trip fixture should include non-ASCII labels, repeated parameter names, fixed edges, correlated errors, means, unsupported definitions and missingness. It should compare the reconstructed graph and equations before comparing fitted values. \cite{exec25_jmed_onyx}

The same principle governs CLI or MCP integration. A request should carry the causal query, versioned diagram, node identities, coefficient status, treatment contrast, graph-search completeness and numerical status. A response should preserve failure denominators and provide a refusal when an exported model changes direction or assumptions. Graph editing, statistical fitting and intervention evaluation are separate services that can be combined only under a tested contract. No sibling-repository integration or operational service is inferred from these proposed interfaces. \cite{exec25_jmed_choice}

\subsection{Reuse decisions and public qualification}
For Tetrad, conditional reuse of graph and SEM components is plausible, but the selected total-effect helper needs the disturbance, fixed-parameter and zero-variance conditions just described. The graph-search cutoff must remain an explicit computation limit. For Onyx, the graphical editor and RAM representation are useful candidates, with backend failure propagation and target-specific export parity as separate admission requirements. Equation-level ports should be considered only when a measured workflow benefit justifies maintaining the same model and uncertainty semantics. Selected GPL and Apache declarations and Onyx's separately listed dependencies require artifact-specific rights review; a top-level license does not clear every bundled component. \cite{exec25_jmed_tetrad,exec25_jmed_onyx,exec25_jmed_choice}

Public qualification can proceed with generated linear systems before costly bank experiments. Enumerate acyclic path products and correlated-disturbance laws; compare graph surgery with covariance ratios; inject fixed-parameter failures and verify restoration; exercise zero treatment variance and bounded path searches; and test singular and near-singular RAM inputs. Round-trip each supported export and retain every rejected case. Then evaluate estimation and uncertainty under independent customers, clusters and shared calendars as distinct designs. A structural total of eleven mistaken for 12.5 across 10,000 customers at a hypothetical USD10 per outcome unit would overstate value by USD150,000. This calculation motivates semantic tests; it is not a bank ROI estimate. \cite{exec25_jmed_choice}

The reviewed sources support these bounded engineering and mathematical routes. They do not resolve arbitrary hidden confounding, causal direction from covariance fit, nonlinear natural effects, general longitudinal sensitivity or independent acceptance. A named Onyx scholarly candidate remains an omission until its genuine original is retained; source-backed technical statements do not substitute metadata for that paper. Complete search tails, additional JVM implementations, installed-artifact trials and rights remain open. \section[C++ mediation and numerical contracts]{C++ mediation: preserve the intervention through numerical and data interfaces}
\label{sec:exec25-cmed}

A bank evaluating a channel-to-engagement-to-repayment mechanism needs more
than a fast matrix library. Let $A$ denote a channel assignment, $M$ subsequent

structural model $M=aA+U_M$ and $Y=cA+bM+U_Y$, with the required assignment

$(a,b,c)=(2,4,3)$ give $8$, $3$ and $11$. These are project derivations in a

uncertainty, and which apparent reuse would silently change the problem.

Four bounded repository searches and selected primary-source inspection identify
four different component roles: OpenMx's R-facing C++ structural-equation engine,
Stan Math's C++ primitives, bsem's R-to-Stan generated implementation, and gwsem's

\subsection{OpenMx: joint algebra, row resampling and numerical failure}

OpenMx's RAM representation provides a natural computational home for a linear
mediation model. Writing $x=Bx+\epsilon$, disturbance covariance $S$ and an
observation-selection matrix $F$, its mathematical covariance target is
\[
 \Sigma=F(I-B)^{-1}S(I-B)^{-T}F^T.
\]
This covariance representation can include correlated disturbances; it does not
make a coefficient product causally identified. A proposed adapter should retain
the graph, variable order, error correlations, measurement model, fixed and
shared parameters, and the intervention contrast separately from the numerical
RAM matrices. Natural-effect restrictions and the banking information clock
remain explicit inputs.~\citep{exec25_cmed_openmx}

The inspected path delegate uses a finite polynomial when its nilpotence logic
permits it and an inverse computation otherwise. In the dense inverse branch it
forms a full-pivot LU decomposition and solves against the identity. The nearby
sparse branch checks the factorization status, records an iteration error and
sets its inverse buffer to missing values on failure. This is a difference in
the inspected local guards, not evidence that every dense singular model is
accepted by the whole package. Complete fit-function propagation and native
solver behavior remain to be qualified. A scalar feedback coefficient
$b=1-\varepsilon$ illustrates the underlying numerical problem: the response
$(1-b)^{-1}=\varepsilon^{-1}$ has derivative $\varepsilon^{-2}$ with respect
to $b$. At $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ these are $1000$ and $1{,}000{,}000$.
Small residuals alone do not bound economically useful effect error near
singularity. At $b=1$ the inverse does not exist; an adapter must return an
explicit failure, not a finite treatment effect.~\citep{exec25_cmed_openmx,exec25_cmed_choice}

The bootstrap implementation offers useful audit structure. Its C++ output
retains each replication's seed, fit, parameter vector and status. The R accessor
selects statuses declared acceptable and warns when fewer than 95 percent pass.
Consequently, uncertainty computed from that accessor is conditional on retained
fits unless the effects of failed fits are separately justified. Report requested,
attempted, failed and retained denominators. The expression evaluator substitutes
the entire retained parameter vector before evaluating an algebra, which is the
appropriate mechanism for preserving within-draw dependence of a product.
For paired illustrative draws $(a,b)=(1,2),(3,6)$, the average product is $10$;
independent recombination of the two marginal sets gives $8$. The difference is
not Monte Carlo noise: it is a changed joint distribution. The displayed
bias-corrected interval uses a normal-quantile bias correction and empirical
type-1 quantiles; its inspected formula has no acceleration term, so it should
not be advertised as BCa.~\citep{exec25_cmed_openmx,exec25_cmed_choice}

Resampling semantics are equally important. The C++ loop draws frequency counts
for each dataset context; it does not by itself synchronize customer identities
across separate data objects. A same-row multivariate record can preserve its
cross-equation measurements, whereas independently resampling linked tables
requires an explicit justification or a different joint sampler. The public R
entry point states its independent-row assumption and rejects detected multilevel
and state-space models unless the user chooses an unsafe override. Such an
override changes execution permission, not the sampling theorem. The selected
C++ loop also contains an unimplemented-parallel marker despite carrying a
parallel flag; this local observation must not become a claim that all OpenMx
parallelism is absent. Reuse should test the exact entry point and report measured
resources.~\citep{exec25_cmed_openmx}

The wrapper checks that a model has been freshly run, while the backend's shown
prior-output compatibility test compares parameter and column counts. These
guards motivate an additional artifact identity over data, graph, parameter
names/order, fit settings, sampling law and seeds before bootstrap continuation.
Equal dimensions do not establish equal scientific requests. Seed replay,
exception restoration of row weights and starts, an empty acceptable-fit set,
ties in interval correction, and dense/sparse singular cases are public
qualification tasks. The source declaration is Apache 2.0; complete distributed
dependencies and any chosen optimizer require their own rights and artifact

\subsection{Stan Math: an auditable numerical layer with a narrow contract}

Stan Math is a candidate numerical dependency rather than a mediation estimator.
The inspected arithmetic matrix solve checks square and compatible dimensions
and delegates to LU. The reverse-mode overload uses a Householder QR solve and
a reverse callback. For $AX=B$, a perturbation obeys
$dX=A^{-1}(dB-dA\,X)$; with output adjoint $G$, the corresponding inputs are
$\bar B=A^{-T}G$ and $\bar A=-\bar B X^T$. These derivative identities explain
the callback and also show why an ill-conditioned $A$ affects both the result
and the gradient. Mathematical agreement does not imply bitwise parity between
the arithmetic and differentiated branches.~\citep{exec25_cmed_stanmath}

The positive-definite solve additionally checks symmetry, non-NaN values and
positive definiteness of the factorization. The inspected positive-definite
inverse checks its LDLT status, positivity and positive diagonal factors. Its
opening comment mentions positive/negative definiteness, but the operative
checks require positivity. Select the routine by its executable mathematical
contract. A covariance factor may be positive definite; the nonsymmetric RAM
matrix $I-B$ generally is not. Passing it to an SPD routine for convenience would
change the supported domain.~\citep{exec25_cmed_stanmath}

\subsection{bsem: inspect the translation from named paths to compiled density}

model uses observed measurement entries, Gaussian latent regressions with unit
residual scale, loading priors and separate missing-value variants. These are
substantive modeling choices. Loading signs supplied to the R interface set
initial values; they do not by themselves constrain every posterior draw or
identify a bank's manipulable treatment.~\citep{exec25_cmed_bsem}

A named-path translation deserves an exact test before integration. The inspected
handler constructs dependent-variable indices in the order of the block names,
but constructs parent groups in the order of the path list. For block order
$(A,M,Y)$ and path list $Y\leftarrow M$ followed by $M\leftarrow A$, these
sequences pair as $M\leftarrow M$ and $Y\leftarrow A$. The intended chain with
coefficients $1/2$ and $3/4$ has product $3/8$; the mispaired equations are a
different model. This is an authored trace of the selected indexing expressions,
not a measured native frequency. An adapter must canonicalize both targets and
parents together, reject duplicate or self edges as appropriate, and test every
permutation of equivalent named input.~\citep{exec25_cmed_bsem,exec25_cmed_choice}

Cycles require more than reordering. Consider two simultaneous equations
$z_1=r z_2+e_1$ and $z_2=s z_1+e_2$, with independent standard-normal errors.

$1/|1-rs|$ when $1-rs\ne0$. A normalized simultaneous-equation density therefore
needs the Jacobian multiplier $|1-rs|$ per observation. At $r=s=1/2$ the
residual product integrates to $4/3$ and the missing multiplier is $3/4$.
An acyclic triangular system has determinant one. The selected Stan text writes
the regression density product, so a translator must establish the intended
acyclic model or derive and implement the correct cyclic density; merely
obtaining posterior draws cannot resolve this distinction. No claim is made
that this static case was run through the package.~\citep{exec25_cmed_bsem,exec25_cmed_choice}

Two smaller interface details can materially alter sensitivity analysis. The R
handler forwards the second parsed coefficient-prior parameter, while Stan uses
its square root as the normal or Cauchy scale. An input second value of $4$
therefore gives normal standard deviation $2$, rather than $4$ under a
conventional standard-deviation reading. Also, the selected Stan data declare
coefficient-prior locations nonnegative, even though the coefficient itself is
unrestricted. A proposed negative prior location must be rejected or translated
with an explicit schema change, never silently reinterpreted. These observations
concern this selected model, not Stan's general prior capabilities.~\citep{exec25_cmed_bsem}

likelihood iterates the observed index set. The temporary means should not be
described as the final inferential imputation. A later R handler applies conditional chain-sign transformations to extracted
loadings, scores and selected coefficients. Its index mapping must preserve the
structural equations before any joint mediation draw is admitted. No missingness
indicator model in the selected density establishes an arbitrary nonignorable
mechanism. Preserve
the target population and missingness assumptions, and retain chains,
diagnostics and joint draws for any mediation functional. Conditional
equation-level reuse is plausible after fixing translation contracts; direct
admission as a banking mediation engine is deferred. GPL-3, rstan and the
complete compiled dependency chain must be reviewed for the intended

gwsem combines genetic-file loading with OpenMx models. The selected compute plan

\section[Remaining-language mediation contracts]{MATLAB, Julia and Stata mediation: compare estimands and execution contracts}
\label{sec:exec25-omed}

A bank comparing mediation packages across languages needs the same scientific
request in every environment: an intervention, its contrast, a mediator law,
an outcome scale, a target population and a sampling design. A mediation
package can otherwise conceal a coefficient product, a nested counterfactual
mean, a modified treatment policy or a descriptive path model. This comparison
develops six concrete component choices: CANlab's MATLAB Mediation Toolbox;
the Julia packages Paramed.jl, Mediation.jl and CausalMediation.jl;
CausalTargeted.jl's compatibility interface; and the Stata cmed collection.
Four bounded repository searches and 55 authenticated original files establish
selected source contracts, not ecosystem saturation. The queries return
irrelevant uses of the word mediation, uninspected candidates and unreturned
results. Revisions are pinned before the 7 October 2026 cutoff; source
descriptions are not native performance evidence.~\citep{exec25_omed_choice}

Consider a bank's reminder assignment $A$, account engagement $M$, and repayment
$Y$. In the declared linear system $M=2A+U_M$ and $Y=3A+4M+U_Y$, the additional
assumptions needed for causal mediation yield direct and indirect unit effects
of $3$ and $8$. The contrast $0\rightarrow2$ gives $6$, $16$ and total $22$.
These values make a useful portable oracle: language and numerical routine may
change, but the contrast must be applied exactly once. With nonlinear outcomes,
several mediators or intermediate confounding, the oracle must change to the
specific identified functional. A fitted path diagram cannot provide that
functional by itself.~\citep{exec25_omed_choice}

\subsection{CANlab: distinguish levels, accepted resamples and interval probability}

CANlab's inspected MATLAB path engine includes single-level and multilevel
branches, ordinary and robust regressions, and bootstrap options. Its dispatch
explicitly rejects some combinations, including robust multilevel computation.
The fast ordinary bootstrap path constructs normal-equation inverses for the
mediator and outcome regressions and returns the product of the two relevant
slopes. This is a concrete reusable path calculation, subject to matrix
conditioning and the scientific assumptions attached to a coefficient product.
The main entry point removes missing observations jointly from the current
variables. In a bank panel, that complete-case set can be a different population
from all eligible customers. Selection and information timing must therefore
be represented outside the regression call.~\citep{exec25_omed_canlab}

The resampling helper draws rows, deletes resamples with no predictor variation,
and replaces them until its iteration cap is reached. It also reseeds the random
generator from the clock. These are substantive reproducibility and sampling
choices. With two predictor values $(0,1)$, four equally likely size-two bootstrap
samples contain two constant samples. Removing them leaves only $(0,1)$ and
$(1,0)$. The sample-mean variance is $1/8$ under ordinary resampling but zero
conditional on acceptance. This exact example is not a claim about a measured
mediation coverage rate; it demonstrates why the replacement rule and every
rejected attempt belong in the reported experiment. A wrapper should capture
the actual index matrix or change seed handling through a reviewed port.
\citep{exec25_omed_canlab,exec25_omed_choice}

The confidence-interval call also needs branch-specific qualification. The
inspected no-covariate branch calls the local BCa helper with $\alpha=0.025$,
while the covariate branch calls it with $\alpha=0.25$. A nearby comment proposes
MATLAB's built-in routine, but that invocation is commented out in the retained
source. The helper computes bias correction, jackknife acceleration and endpoints
from $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$. In the unbiased symmetric limit, these two inputs
correspond to central probabilities 95 percent and 50 percent. The distinction
must survive into output labels and acceptance criteria. Zero jackknife
variation, extreme bias correction, tied resamples and the diagonal selection
from multi-column percentile output are separate tests; a BCa label alone does
not establish correct multivariate intervals.~\citep{exec25_omed_canlab,exec25_omed_choice}

For multilevel data, the main program generally postpones bootstrap inference
to the between-subject summaries unless first-level bootstrapping is requested.
This is potentially useful for customer-level trajectories, but requires an
explicit mapping from subjects to customers, visits and shared calendar shocks.
It does not establish valid arbitrary temporal dependence. The complete-tree
inspection finds a license-named file in a geometry subcomponent, not a blanket
toolbox license. Preserve that distinction and obtain the intended rights and
MATLAB/toolbox entitlement before direct adoption. Conditional equation-level
reuse and public tests can proceed without claiming that the dependency or
statistical qualification is complete.~\citep{exec25_omed_canlab,exec25_omed_choice}

\subsection{Older Julia ports: preserve covariance and actually use the resample}

Paramed.jl describes itself as a port of a Stata parametric-mediation program.
The selected source fits separate models, evaluates analytic contrasts and
constructs delta-method uncertainty using a block-diagonal combination of
model covariance matrices. Even after software repairs, that combination needs
a justification for the relevant joint estimation law. Evaluating covariates
at a mean is also not automatically the same as averaging a nonlinear effect
over the bank's customer population.~\citep{exec25_omed_paramed}

An exact covariance permutation illustrates an immediate translation concern.
The helper assigns its output array to the input array and then overwrites
entries while moving the intercept to the last position. For the positive
definite matrix
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&2\\1&9&3\\2&3&16\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 PVP^T=\begin{pmatrix}9&3&1\\3&16&2\\1&2&4\end{pmatrix},
\]
the intended permutation is nonsingular, with determinant $500$. Tracing the
selected in-place assignments instead gives
\[
 \widetilde V=\begin{pmatrix}9&3&9\\3&16&3\\9&3&9\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is singular. This is an authored finite-array witness, not a native Julia
test. A correct port should use an explicit index permutation or a separate
copy and check symmetry, positive definiteness and the variance of the same
linear combination before and after reordering.~\citep{exec25_omed_paramed,exec25_omed_choice}

The selected two-sided normal-tail calculation also uses the signed statistic
without its absolute value. At $z=-1$, $2\{1-\Phi(z)\}$ is approximately
$1.683$, whereas the usual two-sided normal reference is approximately $0.317$.
An output outside $[0,1]$ is an elementary refusal fixture. Formula construction,
the typed logfile default, older Julia/DataFrames syntax and transitive imports
still require an installed-environment review. The MIT source license supports
consideration of a repair or equation-level port; it does not establish modern
runtime compatibility or validity of every analytic approximation. Direct
admission is deferred.~\citep{exec25_omed_paramed,exec25_omed_choice}

Mediation.jl takes a simulation approach, fitting mediator and outcome models and
perturbing their parameters before simulating potential outcomes. Its selected
bootstrap perturber creates a sampled index vector but refits with the original
index vector. For a deterministic fit, that branch therefore does not create
parameter resampling variation. With observations $(0,2)$, ordinary bootstrap
means are $0,1,1,2$, whose variance is $1/2$; refitting the unchanged observations
always gives mean one. The subsequent outcome simulator can still generate
variation, so it would be wrong to claim that every returned interval has zero
width. The issue is that one source of uncertainty is absent while another
remains.~\citep{exec25_omed_mediationjl,exec25_omed_choice}

The same implementation has distinct asymptotic parameter draws, predictive
simulation for normal, binomial and Poisson families, and substitutions of
observed outcomes for some simulated entries. A proposed adapter must state
whether its interval concerns a sample average, a population effect or a
predictive quantity, preserve the treatment contrast and justify cross-model
dependence. Repairing the unused index is necessary but insufficient. No
license-named file was found in the selected complete tree; distribution rights
remain unresolved.~\citep{exec25_omed_mediationjl,exec25_omed_choice}

\subsection{CausalMediation and CausalTargeted: typed effects need matching laws}

CausalMediation.jl provides useful architectural ideas: explicit effect families,
mediator--outcome confounder fields, refusal of natural effects with declared

has a semantic fingerprint, while a call without relation kinds is otherwise

that an imported graph or certificate is scientifically true. The CausalTargeted

The nested outcome helper exposes the mediator law more clearly than a package

intermediate confounder and at most one requested Monte Carlo draw, it instead

contrast is $E[M^2]=1$, but substitution of $E[M]=0$ gives zero. Similarly, with

calendar dependence.~\citep{exec25_omed_causalmediation,exec25_omed_choice}

The implementation also distinguishes binary-treatment and continuous

list, so equality requires the stated compatibility assumptions. Preserve the
separate categorical-treatment implementation rather than attributing every
continuous-path restriction to the whole package.~\citep{exec25_omed_causalmediation}

The conditional reuse route is to retain the typed request and refusal design,
then qualify each effect-family and treatment-support combination independently.
Public fixtures should test mean substitution, mediator correlation, intermediate
confounding, weight truncation, influence-function centering and numerical
integration error. Selected MIT declarations are useful rights inputs; the

\subsection{Stata cmed: inspect the chosen estimator beneath the common command}

The Stata cmed collection exposes linear-model, weighting, imputation,

\section{Dose-response software: the intervention, the curve and the decision}

A bank choosing the size of a credit-line increase needs more than a smooth

\subsection{Define the causal curve before choosing a fitting routine}
Let $A$ be the offer amount in declared units, $X$ the information available
before the offer, and $Y(a)$ the outcome under a specified intervention assigning
amount $a$. The population response curve is $\mu(a)=E\{Y(a)\}$. Consistency,
conditional exchangeability for each dose, positive conditional exposure density
on the requested population and dose region, and suitable regularity justify
\[
 \mu(a)=\int E(Y\mid A=a,X=x)\,dP_X(x).
\]
For a continuous exposure, the generalized propensity score is a conditional
\emph{density}, $r(a,x)=f_{A\mid X}(a\mid x)$, not the probability of an exact
dose. Conditional means at a point require an appropriate version and
smoothness; a finite sample does not contain repeated observations at every
amount. The CausalGPS original states the causal assumptions and separates
outcome-blind design from analysis. It limits balancing to measured covariates
and refers detailed theory elsewhere. This software paper cannot alone qualify
every estimator or uncertainty procedure.~\citep{exec24_dose_original}

An authored example makes the banking consequence concrete. Suppose $X$ and
$U$ are independent standard Gaussian variables, $A=X+U$, and
$Y=2A+3X+\varepsilon$, with independent centered noise. Then
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(A)}
 =2+3\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,X)}{\operatorname{Var}(A)}=3.5,
 \qquad \frac{d\mu(a)}{da}=2.
\]
At a stipulated value of 100 USD per outcome unit and a 220 USD cost for a
one-unit increase, the historical slope suggests 130 USD, while the stipulated
causal model gives minus 20 USD. These are exact model consequences, not customer
data estimates. A curve-fitting package cannot establish exchangeability, the
information clock, interference, missingness, or intervention consistency.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

A stochastic policy instead assigns density $q(a\mid x)$ and has value
$V(q)=\int\!\int E(Y\mid A=a,X=x)q(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)$ under the
corresponding assumptions. Its ratio is $q(A\mid X)/f_{A\mid X}(A\mid X)$.
The stabilized ratio $f_A(A)/f_{A\mid X}(A\mid X)$ has a particular numerator
and cannot silently represent every customer-specific policy. A curve, an
average contrast, an inverse target dose and an optimized policy require
different output types even when they share density estimation.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{causaldrf: transparent adjustment and transformation contracts}
The inspected \texttt{hi\_est} routine fits a treatment model, constructs a GPS,
fits a user-specified linear outcome surface, and averages predictions after
replacing treatments with a requested grid value and recomputing its GPS.
Accepted families include normal, log-transformed and square-root transformed
normal, count and binary models. This is a useful transparent adjustment
reference; accepting several families does not validate covariate sufficiency.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf}

In the log-normal branch, the expression evaluates a normal density at
$\log(t)$ without the $1/t$ Jacobian of an original-scale log-normal density.
The square-root branch similarly evaluates a normal density at $\sqrt t$.
For $t=4$ and transformed-scale density $1/5$, the corresponding original-scale
log-normal density is $1/20$. At a fixed dose, multiplying all scores by the
same positive dose-only factor preserves the information in the balancing
score; transformation alone therefore does not disprove fixed-dose balancing.
However, a specified finite-dimensional outcome surface in treatment and GPS
can change, and an inverse-density or cross-dose consumer needs the actual
measure and units. A port must distinguish an original-scale density, a
transformed-scale score and an arbitrary balancing coordinate.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_contract}

The inspected \texttt{get\_ci} helper sorts bootstrap curve values separately
at each grid point and selects the ceiling of $0.975B$ and floor of $0.025B$.
It receives coefficient draws; it does not justify their sampling law. With
$B=20$ the lower index is zero, an edge condition for future qualification.
This is an index-expression witness, not a native failure report. Separate
95 percent pointwise intervals also do not imply a simultaneous 95 percent
band. In 100 equally likely authored outcomes, disjoint sets of five misses
at each of two points give 95 percent coverage at each point and 90 percent
joint coverage. Selecting an offer after searching a curve needs a stated
selection-aware or simultaneous uncertainty argument.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{CausalGPS: preserve design diagnostics and population meaning}
The 2023 original describes iterative design with hyperparameter search,
transformations and trimming. The inspected 0.5.1
\texttt{generate\_pseudo\_pop} body instead accepts a counter-weight object,
merges by identifier, computes diagnostics and returns their status. Its header
still describes an adaptive procedure; this inspected body is not itself the
historical search loop. An adapter must follow the pinned interface and record
where tuning actually occurs. Unique observation identifiers, join cardinality
and the retained population belong to its contract.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_original}

The normal GPS branch uses predicted exposure means and a common residual
standard deviation. The kernel branch estimates scale from absolute residuals,
standardizes residuals, and evaluates a kernel density on them. The inspected
residual delegate computes $(a-b)/c$; the density delegate interpolates an
estimated density with constant extrapolation outside its grid. This branch
exports the residual density value. Under a location-scale interpretation with
residual density $g$ and positive scale $s(x)$, the original-scale density is
\[
 f_{A\mid X}(a\mid x)=s(x)^{-1}
 g\!\left(\frac{a-m(x)}{s(x)}\right).
\]
The selected weighting routine divides a marginal exposure density by the
exported GPS. With stipulated marginal density $1/5$, residual density $2/5$
and scales $2$ and $1/2$, this literal ratio is $1/2$ for both observations;
the location-scale ratios are $1$ and $1/4$. This static comparison identifies
a conformance question. It assumes a valid location-scale residual model and
is not an executed benchmark or a finding about every possible GPS use.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

Units provide another exact check. Changing dollars to cents multiplies the
exposure by 100 and divides both densities by 100. The stabilized ratio stays
2 when densities change from $1/5$ and $1/10$ to $1/500$ and $1/1000$.
The reciprocal conditional density changes from 10 to 1,000. Normalizing weights
may cancel a common factor, but does not justify losing measure, scale or the
policy numerator.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

The balance routine compares a selected mean, median or maximum absolute
correlation with a threshold. Zero correlation is not independence: with equal
weights on $X\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}$ and $A=X^2$, covariance is zero while exposure
depends entirely on $X$. This deterministic example also lacks overlap.
Adding independent continuous noise with an everywhere positive density
restores conditional density support without independence, while preserving
zero covariance. Nonlinear diagnostics complement causal assumptions.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

The original includes adverse findings. Its first two illustrated matching
attempts fail a maximum-correlation threshold of 0.1, with maxima 0.198 and
0.123. A third reports 0.094 after retaining 464,194 of 580,244 observations;
its reported effective sample size is about 7,635, below its displayed
recommended 46,419. These are historical reported quantities, not reproduced
results or universal thresholds. The paper omits the detailed interval
calculation and calls the final curve illustrative.
\citep{exec24_dose_original}

The current outcome frontend rejects negative weights but replaces an all-zero
vector by equal weights. It computes plotting normalization using the weight
range, zero for equal weights. Our contract treats an empty weighted population
as unresolved, not permission to change the scientific target. Outcomes 0 and
100 with zero weights make the distinction explicit: replacement yields 50,
while the requested weighted mean has no denominator. In addition,
\texttt{trim\_gps} accesses \texttt{dataset}, whereas the inspected GPS creator
returns \texttt{.data}; exported \texttt{trim\_it} uses \texttt{.data}.
This object-field discrepancy is a static integration question, not a claim
that every supported trimming route fails.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{WeightIt: retain history and the actual uncertainty method}
WeightIt's selected primary text distinguishes binary and multicategory
estimands, continuous stabilized density weights and longitudinal products.
The continuous GLM delegate returns unit weights with no covariates and no
random effects, otherwise estimates a weighted residual scale, and constructs
estimating-equation components only for supported configurations. Kernel,
specified missing-data and random-effects paths are excluded from that
particular M-estimation route. Longitudinal code multiplies period
contributions and stabilization factors. Servicing analysis must supply ordered
predecision histories and censoring conventions: period weights 2 and 3 have
product 6, not sum 5.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

The outcome interface distinguishes M-estimation accounting for estimated
weights, fixed-weight sandwich covariance, and bootstrap routes re-estimating
weights. The inspected dispatcher defaults to HC0 when estimating-equation
components are absent and exposes the selected covariance type. A consumer
must preserve that field and its assumptions. Cluster options likewise require
a justified sampling unit. With 20 independent customers, 25 unit-variance
visits each and within-customer correlation 0.6, the mean variance is
$[1+24(0.6)]/500=0.0308$, versus 0.002 for independent visits. This authored
exchangeable example does not qualify arbitrary shared calendar shocks.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

The trimming interface distinguishes winsorizing from setting weights to zero
and warns that the target can change. Its implementation removes M-estimation
components after trimming; the subsequent covariance route must be reported.
Weights $(1,1,8)$ and outcomes $(0,0,100)$ give mean 80. Capping the last
weight at 2 gives mean 50 while the weight-concentration diagnostic rises from
$100/66$ to $16/6$. A larger effective sample size need not mean less causal
error. Separately, a population with 80 percent customers having effect 10 and
20 percent having effect 100 has average effect 28. Removing the second group
leaves effect 10 for a different population, not a repaired estimate of 28.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{drc and DoseFinding: useful roles after design justification}
The selected drc frontend accepts curve family, outcome type, weights and model
structure. Its effective-dose interface distinguishes relative and absolute
response targets, checks the relative range, and offers no interval, delta,
transformed-scale and inverse-regression routes. These fitting and inverse
response tools do not provide the causal identification argument for a banking
assignment. We retain drc as a descriptive or scientifically justified
post-design comparator and reject automatic curve-to-effect admission.
\citep{exec24_dose_drc}

DoseFinding's inspected MCPMod path tests model-based contrasts, retains
significant shapes, then fits and selects or averages models and computes a
target or effective dose. A nonsignificant result returns no model or target;
it is not a zero effect estimate. The contrast routine accepts either a
covariance matrix or compatible allocation weights and checks exclusivity.
The general route requires supplied estimates and covariance with already
valid scientific and sampling meanings; the normal route obtains them from
its fitted model. Multiplicity-adjusted testing of declared contrasts does not
automatically validate post-selection uncertainty for an optimized policy.
\citep{exec24_dose_dosefinding}

An exact illustration has means $(0,1,2)$ at three randomized doses and
independent mean variances $1/100$. Contrast $(-1,0,1)$ has signal 2, variance
$2/100$ and squared standardized signal 200. After subtracting a common
baseline from the two active means, their covariance is
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}2/100&1/100\\1/100&2/100\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Contrast $(-1,1)$ still has variance $2/100$; discarding shared-baseline
covariance gives $4/100$. A bridge must retain dose order, baseline adjustment,
covariance, degrees of freedom and candidate-model identity.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

For the authored curve $m(a)=4a/(1+a)$, $a\geq0$, an increase of one response
unit requires $a=1/3$. With cost $2a$ in matching value units, maximizing
$m(a)-2a$ instead gives $a=\sqrt2-1$. At amounts 0, 1 and 2, net values are
0, 0 and $-4/3$. The effective dose answers a different question from the net
value optimum. Its uncertainty needs response, cost and selection assumptions
together.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{Reuse decisions and public qualification before bank experiments}
We choose reference use now: causaldrf for transparent GPS adjustment,
CausalGPS for design and balance contracts, WeightIt for point and longitudinal
weighting, drc for fitting and inverse targets, and DoseFinding for experimental
contrasts. An original R worker is a conditional future route when it preserves
objects, diagnostics and uncertainty. A whole-library port lacks a demonstrated
benefit. We reject automatic curve-to-causal and balance-to-identification
admission; native admission awaits exact distributions, dependencies, rights
and semantic evidence.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

Declarations differ. causaldrf declares MIT plus its copyright file, R at least
3.1.2 and imported/optional packages; CausalGPS declares GPL-3, R at least 3.5,
Rcpp compilation and learning/smoothing dependencies. WeightIt declares GPL
at least version 2, R at least 4.1 and method-dependent optional dependencies.
drc declares GPL-2 and R at least 4; DoseFinding declares GPL-3 and compiled
code. These are declarations, not transitive distribution clearance or a legal
conclusion. Rights, maintenance owners, installed versions and optional paths
must be resolved for the actual worker.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_drc,exec24_dose_dosefinding}

Public qualification can precede expensive bank access. Freeze version,
transformation, intervention density, population and dose region. Generate
known-outcome experiments covering confounding, nonlinear balance, weak overlap,
varying scales, dependence and misspecification. Compare analytical targets with
the pinned worker. Test invalid identifiers, empty/extreme weights, trimming,
missing fields, unsupported covariance types, selection, serialization and
process failure. Refit estimated stages under the justified resampling law,
retain failed requests, and distinguish pointwise from simultaneous claims.
Tuning and final assessment must not reuse the same outcomes.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

The scientific owner supplies causal assumptions and sampling units; the
adapter owner preserves units, measures, histories, objects and refusal states;
the rights/platform owner qualifies the distribution; the evaluator registers
independent targets and complete costs. Illustratively, 2,400 USD for 100
requests, 80 conformant outputs and 60 useful outputs gives 24, 30 and 40 USD
per respective denominator. These are not measured prices or adoption returns.
Unmeasured confounding, absent support, arbitrary dependence and policy-selection
uncertainty have no automatic software cure. Other languages, commercial
systems, uninspected delegates and dependency closures remain survey work.
This bounded reference decision admits no native capability and does not close
the broader proposal standard.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}


The chapter's alternatives therefore turn on what can actually be changed:
an offer, a mediator distribution, an exposure amount or an assumption used
to interpret observations. Natural components, dose optima and sensitivity
thresholds are useful only when their distinct meanings reach the financial
decision. Repeated servicing adds a further requirement: an action changes
information and opportunities for later actions. The next chapter develops
that longitudinal clock and separates policy value, censoring and survival
outcomes before comparing the corresponding software.
