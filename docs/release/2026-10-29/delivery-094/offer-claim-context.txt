2.1     Fix the intervention before estimating its value
The preceding accounting calculation defines the question for the first complete study. Let Zi
denote assignment of the offer to eligible household i, Ri its delivery, Di acceptance, and ri
the paid annual rate. These variables describe successive events, not synonyms. Let Bi (z) be
the household’s average eligible deposit balance over the following year under assignment z,
and let Ci (z) be contribution over that same year. For a population fixed before assignment,
the policy targets are

                      τB = E[Bi (1) − Bi (0)],       τC = E[Ci (1) − Ci (0)].

A statement about the effect among acceptors generally answers neither target. Acceptance
can depend on intentions that also determine balances. Dividing an assignment effect by
an acceptance rate additionally requires an instrumental variable argument and changes the
population to the relevant compliers; the derivation and restrictions are in Chapter 7.
   Here the constructed design makes a narrower intervention explicit. Every assigned offer
is delivered, and assignment automatically reprices the eligible balance for one year. Thus
R = Z and the price rule is r = r0 + Z∆r; acceptance does not gate the payment. A voluntary
activation campaign would need a different expense equation. Assume no interference across
households, no lost outcomes, and unchanged funding margin and direct cost. If the expected
no-offer balance is B0 , the incremental expected contribution is

                                τC = (m − ∆r)τB − ∆rB0 − c.                               (2.1)

To obtain this expression, subtract mBi (0) from (m − ∆r)Bi (1) − c, then take expectations.
It charges the rate increase on the balance that would have remained without the intervention.
The familiar accounting shortcut is justified by this particular price rule. It would overcharge
a campaign that paid only newly acquired funds and would misdescribe a campaign whose
delivery itself failed.

2.4     What the implemented example actually returns
The local callable estimator takes the three-column generated data, target weights, four
accounting inputs and α. It returns cell counts and uncertainty, the balance target, its interval,
contribution and its interval, and a conditional decision. The same calculation is available at
the command line from the repository root:
python3 tools/run_rewrite_examples_20261029.py --report-only

The callable result and a separate command-line process agree exactly in the retained execution.
The generated data, code, seed and numerical results are supplied with the study. This is
a specific estimator implemented for the example; it is not evidence that an external causal
package was qualified.
   The earlier MCP walkthrough establishes graph syntax, conditional adjustment, information-
date checks and refusal of a dependent sampling law. The public study in Chapter 29
additionally sends actual fitted estimates and covariance summaries through library, CLI and
MCP. It preserves the resulting interval and conditional or contradicted assignment status.
Neither path exposes the deposit raw-data estimator as an MCP operation. Chapter 31 gives
the observed requests and the initial timeout as well as the successful retry. These separate
operations do not establish a raw-data-to-financial-report MCP endpoint. A future adapter
must carry the complete report, including assumptions and unsupported outcomes, before that
claim becomes available.


2.5     From the household offer to broader funding evidence
Two extensions show what this transparent report leaves to be learned. Chapter 29 estimates
an assignment contrast from the retained public experiment, compares adjusted and unadjusted
estimators, examines influence, and subtracts an explicit cost. Its earnings units and population
are different from the deposit account: the exercise tests an inferential and reporting route,
not transport of an earnings effect into bank balances. Chapter 16 follows institutions rather
than households and asks whether fixed effects can repair a misassigned outcome. They cannot
recover the institution’s identity merely by absorbing a persistent level. The structural funding
and calendar arguments explain the additional shock and dependence assumptions needed for
that journey.
   For the offer study, the next source of information is assignment support and correct
household aggregation; for the institutional panel it is a coherent exposure and outcome
history. A bank cannot substitute a successful estimate in one of these populations for the
