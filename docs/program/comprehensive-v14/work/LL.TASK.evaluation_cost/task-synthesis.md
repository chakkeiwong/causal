# Evaluating causal assistance and its full cost

## The question a bank must answer

A useful causal assistant must improve the decision made with the available evidence at an acceptable total cost. Fluent reports, agreement among agents and successful tool calls are intermediate outcomes. The proposal therefore evaluates the target, assumptions, graph, identification decision, numerical result and action separately. A correct refusal can be valuable; an unsupported but precise number can be harmful. This chapter develops a prospective comparison using public evidence and stipulated mechanisms. It reports no new live-model experiment, analyst-time observation or bank return on investment.

Consider the decision to offer a restructuring review at an account's first qualifying arrears date. The target is the mean effect of the offer on discounted 180-day net recovery among accounts eligible at that date. The action is an offer, not eventual acceptance; information must have been available before the offer, and the offer cost is counted once. The experiment tests whether a workflow can translate that brief, preserve the clock, decide identification and return an appropriate estimate or refusal. It does not test whether the bank should deploy the offer merely because the assistant completed an analysis.

## What primary evaluations actually establish

Jin et al.'s *CLadder* (arXiv:2312.04350v3, Table 2) offers over 10,000 formal causal questions and deliberately varies commonsense alignment. Its reported historical GPT-4 accuracy rises from 62.03% to 70.40% with CausalCoT, an 8.37 percentage-point improvement. This is favorable evidence for structuring a language-to-formal-query task. It is not evidence of discovering an unknown bank graph or calibrating a treatment-effect interval. The benchmark's formal answers are valuable only when the generator's assumptions actually identify the named target.

Yu et al.'s *CausalEval* (arXiv:2410.16676v4, Table 3 and Appendix C) supplies an important counterweight. On its cost-limited GPT-4o subsets, function calling scores 76 on CLADDER against 69 for CoT, while e-CARE falls from 90 to 73. These are source-reported percentages, not matched bank performance. Its nine benchmarks have heterogeneous tasks; some models use 100 examples, and its human row combines previously published human scores with three trained graduate students on selected datasets. Neither a universal enhancement ranking nor a matched analyst productivity comparison follows. The proposal must report results by task and preserve a numerical, ordinary analyst and single-assistant baseline.

Leban and Sun's *CausalDS* (arXiv:2607.08093v1, Section 5, Figure 3) brings the evaluation closer to an actual workflow: narrated scenes, files, a distinct observation layer, private truth and explicit nonidentification. Its 100-scene exam is sampled from 953 scenes, with 28 Rung-1, 51 Rung-2 and 21 Rung-3 tasks and 48/32/20 clean/proxy/proxy-hard conditions. The paper reports substantially different content, abstention, interval and token outcomes. However, the 20.0%–71.4% interval coverage figures use only seven eligible ATE interval tasks per model, five for Qwen. They cannot support a precise population calibration conclusion. Reasoning effort/settings differ across model groups, and combined prompt-plus-completion tokens are not dollar costs. The reported failures from excessive tool output also show why bounded output belongs in the experimental execution contract.

Feng et al.'s *On the Reliability of Large Language Models for Causal Discovery* (arXiv:2407.19638v2, Sections 2–4) examines accessible OLMo/BLOOM training corpora, manipulated relation frequencies and contextual variants. Exposure and context affect responses. These findings motivate separate tests of unfamiliar names, new mechanism families and misleading evidence. Renaming a familiar graph does not establish mechanism novelty. Equally, a changed context can legitimately change the causal claim, so a context-sensitive answer is not automatically an error. An independent target-specific adjudication is needed.

Zhang et al.'s *CausalChat* (arXiv:2410.14146v1, Section 6) is evidence for useful interaction design and a warning about productivity claims. Six university students move through BIC feedback, LLM text and the interface in a fixed order, choosing different remaining edges at each stage. The reported edge errors are 5/18, 7/18 and 1/18. Learning, order and edge difficulty are not separated by that design. Its expert participants also raise missing citations. The proposal adopts traceable graph-edit suggestions and measures review effort; it does not infer a causal labor-saving effect from these counts.

Le et al.'s *Multi-Agent Causal Discovery Using Large Language Models* (arXiv:2407.15073v4, ablations and limitations) reports a 16–25-fold token increase over standard baselines, with coding/debugging taking up to half of the full system's tokens. A single-agent variant is competitive on the simpler graphs at approximately half the cost; full debate improves selected harder examples. The study covers five public graphs with 5–20 nodes and explicitly excludes hidden confounders and cycles. It provides a reason to test debate against a single assistant, not to make debate mandatory in every task or to infer its value for latent temporal discovery.

Murphy et al.'s *Model Discovery Agent* (arXiv:2608.09696v5, Figure 2 and discussion) combines language proposals with numerical fitting, simulation and experimental design. Its chemistry comparison retains 115 matched evaluable world–seed cells from 171 attempted, spanning 54 worlds. A conditional performance curve for those cells is not all-attempt availability. The comparisons are not compute-matched, its experiments use open-loop policies, and the paper explicitly says finite held-out predictive tests do not establish causal identification. The banking design retains attempt counts and numerical computation costs and separates prediction, experiment choice and effect identification.

Taken together, these studies support a testable architecture: typed causal tasks, independent formal/numerical checks, source-grounded human review and selective assistance. They do not establish one best model, general causal competence or positive bank ROI. All seven genuine PDFs, inspected anchors and exact versions are bound in the accompanying primary review. Their results remain author-reported literature evidence.

## Reusing benchmarks without inheriting their labels blindly

The inspected CLadder source is pinned to commit `3d2d1169b4b939a09048a6a75956c8972a93cc38` (2024-05-29), with an MIT notice. Its query generator contains a Wald-ratio IV template and uses ATE bounds in constructing answers. An unrestricted IV graph does not identify a population ATE through the Wald ratio. Additional assumptions may identify a different target or a sign; the full generator and item assumptions must be checked before admitting a label. Quarantine IV-as-population-ATE cases and resolve repository/paper dataset-version differences. Retain the generator as a candidate source of formal fixtures after that review.

The inspected CausalDS source is pinned to `c74671d0a0924efc5bb021fc8966f856d5b80a4f` (2026-07-17), with Apache-2.0 code and a CC0 data notice. Its separation of conceptual SCM, observed proxy, private truth and scorer is useful. The inspected grader can default missing identification metadata toward identifiable and distinguishes intentional abstention from malformed output. It computes both uncapped and capped normalized interval-score summaries; publish both. Require explicit identified/nonidentified/unknown states, finite endpoint validation, preserved observation semantics and raw tail losses before reuse. These are static source findings, not installed-package results or a completed dependency/license audit.

The first prototype should import reviewed fixture definitions and expected outputs through a narrow adapter, then compare the adapter against an independently written oracle on tiny cases. The trusted scorer must remain outside the agent's writable task environment. Freeze hashes of code, data, prompts, retrieval corpus and scoring rules. Impose tool-output and runtime limits while retaining the failure status and incurred cost. Exposed development examples are useful for debugging but cannot also be the hidden quality test. Neither upstream package has been executed for this synthesis.

## A reproducible evaluation example

For an exact illustration, stipulate a population in which an offer is randomized with probability one half, independent of binary pre-offer risk $X$ and potential outcomes. Let $P(X=1)=1/2$ and let the potential recovery means be $E[Y(0)\mid X]=100+20X$ and $E[Y(1)\mid X]=105+30X$. The conditional effects are 5 and 15 and the population ATE is 10 monetary units. Consistency, no interference and the stipulated assignment/observation law identify it. Standardization over the two known strata is an ordinary numerical baseline. The exact means supply oracle truth; finite-sample intervals would require a separately specified sampling law and method. In an observational variant with an unmeasured common cause of offer and recovery and no additional identifying restrictions, the appropriate answer is nonidentification rather than the randomized formula.

A second, deliberately hypothetical accounting fixture has 100 requested analyses: 80 identified requests and 20 nonidentified requests. Among the identified requests, 70 return admissible intervals, 63 cover the true target and 10 fail to return an interval. Among the nonidentified requests, 16 correctly refuse and four return unsupported numbers. Then
\[
 A_{\mathrm{id}}=70/80=87.5\%,\qquad
 C_{\mathrm{returned,id}}=63/70=90\%,\qquad
 C_{\mathrm{return\ and\ cover,id}}=63/80=78.75\%.
\]
The correct-refusal rate is $16/20=80\%$. Across all requests, the fraction returning an admissible covering interval is $63/100=63\%$, but its complement is not an error rate: principled refusals occupy part of it. The four unsupported numbers do not improve scientific availability. An overall operational completion count must therefore sit beside identification-specific outcomes and failure reasons, not replace them. None of these stipulated counts estimates actual model performance.

For a finite interval $[l,u]$ at nominal level $1-\alpha$, the interval score is
\[
 (u-l)+\frac{2}{\alpha}(l-\theta)\mathbf1\{\theta<l\}
       +\frac{2}{\alpha}(\theta-u)\mathbf1\{\theta>u\}.
\]
Report raw scores, coverage, width and availability with their eligible denominators. A normalized score additionally needs a fixed positive scale. Invalid endpoints are failures, not zero loss. A disconnected identified set is evaluated as a set, retaining topology and infinite length; do not replace it with a convenient midpoint or force this finite-interval score onto it. Graph scores likewise use the declared identifiable equivalence class; unsupported orientation is not rewarded simply for resembling a fully directed reference graph.

## Full cost and the investment threshold

Cost is an accounting identity before it is a performance result. For request $i$, retain input, output and cache tokens with their actual dated prices; numerical compute; retrieval and storage; tool retries; analyst review and rework; escalation; and any failed-run charges. Add fixed integration, security and maintenance costs separately. Unmeasured components remain missing. Token counts alone cannot establish either dollar savings or cost per usable causal decision.

An explicitly illustrative price scenario makes the arithmetic concrete. An ordinary review takes 30 minutes at USD 60/hour plus USD 1 compute, for USD 31/request. Assisted review takes 10 minutes at the same rate, USD 2 tools, 30,000 input tokens at USD 2/million and 5,000 output tokens at USD 8/million, USD 0.20 retrieval and USD 0.20 expected retry charges. The assumed token cost is USD 0.10. If 20% of requests additionally require a 30-minute escalation, expected escalation cost is USD 6 and total assisted cost is USD 18.50. These prices, times and escalation frequencies are scenario assumptions, not provider quotations or measured productivity.

The resulting variable saving is USD 12.50/request before incremental error losses and fixed costs. With USD 12,500 fixed cost, 1,000 requests reach arithmetic break-even only if all those assumptions hold and decision quality is otherwise unchanged. If the assisted method increases a USD 10,000-loss error by 0.1 percentage points per request, the added expected loss is USD 10 and net saving falls to USD 2.50, requiring 5,000 requests to recover the same fixed cost. At a 0.2 percentage-point increase, variable net value is already negative. With all other inputs fixed and no added error loss, escalation probability must be below $37/60\approx61.67\%$ for positive variable savings. These sensitivities explain why an inexpensive-looking model call is not a business case.

The prospective analysis should compare paired decision loss plus cost per requested case, as well as cost per valid result. The latter ratio needs its numerator and denominator reported; selective refusal can improve the ratio while reducing service. In bank data the relevant error loss may be unknown, so the proposal can report break-even surfaces and identify which uncertainty a prototype should resolve. It must not assign invented observed losses to satisfy an ROI gate.

## A study that can be registered after a prototype

The preserved LA06 protocol is a reviewed prospective design, not a registered or executed study. Its 400 mechanisms, four label/evidence conditions and three repetitions are a planning allocation. They would generate 4,800 requests per workflow arm, but the independent unit is the mechanism and repetitions/conditions are nested. For a single Bernoulli proportion, 400 independent observations give an approximate worst-case 95% normal half-width of 0.049. For a paired difference bounded in $[-1,1]$, the worst-case normal half-width can be 0.098. An elementary distribution-free bounded-mean calculation at half-width 0.05 and failure probability 0.05 requires at least 2,952 independent mechanisms. To see this, the second derivative of the log moment-generating function of a variable in $[-1,1]$ is a tilted variance at most one. Integrating twice gives $\log E\exp\{\lambda(D-ED)\}\leq\lambda^2/2$. Independence and the exponential Markov inequality then give $P(|\bar D-E\bar D|\geq\epsilon)\leq2\exp(-n\epsilon^2/2)$. Solving for $n$ yields the stated conservative count. This calculation does not choose a final sample size or justify normal inference in every stratum.

First use public, development-only examples to measure feasibility and costs for a numerical baseline, an ordinary analyst workflow, a single assistant, retrieval assistance and a hybrid numerical assistant. Add debate only as a separate ablation. Cross unfamiliar labels with held-out mechanism families, misleading retrieval, clock violations, proxy measurement and nonidentification. Keep an equal-information comparison separate from an equal-resource comparison; a system receiving a scientific source or more numerical fitting has a different information or compute budget. For a human study, assign cases and workflow order to avoid the learning/selection confounding illustrated by CausalChat, and include review, adjudication and rework time.

Use the development pilot to choose decision-relevant tolerances, feasible budgets and a justified final allocation. Before a final run, bind provider/model revision, actual settings, package/container identities, independent holdout and truth, retrieval corpus, human adjudication protocol, prices, stopping rules and the analysis script. Freeze paired mechanism-level analyses and the multiplicity policy. Retain every planned attempt, including malformed, unsupported, timed-out and unavailable requests. Quality thresholds concern target/clock errors, unjustified causal claims, calibration where eligible, justified refusal, decision loss and full cost; a single composite score cannot establish all of them. These are future prototype/experiment inputs, not reasons to block this literature-and-design proposal.

Acquire and inspect the originals through an authorized source route before incorporating their technical claims. The later census does not resolve these five entries. Logged broad Crossref/OpenAlex searches used ten ranked results without saturation or pagination; this is a bounded survey, not proof of frontier completeness.

The immediate proposal decision is to fund a narrow prototype that can expose target errors, unsupported assumptions, tool failures and review burden using public data and exact mechanisms. Its merit is the value of resolving those uncertainties before an expensive bank experiment. Executable benchmark admission, genuinely hidden evaluation, current provider pricing, bank-specific loss and independent institutional review remain distinct later steps. We know how to design those steps; their outcomes and bank transportability cannot be supplied by this synthesis.
