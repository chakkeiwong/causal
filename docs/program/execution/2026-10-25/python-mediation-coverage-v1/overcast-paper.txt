A. Breaking down the Continuous Treatment Marginal Sensitivity Model
Let’s go deeper into the Continuous Treatment Marginal Sensitivity Model (CMSM).

A.1. MSM for binary treatment values [Tan, 2006]
For binary treatments, TB = {0, 1}, the (nominal) propensity score, e(x) ≡ P r(T = 1 | X = x), states how the treatment
status, t, depends on the covariates, x, and is identifiable from observational data. The potential outcomes, Y0 and Y1 ,
conditioned on the covariates, x, are distributed as P (Y0 | X = x) and P (Y1 | X = x). Each of these conditional
distributions can be written as mixtures with weights based on the propensity score:

                  P (Y0 | X = x) = (1 − e(x))P (Y0 | T = 0, X = x) + e(x)P (Y0 | T = 1, X = x),
                                                                                                                       (18)
                  P (Y1 | X = x) = (1 − e(x))P (Y0 | T = 1, X = x) + e(x)P (Y1 | T = 1, X = x).

The conditional distributions of each potential outcome given the observed treatment, P (Y0 | T = 0, X = x) and
P (Y1 | T = 1, X = x), are identifiable from observational data, whereas the conditional distributions of each potential
outcome given the counterfactual treatment, P (Y0 | T = 1, X = x) and P (Y1 | T = 0, X = x) are not. Under ignorability,
{Y0 , Y1 } ⊥
           ⊥ T | X = x, P (Y0 | T = 0, X = x) = P (Y0 | T = 1, X = x) and P (Y1 | T = 1, X = x) = P (Y1 | T =
0, X = x). Therefore, any deviation from these equalities will be indicative of hidden confounding. However, because the
distributions P (Y0 | T = 1, X = x) and P (Y1 | T = 0, X = x) are unidentifiable, the MSM postulates a relationship
between each pair of identifiable and unidentifiable components.

F.2. Observations of clouds and aerosol




                             Figure 7. Workflow of observed clouds from satellite to ingestion by model.


The Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) instrument aboard the Aqua satellite observes the Earth
twice daily at ∼1 km x 1 km resolution native resolution (Level 1) [Baum & Platnick, 2006]. We used the daily mean, 1◦ x
1◦ gridded version (Level 2) in order to somewhat homogenize our observations of clouds and the atmosphere confined to a
region off the coast of South America in the Pacific basin. MODIS observations are fed into the Modern-Era Retrospective
analysis for Research and Applications version 2 (MERRA-2) real-time model in order to emulate the atmosphere and it’s
components, such as aerosol [Gelaro et al., 2017]. Aerosol optical depth at 550nm from MERRA-2 is derived from MODIS
observations of aerosol from multiple satellites (Terra, Aqua, Suomi-NPP), with corrections for sun glint and near-cloud
optical effects [Bosilovich et al., 2015]. We collocated all gridded observations of clouds and reanalysis aerosol with our
meteorological proxies of the environment (EIS, SST, w500, RH700, RH850), then normalized our features before feeding
them into the model.
