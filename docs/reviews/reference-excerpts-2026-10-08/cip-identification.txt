\chapter[Identification and Parametrization]{Identification, Stationarity, and Parametrization}
\label{ch:identification}

\label{subsec:identification}
%======================================================================

This chapter addresses the identification, stationarity, and
parameterization conditions that are prerequisites for the estimation
pipeline of Part~V\@.  The underlying mathematics draws on the affine
rotation-invariance literature \citep{dai2000,joslin2011} and the
AFNS-specific normalizations of \citet{christensen2011}.  The synthesis
problem is that the two-country, multi-asset extension introduces cross-block
interactions (domestic yield factors feeding into the foreign SDF through
$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1^f$) that are not present in single-country models,
requiring new counting arguments and explicit block-sparsity restrictions
to ensure that the model is both identified and estimable.

The exponential affine framework of the preceding sections defines a rich
model with many free parameters. Before estimation can proceed, three
interrelated issues must be resolved: (i) the fundamental constraint linking
the physical dynamics, the risk-neutral dynamics, and the market prices of
risk---and the danger of over-specification if all three are independently
parametrized; (ii) the affine rotation invariance that renders the model
unidentified without normalization restrictions; and (iii) the stationarity
conditions and initial distribution required for well-defined likelihood
evaluation via the Kalman filter. This section addresses each issue in detail,
culminating in a set of sufficient conditions for identification and a
concrete parameterization that facilitates Bayesian estimation.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Fundamental Constraint: \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{P}$}{P}
+ \texorpdfstring{$\Lambda$}{Lambda}
= \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{Q}$}{Q}}
\label{subsubsec:P_plus_lambda}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The most consequential parametrization decision concerns the relationship
among the physical dynamics ($\mathbb{P}$-parameters), the market prices of
risk ($\Lambda$-parameters), and the risk-neutral dynamics
($\mathbb{Q}$-parameters). These three sets are \emph{not} independent: given
any two, the third is uniquely determined. Failure to respect this constraint
leads to over-specification---a model with more free parameters than degrees
of freedom, resulting in a singular or near-singular Fisher information matrix
and an unidentified likelihood.

\paragraph{The change-of-measure relationship.}
In continuous time, the state $\mathbf{X}_t$ evolves under the physical
measure $\mathbb{P}$ as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:X_P_continuous}
d\mathbf{X}_t = \mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{P}}
- \mathbf{X}_t\bigr)\,dt + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,d\mathbf{W}_t^{\mathbb{P}},
\end{equation}
and under the domestic risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}^d$ as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:X_Q_continuous}
d\mathbf{X}_t = (\mathbf m^{\mathbb Q}-\mathbf K^{\mathbb Q}\mathbf X_t)\,dt +
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,d\mathbf{W}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^d}.
\end{equation}
The Girsanov change of measure is defined by the essentially affine market
price of risk (\citet{duffee2002}):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:girsanov}
d\mathbf{W}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^d} = d\mathbf{W}_t^{\mathbb{P}} +
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t\,dt, \qquad
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t = \boldsymbol{\gamma}_0 +
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1\mathbf{X}_t,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^K$ and $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1
\in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$. Substituting~\eqref{eq:girsanov}
into~\eqref{eq:X_P_continuous} and matching coefficients
with~\eqref{eq:X_Q_continuous} yields the \emph{fundamental constraint}:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{Q}} &= \mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{P}} +
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1,
\label{eq:KQ_from_KP} \\[4pt]
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{Q}} &=
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{P}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{P}} -
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_0.
\label{eq:KQthQ_from_KPthP}
\end{align}
These $K^2 + K$ scalar equations link the three drift representations.  The
derived affine drift vector $\mathbf m^{\mathbb Q}$ is primary when
$\mathbf K^{\mathbb Q}$ is singular; a finite $\boldsymbol\theta^{\mathbb Q}$
is used only on nonsingular blocks.  They link the
$(\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{P}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{P}})$,
$(\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{Q}})$, and
$(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_0, \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1)$. Only two of the three
parameter sets are free; the third is \emph{derived}.

\paragraph{The over-specification trap.}
If one independently specifies
$(\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{P}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{P}})$,
$(\mathbf{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbb{Q}})$, and
$(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_0, \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_1)$ as free parameters in the
estimation, the model has $K^2 + K$ more parameters than degrees of freedom.
The likelihood is constant along the $(K^2 + K)$-dimensional manifold defined
by~\eqref{eq:KQ_from_KP}--\eqref{eq:KQthQ_from_KPthP}, the Fisher
information matrix is rank-deficient. A proper prior can regularize these
directions and yield a proper posterior; neither posterior impropriety nor
MCMC failure follows automatically. Nevertheless the redundant directions
remain unidentified by the likelihood and can create difficult geometry.
Prior regularization must not be reported as data identification.

$(\mathbb{P}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda})$, derive $\mathbb{Q}$.}
