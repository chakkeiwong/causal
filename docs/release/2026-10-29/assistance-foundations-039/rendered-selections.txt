64.9.1    Translation must preserve the decision and the information clock

Suppose a collections manager asks whether a reminder should be sent before a payment
deadline. A usable translation specifies the eligible accounts, the decision time, the intervention
actually available then, the outcome horizon and the monetary loss function. A variable
called customer engagement must resolve into a measurement, timestamp and missingness
rule. Replies recorded after the reminder cannot silently become pretreatment adjustment
variables. The proposed graph must state which arrows come from dated process evidence,
which reflect scientific assumptions, and which are uncertain alternatives. A well-formed graph
file cannot supply that missing evidence.
   This is a proposed contract for the library, CLI and MCP interfaces. A translation artifact
should bind the request, source snapshots, decision time, variable definitions, candidate graph
and target query. A downstream identification request should name that artifact and return
assumptions and unresolved alternatives with its formula or refusal. The explanation stage
should cite the same artifact identities. A new prompt, retrieved document or graph revision
creates a new candidate, requiring the appropriate checks again. Original model APIs, local
models or a deterministic questionnaire can provide the translation; no language model is
required to be the scientific authority.
   The survey’s prompt discussion reports sensitivity to wording, demonstrations and their
order. Its hallucination discussion distinguishes contradiction of a source from an assertion not
determined by that source. Unsupported, contradicted and supported claims therefore need
different statuses. An unsupported edge may be a reasonable hypothesis, but repeated prompts
cannot promote it into evidence. Underlying method papers require their own inspected
originals before their technical results justify adoption. (Kaddour et al., 2023)

   The posterior mass of A → B is 81/91, approximately 0.8901, while that of B → C is 90/91,
approximately 0.9890. At a posterior retention threshold of 0.9, the latter orientation can be

The legal-class restriction and joint evidence determine the result. If instead the advice is the
incompatible vector (1, 0), the posterior becomes (9, 1, 9)/19. Each advised orientation then
has posterior mass 9/19. Disagreement with the graph constraints provides a reason to reduce
confidence, not a license to force the fourth, inadmissible graph into the class.
   For a bank, this example illustrates why one cannot independently approve an “advice
causes activity” arrow and a “credit draw causes activity” arrow and assume that both fit the
diagram already justified by data. The variables need precise definitions and clocks before
that particular class is even plausible. If documented time ordering rules out one orientation,
it is additional structural information and should change the class explicitly. It should not be
disguised as stronger language-model confidence. Conversely, if a credible account requires
a hidden common cause, the three-DAG class is the wrong model family; redistributing its
posterior cannot repair the missing graph.


67.10.3     Repeated prompts and the missing adviser law

The constant-error model is attractive because it makes the calculation transparent. Its
applicability to a language model is an empirical question. Long et al. score the two directional
answer tokens and normalize their log probabilities with a softmax. Their fixed prompt gives
deterministic output; the experiments randomize among causal verbs to introduce variation.
This historical procedure concerns text-davinci-002 and text-davinci-003. A softmax score is a
model preference over the presented alternatives, while the needed likelihood concerns error
relative to a true orientation. Agreement between the two quantities requires evidence. (Long
et al., 2023)
  The distinction is especially visible when an assistant asks the same question repeatedly.

   Changing a verb, sampling a different completion or asking a second model does not by itself
establish conditional independence. Models can share training material, prompts can share a
misconception, and several retrieved passages can repeat one source. A practical adviser model
may therefore need grouped errors, variable-pair difficulty, abstention, asymmetric mistakes and
dependence on the wording or supplied documents. These are proposed modeling extensions,
not properties already verified for the bank. An alternative is to keep elicited answers as soft
sensitivity inputs and report how conclusions change as their influence varies. Another is to
use the answers only to prioritize human investigation while leaving the admissible graph class
intact.
