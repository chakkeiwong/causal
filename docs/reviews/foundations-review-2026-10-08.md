## What the argument now establishes

**Graphs and instruments (chapters 1--2).** A constructed bank decision
clock connects SCM and potential-outcome restrictions to adjustment,
recursive front-door ID, conditional-ID rescue, mediation and paired-model
nonidentification. Linear IV moments, LATE/compliance weights, weak
denominators and scalar monotone control ranks have distinct targets and
restrictions. Recovering a rank does not identify an unsupported intervention
mean. General path-specific ID and arbitrary latent controls are not
claimed as supported algorithms.

**Learning and inverse problems (chapters 3--4).** The ATE gradient,
orthogonal remainder, targeting score, local CATE rate argument and
learner-loss comparisons are explicit. Nonlinear IV and proxies share an
operator analysis distinguishing injectivity, stable inversion and regular
estimation of a functional. Global ATE rates do not replace local CATE or
simultaneous-band guarantees. Learned representations need information
preservation or substantive additional restrictions.

**Sequential interventions and controlled states (chapters 5--7).** One
event clock connects the g-formula, weighting, MSMs, SNMMs, augmentation and
a controlled latent likelihood. The fixed-horizon score has a pathwise
proof, general remainder and explicit targeting equations. Incremental and
modified policies change the score; censoring, competing events and
eligibility change the target. Private logging, known-emission recovery,
proxy-value assumptions and dynamic-choice equivalence expose distinct
identification problems. Likelihood fitting and Bellman solution cannot
resolve observational equivalence.
The final review added marked reward-transition identification because
an observed reward can reveal state even when its mean does not. Its exact
counterexample distinguishes compressed summaries from the full observed
law. The proximal-value discussion now states latent bridge existence
separately from solvability of observed bridge equations.

**Panels, shocks, discovery and observation (chapters 8--11).** Panel
moments, cohort effects, structural shocks, LP-IV and dependence conditions
are developed. Discovery distinguishes graph equivalence, functional-model
information, faithfulness and window effects. Observation analysis treats
availability, missingness, measurement inversion, linkage uncertainty and
accounting identities as parts of the statistical experiment. Continuous
time and informative irregular observations are developed in chapter 12;
general recovery from arbitrary coarsening remains outside the claimed scope.

**Decision learning, transport and sensitivity (chapters 13--15).** A finite
policy-class regret proof accompanies the scope of the VC theorem. Adaptive
randomization has a derived mixture confidence sequence and exact repeated
testing counterexample. Exposure-map interference, transport gradients,
proxy-shift failure and invariant-subset inference are explicit.
Sensitivity separates assumption-based identified sets from sampling
confidence sets. A signed OVB discrepancy between Theorem 2 and Appendix
B.4 in the archived primary is recorded; the text uses the sign established
by its projection proof. Exact checks support specified constructions, not
general theorem verification.

**Continuous time (chapter 12).** Controlled diffusion and counting-process
interventions are joined to the decision clock. Exact discrete sampling,
aliasing, local martingale likelihood ratios and informative no-event
filtering are derived under stated restrictions. A lag coefficient alone
does not identify a finer-time structural response.

**Contemporary methods (chapter 16).** Trajectory-law learning, balanced
representations, dynamic orthogonal scores, amortized causal/proximal
inference, history-dependent OPE hardness, dynamic bounds and temporal
discovery are compared at their identifying restrictions. The wide-proxy
counterexample now conditions its nonzero null function on (U1,A), with
exact conditional enumeration; centering only on A was insufficient.

**Software composition (chapter 17).** All 44 projects have a mathematical
contract in software-qualification.json: 51 inspected anchors and eight
selected APIs pinned to cutoff-bounded commits. Target, clock, weights,
graph endpoints and dependence must survive conversion. The lmtp ipsi
transform differs from odds tilting; their gradients are derived and
checked. These are source/document qualifications, with no package runtime
qualification or executed adapter acceptance test.

**Product construction (chapters 18--19).** Seven bounded operations follow
from the mathematical objects and their identification/inference limits.
The design preserves target, observation and sampling laws across adapters.
The scientific protocol separates computation, identification and uncertainty;
its 8 valid and 13 deliberately invalid fixtures check representation and
hash binding. They do not execute a causal backend. Standalone workspaces,
application interfaces, physical execution and acceptance criteria are specified.

**Banking studies and investment (chapters 20--21 and sponsor).** Re-executed
synthetic studies retain negative contribution, logger misspecification,
poor interval coverage and capped fits. Separate finite-path and financial
account checks support the displayed results. The rerun scale measurement
covers precomputed score reduction only. The cost model compares equivalent
outputs and retains low-reuse losses, adoption-ramp costs and unpriced
institutional participation. Product demand and savings remain hypotheses.

## Completion and remaining boundaries

Cross-chapter author review is complete for the bounded proposal scope;
concrete defects and repairs are recorded. Earlier product drafting overlapped
the review, but its final qualification follows the theoretical repairs.
Read sections and finite checks do not justify a claim that every general
theorem in every cited source has been independently proved.

The next construction program must qualify external-package runtimes,
implement adapters and workspaces, validate institutional observation and
assignment mechanisms, and measure equivalent-study effort and demand.

artifacts with narrower scope. None establishes a deployed banking product
or accepted integration with a sibling application.
