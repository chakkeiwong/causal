47.23.3     What the Julia delegates preserve, and what the model must iden-
            tify

The survival proposal becomes more precise when the two named delegates are read alongside
their caller. The selected SurvivalBase revision declares version 0.1.0 in June 2026; Sur-
vivalDistributions declares 0.1.1 in July 2026. Both carry direct MIT notices. These are
pre-cutoff source snapshots, not an observed dependency resolution or a tested installation of
SurvivalModels. Their narrower roles are valuable: SurvivalBase constructs formula terms, and
SurvivalDistributions supplies distribution families and hazard operations. Neither supplies
the treatment assignment, censoring independence or transport assumptions needed for an
intervention recommendation. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026a,c,b)
   SurvivalBase’s Surv term produces two columns: observed time and an event indicator. Its
README explicitly limits the current binding to right-censored observations. The selected
term has no entry-time field or pair of interval-censoring endpoints. The parent general-
hazard likelihood then uses the event contribution minus cumulative hazard from origin. This
composition is coherent for its stated observation type, but passing two convenient columns
cannot encode a delayed-entry experiment. When entry and censoring are independent of
event time conditional on the modeled covariates, an observation entering at time l and exiting
at time t instead contributes the event-time part of the conditional log likelihood
                            ℓ = δ log h(t) − H(t) + H(l),       t > l,                     (47.57)
where h is the hazard, H its cumulative integral and δ indicates an observed event. The last
term conditions on survival to entry. It is not recovered by relabeling entry as an interval-
censoring endpoint or by retaining only the exit time. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026a,b; Causal
Banking Research Project, 2026)
   An exact small construction shows the consequence for risk costing. Two accounts enter
at month four; one defaults at month five and the other is right censored at month six. In a
stipulated exponential model with constant rate λ, the conditional exposure is three account-
months and the maximum-likelihood rate is 1/3. Treating both accounts as observed from

  Even correctly encoded observations need an identifiable parameterization. The parent
general-hazard implementation composes the baseline as

                  h(t | x, z) = h0 (tezα )exβ ,   H(t | x, z) = H0 (tezα )exβ−zα .        (47.58)

Here the first linear predictor governs the hazard level, the second changes the time scale, and
subscript zero denotes the baseline hazard and its cumulative integral. The second expression
follows by changing variables in the hazard integral. The caller’s time and hazard multipliers
implement this distinction; simply multiplying cumulative hazard by the hazard-level factor
would omit the time-change correction. Yet retaining both coefficient blocks in an output does
not prove that the data distinguish them. Take a Weibull baseline with shape k > 0, scale
s > 0 and one covariate shared by the two blocks, so z = x. Then

                             H(t | x) = (t/s)k exp(x{(k − 1)α + β}) .                     (47.59)

Only the displayed linear combination enters the conditional law. Replacing the coefficient pair
by (α + c, β − (k − 1)c) leaves every hazard, survival probability and right-censored likelihood
contribution unchanged. For shape two, the pairs (0, log 3) and (log 2, log(3/2)) are an exact
example. One labels the covariate as a pure hazard-level change; the other assigns part to the
time scale, although they predict the same event distribution. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026b;

   The parent removes coefficient blocks that are inactive by method definition before inverting
an observed Hessian. That avoids one source of singularity, but its general-hazard branch retains
both blocks and does not remove this baseline-dependent ridge. With an exponential baseline,
an accelerated-hazard time coefficient disappears altogether because the baseline hazard is
constant. A finite optimizer result, inverse-Hessian request or documentation calling the active
parameters identified cannot establish separate identification. The defensible alternatives
are to restrict the scientific model, report the identified combination and its uncertainty, or
justify a different baseline and covariate design with diagnostics for weak identification. No
distribution fit alone turns either coefficient into a causal effect of payment holidays. These
statements concern exact parameter redundancy; no optimizer failure frequency is claimed.
(JuliaSurv contributors, 2026b; Causal Banking Research Project, 2026)

represents a logistic distribution for log event time. Writing its location as µ ∈ R and its
positive scale as σ, its median is eµ . The parent’s baseline-fitting path exponentiates every
baseline parameter, however, including this location. For a baseline-only fit without regression
covariates, a median of half a month requires µ = log(1/2) < 0 and is outside that transformed
fitting domain. Expressing the same law in days gives a median of fifteen and location
log 15 > 0. Changing units should not change whether a physical distribution belongs to
the admitted model. A distribution-specific transformation must distinguish unrestricted
locations from positive scales and shapes. Regression may absorb some scale changes in other
designs, so this example is deliberately limited to the baseline-only fit. It neither asserts that
every baseline is affected nor predicts the optimizer’s returned approximation. (JuliaSurv

47.23.4    Tail behavior and the meaning of a survival output

The piecewise-constant hazard delegate exposes a second consequential boundary. It declares
that the last supplied hazard continues after the final finite time interval. Its inspected
quantile loop nevertheless returns that interval’s endpoint when the requested cumulative
hazard exceeds the accumulated finite-grid hazard. The cumulative-hazard tail refers to L
although the argument is D; the expectation method takes L but accesses D. Those names are
not bound by the inspected module. The abstract hazard fallback likewise calls numerical
helpers that the selected module does not import or define. These are specific source obstacles
to admitting these paths, not evidence that every distribution in the package fails. Fixing
a variable name alone would not establish the intended tail law or qualify the numerical
implementation. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026c)
  For a one-month grid interval with hazard 1/5 per month continuing forever, the stipulated
law is simply exponential. Its ninetieth percentile is

                               q0.9 = 5 log 10 ≃ 11.513 months,                           (47.60)

whereas the finite-loop trace returns one month. Survival at that endpoint is still about 0.819,
so the difference cannot be dismissed as a negligible tail. At a stipulated continuous receipt
rate of USD 100 per month until termination, with no discounting, competing event or cost,
expected total receipts are USD 500. Receipts restricted to the first month are only USD 90.63.
Both are legitimate targets when labeled; silently imposing the grid endpoint would substitute
one for the other. A bank requiring a finite valuation horizon should specify that horizon and
integrate to it. A model claiming an unbounded mean must establish a proper tail and finite
expectation. The example reconstructs the source branch algebraically and does not run the
downloaded Julia implementation. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026c; Causal Banking Research
Project, 2026)
   Output names are another source of silent substitution. In the parent, predict_expected
returns cumulative hazard; the survival method exponentiates its negative. Cumulative hazard
is neither a mean event time nor an event probability. If it equals two at the chosen horizon,
the corresponding event probability is 1 − e−2 ≃ 0.865. With an assumed USD 100 loss on
that event, expected loss is USD 86.47, not USD 200. The no-time prediction also evaluates
each training subject at its own observed time, whereas a portfolio decision generally requires
a common future horizon. The adapter must carry target, units, population and horizon with
each array rather than infer them from a method name. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026b; Causal
Banking Research Project, 2026)
   These findings support a conditional comparison, with distinct admission decisions for right-
censored parametric fitting, a piecewise hazard representation and a general-hazard coefficient
model. Preserve the useful formula and distribution interfaces while testing entry/censoring
meaning, parameter domains, common-horizon prediction, likelihood rank, tail inversion and
time-unit changes against independent laws. Include optimizer convergence and uncertainty
failures in the result: the inspected fit stores the minimizer and objective without retaining
a convergence flag in its model fields. Native qualification and transitive dependency review
remain to be done. The direct MIT notices identify the selected components’ declared terms;
they do not establish scientific calibration or clear every dependency. For the banking decision,
choose the least elaborate model whose assumptions and identified outputs answer the question,
and retain uncertainty about the causal interpretation separately from uncertainty within a
fitted survival law. (JuliaSurv contributors, 2026a,c,b)

The Cameron-Lyons survival repository declares version 2.0.0, edition 2024, Rust 1.94 and MIT

receives follow-up min(Te − tA , τ ), while the control clone uses a clock censored at treatment

maximum follow-up five, the treated duration becomes five but its event flag retains the original
event status. The authored trace is (5, 1), while an origin-clock observation administratively

protocol-censored clones a constant based on the trimming option and can then normalise

two and four and their mean-normalised weights are 2/3 and 4/3. Giving both records ten

47.23.6    An interval-data candidate and a justified refusal

The small Rust turnbull snapshot declares version 0.1.0 and edition 2021. No licence grant was
located in the inspected Cargo declaration or complete root tree, so adoption or redistribution
cannot be presumed. Its response type includes interval and entry information, but the

47.23.7    Adoption choices, public prototypes and full costs

The preferred architecture preserves selected original engines behind typed, versioned workers.
JuliaStats is a candidate for narrowly specified right-censored estimation after boundary
qualification; SurvivalAnalysis for its declared parametric response family; SurvivalModels
for a restricted model with verified parameter domains, identifiable outputs, tail behavior
and tie/prediction contracts; and JointSurvivalModels for a stipulated joint distribution after
numerical and random generator checks. Rust components can be reference or future worker
candidates, but the inspected causal entrypoints require semantic repair before admission.
The interval-data prototype remains reference-only while its licence and support questions are
unresolved. Whole-library ports are not justified by language preference alone: a port transfers
maintenance, rights, numerical and semantic obligations without supplying identification.
   A generated/public prototype can test the merit of this architecture before an expensive
bank experiment. Register exact-event, all-censored, zero-time, delayed-entry, interval-only,
tied-event, competing-event, administrative-censoring and malformed-input fixtures; match
each fixture to a target-specific oracle. Compare scalar and vector outputs, time-unit rescaling,
row permutations, random-seed routing and complete estimator resampling. Separately
evaluate uncertainty under a declared independent or clustered sampling law. Finite-law
examples establish those exact laws; public data establish only the observable behaviours and
assumptions they actually support. Neither silently supplies counterfactual bank truth.
