restrictions that may be relevant in empirical work. For example, researchers studying labour
market training and related programs may be concerned about Ashenfelter’s dip (Ashenfelter,
1978), in which earnings for the treated group trend downwards (relative to control) before treat-
ment and upwards afterwards. In this type of setting, researchers might naturally use a polyhedral
 to impose (i) restrictions on the signs of the pre-treatment and post-treatment biases, as well
as (ii) restrictions on the magnitude of the rebound effect relative to the pre-treatment shock.
