69.28.4     A token score does not have the same meaning in three discovery
            engines

The five pinned LLM-and-SCD notebooks expose a complete research route from an initial
graph and bootstrap summaries through two prompts to constrained rediscovery. In a selected
collection cell, the code sums the first token’s top-five probability mass only when the token is
exactly Yes or yes. It separately accumulates No or no. It does not identify all semantically
equivalent strings or turn the unreported mass into an explicit uncertainty object. The resulting
score is a property of the prompted token distribution, not a probability that an edge exists in
the bank. (Masataka Takayama and contributors, 2024)
   An exact authored example makes the parsing boundary visible. Suppose the five tokens
have masses 0.96, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005 and 0.005, assigned to Yes, No, a newline, I and It. The
literal affirmative score is 0.96. Change only the first token to a leading-space Yes; the exact-
string collector assigns affirmative score zero, although a whitespace-normalized semantic class
still has mass 0.96. These are constructed input distributions, not observed endpoint outputs.
They show why unrecognized formats and truncated token lists need explicit treatment before
any scientific constraint is created. Simple normalization would fix this example but would not
establish causal calibration or solve multi-token answer semantics. (Causal banking proposal,
2026x)

below 0.05 as forbidden, above 0.95 as required, and the interval between them as unknown.

an inclusive upper threshold. The 0.05 boundary is unknown in both. The notebook commit

direct A → Y edge exists, but an A-to-Y directed path does. Converting “no direct impact”

69.28.5     A public and generated prototype with a decision criterion

A useful first prototype has two stages. Deterministic semantic fixtures check missing answers,
unsupported methods, token variants, exact threshold boundaries, graph endpoint preservation,
cycles, edge-versus-path conversion and request/result identity. These tests are cheap and
have known expected answers. They establish an adapter contract; they do not estimate live
model quality. Only then should the team profile a matched discovery experiment and register
its final design.
  The experiment should split by mechanism family. Repeated numerical samples, variable
names, retrieved packets and prompts belong inside a mechanism’s cluster. The Gaussian
pair above is a compulsory ambiguity case, accompanied by causally sufficient, non-Gaussian
and latent cases with explicit clocks. Generator truth and effect oracles remain outside the
prompts. Natural descriptions, neutral renamed descriptions and deliberately misleading
descriptions are crossed with no retrieval, applicable original evidence, wrong-domain evidence
and contradiction. Removing meaningful definitions changes the information supplied, so the
comparison must say when it tests robustness and when it tests the value of information.
  Each language arm needs the same numerical inputs and a declared comparator: a dictio-
nary/clock baseline; PC or FCI under the appropriate assumptions; score or non-Gaussian
methods within their qualified regimes; language-only proposals; and soft or hard guidance
with declared strength and refusal rules. Final graphs alone are insufficient. Record class
and endpoint retention, invalid outputs, valid abstentions, conditional effect-set retention and
width, and downstream action regret under known truth. Include every requested case in the
denominator, including parse failures, timeouts, empty outputs and conflicting constraints.
Measure total calls, tokens, retries, numerical resources, latency and adjudication effort.
   Before final runs, freeze versions, rights, splits, targets, sample sizes, replication counts,
tolerances, primary paired comparisons and multiplicity treatment. Development profiling
may set resource caps; it cannot become an unannounced final experiment. Prompt variants
and prior strengths must not be selected on the final mechanisms. An optional live model
experiment needs the chosen endpoint’s actual revision and measured costs, but none of these
design steps requires bank data or a paid experiment now.
   The banking example supplies a transparent economic threshold. Stipulate USD 200 per
outcome unit and USD 60 for the unit offer increase. Net values are USD 40 in the direct-
effect world and minus USD 60 in the latent world. If a planner assigns probability p to the
former, the expected net action value is 100p − 60. Because the observed likelihood is the
same, additional observational records alone cannot update those odds within this two-world
comparison. At p = 0.9 the expected value is USD 30, while perfect knowledge would produce
USD 36 by taking the action only in the favorable world. Thus USD 6 per decision is an
upper bound on the expected value of any information experiment under those assumptions.
At p = 0.5 it is USD 20. A real noisy experiment supplies less information and incurs costs;
neither number is bank ROI or an authorized spending budget. (Causal banking proposal,
2026x)
  The resulting recommendation is to reuse typed evidence, explicit conflict handling and

bounded search guidance as design references; to defer enabled package integration until the
specified contracts are verified; and to reject automatic conversion of response agreement into
causal confidence. The unresolved scientific task is to justify useful graph/effect uncertainty
for the complete adaptive procedure in declared regimes. The small-language-model discovery
lead currently has metadata but no acquired original; its methods cannot support a technical
comparison yet. Broader literature coverage and that original remain explicit survey work.
They do not prevent the proposed public/generated prototype from testing the concrete
semantic and decision boundaries established here.


69.29      Expert-guided graph ensembles and their intervention
           limits
A bank building a diagram from several discovery algorithms will often face agreement about
a relationship but disagreement about its direction. The methods may also miss different
relationships because they use different scores, tests and structural restrictions. Asking a
domain expert to resolve selected disagreements is a plausible use of an assistant. The design
question is what the expert is being asked, how its answer changes the graph, and whether the
resulting graph supports the bank’s intervention query.
   Tench and Demeester propose a dynamic expert-guided ensemble that distinguishes adjacency
from orientation. It counts how many component graphs contain any edge between a pair,
considers pairs in descending frequency, and adds directed edges subject to an acyclicity check.
A lower adjacency threshold determines which pairs are eligible. For eligible pairs without a
strict majority, it asks the expert whether a plausible causal relationship exists. A separate
threshold controls when direction votes suffice and when expert orientation is requested. The
procedure uses equal component weights and a finite query budget. This is greedy graph
construction; the term model averaging does not establish a Bayesian posterior over causal
models. (Tench and Demeester, 2026)


69.29.1    An expert’s causal ordering is not a direct edge

The source deliberately permits an expert to answer about a direct or indirect causal path.
This is weaker than knowing whether two nodes are adjacent in the full graph. In a stipulated
offer–uptake–balance chain O → U → Y , changing the offer may affect balance through uptake
even though O → Y is not a direct edge. An expert can answer the path question correctly
while accepting a spurious direct connection proposed by a component method. The paper
recognizes this distinction in its simulated expert and compares it with an expert that knows
direct edges. (Tench and Demeester, 2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026al)
   The proposed bank interface should therefore carry the proposition being elicited: adjacency,
ancestry, orientation conditional on adjacency, or a temporal exclusion. It should also permit
insufficient knowledge. A reply about plausible ancestry cannot silently become proof of a
direct edge, and a forced binary answer cannot represent every legitimate uncertainty state.
The set of variables matters: marginalizing uptake changes the observed-variable graph even
when the underlying mechanism is unchanged.
  The method’s output is a DAG. Its component comparison includes FCI, while its noisy
benchmarks can contain latent confounding. A directed output cannot by itself retain every
bidirected or unresolved endpoint in a latent graph. Before an adapter is proposed, the
conversion from each component’s graph type must be explicit, with unsupported endpoints
preserved or refused. Acyclicity checks alone do not establish compatibility with an input

and Demeester, 2026)

69.29.4    Resolve the printed query contract before reuse

The prose says that a fixed budget bounds queries and exhausted decisions fall back to majority
voting. The printed Algorithm 1 leaves two details unresolved. For an existence query, the
counter increment lies inside the branch reached only when the expert accepts the connection.
With three eligible minority connections, budget one, and three rejections, a literal trace
makes three expert calls while the printed counter remains zero. Separately, when an accepted
adjacency has two legal directions, neither reaches the orientation threshold, and no budget
remains, the printed conditional chain has no final branch adding the majority direction. An
example with direction votes 3/4 and 1/4, threshold 9/10 and budget zero therefore leaves the
edge unassigned under the printed chain. (Causal banking proposal project, 2026al)

a different scientific question. The method and data lead in the paper are useful acquisition
routes; package names and reported license labels are not sufficient permission or provenance
for direct adoption.

The preceding example leaves a practical question. Can an assistant quantify its uncertainty

Such a workflow would be useful if its final intervals retained the mechanisms relevant to the

neural spline flows. This is a substantive implementation proposal with reported biomedical
comparisons. Its availability permits a more precise assessment than either accepting the

69.30.2    The information inequality remains useful in its stated direction

Theorem 5 follows from convexity and reduction to an event. Let P be a reference distribution,
Sk predictive distributions, and S̄ = Ek Sk their mixture. For an event A, put p = P (A) and
q = S̄(A). Under absolute continuity and finite expected relative entropy,

                                Ek KL(P ∥Sk ) ≥ KL(P ∥S̄) ≥ d(p, q).                          (69.13)

The first step is Jensen’s inequality in the second distribution; the second is the information
