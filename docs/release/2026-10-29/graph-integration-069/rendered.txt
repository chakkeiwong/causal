69.30.3     Independent edge draws need a coherent graph law

For an accepted edge the thesis uses one minus the risk score; for a rejected edge it uses the risk
score. It then samples edges independently and applies topological sorting. Sorting represents
an already acyclic graph in an appropriate order; it cannot make every independently drawn
graph acyclic. The treatment of a failed sort is part of the probability law. The following
example diagnoses the general construction and does not claim that the thesis’s particular
draws contained a cycle. (Molero Pérez, 2026)
  Suppose the only possible arrows are A → B, B → C and C → A, each assigned probability

cannot be justified, retain compatible graphs and propagate their effect sets instead. Neither
route assumes that a linguistic probability score is already a posterior over banking mechanisms.


69.30.4     An interval comparison needs an intervention reference

The thesis trains four groups of 100 flow models: an expert reference graph, the sampled
language-derived graphs, the expert graph’s ordering without its adjacency restrictions, and
random orderings without those restrictions. Within each group, the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles of the fitted effect estimates define an interval. The reported interval-overlap means
are .723, .712 and .301 for the last three groups compared with the fitted expert-graph group.
The first two differ by .011. That contrast describes these fitted model intervals; the table does
not provide a paired uncertainty interval for the difference. It is not evidence that language
assistance generally outperforms knowledge of the causal ordering. (Molero Pérez, 2026)

   Agreement with a fitted expert reference is useful when the task is to reconstruct that
reference. It is not observed intervention truth. If two methods return the identical interval
[.5, .7] while the true effect is zero, their interval overlap is one and both miss the effect. More
generally, percentiles across graph choices and training runs describe variation under that
procedure. A 95% repeated-sampling coverage claim needs a defined sampling experiment, valid
treatment of graph uncertainty and an identified target. A calibrated Bayesian interpretation
instead needs a justified joint posterior and corresponding model assumptions. Repetition
alone supplies neither interpretation.
   The numerical comparator also needs matched information and a suitable test. The
thesis specifies chi-square conditional-independence tests for its PC baseline while prioritizing
continuous clinical variables. Its language workflow additionally draws on outside knowledge.
A fair follow-up would resolve the continuous-variable testing and coding choices, compare
methods given the same admissible knowledge, and keep a numerical-only arm. The thesis
itself acknowledges calibration problems, lost valid edges and the absence of hidden-confounder
handling. Those limitations belong alongside the favorable reported scores. (Molero Pérez,

69.30.5     Retaining the alternative changes interval coverage

The two Gaussian mechanisms in Section 69.29.5 make it possible to examine this distinction
with a known observation law. The forward mechanism has intervention effect 3/5 and the
reverse mechanism has effect zero, while both generate the same standardized joint distribution.
Their ordering is deliberately unresolved; this construction does not reverse an action and an
outcome whose temporal order is known.
   A retained project experiment draws 2,000 independent datasets of 500 observations from
that common Gaussian law. In each dataset, regression of the outcome on an intercept and the
candidate cause gives an ordinary 95% Student interval for the slope. Gaussian independent
observations and the correct linear conditional mean justify that slope interval. It is also
an intervention interval in the forward mechanism. Under the reverse mechanism, the same
calculation estimates a conditional association rather than the intervention effect.

that disagreement. Before releasing a financial recommendation, the workflow must state
the decision-time information that excludes a mechanism, or carry the ambiguity into the
recommendation. Graph proposals, retrieval and flexible conditional models remain candidate
components. Their integration deserves evaluation against an expert-reviewed graph, a
numerical discovery route and an ambiguity-preserving analysis, with withheld mechanisms,

69.31      Domain knowledge as evidence for a causal graph
A collections manager considering a payment holiday needs to distinguish a plausible story
from an intervention that improves customer outcomes. A language model may suggest that
financial strain affects delinquency, that servicing changes financial strain, and that earlier
delinquency affects the servicing offer. Each statement needs a population, a time index,
a variable definition and an evidential basis. Joining the three statements without those
qualifications can create a cycle whose meaning is unclear. A retrieval system can help locate
the underlying evidence, but a retrieved sentence does not by itself establish an invariant
structural mechanism. Two recent clinical studies make the promise and the limitations of
this assistance concrete.


69.31.1     Graph elicitation and the meaning of completeness

Aziz and Brookhart study a hypothetical comparison of statins with no treatment for preventing
cardiovascular disease. They run four prompting strategies on GPT-4o and a zero-shot strategy
on GPT-o1, with three separate conversations per configuration. Two author reviewers assess
consistency, acyclicity, source accuracy, completeness and prompt adherence. This is a study
of fifteen outputs in one familiar clinical scenario, rather than an estimate of performance over
a population of banking questions. The original was published on 30 October 2025. Aziz and
Brookhart (2025)

   Its completeness criterion asks whether the graph contains seven groups of variables drawn
from an ASCVD risk score. This is a useful check for conspicuous omissions, but it does not
validate the graph’s arrows or establish that its adjustment set identifies the statin effect.
Predictors can have different causal roles: a pretreatment common cause, a mediator and a
selection variable cannot be treated interchangeably simply because each predicts the outcome.
In a bank, a strong arrears predictor measured after a servicing offer may be an outcome of
that offer. Including it in a list is different from correctly deciding whether to adjust for it.
   Table 1 and the article’s accompanying text report fourteen acyclic graphs and seven graphs
containing six of the seven criteria. The seven-of-fifteen count does not support the abstract’s
“more than half” wording. Race is absent from all fifteen outputs. That is a reproducible
observation about the study’s criterion; it does not establish a universal requirement to insert a
particular attribute into every causal model. A social attribute’s role needs an explicit account
of the processes it represents and the target population. Blindly adding a variable to improve
a checklist score could conceal the same scientific problem as omitting it. Aziz and Brookhart
(2025); Causal banking proposal project (2026bg)
   The study also separates producing citations from producing reliable evidence. One-shot
outputs supply sources but include nonexistent references and incorrect titles, dates or author
details. Chain-of-thought outputs give explanations without sources in all three runs. The
GPT-o1 zero-shot configuration consistently supplies references and code, yet one code output
needs manual repair. Its third run is described as supplying nine sources and then assessed
using denominators of six; the original outputs are needed before aggregating that run’s
citation accuracy. The source assessments described in the paper use title and abstract review,
which is weaker than checking that primary methods and results support a particular causal
arrow. Aziz and Brookhart (2025)
   One instruction-based output contains arrows in both directions between statin use and
cholesterol. The authors correctly identify the need for temporally indexed nodes if feedback is
intended. The banking counterpart is Dt → At → Dt+1 , where Dt is delinquency information
available before action At . Collapsing Dt and Dt+1 into a single “delinquency” node loses
the information clock. A graph assistant should request that missing distinction rather than
silently reverse an arrow to satisfy a syntactic check. The study supports a brainstorming and
source-checking role; it supplies no validation of a bank effect estimator.
