# Point-treatment reuse requires a precise uncertainty contract

R tmle 2.1.1 is a reference candidate for a single binary offer, including outcome missingness and selected treated/control targets. Repeated customer identifiers do not turn it into a general longitudinal-policy engine. A two-profile authored example gives a repayment difference of 0.1 and risk ratio 17/13; at a stipulated 200 USD per incremental repayment the difference is 20 USD before costs. \citep{exec20_rfx_tmle,exec24_tmle_sources,exec24_tmle_contract}

The inspected intermediate-variable bootstrap fills column four with log risk ratios but exponentiates column three, the risk difference, for its risk-ratio interval. A constant illustrative risk pair of 0.1 and 0.2 gives ratio 2, whereas that substitution gives about 1.10517. This is a static source finding, not a native benchmark failure. An original R worker would require a reviewed correction or qualified successor, named-column oracles and boundary tests before that output is admitted. Bootstrap accounting must also distinguish the original-sample row, resampled rows and missing results. \citep{exec24_tmle_sources,exec24_tmle_contract}

The selected package declaration and source footer contain different permission wording; the short LICENSE file does not resolve that question. Distribution rights, dependencies and native uncertainty remain conditions. Current disposition is reference, with a later original-worker prototype and independent exact oracles after these issues are resolved. No current adapter is admitted. Illustrative cost of 2,400 USD across 120 requested, 100 conformant and 80 useful results gives 20, 24 and 30 USD per respective denominator; these are proposal inputs rather than measured economics. Scientific, adapter, rights/platform and evaluation owners have separate obligations, and public/generated evidence can precede expensive bank work. \citep{exec24_tmle_sources,exec24_tmle_contract}
