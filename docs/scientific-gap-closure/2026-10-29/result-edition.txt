Chapter 1

What a public experiment can tell a
bank

A useful prototype should take an intervention question through to a decision that an economist
can defend. Public data permits that exercise before a bank commits staff time, customer
records and money to a pilot. Its value depends on which question it answers. A public
experiment can test whether an analysis preserves the population, intervention and uncertainty.
It cannot establish that the same intervention works for a bank’s customers.
   The National Supported Work demonstration provides a concrete example. In the retained
experimental subset, the estimated increase in 1978 earnings is about $1,794, with an ap-
proximate 95% interval from $479 to $3,109. These are the archive’s earnings units, without
conversion to today’s purchasing power. The result supports a positive average contrast under
the stated design assumptions. It does not establish that the gain exceeds an illustrative
$1,000 cost. That distinction is exactly the distinction a banking proposal must preserve when
discussing a profitable offer or servicing intervention.


1.1    The population is part of the question
The author archive supplies separate files for 185 treated and 260 control observations with
earnings in 1974, 1975 and 1978. The original paper explains that the subset is drawn from
male experimental participants for whom 1974 earnings can be recovered. Selection uses pre-
intervention information, including assignment month and employment history. The authors
explicitly condition the retained experiment’s interpretation on the original randomisation
being independent of those pre-intervention covariates. (Dehejia and Wahba, 1999; Dehejia,
2026)
   The distinction matters. These 445 people are neither the full demonstration nor a sample of
bank customers. The public files do not contain the original assignment logs or a complete site-
by-month randomisation history. We retain the paper’s selection argument as a substantive
premise. We do not infer it from balance tests. Treatment assignment, rather than an
independently identified effect of treatment receipt, defines the contrast. Consistency, absence
of interference and preservation of the observed cohort are additional premises.
  For participant i, let Yi (1) and Yi (0) denote 1978 earnings under assignment to the pro-
gramme and control. The finite-population target is

conditioning, each field is centred at its full-sample mean and divided by its full-sample

   The common-slope regression fits an intercept, assignment and these controls. The interacted
regression additionally includes every assignment-by-control interaction. At the centred
covariate origin, its assignment coefficient is the difference between the two arm-specific fitted
outcomes averaged over the same pooled covariate distribution. In the design-based analysis
of Lin (2013), the fully interacted estimator has no worse asymptotic precision than the
unadjusted estimator under a fixed number of covariates, bounded moments, a nonsingular
limiting design and treatment proportions bounded away from zero and one. This asymptotic
result does not order the estimated standard errors in one finite dataset.

1.3       Precision, influence and the spending threshold
About 30.8% of observed 1978 earnings are zero; the largest is about $60,308. The largest
leverage is 0.346 for the interacted fit. Its HC3 standard error is about 717, compared with 695
under HC2. Deleting one observation at a time moves the unadjusted point estimate between
about 1,501 and 1,929. This sensitivity is reported without selecting a preferred sample or
substituting a trimmed estimand.
   The predeclared split by zero versus positive 1975 earnings gives similar point estimates,
about 1,711 and 1,691, but very different uncertainty. The respective intervals are approximately
[263, 3,159] and [-875, 4,258]. These are descriptive subgroup contrasts. Their comparison
neither establishes equal effects nor justifies choosing a subgroup after seeing its significance.
A targeted policy would require its own evaluation.

   Let c be a stipulated per-person cost in the same units. Subtracting c from the estimated

unadjusted point comparison is positive, about 794, but its interval is approximately [-521,

spending at that threshold. At c = 2, 000, even the point comparison is negative. Choosing
the relevant rule requires a decision-maker’s loss function; statistical significance alone does
not supply one.

  Sampling uncertainty is also distinct from a failure of the design premises. If an analyst

calculation. The data do not identify B, and the expansion is not a universal correction for
hidden confounding.


1.4    What software agreement establishes
The same rows and specifications were fitted independently with NumPy least squares and
R 4.1.2’s stats::lm. R uses its QR-based fitting route; the HC2 covariance was constructed
explicitly rather than silently using a default homoskedastic standard error. Point estimates,
variances and standard errors agree to within 10−6 in absolute units. The unadjusted covariance
also agrees with direct group-variance arithmetic, and the interacted coefficient agrees with
separate-arm standardisation.(R Core Team, 2021)
   This result supports reusing existing linear-model machinery for this specified calculation.
It does not establish agreement with every causal package, validate an R adapter, or qualify
missingness, survey weights, dependent records or other models. The proposal’s integration
should preserve the choice of target and covariance explicitly. A language boundary is a
manageable engineering problem; an unrecorded change of statistical meaning can invalidate
the economic conclusion.
  The fitted estimates and covariance summaries were then passed through the existing library,
command-line interface and local MCP service. Each returned the same interval. In this
construction the numerator is the estimated effect, the denominator is the fixed value one, and
the supplied covariance is diag(nVb , 0). The service’s Gaussian residual inversion consequently
reduces to the stated normal interval. This is an executed path from public data to fitted
summaries to consistent inference and a local decision report. Raw-data estimation and report
generation are still local scripts, not newly implemented service endpoints.
   Marking the assignment premise contradicted produces a refusal. Keeping it unverified
preserves conditional inference rather than certifying truth. These behaviours make the worked
example useful to an economist: uncertainty and missing evidence survive the change of
interface. They do not qualify a bank platform or a sibling repository.
Chapter 2

When repeated readings add
information

A bank may ask an assistant to recover financial distress from customer notes before assessing
the effect of a servicing action. Reading the same notes several times is attractive because
disagreement is easy to observe. Agreement can be less informative: several readers may share
the same omission. The distinction affects whether text can help control confounding. The
literature on text-based causal adjustment identifies measurement error, collider information
and post-treatment content as separate problems. (Keith et al., 2020)

2.2                                    An evaluation should test the proposed repair
This calculation gives a reason to measure errors against an independent reference rather
than to count agreement between prompts. A validation sample could compare a structured
dictionary, a single extraction, repeated extractions and retrieval-assisted extraction using the
same permitted notes and decision date. Each method should record omitted information,
timing violations and error dependence across cases. An outcome observed after the action
must not quietly enter the pretreatment measurement.
   The economic test is whether the measured improvement changes the causal conclusion
at an acceptable cost. Lower text-classification error alone is insufficient if the remaining
error tracks the action or outcome. Conversely, the shared-error limit suggests a concrete
remedy: obtain an independent measurement or better source information, rather than simply
requesting more readings of the same incomplete account. A second model is not automatically
an independent measurement. Independence requires evidence.
   Graph construction raises the same issue. An assistant can propose mechanisms and
competing diagrams, but the relevant evidence must concern the bank’s assignment and
information process. A formal method can evaluate an intervention under a supplied graph; it
cannot establish the graph’s missing arrows from fluent prose. The next prototype comparison
should therefore count consequential formulation and graph errors, alongside time spent
correcting them. It should keep a questionnaire plus ordinary search as an alternative and
preserve the formal method’s unsupported conclusions.
   The present derivation establishes a limit and a discriminating experiment. It measures
no live model benefit, does not close the unread literature census and supplies no universal
measurement-error correction. Its practical contribution is to identify which additional
information could make repeated assistance useful, and which repetition cannot supply.
