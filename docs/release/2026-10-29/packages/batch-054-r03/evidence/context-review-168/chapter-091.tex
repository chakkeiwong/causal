\chapter{Pilot information, calibration precision and complete streaming inference}
\label{ch:precision-streaming}

The funding desk's question remains the value of a one-month price intervention
over a fixed balance-response horizon. Chapter~\ref{v9:chapter} shows why a
fixed imperfect pilot can move the target of a residual regression. Increasing
pilot information and reducing working memory address different obstacles.
More independent history can reduce nuisance error, but does not create identity
truth or remove finite-sample covariance error. Streaming makes a specified
calculation feasible with less working storage, but leaves its statistical
assumptions intact. This chapter gives an equivalent streaming construction and
compares fixed procedures with enough repetitions to resolve larger calibration
shortfalls. The banking consequences include the extra history required, the
financial width retained, and the cases in which inference remains unavailable.

\section{Preserving the pilot's learning objective}
\label{v10:pilot}

Let a pilot independent of evaluation supply $M$ dates, the six-column design
$X$ of the preceding chapter and the $K=1+2(H+1)$ targets $Z$: the action
followed by the two candidate response curves. The design contains the intercept, driver,
centered driver square and their stratum interactions. Divide the dates
chronologically into $m_1=\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ training dates and $m_2=M-m_1$
validation dates. For each prespecified penalty $\lambda$ the original pilot
computes
\begin{align}
 B_\lambda&=\arg\min_B\{\|Z_1-X_1B\|_F^2+
                              \lambda\|DB\|_F^2\},\\
 \ell_\lambda&=\frac{1}{m_2K}\sum_{j=1}^K
                 \frac{\|(Z_2-X_2B_\lambda)_{\cdot j}\|^2}{s_j^2},
 \qquad s_j^2=\frac1{m_1}\sum_{t\leq m_1}(Z_{tj}-\bar Z_{1j})^2,
\end{align}
where $D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,1,1,1)$. Only the global intercept is
unpenalized, exactly as in the inherited pilot. The first minimizing penalty
in the supplied list is selected, then refitted using all $M$ pilot dates.
This finite-dictionary construction is a particular nuisance estimator for the
inherited partialling-out score \citep{dml2018}; neither its tuning rule nor
the dependent funding inference is an iid theorem from that source.

For either half, successive QR reductions of $[X_b\;Z_b]$ retain an upper
factor $R_b=[R_{Xb}\;R_{Zb}]$. Orthogonality yields, for every coefficient
matrix $B$,
\begin{equation}
 \|Z_b-X_bB\|_F^2=\|R_{Zb}-R_{Xb}B\|_F^2.
 \label{eq:v10:qr-loss}
\end{equation}
Rows removed in a thin factorization contribute zero because all columns of
$[X_b\;Z_b]$ lie in its retained column space. Stacking a preceding factor
above the next data block and reducing again preserves the same norm identity.
Appending the rows $\sqrt\lambda D$ to $R_{X1}$, with zero target rows,
therefore solves the identical training problem. The validation factor gives
the identical standardized loss without storing all predictions. Stacking the
two factors gives the full-pilot refit. These are exact real-arithmetic
identities, independent of the stochastic dependence within a pilot half.

Training variances are accumulated from centered block moments. If two blocks
have sizes $a,b$, means $u,v$ and centered sums $S_a,S_b$, their combined sum is
\begin{equation}
 S_{a+b}=S_a+S_b+\frac{ab}{a+b}(u-v)^2,
 \qquad \bar Z=\frac{au+bv}{a+b},
\end{equation}
componentwise. This avoids cancellation from subtracting two large raw second
moments. Positive target variances and full design rank in each half are checked.
No holdout outcome from the evaluation sample enters pilot tuning.

Floating-point equivalence additionally requires a stable problem. If each
computed validation loss is within $\epsilon$ of its exact value, a unique
minimum separated by more than $2\epsilon$ has the same selected penalty.
This implication is an algebraic diagnostic, not a global error certificate
for every input. The reference exposes all validation losses and their gap;
identical repeated penalties retain the first occurrence. Near ties, ill
conditioning and arbitrary high-dimensional learners require further numerical
or statistical qualification. In the selected independent dense comparisons,
coefficients, loss ordering, financial results and both covariance choices agree
within the reported numerical tolerance, including chunks of one row and
boundaries shorter than the response horizon.

\section{Streaming evaluation preserves the monetary score}
\label{v10:execution}

After the pilot is fixed, write $v_t=A_t-X_t\widehat B_A$ and
$r_t=Y_t-X_t\widehat B_Y$. A first evaluation pass stores, for each stratum
$s\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_s=\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}v_t^2,\qquad
 P_s=\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}v_t r_t,\qquad
 \widehat\eta_s=P_s/Q_s.
\end{equation}
The regular audited inverse supplies the monetary multiplier $c_s$ of the
preceding chapter. A second pass computes the scalar score
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\psi_t=
 \frac{T v_t}{Q_{S_t}}\,(r_t-v_t\widehat\eta_{S_t})^\top c_{S_t}.
\end{equation}
Raw lag products, the global sum, and the first and last $L$ scores recover
globally centered lag covariance across all chunk boundaries. The independent
binomial audit contribution is then added without changing the derivative or
rank guard. Both the horizon rule and Bartlett comparator preserve their
earlier meaning \citep{newey1986}; equivalence of two implementations proves
neither a covariance theorem under another law nor finite-sample calibration.

For block size $b$, design dimension $p$, target dimension $K$ and lag $L$,
working storage is $O(b(p+K)+(p+K)^2+L)$ rather than retaining every pilot
prediction and evaluation score. The input files still occupy $O(M+T)$ rows,
and mapped pages remain part of resident-memory measurements. At fixed
dimension and horizon, the factor size is independent of $M$ and $T$.
Bartlett's growing window gives an additional growing $L$ buffer. The selected
benchmark below uses fixed-horizon covariance; it does not claim constant
total storage or arbitrary-learner streaming.

\section{A more precise comparison of fixed information designs}
\label{v10:precision}

The comparison uses joint regression, a fourfold pilot $M=4T$, and an eightfold
pilot $M=8T$, all with the previously specified horizon covariance. Each
request shares evaluation histories and audit counts; the pilots are nested
prefixes of a separate independent realization. Both growing-pilot sizes
satisfy the sufficient rate of Chapter~\ref{v9:chapter} under its fixed
dictionary and stable-law premises. The number of pilot rows doubles between
the two pilot arms; this information cannot be obtained merely by repartitioning
the same shorter history.

Eight selected designs vary evaluation dates, audit information, persistence
and proximity to singular mapping, and include false exogeneity. The grid is
a structured comparison, not a full factorial or an authentic bank population.
Each design has 2,000 independent requests. Near coverage .95, the approximate
ordinary Monte Carlo half-width is
$1.96\sqrt{.95(.05)/2000}=.00955$; its worst-case value is .02192. Ordinary
Wilson intervals are descriptive. The retained exact binomial inversions use
Bonferroni adjustment across the 24 marginal coverage cells; they give a
simultaneous Monte Carlo bound for those fixed simulation designs, not a
guarantee of causal coverage under another data law. Paired differences use
the observed gain/loss discordances and report their sampling uncertainty.
There is no final-result selection of penalties, covariance, sample size or
favorable design. Unavailable requests remain in all-request coverage; bias,
empirical SD, mean SE and finite width describe the available requests.

Paired pilot-minus-joint coverage differences at the baseline are .0110 and

Neither result establishes general superiority. Every conservative interval
for eightfold minus fourfold pilot coverage includes zero. Even at 4800 dates
the two pilot arms have identical covering counts; doubling pilot history
has no demonstrated coverage advantage here.

The initial normal approximation gives a zero-width paired interval when
both discordance counts are zero. That is not population certainty. Exact
binomial inversion for gain and loss probabilities, followed by a union
bound, gives a conservative difference interval $[L_g-U_l,U_g-L_l]$.

intervals use error .05/24 per cell. Paired simultaneous bounds use .05/48
for each of the two binomial proportions in 24 comparisons. Each family is
controlled separately; no combined .95 guarantee across both families is
claimed. The binomial count inversion is the elementary construction in
Section~\ref{s61:binomial-inversion} applied to independent simulation requests.

\section{Information and adoption boundaries}
\label{v10:limits}

The extra pilot must be independent of evaluation under an invariant relevant
law. A bank cannot replace that condition with arbitrary historical volume;
regime changes or overlapping histories require a different separation and
influence argument. Likewise, more nuisance history cannot create representative
identity truth. Near-singular audit information can still require refusal, and
false scientific declarations can yield precise but invalid financial answers.
Earlier omitted-feature and endogenous-map counterexamples remain relevant.

The selected implementation supplies a bounded scientific operation. It does
not identify a general nonlinear private-state model, retain the true class in
arbitrary latent-graph discovery, or establish rates for an arbitrary learner.
Independent review, authentic bank assignment and source clocks, audit truth,
target storage and concurrency, adopted interfaces and measured analyst costs
remain distinct inputs to product qualification. These distinctions determine
which evidence the funding desk can act on and which claims still need testing. The comparisons separate three investments: pilot history addresses the specified nuisance error, representative audits inform the assignment inverse, and streaming reduces working storage for the same calculation. None substitutes for the others. The next chapter replaces a near-rank refusal with a projected uncertainty set and asks whether its financial width is useful.
