\subsection{Granite's synthetic retention rollout}
\label{sec:granite-retention-example}

The example observes 100 branches monthly from January 2022 through December
2023. Five cohorts of 16 branches begin treatment in January through May
2023; the remaining 20 branches stay untreated throughout. The resulting
2,400 observations contain \RLnum{g8-panel-branch-months} treated branch-months.
The population and adoption schedule are fixed before generating outcomes.
Using zero-based dates $t=0,\ldots,23$, cohort $g=0,\ldots,4$ starts at
$s_g=12+g$.

The monthly example has a seasonal cycle as well as a trend. Stable
branch differences enter through $a_i$; temporary shocks enter through $u_{it}$
and persist into the next month. The untreated closure rate is
\[
 Y_{it}(0)=5+a_i+0.25\sin(2\pi t/12)+0.01t+u_{it},\qquad
 u_{it}=0.4u_{i,t-1}+\eta_{it},
\]
where independent $a_i\sim N(0,0.35^2)$, innovations
$\eta_{it}\sim N(0,0.18^2)$ and stationary initial errors define the synthetic
law. Treatment adds
$\tau_{gk}=-0.4(1+0.1g)\min(k+1,6)$ at event time $k\ge0$, and zero before.
The fixed generator seed is 2026092301. This law has common untreated trends
and no spillovers by construction. Every realized rate is positive; rates
are simulated continuous outcomes rather than integer closure counts.
The observed preperiod mean is \RLnum{g8-panel-pre-mean} per thousand.

Measure each cohort's change from its own last untreated month to the
event month of interest. Subtract the never-treated branches' change over
exactly those calendar dates. For $t=s_g+k$, compare each cohort with the
same never-treated branches $C$:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\tau_{gk}
 =\frac1{n_g}\sum_{i\in g}(Y_{i,s_g+k}-Y_{i,s_g-1})
 -\frac1{n_C}\sum_{i\in C}(Y_{i,s_g+k}-Y_{i,s_g-1}).
 \label{eq:granite-event-study}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:granite-event-study} is the unconditional, no-anticipation
case of \citet[Eq. (2.8), Theorem 1 and Appendix A]{callawaysantanna2021}.
No already-treated branch serves as a control. Let $\mathcal G_k$ contain
cohorts observed at both dates. The displayed event estimate is
$\widehat\tau_k=|\mathcal G_k|^{-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_k}\widehat\tau_{gk}$.
Every cohort contributes through $k=7$; only the January cohort reaches
$k=11$. There is no observed $k=12$. Later estimates therefore describe a
changing cohort mixture, which matters because the treatment law differs
across cohorts.

Uncertainty uses complete branch trajectories. One control branch appears
in several cohort comparisons; it does not become a new independent branch
each time. Collect all of its contributions in one vector before calculating
variation across branches. For each treated stratum,
define its branch vector $H_{ig,k}$ as the observed date difference divided
by $|\mathcal G_k|$ when the cohort contributes, and zero otherwise. For a
control branch define
$H_{iC,k}=-|\mathcal G_k|^{-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_k}
(Y_{i,s_g+k}-Y_{i,s_g-1})$.
Then $\widehat\tau_k=\sum_{h\in\{0,\ldots,4,C\}}\bar H_{h,k}$, and independence
across branches gives the estimated covariance
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Sigma=\sum_h\frac{S_h}{n_h},\qquad
 S_h=\frac1{n_h-1}\sum_{i\in h}(H_{ih}-\bar H_h)(H_{ih}-\bar H_h)'.
 \label{eq:panel-branch-covariance}
\end{equation}
All uses of a control's outcomes enter its vector before taking the covariance.
Treating repeated dates or reused control contrasts as independent would
omit covariance terms. The intervals below use the large-sample normal
multiplier 1.96. They are pointwise approximations with 16 or 20 branches per
stratum, not a simultaneous coverage guarantee.

The preperiod intervals include zero. This is compatible with the generating
law, but does not establish a postperiod counterfactual in an application.
For example, suppose untreated outcomes are identical in both groups before
adoption, then a local shock reduces only the adopting group's untreated
outcome by one unit. With zero treatment effect, all preperiod contrasts
are still zero while DID equals $-1$. No pretrend test can distinguish that
future shock from the intervention without additional information.

\subsection{Discounted contribution from the rollout}

Assume 5,000 active customers per branch, maintained by replacement arrivals,
and two additional relationship managers costing \$60,000 each per year only
while the programme operates. For this synthetic calculation, a permanently
prevented closure is assumed to add \$1,500 of discounted lifetime
contribution at its prevention date. Prevented closures are distinct across
months. These assumptions connect monthly rates to money; the panel alone
does not establish permanence, customer lifetime value or absence of repeated
counting.

Let $d_t=1.05^{-(t-11)/12}$ discount a 2023 month-end amount to the beginning
of that year. Convert each closure-rate reduction into a number of retained
customers, multiply by contribution per customer, and subtract monthly staff
costs. Discount each month's net amount and add it across the branches actually
operating the programme. The minus sign turns a fall in closures into a benefit.
The estimated value for the observed rollout is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V=\sum_{g=0}^4\sum_{t=s_g}^{23}16d_t
 \left[-\frac{5000}{1000}\,1500\,\widehat\tau_{g,t-s_g}
       -\frac{2(60000)}{12}\right].
 \label{eq:panel-retention-finance}
\end{equation}
The formula uses each observed cohort-date contrast. It does not multiply a
single mature effect by a full year at all branches. The estimate implies
\RLnum{g8-panel-prevented} prevented closures before discounting, discounted
benefit of \$\RLnum{g8-panel-benefit-million} million and discounted cost of
\$\RLnum{g8-panel-cost-million} million. Net value is
\$\RLnum{g8-panel-net-million} million. Applying the same branch covariance
to this financial linear combination gives an approximate pointwise interval
of [\RLnum{g8-panel-net-low-million},\RLnum{g8-panel-net-high-million}] million
dollars, conditional on the cost and lifetime-contribution assumptions.
Uncertainty about those assumptions is additional. These are simulated
financial consequences, not observed returns from a bank campaign.
