20.1      Define the economic relationships before searching for
          them
A bank might supply monthly balances, offered rates, accepted products, customer contacts and
arrears to a discovery procedure. A graph over these columns is not yet a graph over economic
mechanisms. The offer precedes acceptance, balances are stocks measured at particular dates,
arrears affect eligibility, and a relationship manager may use private information. The same
table can therefore be compatible with several latent-variable and observation models. The
variables, information dates and interventions in Section 6.3 must be defined before the
statistical search.
   The output to be learned must also be specified. A DAG with no hidden common causes,
a Markov equivalence class of such DAGs, a latent projection, and an equivalence class
of ancestral graphs encode different information. Changing a graph’s serialization without
preserving its class can change the identification argument even when its visible arrows look
identical. Discovery is useful because it can reject some scientific specifications, suggest missing
variables and narrow intervention questions. Its strongest conclusion remains conditional on
the model class and discovery assumptions.


20.2      From a full DAG to observed mixed graphs
Let the full acyclic causal graph have observed vertices V and hidden vertices U . One observed-
variable representation is an acyclic directed mixed graph (ADMG). A directed edge X → Y
in a latent projection can summarize a directed path with hidden internal vertices; a bidirected
edge X ↔ Y can summarize a path with arrowheads at both observed endpoints and hidden
noncolliders internally. Directed cycles remain excluded, but a directed and bidirected edge can
coexist between the same pair. For example, A → Y together with A ← U → Y gives the bow
A → Y , A ↔ Y . Its two marks retain both causal transmission and confounding. Districts
connected by bidirected edges are the objects used by the ID decomposition of Section 6.5.3
(Shpitser and Pearl, 2006).
   A maximal ancestral graph (MAG) used for conditional-independence discovery is a different
representation. In the no-selection setting, it has directed and bidirected edges, no directed or
almost-directed cycles, and every nonadjacent pair can be separated by other observed vertices.

An arrowhead at X on an edge to Y rules out X being an ancestor of Y . The bow above is
therefore not an ancestral graph. Its MAG on just A, Y is A → Y , which records ancestry but
can still represent hidden confounding. Interpreting that MAG as a causally sufficient DAG
would incorrectly license adjustment by the empty set.
   Zhang (2008, Section 3, Definitions 1–3) gives the construction. Join two observed vertices if
there is an inducing path in the full DAG: every observed internal vertex is a collider and every
collider is an ancestor of an endpoint. Hidden internal noncolliders are allowed. Orient the
resulting adjacency by ancestry in the original DAG: a tail at an ancestor and an arrowhead
at a nonancestor. If neither endpoint is an ancestor of the other, use two arrowheads. This
construction preserves the marginal conditional independences and observed ancestry; it does
not uniquely recover the latent DAG or every intervention law.


20.2.1    Separation and endpoint semantics

On a mixed-graph path, a collider has two incident arrowheads pointing into it. A path is
m-connecting given Z if each noncollider is outside Z and each collider is an ancestor of a
member of Z, including itself. The endpoints are m-separated if there is no such path. This
extends d-separation without changing the reason conditioning on a collider can open a path
(Zhang, 2008, Definition 2).
   A completed partially directed acyclic graph (CPDAG) represents a DAG Markov equivalence
class. Its directed edges have that direction in every member; its undirected edges can vary
across compatible DAGs. DAGs are Markov equivalent exactly when they share a skeleton
and unshielded colliders (Peters et al., 2017, Definition 6.24, Lemma 6.25). A partial ancestral
graph (PAG) represents a MAG equivalence class. An invariant arrowhead or tail appears
at the corresponding endpoint; a circle means that endpoint mark varies across represented
MAGs. For example, A ◦→ Y establishes that Y is not an ancestor of A, but leaves the status
at A unresolved. It does not say that A is definitely a cause of Y . Nor do endpoint circles vary
independently: allowed completions must satisfy the whole graph’s ancestral and equivalence
constraints (Zhang, 2008, Definition 3).
   These definitions exclude selection variables. General ancestral graphs can also contain
undirected edges encoding selection structure. A bank sample restricted to approved borrowers
or surviving accounts cannot inherit a no-selection theorem merely by removing the selection
column. An ADMG causal model may also imply observable restrictions beyond conditional
independence; learning its MAG does not preserve all of that structural information. This is
one reason to retain an assumed economic model alongside, and separately from, a discovered
equivalence graph.


20.3     Conditional independence: oracle logic and finite infor-

Under causal sufficiency, the causal Markov property makes graphical separation imply inde-
pendence. Faithfulness supplies the converse. With a perfect conditional-independence oracle,
the PC procedure starts with a complete undirected graph and removes X − Y when it finds
X ⊥ Y | S. Searching growing subsets of current neighbors uses the fact that nonadjacent
DAG vertices can be separated by the parents of one endpoint. It stores each separating set.

  With latent variables, observed parents need not supply all required separators. FCI adds
searches over possible separating sets and uses ancestral-graph orientation rules, returning
a PAG under its assumptions. Simply relabelling PC’s undirected output a PAG does not

independence null does not establish independence. Testing many adaptively selected condi-
tioning sets can create incompatible separating statements. Sparse graphs reduce the number
and size of the sets, but variables that are almost deterministically related can make both
estimation and testing unstable.

while a restricted functional model identifies a direction if its noise and function assumptions
are appropriate. Neither a neural function approximator nor a small acyclicity loss can
supply evidence for independent disturbances. Residual independence, sensitivity to variable
transformations, held-out model adequacy and alternate graph classes are therefore relevant
diagnostics. Passing them leaves the substantive assumptions conditional.


20.5      Temporal discovery must preserve the clock and hidden
          history
For an unrolled process graph, time order can exclude arrows from a future decision into
an earlier outcome. A cycle in the summary graph, such as balance affecting next month’s
offer and offer affecting next month’s balance, is compatible with an acyclic unrolled graph.
Instantaneous cycles at the observation scale need a different model; forcing an arbitrary
within-month ordering imposes a restriction.
   PCMCI-type methods reduce conditioning sets and test lagged links with conditions chosen
to control autocorrelation and common parents. PCMCI+ also treats contemporaneous
structure. Runge (2020, Assumptions 1 and Theorems 1–2) requires causal sufficiency, Markov
and adjacency-faithfulness conditions, consistent CI decisions, stationarity, time order and
a maximum lag covering the true parent lags. Its stated setting excludes selection and
measurement error. Collider-rule choices determine the corresponding completeness claim.
These conditions explain both its usefulness for repeated mechanisms and why it cannot
automatically certify effects from a monthly table of selected accounts.
   LPCMCI permits latent confounders but changes the graph object and conditioning problem
(Gerhardus and Runge, 2020, Sections 2.1–2.3). When only a finite lag window is retained,
earlier observed variables are themselves marginalized. Increasing the window can remove
edges inside the old window because a previously hidden separator becomes observed. Thus a
maximum-lag choice is partly a scientific observation choice, not merely a hyperparameter.
The primary paper also distinguishes how stationarity constrains repeating adjacencies and
orientations. Its stationary, acyclic, no-selection model is not a general theorem for arbitrary
missing or irregular event histories.
  The subsampling example in Section 17.4 makes this restriction concrete: a two-day series
cannot distinguish daily coefficients 0.7 and −0.7. Temporal order does not restore the lost
daily response. Aggregated flows can additionally induce dependent innovations and change
the conditional-independence structure. A discovery operation must therefore record the
observation operator, lag window, contemporaneous rules and stationarity regime alongside

20.6.1    What the construction teaches a banking decision

In a Gaussian chain X → A → Y with action coefficient 0.7, the unordered population law
permits effects {0, 0.7} in this class. Across 500 samples of size 600, the outer effect set contains
the true effect in all samples, but also contains both zero and 0.7 in all samples. Its median
width is 21.13, and 1.8% of runs have an unbounded set. A supplied order X ≺ A ≺ Y
identifies 0.7 in the population; even then the conservative finite-sample set contains both zero
and 0.7 in 83% of samples, with median width 10.69. Tightening computation or gathering
more information has economic value when these alternatives lead to different decisions.
   A hidden-common-cause experiment is more revealing. Its true action effect is zero, but its
observational covariance admits a sufficient ordered model with effect 0.4. The returned set
misses zero in all 500 runs, even though the covariance region covers the actual covariance
in 94.4% of samples. Coverage of the observed law cannot validate the causal model class.
For 500 successes or failures, a two-sided 95% Wilson interval has an endpoint about 0.9924
or 0.0076; the zero plug-in Monte Carlo standard error at these extremes is not certainty. A
measured common cause, a credible instrument, or randomized assignment would change the
information available for this query. A larger observational sample alone would not identify
which class is appropriate.

Suppose the graph is a DAG, CPDAG, or a no-selection MAG/PAG, and the target is the

hold for every compatible causal model, not just for a conveniently oriented representative.
Generalized adjustment makes this requirement precise (Perković et al., 2018, Definitions 1–4,
Theorem 5).
   A possibly directed path has no arrowhead pointing backward along its direction of traversal.
It is proper relative to an exposure set if only its first vertex is an exposure. An edge A → B
is visible in a MAG/PAG if graph structure certifies that the relevant latent confounding
explanation is absent. A sufficient configuration is V ∗ → A → B with V not adjacent to B;
the full definition also permits a collider path into A whose internal vertices are parents of B.
All directed DAG/CPDAG edges are visible by convention. The criterion has three parts:
1. Every proper possibly directed path from the exposure to the outcome starts with a visible
   edge out of the exposure (amenability).
2. The adjustment variables avoid every possible descendant of a non-exposure vertex on such
   a path (the forbidden set).
3. They block every proper definite-status noncausal path from exposure to outcome. A
   definite noncollider has an incident tail, or is the middle of the appropriate unshielded
   circle-marked triple; colliders have two incident arrowheads.
The cited theorem is sound and complete for adjustment in its stated graph classes. Failure
can mean that a front-door or other identifying functional is needed, or that the target is not
identified. It does not by itself choose between those conclusions. The visible-edge condition
also explains why the two-node MAG A → Y cannot be treated as the corresponding causally
sufficient DAG.

   For an exact Gaussian example, let (A, M, Y ) have unit variances and covariances
(ΣAM , ΣM Y , ΣAY ) = (1/2, 1/2, 1/4). It has A ⊥ Y | M and no other pairwise conditional
independence. The CPDAG is A − M − Y . The three compatible DAGs are A → M → Y ,
A ← M → Y , and A ← M ← Y . Gaussian regressions supply independent structural errors
for each. The total effect of A on Y is 1/4 in the first model and zero in the other two. The
observed law therefore identifies the set {0, 1/4} within this causally sufficient linear class. An
arbitrarily accurate estimate of its covariance does not decide whether an offer changes the
eventual financial outcome. A credible decision-clock restriction that A precedes M would
orient the first edge; an experiment on A would distinguish these effect hypotheses. These are
additional sources of information.
   Sampling error is separate from this structural set. If a graph-set procedure contains the
true graph with probability at least 1 − α, and a confidence set valid for that true graph
misses its target with probability at most β, their union construction can have coverage at
least 1 − α − β by the union bound. Obtaining those two properties is the hard part. In
particular, post-selection confidence intervals for a chosen graph do not supply the second
property without an appropriate uniform or independent-sample argument. A bank report
should distinguish graph uncertainty, nuisance/parameter uncertainty and uncertainty about
the substantive model class.
  The useful next experiment is one that changes the decision-relevant identified set. Resolving
an edge that all admissible policies ignore can have little value. In contrast, a randomized
encouragement, a new predecision proxy, finer timing or a deliberately held-out intervention
environment may distinguish the causal explanations that lead to different banking actions.
The scientific request should name that distinction and its expected value before a discovery
engine ranks variables solely by edge confidence.


20.10       The resulting discovery operation
A reusable discovery result contains its graph class, endpoint marks, background restrictions,
CI or likelihood assumptions, observation and time-window definition, statistical tuning and
unresolved alternatives. It can feed adjustment/identification queries over a bounded supported
class. It should return a common functional, a set of candidate effects, or an explicit unresolved
query. These are distinct from returning a best-fitting DAG. Automated data organization
must next preserve the variables and information sets on which these conclusions depend.
   The detailed comparisons now separate three uses of discovery. A fitted temporal search
first supplies order sensitivity conditional on its selected graph. An ancestral-margin example
then checks which hidden-variable graph object is being evaluated. Finally, a covariance region
is mapped into a latent effect set under explicit structural restrictions. These procedures
answer different questions about the same gap between a graph and a decision.

20.12      Evaluate the observed margin, then ask a causal question
Hidden common causes can create adjacencies absent from the observed portion of a structural
DAG. Consequently precision against that structural portion is not a measure of recovering
the hidden-variable method’s target. A finite-window construction makes the distinction exact.
Observe price, uptake and balance at dates 0 and 1. At each date an unobserved liquidity
state causes price and balance; price causes uptake, which causes balance. Each variable and
the liquidity state have a persistence edge to date 1. Independent initial innovations define
the beginning of the experiment. There is no silently discarded infinite past.
   For this finite DAG D and observed set O, join observed nodes i, j if no subset of O \ {i, j}
d-separates them in D. This gives the adjacency of its maximal ancestral margin. Put a tail at
i when i is an ancestor of j in D, and an arrowhead otherwise. There are no selection variables,
so no undirected selection edges are required. An adjacency with a tail may represent an
inducing path, not a directly observed structural coefficient. A bidirected edge records neither

20.12.2    An unresolved query and its information requirement

For an action/outcome pair with unrestricted hidden confounding, compare two SCMs: U ∼
Bernoulli(1/2) and A = U in both, with Y = A in one and Y = U in the other. Both produce
P (A = Y = 0) = P (A = Y = 1) = 1/2. Their average action effects are 1 and 0. Thus even
an exactly known observed distribution and adjacency do not identify this query. A larger
sample or a preferred completion cannot distinguish the two models.
   Additional feasible randomization, observed action-relevant confounders with support, a
defensible instrument with its own target, or validated front-door restrictions may resolve a
suitably redesigned question. Without such information the product returns the ambiguity
and the requested additional evidence. It does not turn circles into a recursive order. Fresh
estimation data address reuse of outcomes after graph selection; they do not make the
selected graph causally correct. The hidden-variable query operation therefore records whether
identification comes from an asserted scientific graph, an invariant equivalence-class conclusion
or an unresolved discovery result.

20.14      A faster graph search can preserve the wrong relationship
Before a bank asks whether a higher deposit rate attracts balances, it must distinguish the
effect of changing the rate from the information that led it to offer that rate. A graph search
can help compare explanations, but its computational shortcuts have scientific consequences. A
link retained because the search stopped is a different finding from a relationship that survived
the relevant tests.
   The detailed Java comparison in Section 35.4.2 identifies a concrete difficulty in the inspected
Aitiotita revision. Beyond conditioning depth two, one skeleton-search path tests a pair
conditional on the union of its neighbours. If dependence persists, it marks the link undeletable
and omits subsequent subset searches. That rule would be safe only if dependence after adding
variables excluded independence under a smaller conditioning set. The retained generic callback
allows independence procedures for which that implication fails. (Aitiotita contributors, 2023)
   This study examines a repair: continue the declared subset search, and report an unfinished
search explicitly when its budget is exhausted. The calculations compare project implemen-
tations with exact independence oracles. They do not execute the Java distribution. The
distinction matters because the inspected bundled linguistic oracle is not a Gaussian test, and
the orientation and graph-export stages require separate evaluation.

Exact skeleton recovery rises from nine out of 200 for the six-variable task to 173 out of 200
for the five-variable task. The graph tasks differ because the latter omits C; retained paired
records also compare the common five-variable edges.
   The larger-coefficient case improves much less. At n = 2000, interval coverage is still
only 3.5 per cent and the reduced skeleton is wrong in 194 samples. Even after removing C,
conditioning on other variables leaves some direct-edge correlations close to zero. Correct
timing is useful economic information; it does not guarantee a favorable statistical signal or
establish faithfulness.


20.15.5     What the proposal should require before valuing a graph

The study supports a conditional recommendation. Keep full subset search as a logical reference
and report unfinished searches honestly. Also retain the economically defended information
clock, assess the signals required by the discovery procedure, and justify the adjustment used
for the particular lending contrast. A graph can help formulate questions without supplying a
qualified effect estimate. A significance setting such as 0.05 should never be presented as a 95
per cent probability that the entire graph is correct.
   The numerical checks target the study itself. Exact rational covariance and independently
implemented graph/path tests agree on every declared population query in the faithful cases.
Precision-matrix and residual-regression partial correlations agree within 5 × 10−15 in 486
sample checks. Independent QR residualization reproduces 270 retained interval calculations
within 7 × 10−16 ; reversing variable order preserves the checked full-search skeletons. These
checks reduce implementation error in the examples. They cannot establish a universal
calibration guarantee.
   All 54 first-study method/threshold cells and all 36 timing-follow-up cells are retained,
including adverse results. The follow-up was specified after the first study and is described
as such. Neither experiment evaluates native R or Java software, arrow orientation, latent
confounding, a live language model or an actual bank. Those questions retain their own tests.
