   The deterministic formulation baseline has three layers. A schema check verifies that
population, unit, intervention, comparator, horizon, clock and scale are present. A semantic
check compares these fields with the supplied brief and dictionary: a post-contact duration
cannot become a pre-contact covariate, an invitation cannot silently become actual uptake, and
a requested all-eligible effect cannot become a recipients-only effect. A formal check evaluates
identification under the explicitly supplied candidate graph and returns an expression, model-
relative nonidentification, unsupported scope or an unresolved input. These checks validate
different things. A well-formed schema does not prove the story is true, and a correctly
evaluated graph does not prove its edges.
   For a generated benchmark, the reference can enumerate a finite set of admissible formu-
lations Q(b) for brief b. An explicitly resolved brief may have one acceptable target. An
ambiguous brief may have several and require the system to request or retain the missing
distinction. A baseline can return those alternatives directly from the supplied reference
fields and use exact calculations such as the example above. This establishes a transparent
lower-cost comparator and exposes what a language model is expected to add: interpretation
of unstructured language, evidence retrieval and useful clarification, rather than numerical
work already handled by a deterministic engine.


65.11.4    A convincing explanation is not yet an intervention estimate

A corporate borrower says that a tight credit line prevents it from accepting an overseas order.
A language assistant may recover the relevant passage, connect it to the financial statements
and explain why working capital matters. The lender still faces a different question: how would
a specified change in credit terms affect exports, repayment and contribution? The answer
depends on why the borrower faces the constraint and what the proposed facility changes.
Good extraction is useful evidence preparation. It does not supply the missing comparison
between alternative lending decisions.

first extracts financing constraints, then export outcomes, and finally asks a language model
to explain a mechanism using economic theories. The first extraction records a constraint,
its severity, supporting evidence and type. The second records an export metric, value,
description and direction. These explicit intermediate records would help an analyst challenge
an unsupported interpretation of a company report. Equations (1)–(10) define the information
passed between these stages. (Li, 2025, Sections 3.1–3.4)

Knowledge claims need a task-specific test

Mugleston and colleagues discuss a broader philosophical question: whether the internal
representations of a language model deserve to be called knowledge. Their argument considers
justified true belief, compressed representations, contextual understanding and the limitations
of the Chinese room analogy. Section 5 also distinguishes informative coherence from merely
repeating an answer, and Section 9 leaves important claims about understanding unsettled.

The paper describes its purpose as a thought experiment. (Mugleston et al., 2025, Sections
3.3–6 and 9)
   That discussion is relevant to how an analyst interprets assistance, but it does not resolve a
lending estimand. A system can organize useful knowledge without knowing which of the two
mechanisms in Figure 65.1 generated a particular dataset. Conversely, calling the system a
statistical text generator does not establish that a specific extracted passage is wrong. The
practical assessment concerns the record, the proposed causal restriction and the decision that
follows from it. Philosophical labels cannot replace those checks.
   For a credit decision, the first commitment is an action the lender can carry out: for
example, an offer with specified limit, maturity, price and eligibility at a fixed date. Setting
an abstract constraint indicator to zero is not automatically that offer. Take-up, competing
lenders, borrower responses and the cost of capital can intervene between the offer and exports.
The target may concern an offered facility, the facility actually drawn, or net contribution
after expected losses. Those quantities require distinct comparisons.
   A useful assisted study would retain the quoted passages and dates, distinguish the lender’s
action from the borrower’s condition, and set out alternative causal explanations. An analyst
could then defend additional measurements, a suitable experiment or a credible source of
assignment variation. The evaluation should score faithful extraction, correct formulation and
justified uncertainty separately. A strong extraction score is valuable on its own; the effect
estimate becomes valuable only when its identification and financial interpretation are also
supported.


65.11.5     Evaluation before a costly bank study

The initial evaluation can use public data dictionaries and openly documented financial
examples alongside generated causal mechanisms. Public data support questions about
available variables and clocks; they usually do not supply intervention truth. Generated
mechanisms can supply that truth for the declared model. A reference author should create
matched examples that vary one decisive feature: invitation versus uptake, all eligible versus
treated population, pre-decision versus post-decision measurement, complete variables versus
noisy proxies, or a single action versus repeated treatment. The target-change example supplies
one exact reference family. This is a proposed evaluation design; it is not a reported live-agent
comparison.
   Train/development and final evaluation should be split by the underlying scenario or
structural model, not merely by paraphrases of the same question. Otherwise a system can
learn the reference target from exposed near-duplicates. All requests should remain in the
evaluation denominator, including ambiguous requests, timeouts, unsupported estimands,
malformed answers and justified nonidentification. Report target and clock fidelity, unjustified
resolution of ambiguity, false causal answers, valid abstention, numerical error where a target
is actually identified, interval availability and coverage where ground truth permits them, and
total resource use. A high completion rate alone rewards an agent for confidently answering
questions that should remain unresolved.
   Retrieval, structured prompting and an actor/critic loop should be compared against the
same deterministic baseline and fixed task families. A retrieval ablation tests whether source
passages improve interpretation. A critic ablation tests whether additional review catches errors,
while reporting the added calls, time and newly introduced mistakes. A graph-perturbation
comparison tests whether a system notices that an assumption changed. Repeated runs
reveal stability. These studies need their actual model, tool, prompt, data and stopping-rule
identities frozen before final evaluation. Agreement across repeated runs is useful evidence

about stability; it is not evidence that the agreed answer is causally correct.
   Several gaps remain scientific. A general method for recovering stakeholder intent from
incomplete language is not established. The immediate remedy is to preserve admissible
alternatives and make the missing decision explicit. A general guarantee that retrieval or
debate validates causal edges is also absent; the current studies justify investigating those tools
with factual and graph-specific references, not treating consensus as truth. Identification from
noisy proxies, dependent observations or repeated interventions requires the corresponding
statistical model and method. Finally, whether an assisted workflow saves bank analyst time
or changes an investment decision requires a later workflow study after a bounded prototype
shows merit. Those gaps narrow the claims and motivate specific experiments; they do not
prevent writing or testing the proposal’s public/generated formulation contract. The next
two studies develop decisions within that contract: which population or weight class the
result protects, and how a clarification workflow resolves a symbolic goal before selecting tools.
Neither statistical robustness nor goal completion can replace an explicit choice of the intended
causal question.


65.12      Whose effect is the assistant being asked to estimate?
A banking assistant translating an offer proposal must specify whose outcome the offer is
intended to improve. “The average effect is positive” leaves open whether the average describes
existing customers, a new acquisition population, an equal mix of branches, or a precision-
weighted statistical summary. These choices can lead to different actions even when every
group effect is known. Vilfort’s Robust Inference for Weighted Estimands provides a useful
statistical treatment of this ambiguity Vilfort (2026). It is an econometric contribution to
specifying and checking targets, rather than an algorithm for language-model discovery. Its
value for a causal assistant begins after the group effects have a defensible interpretation.

65.12.1     Separate identification, aggregation and sampling uncertainty

Let θ = (θ1 , . . . , θK )′ contain effects for K customer groups, with each entry measured in the
same units and over the same horizon. A weight vector w defines
                                     τw = w′ θ,      1′ w = 1.                               (65.14)
Normalization alone permits negative entries. If the question concerns an actual customer
mixture, require the simplex ∆K = {w : w ≥ 0, 1′ w = 1}. A signed regression contrast may
be a legitimate statistical target, but it should not be described as the mean outcome of an
unspecified population. Group definitions, treatment regimes, calendar times and horizons must
be compatible before aggregation. A weight change cannot repair an effect whose identification
assumptions have failed.
  The exact experiment studied in the paper assumes
                                    θb ∼ N (θ, Σ),     Σ ≻ 0,                                (65.15)
where K is fixed, Σ is known, and the baseline weights and alternative class are fixed and
known. The full joint covariance matters; separate group standard errors do not supply it. Let
Λ be a nonempty compact convex class of alternative normalized weights. It represents stated
ambiguity or disagreement about the target, rather than an automatically identified set of
possible bank populations. A target outside this class receives no protection from robustness
calculated inside it. Choosing the class after inspecting estimated effects also changes the
statistical problem.
