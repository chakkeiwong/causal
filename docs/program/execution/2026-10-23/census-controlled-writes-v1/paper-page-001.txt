                                         1    I NTRODUCTION

                                         Large language models recall facts remarkably well (Kaplan et al., 2020; Srivastava et al., 2023),
                                         yet on tasks whose correctness depends on composing several rules or facts from a fixed source
                                         of truth, token generation often imitates a derivation rather than executing one (Dziri et al., 2023;
                                         Kordjamshidi et al., 2026). On real mathematical dependency graphs and multi-hop legal reasoning,
                                         chain-of-thought and retrieval baselines plateau well below what the underlying formal structure
                                         allows, and increasing the reasoning budget or the number of sampled traces yields diminishing
                                         returns (Wei et al., 2022; Lewis et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023). The central difficulty is ensuring that
                                         an answer follows from the stated premises: a chain-of-thought trace can appear coherent even when
                                         it skips a necessary inference or introduces an unsupported intermediate conclusion. Legal, medical
                                         and formal-mathematics deployments make this concrete, since a downstream decision cannot rest
                                         on an answer whose intermediate steps are neither auditable nor tied to source.
                                         A natural response is to delegate derivation to a symbolic executor once the model has proposed the
                                         relevant facts and rules (Pan et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2023). Execution is deterministic and cheap
                                         to check, and it turns a multi-hop question into a query against a well-defined logical state. But
                                         execution alone is not a correctness certificate: a valid derivation from a premise that misrepresents
                                         the source produces a wrong answer just as convincingly as a hallucinated chain (Figure 1). A model
                                         can arrive at the same wrong answer either by fabricating an intermediate conclusion in a chain of
                                         thought or by writing it into the solver’s premise set and letting the executor rediscover it. To rule
