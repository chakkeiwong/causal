# Synthetic-control software must preserve donor, target and uncertainty semantics

A treated regional portfolio has an observed postintervention outcome but an unobserved untreated path. A useful synthetic-control proposal must explain which donor combination or factor restriction identifies that path, what information selected it and how uncertainty relates to the assignment and sampling law. The inspected community Synth command inside Stata and the gsynth/fect R routes illustrate distinct convex-combination and latent-factor approaches. This synthesis reconciles their already integrated source developments and exact references; it does not establish a native vendor comparison or universal software coverage. \citep{exec19_synth_stata_source,exec21_gsynth_wrapper,exec21_gsynth_engine}

For treated unit T and post date t, write tau_Tt=Y_Tt(1)-Y_Tt(0). A convex donor predictor substitutes sum_j w_j Y_jt for Y_Tt(0), with nonnegative weights summing to one. Even perfect pre-fit does not prove this counterfactual equality. Donors must be unaffected by the intervention and anticipation, the measurement windows must be comparable and the model must carry the relevant relationship into the postperiod. Donor eligibility is a scientific restriction; being present in a rectangular panel is only a data property.

The independent finite example has donor paths D1=(90,110,120), D2=(110,130,140) and treated path T=(100,120,150), measured in thousands of USD. The first two dates are preintervention. Equal weights fit both preperiods exactly and predict 130 after treatment. Stipulating that this prediction equals the true untreated outcome yields a USD 20,000 effect. If donor 2 receives a spillover of 20, its last observation is 160 and the same weights predict 140, reducing the apparent gap to 10. Selecting weights after observing the treated outcome can choose (1/4,3/4), giving synthetic path (105,125,150) and zero post gap. The true stipulated intervention remains present. Both donor contamination and postoutcome selection can destroy the interpretation of a fitted gap. \citep{exec19_synth_contract_reference}

The selected Synth-for-Stata 0.0.8 source at commit ae99fd1ed100e8f5ea38386f18f02e61a90956e6 is community software using a commercial host, not an authenticated vendor-native estimator. Its command declares Stata 13 compatibility. Donors default to all other units and an explicit list requires at least two. Predictor means omit missing entries and reject wholly missing predictors, so units can contribute different dates. Defaults use preintervention predictor and MSPE periods, but explicitly supplied MSPE dates are checked for panel membership without a separate preintervention inequality in the inspected branch. The proposed boundary therefore requires frozen donor eligibility and measurement/selection windows. Later unaffected predictors need a substantive justification recorded with the design. \citep{exec19_synth_stata_source}

Its source also shows why an optimizer response is not a validity certificate. Help and command syntax disagree on constraint margin (0.05 versus 0.005) and precision parameter (7 versus 12). RMSPE is calculated before an opaque compiled Mata weight-rounding step. The inspected C wrapper stores the primal output and returns zero without branching on pr_loqo's termination status, although the header distinguishes optimal, suboptimal, iteration-limit and infeasible cases. A future qualification must compare the binary with source, propagate termination, check constraints and distinguish displayed weights from those used for diagnostics. These are source observations; no native failure rate was measured. Conversely, the negative squared-error objective passed to a maximizer is internally coherent and should not be mislabeled as a sign defect. \citep{exec19_synth_stata_source}

Gsynth 1.4.0 is an inspected compatibility wrapper around fect, with reviewed dependency candidate fect 2.4.5. Its documented weight argument is declared but never forwarded or otherwise used. That source-level omission matters because target weights can reverse a decision. Two equally represented cells with effects (-6,10) have mean 2; weights (9,1) give -22/5. At cost 1 the respective stipulated net values are 1 and -5.4. Exposure duration also changes the target: one unit with effect 10 for three treated dates and another with effect -6 for one date give a treated-cell average 6 and a unit average 2. A cost of 4 changes the corresponding margins to 2 and -2. These are exact authored examples, not observed wrapper outputs. \citep{exec21_gsynth_wrapper,exec21_gsynth_reference}

The selected fect source distinguishes fitting weights from aggregation weights: W fills both roles, W.agg changes aggregation and W.est alone leaves aggregation unweighted. The inspected path rejects distinct columns for the two roles and drops zero-weight rows. A migration to the underlying engine therefore needs explicit choices and retained-population reporting. It cannot assume the wrapper's call string proves that requested semantics reached the engine. The wrapper/direct defaults also differ in fixed effects (unit/two-way), CV folds (5/20) and tolerance (1e-3/1e-5). \citep{exec21_gsynth_engine,exec21_gsynth_wrapper}

A factor model replaces convex donor fit with restrictions such as Y_it(0)=alpha_i+xi_t+x_it' beta+lambda_i' f_t+epsilon_it. Preperiod observations must reveal the treated loading in directions relevant after treatment. In the independent rank witness, factors are (1,0),(2,0) before treatment and (1,1) afterwards. Donors with loadings (0,1),(1,1) have paths (0,0,1),(1,2,2). Treated loadings (2,0) and (2,4) both fit prepath (2,4), while the same observed post outcome 8 corresponds to untreated outcomes 2 or 6. Effects are therefore 6 or 2, with opposite net decisions at cost 4. The direction (0,1) lies in the pre-factor null space and matters postintervention. Repeating preperiod rows or adding donors can pass simple count guards without identifying that loading. \citep{exec21_gsynth_reference}

This is an information gap outside a full pre-factor-rank qualification, not a refutation of an estimator's theorem within its assumptions. Informative preperiod variation, a justified loading restriction, an external measurement or a prospective experiment can address a specified ambiguity. Choosing a rank by cross-validation cannot establish those restrictions. The inspected engine has count/rank guards and inverse-solve failure paths; a nearly singular successful solve still needs diagnostics. Its gsynth method forces a never-treated pool and rejects reversals; missingness and minimum preperiod rules can remove units. A family-level claim that some methods allow switching treatment cannot erase restrictions of the selected method. \citep{exec21_gsynth_engine}

Uncertainty must identify what varies. For regional placebo comparisons, freeze eligible assignment units, donor exclusions, pre-fit filters, tail direction and all tuning/refitting. One equally or more extreme statistic among five eligible statistics, including the treated case, gives the arithmetic fraction 1/5; filtering to two gives 1/2. Neither is automatically a valid p-value without the appropriate assignment or exchangeability argument. Failed fits cannot disappear from the reported denominator. Donor dependence, common time shocks and selective fit filters may invalidate a simple ranking argument. \citep{exec19_synth_contract_reference}

The reviewed gsynth route requests parametric uncertainty by default; IFE/matrix-completion routes default to bootstrap and may replace unsupported parametric requests with a warning. Automatic error strategies depend on missing cells. Selected case-bootstrap paths resample units or clusters and can retry conditional on support. Ordinary jackknife warns that a supplied cluster argument is ignored. Average-effect bootstrap standard deviations discard missing draws. The report must consequently preserve all attempted, valid and failed draws, the actual resampling unit, refitting and rank selection, and uncertainty unavailable after failures. A normal interval computed from surviving draws is not itself a coverage argument. Native calibration, donor/time dependence and post-selection uncertainty remain future declared-regime trials. \citep{exec21_gsynth_wrapper,exec21_gsynth_engine}

The proposal retains gsynth as a compatibility reference and favors evaluating an explicitly configured fect worker. Directly porting the full engine would duplicate conversion, weighting, selection and uncertainty before a behavioral baseline exists. Classic convex synthetic control, generalized factor models, matrix completion, cohort-time DID and state-space approaches remain substantive alternatives whose targets and identifying assumptions must be compared. More flexible fit alone is not a ranking criterion. A shared request must carry unit/time keys, action clock, donor universe, masks, units, fitting and aggregation weights, rank/selection rules and inference protocol. A result must retain predicted paths, balance, exact retained populations, constraints, convergence, warnings and separately typed effects and uncertainty across library, CLI and MCP.

Source rights and commercial scope remain explicit. Synth's README states GPL2-or-later while inspected optimizer notices restrict unauthorized commercial use. Admission needs authoritative clarification of the exact distribution and correspondence of compiled components. The wrapper/engine MIT declarations and holder files do not finish the dependency/license-template review. No process boundary by itself resolves these issues. EViews, SAS, IBM, MathWorks and other hosted or native commercial alternatives remain source-specific open comparisons; an uninspected product is not a demonstrated absence. Public/generated references can justify a prototype and expose contract failures before expensive bank experiments, while the bank's donor eligibility, assignment truth and actual economics remain separate evidence. \citep{exec19_synth_stata_source,exec21_gsynth_wrapper,exec21_gsynth_engine}
