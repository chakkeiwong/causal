43.21.5     Static hazards define focused prototype tests

In pinned XBCF’s combined R predictor, the default propensity branch refers to z, which is not
a formal argument. The function assigns predictions to obj1 and obj2, then reads obj before
its local assignment at the end. The separate treatment-draw predictor uses its own local
object. These are particular source paths requiring isolated reconciliation, not failures observed
by executing the package. An enclosing environment, later release or different entrypoint must
not silently replace the inspected route. (xbcf contributors, 2026)
  In the selected Python wrapper, the zero-outcome-variance branch constructs ValueError
without raising it, and adjusted fitted draws are assigned inside the standardization branch.
Prediction has separate restoration logic. Tests should cover constant outcomes, both stan-
dardization settings, fit-versus-predict parity and return formats. The native loop alternates
prognostic and treatment updates and updates scales; stored output must be checked against
wrapper order. Source resemblance does not prove equivalent R and Python interfaces. (xbcf
contributors, 2026)
   The selected flexBCF trainer derives the control count from prognostic and treatment design
sizes and offsets treatment updates accordingly. Its public wrapper transposes the supplied
matrices and passes the treatment indicator without rearranging rows. The same-revision
manual explicitly requires controls before treated subjects; its worked construction supplies
prognostic predictors for all subjects but treatment predictors only for treated subjects. The
native traversal and residual offsets follow that arrangement. An adapter must apply one
common permutation to outcomes, treatment, identifiers and prognostic predictors, then form
the treated-only design in the corresponding order. Sorting just the treatment vector would
corrupt the correspondence. For subjects (a, b, c, d) with labels (1, 0, 1, 0), a stable valid order
is (b, d, a, c) and the treatment design contains (a, c). This identity check is an authored fixture,
not a fitted forest. (flexbcf contributors, 2026; flexBCF contributors, 2025; Causal banking
proposal project, 2026hd)

43.21.6     Historical performance supports an experiment

The BCF paper offers evidence for its design and relevant limitations. Its targeted-selection
example improves bias and coverage over ordinary BART under that simulation; broader
experiments do not give uniformly nominal coverage. In the retrospective ACIC 2016 compari-
son, Table 4 reports nominal 95% interval coverage of 82% for BCF, 88% for propensity-score
BART, 81% for BART and 58% for the compared causal random forest. BCF’s mean interval
length is 0.026 versus 0.055 for that forest. Shorter intervals and lower estimation error coexist
with undercoverage. The paper discusses this limitation and supplies no general frequentist
coverage theorem for its credible intervals. (Hahn et al., 2019)
   These are historical reported results, not a project reproduction or ranking of today’s
implementations. They motivate public experiments reporting effect error, coverage, width,
failures and decision consequences together. Known counterfactual generators can vary
overlap, targeted selection, effect sparsity, response nonlinearity, propensity misspecification
and dependence. Public benchmarks provide complementary designs whose versions, targets
and access conditions must be fixed. Failed fits and invalid requests stay in the denominator.
Posterior uncertainty, Monte Carlo error and repeated-sampling coverage must remain separate:
more draws do not repair a wrong causal target or unmeasured confounding. (Hahn et al.,
2019; Causal banking proposal project, 2026az)


43.21.7     A scalable longitudinal forest still needs an identification model

The original flexBCF study by Kokandakar et al. (2023) supplies a consequential comparison
between computational feasibility and scientific adequacy. The inspected original is arXiv
version 2, posted on 11 May 2023 and carrying a 15 May 2023 title-page date. It is distinct
from both a fresh replication and an audit of the selected 2025 package. The authors analyze
the 2022 ACIC challenge: 3,400 synthetic datasets, comprising 200 replications of each of
seventeen mechanisms, with treatment assigned to medical practices after two pre-treatment
years. For banking, the analogous question is the effect of a branch-level intervention on
customers served by treated branches. It requires a branch assignment clock, a specified
customer population and a defense of the comparison across branches. (Kokandakar et al.,
2023)
  Their identification argument uses consistency and no anticipation, overlap, and conditional
independence of untreated outcome changes and assignment given baseline covariates. Define
Z as branch assignment, X as permitted baseline information, s as a pre-intervention date
and t as a post-intervention date. Let mzt (x) = E(Yt | Z = z, X = x). The corresponding
conditional effect among treated units is

                       τT (x, t) = m1t (x) − m0t (x) − {m1s (x) − m0s (x)}.                (43.51)

The paper proves this difference-in-differences identity in its Appendix S1. Its stated conditional
independence is stronger than the equality of conditional untreated mean changes needed for
this mean identity. A portfolio ATT must further average over the declared treated population;
an unqualified all-customer average is a different target. Neither a fitted propensity feature
nor a large number of customer rows verifies the identifying assumptions. (Kokandakar et al.,
2023)
   The estimation model adds a separate restriction. It represents the outcome mean by a
common prognostic function of covariates and time, plus a treatment function activated only
after intervention. Before intervention, that specification forces treated and untreated groups
with the same included covariates to have the same expected outcome level. Parallel untreated

trends alone do not impose equal baseline levels. To see the distinction, consider equally likely
branch groups with no varying observed covariates. Their pre-intervention monetary means
are USD 10 and USD 14. Untreated outcomes rise by USD 3 in both groups; the second
group receives an intervention adding USD 2. Observed post-intervention means are therefore
USD 13 and USD 19. The post-period gap is USD 6, but subtracting the USD 4 pre-period
gap identifies the USD 2 effect. At an assumed USD 3 per-customer intervention cost, the
identified margin is USD −1, whereas treating the post-period gap as the effect yields USD 3.
This exact law satisfies overlap and parallel untreated increments while violating equal baseline
levels. It does not assert that a particular fitted forest necessarily returns the post-period
gap. (Kokandakar et al., 2023; Causal banking proposal project, 2026he)
   The challenge analysis also conditions the treatment component on some post-treatment
practice-composition summaries. The authors explicitly assume that intervention does not
change attrition and hence practice composition. A bank must examine that assumption when
assistance changes account closure, migration or the customers remaining at a branch. If
composition responds to intervention, conditioning on its later value can change the causal
question or introduce selection. A serious alternative is to model changes under a defended
trend restriction, with a fixed population and dependence-aware evaluation; it is not enough to
feed later composition into a flexible learner and retain the original target label. (Kokandakar
et al., 2023)
   Computational improvement did not remove the inferential limitation in the reported
challenge. For nominal 90% overall-ATT credible intervals, the original beneficiary-level
submission had mean coverage 52.15% across the seventeen mechanisms; the practice-level
analysis had mean coverage 83.68%. Its mean RMSE rose from 20.54 to 23.04 when aggregated
to practices. The paper reports that flexible propensity estimation often increased interval
width and coverage, while point-estimation differences were modest. These historical summaries
are attributed results, not independent reconstructions, and they do not establish a universal
preference for aggregation, a propensity learner or a sparsity prior. The authors discuss
outcome-model misspecification, omitted pre-treatment outcomes or trends, and limited
sensitivity analysis as explanations and limits. (Kokandakar et al., 2023)
   The study motivates examining tree persistence, categorical partitioning and memory
allocation, but its runtime narrative supplies no matched bank total-cost estimate. Nor does
its empirical performance diagnose the later pinned caller defect above: these concern different
versioned evidence. For a bank prototype, the useful comparison separates the identifying
trend restriction, additional response-model restrictions, customer and branch sampling units,
composition assumptions, and the reporting path. Faster computation merits adoption only
when the resulting estimand and uncertainty remain appropriate to the decision. A failed
calibration or an unsupported composition assumption should reverse an otherwise attractive
computational recommendation. (Kokandakar et al., 2023; flexBCF contributors, 2025)
