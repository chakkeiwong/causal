The decision before the model

A bank considering a higher deposit rate faces a deceptively simple question: will the extra
funding be worth the interest it must pay? Customers who choose a generous rate may already
intend to move money. An increase in recorded balances may be a transfer from another
account at the same bank. Competitors may change their prices at the same time. A forecast
of balances, however accurate, cannot by itself separate these explanations.
   Causal analysis asks how the bank’s choice changes an outcome relative to a specified
alternative. The alternative matters as much as the proposed action. An invitation to open
an account, activation of that account, and a change in the rate paid on an existing balance
are different interventions. Their costs, beneficiaries and effects need not coincide. A useful
analysis begins with this economic distinction and carries it through the data, statistical
assumptions and financial calculation.
   The same discipline applies beyond deposits. A collections team choosing when to contact
a borrower must account for information revealed by earlier contacts. A lender changing an
eligibility threshold must distinguish the effect on marginal applicants from the effect on all
customers. Treasury must separate an external funding shock from the bank’s own response.
In each case, the bank wants to compare feasible decisions, and the available observations may
support only part of that comparison.
  The argument develops from these choices. Economic questions lead to causal models;
the models reveal which comparisons the data can identify; estimation then measures those
comparisons and their uncertainty. Repeated decisions and imperfect observations complicate
the analysis. Existing software offers many of the necessary methods, but its assumptions
and definitions must match the question. Language models may help economists formulate
questions and examine evidence. Public data and controlled numerical studies can establish
which ideas merit a prototype before a bank pays for a larger experiment.
   The proposal is for a common analytical service that makes this work easier to repeat without
changing the underlying economics. Its case rests on the quality and cost of completed studies,
including studies that show why an effect cannot be learned from the available information.
The reference alternative is a capable quantitative team using established methods. A new
service must justify its additional cost against that alternative.
   The mathematical development remains part of the argument. Symbols identify economic
quantities, assumptions state what the comparison requires, and examples expose where a con-
clusion fails. Constructed examples and simulated populations are described as such. Findings
from public data retain their population and measurement limits. Proposed capabilities are
distinguished from the calculations that have actually been performed. The literature cutoff is
7 October 2026; source editions and acquisition dates are documented separately.

The argument in nine parts

Part I starts with a bank’s decision and develops the distinction between selection, identification
and estimation. Part II follows customers and institutions through time. Part III considers
uncertain causal relationships, imperfect records and the transfer of results between populations.
   Parts IV and V compare available methods and software. Discovery and identification come
first, followed by effect estimation, repeated decisions, panel comparisons and commercial
choices. Detailed package discussions sit beside the questions they address, so that a reader
can compare alternatives without following the order in which they were acquired.
   Part VI examines assistance from language models: turning a business question into a
research design, drawing and questioning causal diagrams, supporting discovery, and explaining
results. Part VII develops practical use through research libraries, command-line tools and the
Model Context Protocol. These implementation choices follow the economic and statistical
requirements.
   Part VIII brings public data and banking studies together. It asks what the evidence
establishes, how the conclusions depend on information and assumptions, and where another
measurement would be more useful than another estimator. Part IX turns those findings into
an investment question: what to build, what to reuse, and which experiment could change the
choice.

1.1     More business is not necessarily more value
Consider a deposit offer to a defined group of households. The bank can send the offer or
leave its current terms unchanged. For household i, let Yi (1) be the contribution over the
next quarter if the offer is sent and Yi (0) the contribution under the alternative. Contribution
includes the agreed funding value, interest expense and servicing costs over the same quarter.
The average effect for the eligible population is

                                     τ = E{Yi (1) − Yi (0)}.

The expectation averages over households, rather than accounts or branches. That choice
prevents a household with several accounts from receiving several votes merely because of the
way the records are stored. The definition would change if management wanted the average
effect per pound of existing deposits. The formal potential-outcome development follows in
Chapter 2.
   A simple financial calculation illustrates why the outcome needs care. Suppose a rate
increase attracts an additional balance ∆B for one year. Let m be the contribution per pound
of that extra balance before the cost of the rate increase, ∆r the increase in the annual rate,
B0 the existing balance that also receives it, and c the direct cost of the offer. Under the
illustrative assumptions that balances remain constant during the year and that the increase

1.2     Selection can imitate an effect
Comparing customers who accepted an offer with those who did not generally mixes the offer’s
effect with the reasons for acceptance. The distinction can be seen without a complicated model.
Suppose, as a constructed example, that half the eligible customers have high underlying
demand for deposits and half have low demand. Their balances without an offer would be
£200 and £100 in the units of the example. Assume the offer adds £20 for every customer.
If 80% of recipients are high-demand customers but only 20% of nonrecipients are, observed
average balances are

                           E[Y | A = 1] = 0.8(220) + 0.2(120) = 200,
                           E[Y | A = 0] = 0.2(200) + 0.8(100) = 120.

The observed difference is £80, although the specified effect is £20. The remaining £60 is a
difference in the composition of the two groups. The calculation assumes equally sized recipient
and nonrecipient groups, consistent with the population shares. Random assignment in this
example would make the groups comparable in expectation; an observational analysis needs
an additional argument about demand and treatment selection.
   This is why identification precedes precision. A more accurate estimate of £80 would
not answer the stated question. Nor does predicting balances well reveal which part of the
difference the bank can change. Randomized invitations, eligibility rules, sufficiently rich
pre-offer information and credible structural restrictions offer different ways forward. They
identify different quantities under different assumptions, developed in Chapters 2 and 88 and
the intervening analysis.

inference chapters; it should also remain visible in a management report.

1.5     The case for a shared analytical service
The proposal follows from this economic sequence. A common service could help analysts
retain the population, action, information date and financial outcome as a study moves from
formulation to estimation and interpretation. Existing methods should be reused where their
assumptions and capabilities fit. An analysis that finds insufficient information should explain
the missing comparison and, where possible, the measurement or experiment that would resolve
it.
   The value of language assistance must be assessed within that sequence. A clearer formulation
or a useful criticism of a causal diagram can save analyst time. A fluent but unsupported
assumption can make the study less reliable. The comparisons in Part VI therefore concern
particular research tasks and their errors, rather than general impressions of intelligence.
Public evidence can test some of these tasks before confidential bank data are needed.
  The scientific and investment questions meet at the same point: whether a better-supported
decision is worth its cost. The chapters that follow supply the causal arguments, statistical
methods, software comparisons and evidence needed to make that judgment.

2.1     Begin with the intervention, unit and observation process
Consider a household eligible for a preferential deposit product. Let Z denote the bank’s
invitation, A actual activation, M a subsequent change in balances and Y the ninety-day
funding contribution. An intervention on Z is operationally different from forcing A = 1.
An increase in the database field recording A is different again. The bank can randomize
an invitation; it cannot generally compel activation or create liquidity merely by changing a
recorded balance. A causal question must identify the mechanism that the proposed decision
actually changes.
   The unit is a household rather than an account when money can move between accounts
owned by the same people. The outcome includes an agreed transfer price, interest paid and
servicing cost, with movements within the household consolidated. This is part of defining
Y , rather than a correction performed after estimating an account-level effect. For a fixed
population and horizon, write Y (a) for the outcome under a specified activation intervention,
and Y Z=z for the outcome under invitation assignment z. Two initial targets are

                     θA = E{Y (1) − Y (0)},        θZ = E{Y Z=1 − Y Z=0 }.                 (2.1)

They differ even in an ideal randomized invitation experiment. An invitation may have costs or
affect trust without activation. Such effects belong to θZ and threaten an exclusion restriction
used to infer θA .
  Consistency means that a household exposed to the specified action has its corresponding
potential outcome. It requires clarity about price, duration, channel, eligibility, execution and
outcome measurement. It is not ensured by giving all those interventions the same binary
code. Interference would require Yi (a), or a justified exposure mapping, instead of Yi (ai ).
The present single-period derivations assume a population of noninterfering units and a fixed
version of each action. Cluster or network designs must change those assumptions explicitly
(Hernán and Robins, 2020, Chapters 1–3).
  For clarity most graph calculations below use discrete variables and sums. Continuous
versions use conditional densities or probability kernels, with absolute continuity and inte-
grability stated at the estimation stage. This distinction matters: an intervention at one
exact continuous treatment value can be identifiable as a conditional-law functional without
admitting the same regular root-n estimator as a binary-treatment average effect.

2.2     Structural equations define what an intervention changes
Definition 2.1 (Acyclic structural model). An acyclic structural causal model M consists of
endogenous variables V = (V1 , . . . , Vd ), exogenous variables U , a joint law PU , and assignments
                              Vj := fj (Vpa(j) , Uj ),   j = 1, . . . , d,
whose endogenous parent graph is a DAG. A hard intervention do(A = a) replaces the
assignment for A by the constant a and retains the remaining assignments and exogenous law.

   The colon distinguishes an assignment from an algebraic identity that may be inverted
freely. A regression residual is constructed to be orthogonal to fitted regressors; a structural
disturbance represents omitted causes and need not be orthogonal to the action. Autonomous
mechanisms are what make intervention replacement meaningful (Pearl, 2009, Sections 3.1–3.2).
   If all Uj are mutually independent, the model is Markovian relative to its displayed endoge-
nous DAG. Hidden common causes can instead be displayed as latent parents, producing a
larger DAG, or left as dependence in PU . One must not draw an observed DAG and silently
assume independent disturbances after omitting a common cause. Both the functions and the
disturbance law are components of the causal model.
   Potential outcomes can be generated by recursively evaluating the modified assignments
at the same exogenous realization: Y (a) = fY (. . . ; a, U ). This constructs consistency and
expresses P {Y (a)} as the distribution of Y in the intervened model. Conversely, a potential-
outcome analysis need not posit mutually independent disturbances for every structural
equation. In particular, assumptions sufficient to identify each single-world intervention
distribution need not determine joint quantities involving incompatible interventions.
   The SWIG construction makes this distinction explicit. Split an action node into its random
part A, retaining incoming arrows, and a fixed part a, retaining outgoing arrows. Relabel its
descendants as counterfactual variables. For X → A, X → Y , A → Y this yields a graph for
(X, A, Y (a)) in which A ⊥ Y (a) | X can be read by separation. It does not simultaneously
supply a graph for the unobserved pair (Y (0), Y (1)). The SWIG framework covers the less
restrictive FFRCISTG model as well as the independent-error structural submodel (Richardson
and Robins, 2013, Section 2, node splitting and modularity). Consequently, identifying a
mean effect does not identify individual gains or their variance: Var{Y (1) − Y (0)} additionally
involves the unobserved Cov{Y (1), Y (0)}.

assigned next, A records subsequent activation, M is later behavior, and Y closes the horizon.
Let U be unrecorded liquidity need and V a servicing difficulty that also affects outcomes. Let

is shown in Figure 2.1. It is an assumption to be challenged with process owners and data

   Each omitted arrow makes a substantive claim. Absence of Z → Y and Z → M says
that the invitation affects the economic outcome only through activation. Absence of causes

  A useful construction procedure has five concrete steps.
1. Define the actor, feasible action, unit, economic endpoint and clock. Separate assigned,
   received, recorded and measured quantities.
2. List causes of the action and of the endpoint, including private decisions, calendar conditions,
   common counterparties and selection.
3. Order mechanisms at the finest relevant time resolution. Replace apparent contemporaneous

4. Mark what is observed and when it becomes available; draw observation and inclusion
   mechanisms instead of treating missingness as a blank cell.
5. Examine every exclusion needed by the intended identification route. Record its business
   justification, plausible violations and what an experiment or additional measurement could

This procedure separates a causal diagram from data lineage. A lineage edge from transactions
to an aggregated balance describes a calculation. It does not mean that intervening on the
stored aggregate changes the underlying transactions. Deterministic accounting identities also
create conditional dependencies that require care in discovery algorithms relying on generic
faithfulness assumptions.


2.4     Paths, colliders, latent variables and learned graphs
A path ignores the direction of its successive edges. An internal node is a collider when
both adjacent edges have arrowheads into it. A DAG path is active conditional on S when
every noncollider is outside S and every collider has itself or a descendant in S. Two sets
are d-separated when no such path connects them. In a distribution Markov to the DAG,
d-separation implies conditional independence (Pearl, 2009, Definition 1). Faithfulness is the
additional converse assumption used by many discovery procedures; it is not needed to deduce
independences from a stipulated causal graph.
  In the bank graph, conditioning on X blocks A ← X → Y but leaves A ← U → Y open.
Adding M can block a causal path and therefore change the target. Conditioning on A opens
the collider Z → A ← U → Y : invitation and latent need need not remain independent among
activators. This is why analyzing compliance strata defined by observed receipt is not the

2.5.3    District decomposition and an obstruction certificate

In a semi-Markovian ADMG a district is a maximal set joined by bidirected paths. A singleton
without a bidirected neighbor is also a district. For S ⊆ V , define the interventional kernel
Q[S] = p(s | do(v \ s)). It is a function of s and the fixed outside values; in general it is not
the ordinary conditional p(s | v \ s). Shared hidden disturbances couple variables within a
district. Integrating over distinct independent latent groups factorizes an intervention kernel
over districts in its remaining graph.
   The ID algorithm exploits this decomposition recursively. With no intervention it marginal-
izes the supplied law. It first restricts to ancestors of the requested outcome, summing
irrelevant variables. It can then add interventions on remaining nonancestors in the graph
with the original intervention nodes removed, because they cannot change the target in that
graph. When the remaining graph has several districts, it identifies their kernels separately,
multiplies them, and marginalizes nonoutcome variables. When one district remains, it either
extracts topologically ordered conditional factors for a district already available in the current
kernel, recurses inside a larger containing district, or returns a failure witness. These steps
are made precise in Shpitser and Pearl (2006, Section 4, Figure 2); they apply to its specified
semi-Markovian model class.
   The obstruction is a hedge. An R-rooted C-forest is a bidirected-connected subgraph whose
observed nodes have at most one directed child, with childless root set R. Two such forests
F ′ ⊂ F form a hedge for the query when F meets the intervention set, F ′ does not, and their
common roots are ancestors of the outcome in the intervened graph. The source proves that
such a pair witnesses nonidentification and establishes completeness of ID for the specified
queries. These are inherited theorems; the following small case makes their practical meaning
concrete.
   For the bow graph A → Y , A ↔ Y , take F = {A, Y } and F ′ = {Y }, with common root Y .
The larger forest contains A and the smaller does not. Thus failure to identify p(y | do(a)) is
certified, rather than merely caused by a search routine finding no adjustment set. Section 2.6
constructs two positive observed-law examples witnessing that same failure. The front-door
graph avoids this obstruction for the total effect through an identifiable composition of district
kernels.
   Conditional interventional queries require another distinction. Dividing an identified p(y, z |
do(a)) by its z margin works when that joint law is identified and the denominator is positive,
but failure to identify the joint law need not settle the conditional query. The IDC algorithm
first exchanges eligible conditioning variables for interventions using do-calculus, then invokes
ID and normalizes the remaining joint kernel (Shpitser and Pearl, 2006, Section 5, Figure 3).
Its input is a conditional interventional distribution, not a generic cross-world mediation or
personalized counterfactual request.

2.7.1    Edge assignments and a single-world alternative

For the simple independent-error system M = fM (A, X, UM ) and Y = fY (A, M, X, UY ), with

2.8     What the evidence allows us to conclude
The mathematical input is an intervention, target population, observed information and
model class. Its identification output is either a functional with an assumption-dependent
derivation, a set of possible values, or a demonstrated failure of identification. A missing
software implementation is a fourth state: it says nothing about identification.
  For the graph in Figure 2.1, a result cannot be labeled “adjusted ATE” after simply fitting Y
on (A, X). A valid result might instead report the randomized invitation effect, the consistency
bounds, or an IV analysis with extra assumptions. If multiple candidate graphs remain,
compute the effect for each supported graph or carry their union of identified sets. Reporting
the spread across two handpicked graphs is only a sensitivity exercise for those graphs, not a
confidence interval covering all plausible causal structures.

3.1     Why an invitation is not an adjustment covariate
Suppress X, M, C temporarily and consider Z → A → Y with A ← U → Y . In a randomized
invitation study, Z changes receipt while U confounds receipt and outcome. The graph
expresses that Z is independent of the common causes and that all directed paths from Z to Y
pass through A. Neither condition is equivalent to Z ⊥ Y | A: conditioning on receipt opens
the collider path through U . An empirical association between Z and Y among activators is
therefore not by itself evidence against exclusion.
   The graph alone does not generally identify E[Y (1) − Y (0)] (Pearl, 2009, Section 3.5). Linear
homogeneous effects, monotone binary compliance, and a nonparametric structural response
each add different restrictions. This chapter develops the first two and connects them to the
inverse problem that arises in the third. Calling all three outputs an “IV effect” would lose
the scientific distinction.

3.8     Additional assumptions change the answer
The invitation experiment can return an intention-to-treat effect under randomization. A
homogeneous linear model can return a structural coefficient and moment-based uncertainty
under rank conditions. Monotone binary compliance can return a complier effect and its
population weights. An unrestricted IV graph can instead return bounds or an explicit
nonidentification result. A regularized inverse problem returns an estimate conditional on a
function class, operator restrictions and regularization.
   Each result must carry the original intervention, population, assumptions, first-stage evidence,
score or likelihood, dependence unit and uncertainty method. A weak first stage should
affect the inference state; a disputed exclusion should affect the causal assumption state.
Combining them into one opaque confidence score would conceal which remedy is required.
Additional randomization can improve support; a different encouragement may improve
relevance; measuring an omitted mechanism may alter the graph. None of these changes is
equivalent to running a more accurate predictor.

4.1    Four different learning problems
Suppose Chapter 2 has justified adjustment by X. The observed data for an independent

changed actions. The two losses are not interchangeable, and a random train/test split cannot
by itself bridge them.

4.7     Implications for estimation in the bank
The product should accept an identified functional or moment, an explicit target measure,
nuisance roles and a sampling model. It can then choose compatible learners, splitting schemes
and uncertainty methods. The output must identify whether it is an ATE, an overlap-weighted
projection, a CATE curve, a complier curve or a policy value. A response predictor can be
shared across applications without transferring the causal validity of any particular application.
   Diagnostics should correspond to the mathematics: nuisance residuals and support, score
stability, fold leakage, local moment rank, regularization sensitivity and policy-selection rules.
Predictive validation is useful for nuisance fit; it cannot certify exchangeability, instrument
exclusion, causal representation or transport to another bank. These distinctions also determine
what automated data organization is allowed to discard or aggregate. The next chapter develops
inverse learning with endogeneity and proxies. Chapter 6 then makes the information set
dynamic and shows why sequence prediction is still only one part of the causal problem.

6.10      What a state-space adapter must preserve
A numerical filter consumes transition and observation kernels, initial state, parameter values
and masks. A causal adapter additionally needs the action clock, logger information, inter-
vention mechanism, reward definition and source of identification. It must specify whether
a missing measurement is exogenous, conditionally ignorable, or modeled through a state-
dependent observation process. Omitting an endogenous mask changes the likelihood and may
change policy identification.
   For continuous states, sums become integrals and Kalman, nonlinear Gaussian, particle or
variational methods approximate different objects. The state-space adapter should report the
actual likelihood approximation, not only the latent-state dimension. Label fixing, deterministic
lag coordinates, parameter priors and reward integration must be coherent across fitting and
intervention evaluation. This is how a numerical filtering library can be reused without
attributing causal guarantees to its numerical accuracy.
  The customer and the bank may also be different decision makers. The bank’s POMDP
maximizes a supplied bank reward. A model of customer activation, refinancing or default
requires utility, beliefs, information and transition restrictions of its own. Estimating a
customer’s dynamic choice likelihood is not equivalent to optimizing the bank’s reward;
equilibrium response adds another layer. Chapter 8 develops that inverse decision problem;
Chapters 10 and 11 connect structural shocks and feedback to it.

8.1     What a hidden-state fit must establish
The likelihood and policy-replacement recursions in Chapter 6 leave an identification question
open. Which controlled transition, reward and observation kernels can the recorded law
determine? The answer depends on who sees the state, how actions are assigned, what is
measured, and which actions the target policy will use. A repayment trajectory can identify a
useful predictive state without identifying the effect of an untried restructuring.
   For an uncontrolled stationary K-state HMM, Alexandrovich et al. (2016, Assumptions
A1–A3, Theorem 1) identifies a full-rank ergodic transition matrix and distinct emission
distributions from a block of 2K + 1 observations, up to state-label permutation. Distinct
emissions need not be linearly independent in that theorem. It is a result about the specified
uncontrolled observation law. Treating a privately selected action as an exogenous input
changes its factorization, so the theorem cannot simply be transferred to that fit.
   Identification of the value of one supported policy can require less information than iden-
tification of every state parameter. Conversely, identifying all parameters of one chosen
observational model says nothing about excluded causal models with the same observed law.
The following finite construction separates these issues explicitly.

19.1     Start with the banking comparison
A bank considering a causal library faces several different purchasing and engineering decisions.
It may need a defensible effect of an invitation, a description of plausible causal directions
among funding variables, or a policy for repeated servicing decisions. A package that produces
a directed graph does not necessarily answer the first question. A package that estimates an
average treatment effect does not necessarily answer the third. The survey therefore takes a
scientific operation as its unit of comparison: an input with a declared observation law, a causal
target, assumptions connecting the two, and an output whose meaning survives conversion
into the bank’s units.
   This choice changes what counts as competitive capability. Consider a request to increase
deposit funding by changing the offered rate. A system that returns several compatible
effects has provided useful information if the observations cannot distinguish their underlying
mechanisms. A system that returns one number after choosing a convenient graph may have
answered a more restrictive question. The comparison must expose the restriction before
comparing accuracy, speed or convenience. Similarly, a symbolic expression for an effect and
an estimate of that expression are different accomplishments. The expression identifies what
information is needed; its numerical evaluation adds statistical and computational obligations.

19.2      The scope of the comparison
The landscape has two dimensions that should be searched separately. The method dimen-

   The publication cutoff is 7 October 2026. Acquisition time, software revision, manual
generation date and scholarly publication date are retained separately. This matters even for
apparently simple documentation: the inspected pcalg manual identifies version 2.7-12 and a

The example separates uncertainties that are often compressed into one picture. Even with
the population covariance known exactly, the three causal representations disagree about
the intervention. Finite observations introduce additional uncertainty about covariance and
conditional independences. If a graph is chosen from those observations, subsequent effect
estimation also depends on that choice. A graph-equivalence multiset is consequently not a
confidence interval with a nominal repeated-sampling coverage level. Taking its convex hull
may be useful for a conservative decision rule, but the hull loses information about disconnected
possibilities and multiplicity.

19.9     From search records to a defensible coverage claim
A systematic comparison needs a rule for what one entry represents. Let P be the set of
canonical implementations, A the method areas and L the language or commercial access paths.
A source may support a relation (p, a, ℓ) ∈ P × A × L. This is a many-to-many relation. One
implementation can supply several methods; one method can have several implementations;
and a wrapper can expose an implementation in another language. Counting each relation as a
new package would overstate the number of independent alternatives. Collapsing all relations
to the package name would conceal unsupported methods and access paths. The survey
therefore needs both a canonical implementation index and an operation-specific comparison.
   The pcalg example illustrates this distinction without requiring a hypothetical new package.
Its PC discovery and IDA effect calculation belong to the same implementation family, but
their inputs and outputs differ. Reading the PC interface does not establish an IDA claim.
Similarly, the documented R interface does not establish that a proposed Python process
wrapper has preserved its graph semantics. The inspected manual supports the named native
operations; the wrapper requires additional evidence (pcalg contributors, 2026). The canonical
entry can share identity and license facts while keeping separate evidence for each operation.
  For each comparison, define the claim as a tuple

                                      c = (p, v, a, ℓ, d, q, o),

where v is a release or source revision, d the supported data regime, q the scientific request
and o the output meaning. A useful source record answers a specific question about this tuple.
A manual can describe an operation on complete iid continuous records without establishing
behavior on clustered monthly histories. A license can establish a permission subject to
conditions without establishing algorithmic correctness. An executed roundtrip can establish
endpoint preservation without establishing coverage of a confidence interval. These evidence
types are complementary; none should replace the others in a single undifferentiated confidence
score.
   The comparison can use five explicit evidence states: a discovered lead; an identified primary
source; a technically inspected claim; an executed behavioral reference; and a statistically
evaluated claim in a declared law. An entry moves between these states only for the relevant
claim tuple. Failure is retained separately: an inaccessible manual is an acquisition failure, an
unsupported input is an interface restriction, and an invalid effect calculation is a substantive
problem. Each implies a different next action. A search result identifying a package cannot
advance a numerical claim, and a successful trial cannot establish that an undocumented
edition shares the same feature.

with a reviewed disposition measures completion of that declared plan. It does not estimate
the fraction of all software in the world that has been found. Ranked bibliographic results

37.3.1    Two restrictions with different zero points

Consider an offer that starts at time 1, with one earlier comparison at −1 and an untreated
reference period 0. Let δt be the difference between treated and comparison groups’ untreated
mean outcomes, expressed relative to its value at time 0, so δ0 = 0. Assume no anticipation
through time 0, a fixed population and a coherent, equally spaced calendar. Write x = δ−1
and y = τ + δ1 , where τ is the postperiod treatment effect in the same outcome units. These
are population contrasts, not estimated coefficients. An outcome can be quarterly contribution
in dollars per eligible exposure, provided the same denominator and accounting convention
apply in all periods.
   A relative-magnitude restriction with multiplier m ≥ 0 bounds the next untreated first
difference by m times the absolute preperiod first difference. An absolute smoothness restriction
with M ≥ 0 bounds the change in that first difference. In this three-period case they give
                 |δ1 | ≤ m|x|,            IRM (x, y; m) = [y − m|x|, y + m|x|],             (37.1)
             |δ1 + x| ≤ M,                ISD (x, y; M ) = [y + x − M, y + x + M ].         (37.2)
The first interval follows by substituting every admissible δ1 into τ = y − δ1 ; the second follows

37.3.3    Defaults, grids and failed numerical searches

The pinned source exposes substantive distinctions between entrypoints. The unrestricted
smoothness interface defaults to a fixed-length confidence interval and the first postperiod
target. Its automatically selected tuning grid uses a preperiod upper-bound calculation with
significance level 0.05 when multiple preperiods are present, even if the requested interval
level differs. The relative-magnitude interface defaults to the C-LF method, ten tuning values
between zero and two, and 1,000 inversion grid points. The did bridge has a default of 100
grid points and forwards that argument to relative magnitude but not to its smoothness call.
A wrapper should record the executed options explicitly and require deliberate tuning choices
for a decision analysis (Ashesh Rambachan, Jonathan Roth and contributors, 2026b,a).
   At the lower relative-magnitude level, membership is retained at grid nodes after union
across signed preperiod components. Default bounds are plus or minus twenty post-target
standard errors around zero. The inspected high-level path reduces accepted nodes to their
minimum and maximum. This is a numerical summary, not a certificate that the continuous
confidence set is connected, fully searched or bounded. An empty set of accepted nodes on the
coarse grid {−1, 1} is compatible with the nonempty continuous set [−0.2, 0.2]. Accepted nodes
−2, −1, 1, 2 with rejection at zero have hull [−2, 2], which fills a rejected gap. Acceptance at a
search boundary leaves the extent beyond that boundary unresolved. These are elementary
grid counterexamples, not observed outputs from running the package (Ashesh Rambachan,
Jonathan Roth and contributors, 2026b; Causal banking proposal project, 2026bh).
   Solver status creates a different concern. In the inspected population-bound helpers, when
both linear-program statuses are nonzero, the smoothness path returns the unadjusted target
as both endpoints with a warning; the selected relative-magnitude component path makes
a corresponding return without a local warning. The inspected alternative branch does not
separately guard a single failed optimum. These source paths do not prove that any particular
runtime input triggers a failure, but they rule out treating every returned point interval as
certified identification. A proposed adapter must distinguish infeasibility, unboundedness,
convergence failure and an unresolved status. Capturing warnings alone is insufficient if a
helper discards status; a pinned patch or lower-level instrumentation may be required before
admission (Ashesh Rambachan, Jonathan Roth and contributors, 2026b).
   The same principle applies to covariance and restrictions. Nonconformable dimensions
cause refusal, while asymmetry and negative eigenvalues generate warnings rather than a
statistical repair. Fixed-length paths warn that requested sign or shape restrictions are unused.
The initial proposed contract should refuse an unexplained invalid covariance and either
refuse an ignored restriction or report the executed, weaker restriction explicitly. A silent
positive-semidefinite projection would change the supplied uncertainty model; it needs its own
justification.

48.2     Matching the experiment and its denominator
A comparison requires a common request population before it requires a common timer. Let
Q be the prespecified distribution of requested cases, including difficult and unsupported
cases relevant to the proposed service. For each route, retain a disposition Rj (q) such as
computed, unsupported, unavailable, or invalid input, together with the target and uncertainty
object actually returned. The intended denominator is the requested workload. A conditional
accuracy figure among successful outputs remains useful, but it answers a different question
from the probability of receiving a correct, usable result for an arbitrary requested case.
   For estimator comparisons, a matched case must bind the population measure, intervention,
horizon, information clock and uncertainty meaning. The two procedures may legitimately
use different computational strategies. They may also use different statistical methods if the
purpose is a method comparison, provided the target and admissible information are held
fixed and each method’s assumptions are exposed. A port-equivalence study has a narrower
objective: it tests whether the intended procedure survives translation, including defaults,
failure behavior, randomness and diagnostic outputs. Mixing these objectives would make a
better result ambiguous between a scientific change and a faithful implementation.
   The first prototype should contain three complementary groups. Exact finite or analytic
constructions test meanings and numerical identities. Generated sampling experiments test
operating characteristics under explicit truth. Documented public datasets test preparation,
interfaces, and useful questions within their evidence limits. Agreement on public observational
data is not itself a causal-truth label. A benchmark with known intervention assignments
supports a stronger comparison only for its documented population and design. The proposal
can therefore establish the merit and practicality of a later bank experiment without pretending
that public data reproduces private bank assignment, identity or operational truth. (Causal
banking research project, 2026as)
   Workload matching also includes resource and information budgets. Both routes should
receive the same permitted data and expert restrictions; preprocessing, search, tuning and
human repair must be charged consistently. If one candidate uses a larger graph-search
budget, report both a fixed-budget comparison and the declared cost–quality tradeoff. For a
bridge, include serialization, data movement, environment startup, numerical work, uncertainty
construction and publication. Warm and cold executions are distinct operating scenarios.
Reuse of a fitted model or cached result is valid only when the identity and invalidation rule
agree with the intended service.

48.5      A port must preserve the full procedure
Porting has several possible meanings. A mathematical reimplementation may reproduce
a functional using a different algorithm. A compatibility port may seek the same defaults,
accepted inputs, warnings and output semantics as a specified release. A new method may
deliberately improve an estimator or uncertainty construction. Each can be useful, but each
requires a different comparison. The product should not retain an upstream method name
while changing its weighting measure, policy target or interval construction without declaring
that change. Source-level agreement is particularly weak when a caller changes the meaning of
an otherwise correctly translated routine.

61.1     The benchmark unit is a causal problem
An agent can produce a plausible report, execute valid code and still answer the wrong causal
question. Evaluation must follow the entire chain from business meaning through assumptions
and identification to uncertainty, availability and decision loss. A benchmark of text fluency,
graph edit distance or successful tool calls measures only part of that chain.
   CLadder demonstrates the value of formal questions with varied verbalizations, including
commonsense and deliberately unfamiliar or conflicting stories. The inspected paper also
identifies language-to-formal-tool formulation as a continuing challenge. Its historical binary-

coverage, decision loss or banking readiness.Jin et al. (2023)
  CausalDS expands the evaluation object to narrated synthetic mechanisms, data and a
separate observation model. Its inspected results show why symbolic success, uncertainty

61.3     A target error can survive perfect arithmetic
An exact project IV construction illustrates a benchmark-label hazard. Let a randomized
binary instrument Z affect treatment for half the population, the compliers; the other half
never receives treatment. Set Y (0) = 0 for everyone, Y (1) = 1 for compliers and Y (1) = 3 for
never-takers. Treatment equals Z for compliers and zero otherwise. Randomization, exclusion
and monotonicity hold in this construction.
   The observed outcome difference across instrument values is 0.5 and the treatment-probability
difference is 0.5, so the Wald ratio is one. The population ATE is two. Changing the never-
takers’ unobserved treated outcome changes the population ATE without changing the observed
law. A scorer that calls the unrestricted Wald ratio the population ATE would reward a target
substitution. This is a finite counterexample supporting review of generator assumptions, not
a claim that every IV question in a named benchmark uses this construction.
   The same concern applies to trimming, overlap weighting and selected populations. A
language answer can reproduce the right formula for a different target. Evaluation should
therefore compare the population, measure, action, comparator, horizon and units before
comparing the numeric answer. Critical semantic errors deserve their own count, even when a
numeric discrepancy happens to be small in the test mechanism.

68.15.4     Retention classes must remain separate from readable resources

An accepted report root, an unfinished job pin, a reproducibility hold and a custodial hold
have different owners and release conditions. The storage design should record which class
is retaining each object. A completed report may no longer be exposed to an agent while a

an openly distributed method description. Physical deduplication of identical bytes may save
space while their custody descriptors remain different. A release condition on one descriptor
cannot silently release a hold belonging to another owner. The collector should compute the
union of admissible retention obligations, then apply store-specific deletion authority. The
proposal cannot specify actual institutional retention periods or assert that indefinite retention
is lawful. Those are future owner inputs. Its current contribution is to expose the interfaces at
which such inputs change the deletion decision.

their descriptors point to absent input or method identities. Before making restored roots
visible, compute the dependency closure against the restored inventory, validate the recorded
identities and classify missing dependencies. A report with a missing input can be retained
as an incomplete historical artifact while remaining ineligible for a reproducibility claim.

result. A recovery test should deliberately omit one dependency and verify this distinction.
   The resulting prototype protocol is concrete. Freeze an immutable dependency fixture,
declare the root and pin lifecycle, enumerate or inject the publication/collection interleavings,
interrupt a publisher before pin release, attempt stale-token publication, and restore a partial
inventory. Record every admission, rejection, published root and missing dependency. Measure

68.17.1     Identification and refusal in a concrete offer graph

Chapter 75

What the banking studies establish

The preceding design becomes concrete when the same analysis is followed from its intervention
to its financial account. This chapter uses published banking designs to explain which
information is useful, and reproducible synthetic studies to test selected estimators and
interfaces. The simulated populations and cash schedules are deliberately specified. None is
data from JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, HSBC or another institution.


75.1     What banking evidence can transfer
Published estimates arrive with an institutional design and population. In Drechsler et al.
(2017, printed pp.2–4), the deposits-channel argument uses within-bank branch comparisons
across local market concentrations. Bank-specific lending opportunities are controlled through
the comparison; the interpretation relies on a bank being able to transfer funding across its
branches. The subsequent lending analysis compares banks within a county and uses their
deposit-market exposure. Those are different aggregation levels and identifying comparisons.
The design supports a mechanism linking market power, deposit pricing and transmission, not
an effect for any new household retention offer.
   Relationship banking adds endogenous selection. The mover design in Basten and Juelsrud
(2026, Section 4.2.1, equations (6)–(8)) instruments an existing deposit relationship with the
bank’s market share in the household’s former municipality, while controlling current share
in the new location. It investigates stickiness and persistent preferences. The exclusion that
prior market availability affects later borrowing through the relationship remains substantive;
the mover population also limits transfer. This is a useful banking connection to Chapter 3,
not a universally valid instrument recipe.
   For credit supply, the preserved 2006 version of Mian and Khwaja (2006, Section III.B,
equation (5)) compares lenders to the same firm after differencing. Firm effects remove
common borrower-demand shocks. The paper explicitly discusses the multiple-bank population,
anticipated adjustments and bank-specific demand components that might remain correlated
with funding shocks. A same-borrower comparison is therefore much stronger than a pooled
lending regression, while still requiring a defensible residual supply variation.
   The HARP design of Agarwal et al. (2020, printed pp.2–3) compares program exposure
for similar agency and non-agency high-LTV mortgages. It distinguishes selected refinancing
outcomes from program-level differences including take-up. The product lesson is to retain
eligibility, assignment, application, acceptance and closing separately, and evaluate the original
eligible population. A borrower cash-flow gain is not automatically a gain to the bank that
owns or services the old mortgage.

87.1      Value belongs to a decision and an information set
The value of an agent is the change it makes to a feasible decision after including its information,
review and operating costs. Producing a longer report, drawing more edges or executing a
notebook faster can help that decision, but none is the economic target itself. The banking
application must specify the action, baseline workflow, affected population, financial horizon
and loss before comparing systems.
   Let a range over feasible actions and let V (a, θ) be their monetary value under a causal
state θ. An information set I might contain observed data, authenticated design records and
unresolved alternatives. If a justified probability law for θ is available, an expected-value
decision solves
                               a∗ (I) ∈ arg max E{V (a, θ) | I}.
                                               a

This expression is a decision rule conditional on a probability model. A language model’s
self-reported certainty, the frequency of repeated answers, or a fraction of retrieved documents
is not automatically that probability. The distinction matters most when a small change in
the assumed probability changes a lending, servicing or funding action.
   If the evidence instead supports a set Θ(I) without probabilities, the bank needs an explicit
ambiguity policy. For example, a robust rule can maximize inf θ∈Θ(I) V (a, θ). Expected value
and this robust criterion answer different decision questions. The software must preserve the
information needed for either and require the policy to be named. It must not manufacture
a distribution by assigning equal weight to whichever graphs an algorithm happened to
enumerate.

87.4     Four banking journeys require different evidence
For a funding decision, assistance can assemble the rate action, balance units, margin horizon
and policy constraints. It can also flag a mismatch between calendar covariance and an
assumed independent sampling unit. Value arises only if these corrections improve a decision
or avert an error relative to the baseline. A conservative interval wider than the bank’s action
threshold may be scientifically defensible and economically inconclusive.
   For sequential servicing, the central object is a policy adapted to information available at
each decision. The total bill includes state measurement, assignment logging, intervention
delivery and customer outcomes, as well as model calls. An agent can help describe a dynamic
regime, but a static ATE translator cannot evaluate it by substituting a single treatment
indicator. The economic study must compare genuinely feasible policies under common
information and count unsuccessful or unavailable evaluations.
   For discovery followed by intervention, a visually simpler graph can conceal alternative
effects. The useful information is whether additional evidence changes the set of financially
acceptable actions. More accurate recovery of irrelevant edges may have little value for the
selected decision. Conversely, one mistaken orientation near the treatment or outcome can
reverse a policy recommendation. Graph accuracy and decision loss should both be reported,
with neither used as a proxy for the other.
   For trustworthy observations, a language system can reconcile field names and suggest
ownership or inclusion questions. The bank must still establish actual linkage, label and audit
truth. Reviewing a source dictionary may prevent a semantic error; it does not supply sampled
identity labels or independent calendars. The cost of obtaining those scientific inputs belongs
in the information budget, and their absence remains a legitimate stop condition for an effect
calculation.


87.5     A complete cost model
For request i, a prospective accounting identity is

           Ci = Ci,model + Ci,retrieval + Ci,numeric + Ci,review + Ci,data + Ci,operation .

The terms represent distinct resource uses, not independently measured values in the current
project. Model cost includes all calls and retries. Retrieval includes acquisition and any
permitted indexing. Numerical cost includes the complete fit, uncertainty calculation and
serialization. Review includes time spent on wrong, refused and unsupported answers. Data

support.
   A natural-workflow comparison measures the quality and cost of complete workflows. A
matched-budget comparison holds selected resources fixed to study one contribution. The
two can produce different rankings without contradiction. An agent that improves answers by
using more expert review may be valuable, but the result is about the combined workflow. It
does not isolate autonomous reasoning quality.
   The experimental unit must also match the cost and outcome calculation. Repeated prompts
about one mechanism are correlated observations within that mechanism. Counting them
as independent bank decisions understates uncertainty and can exaggerate the amount of
evidence. Error bars for incremental value should use the prespecified mechanism or decision
clusters and retain the paired comparison wherever both workflows face the same problem.

Chapter 88

The investment case and its
alternatives

A common causal product earns its cost by improving repeated scientific work. The appropriate
alternative is a capable quantitative team composing the same open-source methods with
ordinary engineering, documentation and review. Such a team can produce good causal studies
without a new platform. The investment question is whether the shared representations and
qualified operations reduce the cost of equivalent work or enable additional valuable decisions,
after construction, support and adoption are included.


88.1      Define equivalent output before pricing reuse
An equivalent completed study has a reconstructed population and observation clock, a clear
intervention and financial outcome, a defensible identification argument or useful nonidentifi-
cation conclusion, appropriate inference, reproducible artifacts and review. The comparison
must include negative results. An early finding that a requested target is unidentified can save
substantial work; forcing every study to return a numerical effect would reward scientific error.
  The proposed service would help analysts reconstruct what was known when a decision was
made, keep the population and outcome consistent, document why an effect is identified, and
carry uncertainty into the financial comparison. It would reuse existing numerical methods
where their assumptions fit. Each business would still specify its products, costs and constraints.
A mortgage policy, an individual collections decision and a macroeconomic shock need not
share the same economic quantity of interest.
   Comparator costs should include the same required outputs and review quality. Reduced
effort from omitting uncertainty or failing to investigate weak support is not a saving. Con-
versely, building a generic model interface for a one-off study can cost more than a small,
clearly documented expert analysis. The portfolio of genuinely repeated requests, not the total
number of database rows, determines the economic opportunity.

88.6     An independent offering must price institutional variation
An independent product can distribute the scientific core as an embedded library and provide
the three workspaces with deployment, support and upgrade services. A design-partner
engagement is a plausible first commercial form: a named study portfolio, paid integration

sales-cycle or customer-retention estimate is established by this proposal.

customizations remain owned by the customer.

scientific layer tied to named repeated decisions. Its expansion should follow measured reuse

Chapter 89

When a bank experiment becomes
worth its cost

89.1     The evidence needed before investing
The investment decision requires a comparison of feasible alternatives, their costs and the
evidence for their expected benefits. Reproducible public and simulated studies can support
that comparison within their stated assumptions. A restricted result may justify a narrow
prototype while leaving broader research questions unresolved.
   Public evidence informs three choices: which existing methods to reuse, where language
assistance is useful, and how analysts should conduct a reproducible study. A dataset can
reveal a timing error or support a controlled comparison without settling every question about
those choices.
   Later bank qualification asks a different question: do the assumptions, precision, workflows
and economics hold for a specific institution? That stage may need genuine offer histories,
logging information, independent labels, owner-reviewed semantics, production constraints
and prospective outcomes. The relevant input should be requested only for the capability and
target population under test. An internal bank pilot is not required to demonstrate that the
proposal has a reproducible public baseline and a sensible experiment design.

Let d ∈ {stop, build, pilot} denote an investment choice, θ unresolved technical and economic
conditions, and U (d, θ) its net value. Let E denote current public/prototype evidence and Z

sample information is

full incremental cost, including data preparation, customer burden where relevant, security
review, labeling, analyst time and opportunity cost. This is a project decision framework, not

89.3     Conditions for moving to expensive evidence
An actionable next-experiment recommendation specifies the proposed intervention, target
population, candidate baseline, decision-loss threshold and the smallest information addition
that could alter the decision. It also states an explicit stop or redesign outcome. A bank
experiment should not be recommended merely to fill a missing-evidence cell.
   For funding, the missing addition may be time-stamped offers and an assignment mechanism,
not more aggregate deposits. For servicing, it may be feasible-action and hardship information
at each decision time. For discovery, it may be one feasible intervention that distinguishes
competing graphs. For observation construction, it may be a representative independently
resolved sample of uncertain identities with known inclusion probabilities. Public data help
design each measurement and estimate its computational burden, while the bank supplies the
later population-specific truth.
  Authentic institutional approval, independent acceptance, actual workflow prices and pro-
duction service levels remain future qualification requirements. A proposal can be complete
without them. It cannot honestly claim those outcomes without obtaining them.

The survey has established a usable portfolio and a concrete public-data acquisition/profile,
not six executed new studies. Current-source web snapshots still need exact data-file vintages
for future analyses. Several benchmark datasets need data-specific rights checks. Mortgage
histories require registration and use review; the HMDA publication endpoint was inaccessible
in this run. The Criteo license is a material constraint for a commercial proposal. These affect
individual options; project-generated alternatives and the already local public data keep the
prototype work moving.
   No inspected public financial source supplies the complete logged bank servicing action
process or independent real-bank linkage truth. That finding is limited to the surveyed sources.
It calls for transparent semi-synthetic experiments now and a justified targeted measurement
later, rather than a claim of impossibility. Public reasoning benchmarks also cannot establish a
live agent’s quality without an actual controlled run, nor resolve the business owner’s normative
choice of outcome and feasible intervention.
