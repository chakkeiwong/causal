3.2    Assumptions

Standard approaches to causal discovery rely on strong assumptions such as Causal Markov, faith-
fulness, and observability, which we discuss in Appendix B.1. However, rather than resting our
method on these formal assumptions that are often violated in practical settings [Montagna et al.,
2023], we focus on weaker assumptions which we can reasonably expect to hold in practice, moti-
vating an expert-guided ensembling approach for causal discovery. Given these assumptions and the
deliberate involvement of an (imperfect) expert, we correspondingly rest our method on empirical
validation rather than formal guarantees of performance in the infinite data limit.

Assumption 5 (Query value). The expected value of querying an edge is positively correlated with

3.3    Objective

Given a set of learned graphs G = {G1 , . . . , GM } and a limited query budget B for accessing an
imperfect expert, the goal is to learn a graph Ĝ that is as close as possible to the unknown ground-
truth graph G⋆ .
