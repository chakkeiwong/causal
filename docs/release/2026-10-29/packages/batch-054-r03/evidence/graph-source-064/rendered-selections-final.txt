Which tasks merit language
assistance?

Assistance should be selected by the task it improves. A system that answers questions about a
supplied causal mechanism has not thereby shown that it can discover a defensible mechanism
for a new bank. The evidence synthesis in Section 27.1 and the detailed studies support a
restricted comparison, with a questionnaire and ordinary search as serious alternatives. They
do not establish a measured saving for this proposed product.


27.1      Mechanisms, timing and the interpretation of a diagram
A retention request can mean an invitation, acceptance, a price change or a repeated contact
rule. The distinction determines the population and intervention before any graph is evaluated.
Question clarification and graph critique are plausible assisted tasks in the inspected literature.
(Kıcıman et al., 2023; Zheng, 2026)
   Each proposed edge needs an economic interpretation, an information date and supporting
evidence. A graph with unresolved directions can already guide a decision when every credible
member implies the same action. Conversely, one wrong orientation can reverse a financial
sign even when most edges are correct. The mixed LACR retrieval results support testing the
retrieval choice itself. (Zhang et al., 2024b)
   Formal reasoning under a supplied mechanism and eliciting the mechanism of a new bank are
different tasks. CLadder and causal-learn+ motivate different comparisons on that distinction.
In a repeated policy, later observations cannot silently become pretreatment adjustment
variables. An unsupported calculation calls for an explanation of the missing information or a
revised question. (Jin et al., 2023; Zheng, 2026)

27.4     What repeated readings add to measurement
A bank may ask an assistant to recover financial distress from customer notes before assessing
the effect of a servicing action. Reading the same notes several times is attractive because
disagreement is easy to observe. Agreement can be less informative: several readers may share
the same omission. The distinction affects whether text can help control confounding. The
literature on text-based causal adjustment identifies measurement error, collider information
and post-treatment content as separate problems. (Keith et al., 2020)

27.6      What causal reasoning benchmarks establish
A banking analyst asks several different questions of an assistant. Can it translate a request for
higher deposits into an intervention and a measurable target? Can it distinguish information
known before an offer from information created by the offer? Can it explain which arrows in a
diagram are assumptions, and recognise when several diagrams fit the same observations? A
correct answer to a narrative puzzle is useful evidence about some of these abilities, but it
does not establish all of them.


27.6.1    Explanations deserve their own test

Wang and Shen examine three constructed scenarios: coin operations, interrupted file downloads
and a hypothetical vaccination calculation. Four variants of each scenario and three prompting
approaches give 36 prompt conditions. Their scoring distinguishes a correct result and
explanation, a flawed explanation, and an incorrect result. This is valuable because a correct
number can accompany a mistaken economic argument. The study reports mixed changes
from examples and guided reasoning, rather than a uniformly helpful prompting intervention.
(Wang and Shen, 2024)
   The terms used in a benchmark also need scrutiny. The paper calls reasoning from an
observed effect to a cause actual causality. That task description does not, by itself, specify a
formal criterion for an individual’s actual cause. Its smallpox case asks which source produces
more deaths in a stipulated population. With the given numbers, vaccination reactions
produce 990,000(0.01)(0.01) = 99 deaths, while disease among unvaccinated people produces
10,000(0.02)(0.20) = 40. Those population counts differ from the per-person risks, 0.0001
and 0.004. Confusing a total with a risk is an estimand error; the arithmetic alone does not
establish a general Simpson-reversal mechanism. These are deductions from the paper’s stated
example, not new medical evidence.
   The banking analogue is direct. A large customer group can contribute more defaults
while offering a smaller default risk per account. A deposit offer can increase gross balances
without increasing net contribution. An assistant should state the denominator and economic
target before presenting the answer. The paper’s prompts provide useful stress cases for that
distinction. The small scenario set and model configurations do not support a current ranking
of assistants for banking work. We have inspected the published methods and results; we have
not reproduced its model calls or inspected every supplementary response.

27.6.2    Scale does not remove uncertainty about the answer key

MDC-Bench broadens the comparison to 8,422 questions across seven disciplines, including
business, economics and management. Its tasks distinguish reasoning, intervention, decision
and counterfactual questions. Questions are constructed from causal narratives extracted from
preprints, with model-generated alternatives and a manual sampling check. The published
protocol identifies model versions, prompt templates, temperature and output limits. (Wang
et al., 2026, Sections 2–3 and Appendix A)
   Its harder condition requires two ranked answers instead of one. For example, the reported
Claude thinking configuration has average accuracy 84.56% in the basic condition and 59.25% in
the harder condition. These are different response requirements on the same benchmark; their
difference is not a measured deterioration in one fixed banking task. The benchmark is useful
for comparing errors by causal structure and question type, provided its answer-generation
assumptions remain visible.
   The detailed prompts reveal an important distinction. Reverse reasoning may use necessity
and sufficiency, or otherwise select an abductive explanation judged probable from background
knowledge. Intervention questions construct an altered causal narrative; the answer alternatives
are ranked by their judged plausibility. These procedures do not supply a known structural
equation system, a randomised treatment effect or an identified posterior over causal diagrams.
Appendix A’s sampling review helps detect poor questions but does not turn model-generated
causal statements into independent economic truth. The authors also discuss weak long-chain
relations and distractors that may be too easy. (Wang et al., 2026, Limitations; Tables 28–29
and 39–40)


27.6.3    An agent architecture still needs an identified effect

Chen combines language models, diffusion models and interacting agents in a study of climate-
policy sentiment. Section 6 describes 43,943 tweets and comparisons with topic, language
and diffusion baselines. It reports F1, AUC, causal-chain precision, counterfactual error and
simulated intervention outcomes. Table 4 removes individual components to examine their
contribution. These are reported results of the paper; the model was not reproduced locally.
(Chen, 2025, Sections 5.6 and 6; Tables 1–4)
   The causal interpretation needs more than those comparisons. Equation (16) factorises a
joint distribution according to a graph. Equation (17) defines a conditional treatment contrast.
Neither statement alone identifies that contrast from the observed tweets. Equation (19)
compares predicted and reference counterfactual outcomes, but its accompanying text allows
the reference to come from annotations or simulated baselines. Section 6.1 does not supply
enough detail to reconstruct an independently identified counterfactual for each tweet. F1 and
AUC assess labelled prediction; they do not repair that missing identification argument.
   The useful proposal lesson is to separate an architecture from the evidence for a decision. A
bank may test whether a language model helps extract a policy variable or whether a simulator
represents a declared mechanism. It still needs a defensible treatment assignment, observation
model and counterfactual comparison before attributing a financial gain to the policy. The
reported architecture is a research lead. Its stated improvements are not evidence that the
proposed banking service already delivers causal or commercial benefits.

27.6.4    A useful banking comparison

The proposal should use such benchmarks to diagnose formulation and reasoning failures, then
add tasks whose consequences can be checked independently. A compact questionnaire and an
executable calculation are necessary comparators. For a deposit question, all methods should
receive the same decision date, eligibility rule, alternative offer and contribution definition.
For servicing, all should see the same observed history and reasons for dropout. For a diagram,
an admissible set should remain admissible when the observations cannot orient an edge.
   The resulting assessment has distinct outcomes: whether the target is correct, whether a
proposed adjustment is justified, whether an unknown effect remains unknown, whether the
numerical calculation is correct, and how much analyst time the assistance saves. Independent
adjudication is needed for open-ended economic formulations. Generated cases with known
mechanisms can test specific errors before an expensive bank exercise. The public experimental,
servicing and linkage studies in this volume provide numerical cases of that kind, but no live
assistant has yet been evaluated on them.
  The inspected papers therefore sharpen the proposed experiment rather than establishing
a benefit from the proposed service. Repeated agreement among prompts is especially weak
evidence when their errors share a source, as the repeated-measurement calculation shows.
The useful next comparison measures consequential mistakes and time on held-out tasks, with
human adjudication and a fixed cost allowance.


27.7     A positive workflow result and its limits
The retained ORCA study gives a more direct reason to test assistance in a completed
workflow. Twelve participants used a counterbalanced within-subjects design; the baseline
assistant already had tools, step gates and minimal state. The paper’s Figure 5 reports total
task durations of 1,395 seconds for the baseline and 1,107 seconds for ORCA, a reported
reduction of about 21%. Table 1 reports completion rates 0.75 and 0.83. This is positive
published evidence for a structured workflow on the study’s relational, generated causal tasks.
It is not a measured benefit for this banking proposal. (Chung et al., 2026, pp. 3–5)
   The interpretation changes when the outcome definitions and all attempted analyses are
retained. Completion means producing an ATE, regardless of accuracy; discovery success
requires the treatment and outcome nodes to be present. The paper’s confidence-interval
metric is described as an estimated ATE falling within a ground-truth interval, so its reported
0.083 and 0.50 must not be presented as conventional repeated-sample confidence coverage.
The full-data condition comparison for absolute ATE error has p = 0.321; after excluding
configuration-error outliers, the reported OLS comparison has p = 0.020. Both belong in an
assessment of completed work because configuration errors are among the failures a service is
supposed to avoid. The abstract’s claimed 42-percentage-point completion gain also differs
from the displayed 0.75-to-0.83 comparison; this synthesis uses the stated table comparison
rather than reconciling that discrepancy by invention. (Chung et al., 2026, pp. 1, 5, 17–18)
   The proposal should therefore test whether shared intermediate results and question clari-
fication save full effort while preserving scientific quality. It should retain all configuration
failures and report separately the time spent by the analyst, tool execution and correction. The
external study supports that experiment’s plausibility. Its small participant sample, specified
tasks, outcome definitions and analysis exclusions prevent a claim that the same benefit is
established across bank studies.

27.8      Identification, execution and reporting
CLadder evaluates formal causal questions under constructed mechanisms; its reported results
concern historical models and that supplied task. It does not measure elicitation of a new
bank mechanism. (Jin et al., 2023) For identification, compare assistance with a defined
formal procedure, requiring the assistant to preserve both the answer and its conditions. An
appropriate refusal must explain the missing restriction, not simply return an empty result.
The benchmark literature and the retained mechanism-clustering critiques give reasons to keep
failed cases and uncertainty denominators in the evaluation.
  For execution, the existing local interface study shows equality for four constructed requests,
not model-driven analysis quality. A scripted workflow is the credible baseline. Compare any
assistant on request construction, faithful tool use, errors detected and correction effort. Do
not give it credit for a calculation performed by the formal tool while omitting the costs of
choosing or correcting the request.
   For reporting, a deterministic template is a strong comparator because it can already state
the estimand, assumptions, interval and scope. The assistant’s proposed value is explaining
why the result changes a decision or why it cannot yet support one. Check that explanation
against the actual result and the retained sources. A report that turns a set crossing zero into
a positive recommendation fails even if its prose is persuasive. No local live-model evaluation
establishes an incremental benefit for this reporting task.
   The preferred first assisted task is therefore question clarification with evidence retrieval
and explicit alternatives, evaluated against the questionnaire. Identification and numerical
results remain delegated to defined procedures. Expansion to autonomous discovery, variable
construction or recommendations requires task-specific evidence. The frontier evidence includes
the three additional inspected originals discussed in Section 27.6. The preceding inventory
recorded 85 inspected and 28 unread entries. The financing-and-exports and epistemology
originals now developed in Section 65.11.4 brought the preceding edition to 87 inspected and
26 unread. The financing study supports a bounded extraction and explanation comparison;
its scores do not identify a lending effect. The epistemology discussion supplies no separate
intervention estimate. These readings sharpen the limits of assistance without establishing live
banking benefit. The remaining omissions still limit broad claims. A live comparison requires
a fixed model version, held-out questions, matched information, independent adjudication and
a budget. (Li, 2025; Mugleston et al., 2025)
   The end-to-end workflow in Section 69.30 adds a further primary comparison. It turns a
prompt-based risk score into sampled graphs and fitted effect intervals, but its uncertainty
interpretation needs the additional conditions developed there. A separate thesis by Ait Lhouss

unresolved denominators prevent using its reported review coverage or productivity results

69.28.5     A public and generated prototype with a decision criterion

A useful first prototype has two stages. Deterministic semantic fixtures check missing answers,
unsupported methods, token variants, exact threshold boundaries, graph endpoint preservation,
cycles, edge-versus-path conversion and request/result identity. These tests are cheap and
have known expected answers. They establish an adapter contract; they do not estimate live
model quality. Only then should the team profile a matched discovery experiment and register
its final design.
  The experiment should split by mechanism family. Repeated numerical samples, variable
names, retrieved packets and prompts belong inside a mechanism’s cluster. The Gaussian
pair above is a compulsory ambiguity case, accompanied by causally sufficient, non-Gaussian
and latent cases with explicit clocks. Generator truth and effect oracles remain outside the
prompts. Natural descriptions, neutral renamed descriptions and deliberately misleading
descriptions are crossed with no retrieval, applicable original evidence, wrong-domain evidence
and contradiction. Removing meaningful definitions changes the information supplied, so the
comparison must say when it tests robustness and when it tests the value of information.
  Each language arm needs the same numerical inputs and a declared comparator: a dictio-
nary/clock baseline; PC or FCI under the appropriate assumptions; score or non-Gaussian
methods within their qualified regimes; language-only proposals; and soft or hard guidance
with declared strength and refusal rules. Final graphs alone are insufficient. Record class
and endpoint retention, invalid outputs, valid abstentions, conditional effect-set retention and
width, and downstream action regret under known truth. Include every requested case in the
denominator, including parse failures, timeouts, empty outputs and conflicting constraints.
Measure total calls, tokens, retries, numerical resources, latency and adjudication effort.
   Before final runs, freeze versions, rights, splits, targets, sample sizes, replication counts,
tolerances, primary paired comparisons and multiplicity treatment. Development profiling
may set resource caps; it cannot become an unannounced final experiment. Prompt variants
and prior strengths must not be selected on the final mechanisms. An optional live model
experiment needs the chosen endpoint’s actual revision and measured costs, but none of these
design steps requires bank data or a paid experiment now.
   The banking example supplies a transparent economic threshold. Stipulate USD 200 per
outcome unit and USD 60 for the unit offer increase. Net values are USD 40 in the direct-
effect world and minus USD 60 in the latent world. If a planner assigns probability p to the
former, the expected net action value is 100p − 60. Because the observed likelihood is the
same, additional observational records alone cannot update those odds within this two-world
comparison. At p = 0.9 the expected value is USD 30, while perfect knowledge would produce
USD 36 by taking the action only in the favorable world. Thus USD 6 per decision is an
upper bound on the expected value of any information experiment under those assumptions.
At p = 0.5 it is USD 20. A real noisy experiment supplies less information and incurs costs;
neither number is bank ROI or an authorized spending budget. (Causal banking proposal,
2026x)

69.29      Expert-guided graph ensembles and their intervention
           limits
A bank building a diagram from several discovery algorithms will often face agreement about
a relationship but disagreement about its direction. The methods may also miss different
relationships because they use different scores, tests and structural restrictions. Asking a
domain expert to resolve selected disagreements is a plausible use of an assistant. The design
question is what the expert is being asked, how its answer changes the graph, and whether the
resulting graph supports the bank’s intervention query.
   Tench and Demeester propose a dynamic expert-guided ensemble that distinguishes adjacency
from orientation. It counts how many component graphs contain any edge between a pair,
considers pairs in descending frequency, and adds directed edges subject to an acyclicity check.
A lower adjacency threshold determines which pairs are eligible. For eligible pairs without a
strict majority, it asks the expert whether a plausible causal relationship exists. A separate
threshold controls when direction votes suffice and when expert orientation is requested. The
procedure uses equal component weights and a finite query budget. This is greedy graph
construction; the term model averaging does not establish a Bayesian posterior over causal
models. (Tench and Demeester, 2026)


69.29.1     An expert’s causal ordering is not a direct edge

The source deliberately permits an expert to answer about a direct or indirect causal path.
This is weaker than knowing whether two nodes are adjacent in the full graph. In a stipulated
offer–uptake–balance chain O → U → Y , changing the offer may affect balance through uptake
even though O → Y is not a direct edge. An expert can answer the path question correctly
while accepting a spurious direct connection proposed by a component method. The paper
recognizes this distinction in its simulated expert and compares it with an expert that knows
direct edges. (Tench and Demeester, 2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026al)
   The proposed bank interface should therefore carry the proposition being elicited: adjacency,
ancestry, orientation conditional on adjacency, or a temporal exclusion. It should also permit
insufficient knowledge. A reply about plausible ancestry cannot silently become proof of a
direct edge, and a forced binary answer cannot represent every legitimate uncertainty state.
The set of variables matters: marginalizing uptake changes the observed-variable graph even
when the underlying mechanism is unchanged.
  The method’s output is a DAG. Its component comparison includes FCI, while its noisy
benchmarks can contain latent confounding. A directed output cannot by itself retain every
bidirected or unresolved endpoint in a latent graph. Before an adapter is proposed, the
conversion from each component’s graph type must be explicit, with unsupported endpoints
preserved or refused. Acyclicity checks alone do not establish compatibility with an input

69.29.3    The reported evidence is informative and conditional

The main experiments use seven benchmark network structures with synthetic categorical
samples, clean and corrupted versions, four sample sizes from 100 to 100,000, and twenty
column orderings. The principal simulated expert is correct on 70% of existence questions and
90% of orientation questions. Thresholds of zero for adjacency and 0.9 for orientation, with a
budget of twice the node count, were selected using separate randomly generated networks.
The paper reports broad gains in its graph scores, alongside a precision–recall tradeoff and
varying comparison denominators when algorithms fail or return undefined precision. These
are reported experiments; they are not newly reproduced results in this proposal. (Tench and
Demeester, 2026)
   The appendix tests a narrower real-model setting on five clean networks, using gpt-5-nano-
2025-08-07 and excluding Pathfinder because its variable names lack the needed semantics
and Formed because many edges represent diagnostic rather than causal relations. Expert

69.29.4     Resolve the printed query contract before reuse

The prose says that a fixed budget bounds queries and exhausted decisions fall back to majority

counter increment lies inside the branch reached only when the expert accepts the connection.

makes three expert calls while the printed counter remains zero. Separately, when an accepted
adjacency has two legal directions, neither reaches the orientation threshold, and no budget
remains, the printed conditional chain has no final branch adding the majority direction. An
example with direction votes 3/4 and 1/4, threshold 9/10 and budget zero therefore leaves the
edge unassigned under the printed chain. (Causal banking proposal project, 2026al)

two argument-order requests, account for retries and invalid answers, and define the behavior

snapshot and relevant settings. Otherwise a cost-saving cache can silently reuse evidence for
a different scientific question. The method and data lead in the paper are useful acquisition
routes; package names and reported license labels are not sufficient permission or provenance
for direct adoption.

69.30      When an end-to-end graph workflow still lacks calibrated

comparisons. Its availability permits a more precise assessment than either accepting the
integration as calibrated or dismissing it merely because language models propose the graphs.

69.30.1     A success ceiling gives an error floor

Write the Bernoulli relative entropy, in nats, as
                                        p               1−p
                      d(p, q) = p log     + (1 − p) log     ,       0 < q < 1.

The thesis’s equations (14)–(16) average clipped positive log-probability lifts for the selected
answer under original and masked prompts. It then uses this quantity as an information
budget in a Bernoulli inequality and defines the largest compatible success probability. For
any stipulated nonnegative budget ∆, the logical implication of that inequality is

                 d(p, q) ≤ ∆,       pmax (∆, q) = sup{u ∈ [0, 1] : d(u, q) ≤ ∆},
                                                                                          (69.12)
                                p ≤ pmax (∆, q)    =⇒     1 − p ≥ 1 − pmax (∆, q).

The complement is therefore a lower bound on error under this premise. The thesis calls it an
upper bound and subsequently uses it to set graph-sampling probabilities. That interpretation
does not follow from the displayed inequality. With q = .1 and ∆ = .5, the upper endpoint is
approximately .49505, giving complement .50495. Yet p = .1 satisfies the inequality and has
error .9. This exact counterexample concerns the direction of the bound; it does not assert
that every estimated graph in the thesis is wrong. (Molero Pérez, 2026)
   There is a further distinction between the score and the premise. Evaluating a clipped
log-lift at one selected answer does not generally evaluate an expectation over the full answer
distribution. Nor does masking a prompt establish the probability that its causal assertion is
true in the intended bank population. A separate calibration argument is needed before the
score can stand for the information budget or the causal correctness probability in (69.12).
Averaging repeated calls reduces some Monte Carlo variation conditional on the response
mechanism; it cannot by itself supply either missing interpretation.


69.30.2     The information inequality remains useful in its stated direction

The thesis cites the 2025 version of Chlon and colleagues’ information-budget paper. Its
Theorem 5 follows from convexity and reduction to an event. Let P be a reference distribution,
Sk predictive distributions, and S̄ = Ek Sk their mixture. For an event A, put p = P (A) and
q = S̄(A). Under absolute continuity and finite expected relative entropy,

                                Ek KL(P ∥Sk ) ≥ KL(P ∥S̄) ≥ d(p, q).                      (69.13)

The first step is Jensen’s inequality in the second distribution; the second is the information
lost when the outcome is reduced to membership of A. Equality for the entire chain requires
equality in both steps. Sufficiency of the event for distinguishing P from the mixture addresses
the second step; it does not eliminate a positive mixture Jensen gap. (Chlon et al., 2025, 2026)
   A necessary information requirement is not a success guarantee. Consider the project’s
four-outcome construction

            S = (.05, .05, .001, .899),      P = (.05, .05, .899, .001),    A = {1, 2}.

Use the same S for every prompt. Both event probabilities are .1, but

                   KL(P ∥S) = .898 log 899 ≃ 6.10755 > d(.95, .1) ≃ 1.99421.

The extra divergence lies within the failure event. Exceeding the information requirement
associated with a target success probability of .95 has not raised actual reference-event

69.30.3     Independent edge draws need a coherent graph law

For an accepted edge the thesis uses one minus the risk score; for a rejected edge it uses the risk
score. It then samples edges independently and applies topological sorting. Sorting represents
an already acyclic graph in an appropriate order; it cannot make every independently drawn
graph acyclic. The treatment of a failed sort is part of the probability law. The following
example diagnoses the general construction and does not claim that the thesis’s particular
draws contained a cycle. (Molero Pérez, 2026)
  Suppose the only possible arrows are A → B, B → C and C → A, each assigned probability
4/5. Under independent draws,

The conditional numerator is (4/5)[1 − (4/5)2 ] and the denominator is 1 − (4/5)3 . Rejecting
cyclic draws changes the edge probabilities; deleting an edge instead creates another distribution
determined by the deletion rule. Indeed, no distribution supported on DAGs can retain all
three proposed marginals: a DAG contains at most two of these arrows, whereas their proposed
expected count is 12/5. This strengthens the majority-cycle example in Section 69.29.2:
structural validity and marginal probability interpretation cannot be checked independently.
   A usable alternative defines a joint law on an explicitly admissible model class, records its
prior and likelihood interpretation, and tests sensitivity to that class. When those probabilities
cannot be justified, retain compatible graphs and propagate their effect sets instead. Neither
route assumes that a linguistic probability score is already a posterior over banking mechanisms.


69.30.4     An interval comparison needs an intervention reference

The thesis trains four groups of 100 flow models: an expert reference graph, the sampled
language-derived graphs, the expert graph’s ordering without its adjacency restrictions, and
random orderings without those restrictions. Within each group, the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles of the fitted effect estimates define an interval. The reported interval-overlap means
are .723, .712 and .301 for the last three groups compared with the fitted expert-graph group.
The first two differ by .011. That contrast describes these fitted model intervals; the table does
not provide a paired uncertainty interval for the difference. It is not evidence that language
assistance generally outperforms knowledge of the causal ordering. (Molero Pérez, 2026)

   Agreement with a fitted expert reference is useful when the task is to reconstruct that
reference. It is not observed intervention truth. If two methods return the identical interval
[.5, .7] while the true effect is zero, their interval overlap is one and both miss the effect. More
generally, percentiles across graph choices and training runs describe variation under that
procedure. A 95% repeated-sampling coverage claim needs a defined sampling experiment, valid
treatment of graph uncertainty and an identified target. A calibrated Bayesian interpretation
instead needs a justified joint posterior and corresponding model assumptions. Repetition
alone supplies neither interpretation.
   The numerical comparator also needs matched information and a suitable test. The
thesis specifies chi-square conditional-independence tests for its PC baseline while prioritizing
continuous clinical variables. Its language workflow additionally draws on outside knowledge.
A fair follow-up would resolve the continuous-variable testing and coding choices, compare
methods given the same admissible knowledge, and keep a numerical-only arm. The thesis
itself acknowledges calibration problems, lost valid edges and the absence of hidden-confounder
handling. Those limitations belong alongside the favorable reported scores. (Molero Pérez,
2026)
   For the bank, the investment conclusion follows from the retained example in Section 69.29.5.
Its observationally identical mechanisms imply margins of USD 20 and USD −40 under
the same stated valuation and cost. A narrow distribution of repeated fits cannot resolve
that disagreement. Before releasing a financial recommendation, the workflow must state
the decision-time information that excludes a mechanism, or carry the ambiguity into the
recommendation. Graph proposals, retrieval and flexible conditional models remain candidate
components. Their integration deserves evaluation against an expert-reviewed graph, a
numerical discovery route and an ambiguity-preserving analysis, with withheld mechanisms,
interval coverage, decision loss and complete review cost assessed separately.


69.31      Domain knowledge as evidence for a causal graph
A collections manager considering a payment holiday needs to distinguish a plausible story
from an intervention that improves customer outcomes. A language model may suggest that
financial strain affects delinquency, that servicing changes financial strain, and that earlier
delinquency affects the servicing offer. Each statement needs a population, a time index,
a variable definition and an evidential basis. Joining the three statements without those
qualifications can create a cycle whose meaning is unclear. A retrieval system can help locate
the underlying evidence, but a retrieved sentence does not by itself establish an invariant
structural mechanism. Two recent clinical studies make the promise and the limitations of
this assistance concrete.
