17.1      A temporal prediction is not yet a shock intervention
In treasury and deposit management, a bank may ask how a policy-rate shock changes balances
over the next year. In collections it may ask how repeated assistance changes repayment
along a customer trajectory. Both have time indices, but the interventions differ. A structural
macroeconomic shock perturbs one innovation while the system’s other mechanisms continue
to respond. A sustained policy replaces a sequence of decision rules. A forecast conditions on
observed historical values. These are different operations on a dynamic model.
   Let Xt be a vector of observed series and let Ft be their observed past. Granger noncausality
of series X j for X i is a restriction saying that removing the past of X j from the conditioning
information does not change the conditional law, or in its mean version the conditional mean,
of Xti . It is a property of a specified information set. It acquires an intervention interpretation
only under extra conditions on hidden causes, timing, instantaneous effects and structural
innovations (Peters et al., 2017, Chapter 10, Section 10.3).
   For a simple counterexample, let independent Ut cause Xt = Ut + ηt and Yt+1 = Ut + νt+1 ,
with independent noise. The past of X helps predict Y even though intervening on the
measurement Xt leaves Ut and Yt+1 unchanged. Conversely, an entirely instantaneous effect of
Xt on Yt with independent observations over time need not generate any predictive benefit
from lagged X. Neither failure is repaired by a more accurate nonlinear prediction model.

17.3.1    Direct projections and VAR propagation

An LP-IV estimates a separate instrumented regression for each horizon. A proxy-SVAR first
recovers reduced innovations and dynamics, then identifies a structural impact column from
instrument–innovation covariances and propagates it. Under suitable invertibility, structural
shocks are recoverable from present and past observed innovations. That condition is additional
information about the observation system. The direct LP-IV argument above can identify a
response without a finite-order invertible VAR, but its lead/lag exclusion is strong. Under
different control-variable conditions the requirements of the two approaches can coincide (Stock
and Watson, 2018, Sections 2–3).
   A scalar illustration shows what noninvertibility loses. Let Xt = εt + θεt−1 with Gaussian
white noise of variance σ 2 and |θ| > 1. Its autocovariances are identical to those of the
invertible representation with coefficient 1/θ and innovation variance θ2 σ 2 : both have variance
(1 + θ2 )σ 2 and lag-one covariance θσ 2 . Higher-lag covariances vanish, so the Gaussian laws
agree. Their unit-impact structural responses at horizon one are nevertheless θ and 1/θ.
Choosing the invertible likelihood representation is a normalization of observable prediction;
calling its innovation the economic shock needs justification.
   LP estimators have more freedom across horizons; VAR propagation imposes restrictions
through powers of common lag matrices. This creates an estimation bias/variance comparison
after identification, not a theorem that direct projections always answer a causal question or
that VARs always have lower error. The constructions and inference alternatives are reviewed
by Jordà and Taylor (2024, Sections 2 and 6). Moreover, identifying the normalized response
does not automatically identify the shock’s variance, every historical shock realization, or its
share of forecast-error variance. Those require additional recoverability and normalization
information (Stock and Watson, 2018, Section 2.5).

17.6.3     Weak instruments require a different result object

Do not divide by a weak normalization to construct the inferential pivot. For a proposed
response b, test the zero mean of Utj − bDt . Write SU U , SU D , SDD for the corresponding entries
of the Bartlett matrix of the centered joint products (Ut , Dt ). Accept b if

                            n(Ūj − bD̄)2 ≤ c2 (SU U − 2bSU D + b2 SDD ).                              (17.9)

This is one quadratic inequality Ab2 + Bb + C ≤ 0, with A = nD̄2 − c2 SDD , B = −2nŪj D̄ +
2c2 SU D , and C = nŪj2 − c2 SU U . Its solution can be an interval, two unbounded rays, the
whole line, an empty set, or a degenerate singleton/half-line. These cases must survive the
interface. At the true b, the score CLT does not require dividing by q, and remains applicable
along bounded weak-instrument sequences with a nondegenerate residual-score variance and
fixed stability margin. Bonferroni inversion across coordinates yields simultaneous coverage of
the true response vector. At q = 0 the normalized causal response is not identified by these
moments; a statistical set cannot repair that. No first-stage threshold is used to switch silently
between guarantees.

17.11.3    Choosing a route for a bank’s actual clock and panel

separately identified structural target. An error-correction model is appropriate when levels
have economically justified cointegrating restrictions; estimating rank and deterministic trends

answer change effects, with level responses reconstructed by cumulation under the maintained

18.3      Equilibrium and an intervention on a feedback rule
The steady balance response to a bank’s pricing rule differs from its response to a clamped
offered rate. For a small illustrative model, let x denote scaled deposits and r a rate deviation:
                  ẋ = −x + br + u,         ṙ = −κ(r − r0 + cx),          b, c, κ > 0.
The customer disturbance u is constant for a trajectory. The bank lowers its incentive as
deposits increase; r0 is the intercept of that rule. The system matrix has negative trace −(1+κ)
and positive determinant κ(1 + bc), so it converges to its unique equilibrium:
                                       br0 + u

Changing the rule intercept has derivative ∂x∗ /∂r0 = b/(1 + bc). Fixing r(t) = r̄ removes
its feedback equation and instead yields x∗ = br̄ + u, with derivative b. If only u varies

negative fitted rate–balance relation is compatible with a positive structural rate response in

   This calculation requires convergence under the relevant intervention. Setting derivatives
to zero gives candidate equilibrium equations; it does not establish that actual paths reach
them. Bongers et al. (2022, Definition 4.9, Theorems 4.11 and 4.18) formalizes equilibration
and its commutation with steady interventions under the stated convergence conditions.
Nonconverging paths, multiple solutions, and selection of only equilibrated units need separate
analysis. A stationary diffusion generally fluctuates forever: an invariant distribution is not a
pathwise equilibrium obtained by setting Brownian forcing to zero.

exposes the feedback distinction but is not a model of an entire banking market. A portfolio
calculation holding competitor rates fixed and a market equilibrium counterfactual must be
different product requests, with different maintained mechanisms.
