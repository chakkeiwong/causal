\section{Estimating heterogeneous effects}
\label{sec:uplift-learners}

Identification supplies a conditional mean contrast, not an estimator of it.
A learner must approximate that contrast from finite, noisy observations.
The methods differ in how errors in the outcome and assignment models
affect the estimated treatment contrast.

The T-learner fits $\hat\mu_a(x)$ to the observed outcomes in each arm and
uses $\hat\tau_T(x)=\hat\mu_1(x)-\hat\mu_0(x)$. Its error is the difference
of the two response-model errors; neither a good response fit nor high
classification accuracy alone establishes a good treatment-effect estimate.
The S-learner instead fits one response function with treatment as a feature.
Shared structure can help, while regularization that suppresses treatment
interactions can hide heterogeneous effects.

One route converts observed outcomes into a response whose conditional
mean is the treatment effect. A treated outcome receives positive weight,
a control outcome negative weight; dividing by the corresponding
assignment probability corrects their different chances of observation.
The transformed response can be very noisy even when its mean is right.

\begin{proposition}[Transformed-outcome reduction]
\label{prop:transformed}
Under conditional exchangeability, consistency and overlap, define
\begin{equation}
Z_i=\frac{W_iY_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}.
\label{eq:transformed-outcome}
\end{equation}
Then $\E[Z\mid X=x]=\tau(x)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional exchangeability gives
$\E[WY\mid X]=e(X)\E[Y(1)\mid X]$ and
$\E[(1-W)Y\mid X]=(1-e(X))\E[Y(0)\mid X]$.
Dividing and subtracting proves the identity. A regression of $Z$ targets
this conditional mean only to the extent that its function class and
estimation procedure recover that regression.
\end{proof}

Small propensities amplify outcome noise. Uplift trees instead form groups
using contrasts between treatment and control outcomes
\citep{rzepakowski2012decision,radcliffe2011real}. Honest causal trees use
separate observations to choose splits and estimate leaf effects
\citep{athey2016recursive}. Causal forests aggregate suitably constructed
trees; their pointwise inference requires the sampling, overlap, regularity
and tree conditions of the relevant analysis
\citep{wager2018causalforests}. Generalized random forests express the local
estimate through weighted moment equations, permitting targets beyond a
mean treatment effect \citep{athey2019grf}. A generic random forest does not
acquire these inferential properties merely by being used twice in a
T-learner. Instrumented and quantile targets require their own identifying
moments and assumptions.

The X-learner of \citet{kunzel2019metalearners} predicts each observed
customer's missing alternative outcome. For a treated customer, subtract
the predicted control outcome from what happened. For a control customer,
subtract what happened from the predicted treated outcome. Fit an effect
regression to each set of these imputed differences, then blend them:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\tilde D_i^1&=Y_i-\hat\mu_0(X_i)\quad(W_i=1),\\
\tilde D_i^0&=\hat\mu_1(X_i)-Y_i\quad(W_i=0),\\
\hat\tau_X(x)&=g(x)\hat\tau_0(x)+(1-g(x))\hat\tau_1(x),
\end{split}
\label{eq:uplift-x}
\end{equation}
where each $\hat\tau_a$ regresses the corresponding imputed effect and
$g(x)=e(x)$ is one suggested blend. When treatment is rare, this blend
places more weight on $\hat\tau_1$, fitted to treated pseudo-effects whose
control response is imputed from the abundant control observations. It does
not place more weight on $\hat\tau_0$ merely because that regression has
more rows. The inspected arXiv version's equations (5)--(9) and Algorithm 3
specify this orientation.

Their Simulation 1 uses one percent treatment and a comparatively simple
CATE against a more complex baseline response. Their balanced designs also
illustrate a complex CATE favoring separate response fits and a zero CATE
favoring shrinkage toward zero. No learner dominates all these designs.
The paper's BART/RF guidance is conditional on those comparisons; it cannot
select a banking default from treatment imbalance alone. Sample size,
function approximation, outcome noise and the downstream contact decision
must also be assessed. The finite-dimensional example below makes those
choices explicit and compares four regression-based learners.

Another route removes the predictable baseline from both outcome and
assignment. The outcome residual asks how the observation differs from its
usual level at those covariates. The assignment residual asks how treatment
differs from its usual probability. Their relation isolates the effect,
with the remaining noise collected in $\varepsilon_i$.

\begin{proposition}[Robinson decomposition and the R-learner]
\label{prop:rlearner}
Under the same identifying assumptions, write $m(x)=\E[Y\mid X=x]$.
Then
\begin{equation}
Y_i-m(X_i)=(W_i-e(X_i))\tau(X_i)+\varepsilon_i,
\qquad \E[\varepsilon_i\mid X_i,W_i]=0.
\label{eq:robinson}
\end{equation}
The population squared residual loss identifies $\tau$ wherever overlap holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $Y=\mu_0(X)+W\tau(X)+\varepsilon$ and use
$m(X)=\mu_0(X)+e(X)\tau(X)$. Subtraction gives
\cref{eq:robinson}. For a candidate $t(x)$, conditional expected squared
loss equals
$\E[\varepsilon^2\mid X=x]+e(x)(1-e(x))(t(x)-\tau(x))^2$,
because the cross term has zero expectation. Its second term is minimized
at the true effect. Under a restricted function class, the minimizer is the
corresponding overlap-weighted approximation.
\end{proof}

\citet[Section 2, equations (1)--(4)]{nie2021quasi} replace $m$ and $e$
by predictions fitted without the observation's fold and minimize this loss
with a chosen complexity penalty. Their quasi-oracle theory studies specified
estimation classes and rate conditions; it is not an unconditional guarantee
for any regression package or tuning rule.

The doubly robust (DR) learner starts from a predicted treated-minus-control
difference. It then corrects that prediction using the observed outcome's
error in the assigned arm, weighted by the chance of that assignment.
The first term below is the prediction; the next two are those corrections.
Regressing this augmented response on $X$ estimates the conditional effect:

\begin{equation}
\hat\varphi_i=\hat\mu_1(X_i)-\hat\mu_0(X_i)
+\frac{W_i\{Y_i-\hat\mu_1(X_i)\}}{\hat e(X_i)}
-\frac{(1-W_i)\{Y_i-\hat\mu_0(X_i)\}}{1-\hat e(X_i)}.
\label{eq:dr-pseudo}
\end{equation}
Condition on the training observations used for these nuisance estimates,
so they are fixed for the held-out row. The remaining conditional bias
has a useful structure: an assignment-probability error multiplies a
combination of outcome-model errors. If either part is exactly correct,
this product vanishes. Iterated expectation gives
\begin{equation}
\E[\hat\varphi\mid X=x]-\tau(x)
=(\hat e-e)\left\{
\frac{\hat\mu_1-\mu_1}{\hat e}
+\frac{\hat\mu_0-\mu_0}{1-\hat e}\right\}(x).
\label{eq:uplift-dr-bias}
\end{equation}
Either correct response means or a correct propensity removes this
conditional bias, under exchangeability and overlap. A subsequent regression
still has sampling and approximation error. In
\citet[Algorithm 1, Theorem 2 and Section 7.4]{kennedy2023towards}, comparison
with an oracle regression additionally requires stability of the second
stage and convergence of the estimated pseudo-outcome. The relevant remainder
is a smoothed product of nuisance errors relative to that regression's oracle
rate. ATE inference and conditional-effect inference are therefore distinct.
\section{Neyman orthogonality and double machine learning}
\label{sec:uplift-dml}

The nuisance functions are necessary ingredients, such as assignment
probabilities and baseline outcomes, rather than the treatment effect we
ultimately want. Orthogonality asks for an estimating equation that is
locally insensitive to small errors in those ingredients. It is a property
of the equation at the truth, not a claim that estimation errors disappear.

\begin{definition}[Neyman orthogonality]
\label{def:orthogonality}
A moment $\E[\psi(D;\theta,\eta)]=0$ for target $\theta$ and nuisance
$\eta$ is Neyman orthogonal if its directional derivative with respect to
$\eta$ vanishes at the truth in the permitted nuisance directions.
\end{definition}

For the ATE, use $\psi=\varphi-\theta$. The conditional remainder in
\cref{eq:uplift-dr-bias} contains a product of propensity and response
errors, so its first derivative vanishes when both errors are zero.
This calculation explains the orthogonality; it does not remove the
identification assumptions. \citet{chernozhukov2018dml} combine orthogonal
scores with sample splitting and cross-fitting to control overfitting in
low-dimensional targets such as an ATE or an elasticity.

The product structure explains the required accuracy. One ingredient can
converge more slowly if the other converges faster; their combined error
must become negligible relative to the usual $1/\sqrt n$ sampling scale.
Here $\|\cdot\|_2$ measures root-mean-square error over the covariate
population, and $o_p$ means a smaller order in probability. For their ATE
score, a sufficient rate condition is
\begin{equation}
\|\hat e-e\|_2\,\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\|\hat\mu_a-\mu_a\|_2
=o_p(n^{-1/2}),
\label{eq:uplift-dml-rate}
\end{equation}
together with the moment, overlap, nuisance consistency and variance conditions
of Assumption 5.1 and Theorem 5.1 in the inspected arXiv version.
Having both errors be $o_p(n^{-1/4})$ suffices for the product condition;
merely stating an order $n^{-1/4}$ does not establish it. When assignment
probabilities are known, the propensity error is zero and the rate requirement
relaxes, though the other conditions still matter. Influence-function standard
errors follow from a verified asymptotic expansion and appropriate sampling
structure. They are not supplied automatically by cross-fitting.

For a constant effect, residualizing outcomes and treatment in
\cref{eq:robinson} gives the familiar partially linear, Frisch--Waugh--Lovell
construction. A varying CATE generally has a different convergence rate and
requires a separate inference argument. Neither theorem guarantees that an
estimated sign is correct for each customer, or that an estimated contribution
will be realized on the bank's next campaign.

\section{When there is no experiment}
\label{sec:uplift-observational}

Historical offer assignments can depend on information absent from the
analyst's data. The first task is to explain why the comparison would identify
a causal quantity. Adjustment, policy discontinuities and encouragement
designs make different assumptions and identify different populations.

\begin{assumption}[Conditional exchangeability and overlap]
\label{ass:unconfounded}
$\{Y(1),Y(0)\}\perp W\mid X$ and $0<e(x)<1$ on the target population.
Consistency and the observation conditions in \cref{ass:sutva} also hold.
\end{assumption}

Observed treatment frequencies can reveal poor overlap. They cannot test
whether all joint causes of assignment and potential outcomes have been
measured. The adverse experiment below illustrates that distinction.

Weight an observed treated outcome by the inverse chance of treatment.
Under exchangeability, it then represents the treated potential outcome
for comparable customers regardless of their actual assignment. The
algebra shows the assignment probability cancelling, while the assumptions
explain why it is legitimate to do so.

\begin{proposition}[Inverse-propensity identification]
\label{prop:ipw}
Under \cref{ass:unconfounded} and integrability,
\begin{equation}
\E\!\left[\frac{WY}{e(X)}\right]
=\E\!\left[\frac{e(X)\E[Y(1)\mid X]}{e(X)}\right]
=\E[Y(1)].
\label{eq:ipw}
\end{equation}
The control identity uses $(1-W)Y/(1-e(X))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $X$, apply consistency and exchangeability to the numerator,
and cancel the positive propensity. Iterating expectations gives the result.
\end{proof}

The balancing-score result of \citet{rosenbaum1983propensity} motivates
propensity matching and stratification. Estimating a score does not by itself
establish exchangeability or adequate balance, and double robustness in
\cref{eq:dr-pseudo} addresses nuisance estimation within the identifying
model rather than omitted confounding outside it.

Difference-in-differences compares changes in an affected group with changes
in a comparison group. Its causal interpretation requires a parallel-trends
restriction on untreated potential outcomes, together with suitable anticipation
and composition conditions. Pretreatment event-study coefficients can expose
some violations but cannot prove the counterfactual post-treatment restriction.
Heterogeneous effects with staggered adoption also change what a two-way
fixed-effects coefficient averages \citep{roth2023trending};
\cref{sec:panel-causal} develops those issues. Synthetic controls in
\cref{sec:mmm-geo} construct comparison paths under their own restrictions.

For a sharp regression discontinuity, suppose treatment is assigned when
a running variable $R$ reaches a cutoff $c$. Continuity of each potential
outcome's conditional mean at $c$ and observations on both sides let us
compare customers just above and just below the threshold. The limits
remove the distance from the threshold, leaving a local treated-minus-control
contrast:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{RD}}=
\lim_{r\downarrow c}\E[Y\mid R=r]
-\lim_{r\uparrow c}\E[Y\mid R=r].
\label{eq:rdd}
\end{equation}
The first limit reveals the treated potential mean and the second the
untreated mean at the cutoff. Sorting around the threshold, another policy
changing at the same cutoff, bandwidth choice and finite-sample bias all
matter \citep{imbenslemieux2008rd}. Credit-score cutoffs motivate banking
applications such as \citet{agarwal2018passthrough}. The resulting effect is
local to the cutoff, even when the bank wants a decision for a wider population.

An encouragement design randomizes an invitation while allowing take-up to
vary. With a nonzero first stage, an exclusion restriction, instrument
independence and monotonicity, the Wald contrast identifies an average effect
for compliers \citep{angristimbensrubin1996iv}. The exclusion restriction fails
if the invitation changes the outcome other than through the defined treatment.
Fuzzy discontinuity designs require analogous conditions. These estimands
need not equal the population ATE used in an unrestricted targeting problem.
