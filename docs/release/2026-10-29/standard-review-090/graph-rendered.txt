The preceding equivalence-class argument concerns a population law. A finite-sample construc-
tion can preserve that ambiguity without assuming that a selected graph is correct. Consider
independent observations of three jointly Gaussian variables (X, A, Y ), a positive-definite
covariance Σ, and the class of linear acyclic structural equations with independent disturbances.
The mean is unrestricted. A supplied ordering may restrict this class; causal sufficiency is a
substantive assumption. We give a deliberately small, conservative reference construction. It
provides a standard against which faster discovery proposals can be judged.
   Use the nine directions V = {ei : 1 ≤ i ≤ 3} ∪ {ei + ej , ei − ej : i < j}. For each v, the sample
variance s2v has pivot (n − 1)s2v /(v ′ Σv) ∼ χ2n−1 . Give each variance a two-sided interval with
error α/9. The union bound shows that all nine intervals contain their population quantities
with probability at least 1 − α, without independence between pivots. Diagonal entries follow
directly, and
                           Σij = {Var(Xi + Xj ) − Var(Xi − Xj )}/4
gives interval bounds for off-diagonal entries. The resulting entrywise box is an outer region: it
ignores some joint restrictions and may include non-positive-definite matrices. Keeping these
extra matrices can enlarge the answer but cannot remove the true covariance on the coverage
event.
   Enumerate the 25 labeled three-vertex DAGs, or the subset respecting a declared time
order. For each graph, list its d-separations. In three variables these require either Σij = 0
or Σij Σkk − Σik Σjk = 0. Discard a graph only if interval evaluation of one required equality
excludes zero. This is an outer feasibility test, not proof that every retained graph has a
compatible covariance in the region. It needs the Markov property, but no lower bound
on nonzero partial correlations and no faithfulness assumption. The true graph cannot be
discarded when its covariance is inside the region.

20.6.1    What the construction teaches a banking decision

In a Gaussian chain X → A → Y with action coefficient 0.7, the unordered population law
permits effects {0, 0.7} in this class. Across 500 samples of size 600, the outer effect set contains
the true effect in all samples, but also contains both zero and 0.7 in all samples. Its median
width is 21.13, and 1.8% of runs have an unbounded set. A supplied order X ≺ A ≺ Y
identifies 0.7 in the population; even then the conservative finite-sample set contains both zero
and 0.7 in 83% of samples, with median width 10.69. Tightening computation or gathering
more information has economic value when these alternatives lead to different decisions.
   A hidden-common-cause experiment is more revealing. Its true action effect is zero, but its
observational covariance admits a sufficient ordered model with effect 0.4. The returned set
misses zero in all 500 runs, even though the covariance region covers the actual covariance
in 94.4% of samples. Coverage of the observed law cannot validate the causal model class.
For 500 successes or failures, a two-sided 95% Wilson interval has an endpoint about 0.9924
or 0.0076; the zero plug-in Monte Carlo standard error at these extremes is not certainty. A
measured common cause, a credible instrument, or randomized assignment would change the
information available for this query. A larger observational sample alone would not identify
which class is appropriate.

20.8      Adjustment over an equivalence class
Suppose the graph is a DAG, CPDAG, or a no-selection MAG/PAG, and the target is the
total effect of A on Y . An adjustment set must make
                                                   Z
                             p(y | do(A = a)) =        p(y | a, z)p(z) dz

hold for every compatible causal model, not just for a conveniently oriented representative.
Generalized adjustment makes this requirement precise (Perković et al., 2018, Definitions 1–4,
Theorem 5).
   A possibly directed path has no arrowhead pointing backward along its direction of traversal.
It is proper relative to an exposure set if only its first vertex is an exposure. An edge A → B
is visible in a MAG/PAG if graph structure certifies that the relevant latent confounding
explanation is absent. A sufficient configuration is V ∗ → A → B with V not adjacent to B;
the full definition also permits a collider path into A whose internal vertices are parents of B.
All directed DAG/CPDAG edges are visible by convention. The criterion has three parts:
1. Every proper possibly directed path from the exposure to the outcome starts with a visible
   edge out of the exposure (amenability).
2. The adjustment variables avoid every possible descendant of a non-exposure vertex on such
   a path (the forbidden set).
3. They block every proper definite-status noncausal path from exposure to outcome. A
   definite noncollider has an incident tail, or is the middle of the appropriate unshielded
   circle-marked triple; colliders have two incident arrowheads.
The cited theorem is sound and complete for adjustment in its stated graph classes. Failure
can mean that a front-door or other identifying functional is needed, or that the target is not
identified. It does not by itself choose between those conclusions. The visible-edge condition
also explains why the two-node MAG A → Y cannot be treated as the corresponding causally
sufficient DAG.
