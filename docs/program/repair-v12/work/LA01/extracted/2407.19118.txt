Experimental Details
All experiments where conducted using the API for GPT-4-Vision 0125-Preview provided within the Azure Open AI
studio. We generated our demonstrative examples using GPT-4 with a temperature parameter of 0.7, a maximum of
800 tokens per response and a “Top P” parameter of 0.95. For each experiment, we created a “system prompt” that
describes how the model should react to each new user prompt. In what follows, we provide samples of system prompts
used to generate the model responses in Figures 3C and 3D of the main paper.

Sample 1

 System Prompt:
    You are an AI assistant tool that helps statisticians design and analyze the results of observational studies.
 Whenever the researcher shows you the results of the study (i.e., in the form of survival or cumulative incidence
 curves) do the following:
    1- Describe the scientific figure or table provided so that the researcher ensures your understanding of the results
 is correct.
    2- Check for evidence of residual confounding and inform the researcher if you have found any.
    Please provide decisive answers and keep your responses concise. Do not provide generic answers that are not
 related to the specific question provided by the researcher.


 User Prompt:
   Can you help me asses the validity of the results from this observational study?




 GPT-4 Response:
    The figure is a cumulative hazard plot comparing two groups over time since a positive SARS-CoV-2 test: one
 treated with nirmatrelvir-ritonavir and one untreated. The x-axis represents time in days, and the y-axis represents
 the cumulative hazard. The treated group is represented by the black line, while the untreated group is represented
 by the red line. The shaded areas around the lines likely represent confidence intervals. The number of individuals
 at risk in each group at various time points is listed below the graph.
    The log-rank p-value is 0.00048, indicating a statistically significant difference in the cumulative hazard between
 the two groups over the 28-day period.
    Regarding residual confounding, the figure alone does not provide enough information to assess this. To evaluate
 residual confounding, we would need additional information on how well the study controlled for potential
 confounding variables, such as demographics, comorbidities, and other treatments received. Without this information,
 we cannot conclusively determine whether residual confounding has been adequately addressed.

  As we can see, the model is unable to determine whether there are signs of residual confounding in the result provided
by the user. In the next sample, we incorporate typical signs of confounding in survival estimates within the system

    The figure provided is a cumulative hazard plot comparing two groups over time since a positive SARS-CoV-2

 lower event rate (such as hospitalization, severe disease, or death) in the treated group. The separation between the
 curves appears to start at day 0 and continues to widen over time. The log-rank p-value is 0.00048, suggesting that
 the difference in cumulative hazard between the two groups is statistically significant.

 age, comorbidities, etc.) that are not accounted for in the analysis.
