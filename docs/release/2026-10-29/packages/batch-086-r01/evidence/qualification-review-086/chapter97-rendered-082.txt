Chapter 97

Servicing policies with estimated
action and observation probabilities

A servicing team wants to compare an assistance rule with ordinary collection. Its historical
action probabilities may not have been retained, and some customers disappear from the
analytical records before their next repayment is measured. These are two estimation problems
only after a more basic scientific question has been answered: did the recorded history include
enough information to remove action and observation selection? A highly accurate model of
recorded actions cannot answer that question on its own.
  This chapter develops a restricted route with estimated probabilities, a policy selected
on separate training histories, and an explicit finite-sample confidence statement. It also
constructs two observationally indistinguishable worlds in which that route answers different
causal questions. The confidence construction and finite examples are project derivations.
The longitudinal treatment/observation identification principle is inherited from Hernán and
Robins; the importance of independent policy evaluation and cumulative, rather than current-
action, ratios is also explicit in Jiang and Li. Their results do not themselves prove the
composition below. (Hernán and Robins, 2020; Jiang and Li, 2016; Causal banking research
project, 2026ad,ae)


97.1     The servicing information clock and the eligible popula-
         tion
Fix a finite horizon T . An independently sampled customer has a complete potential history
with pre-action information Ht , binary assistance At , and subsequent net contribution Rt ,
for t = 0, . . . , T − 1. History contains every recorded past variable used by the rule or
the assignment mechanism, including earlier rewards when relevant. A finite table, or an
explicitly sufficient finite summary, indexes the probability models. Arbitrary discretization of
a continuous history does not establish sufficiency.
   Let O−1 = 1. Among records still observed at date t, the bank first records Ht , then selects
At , then the observation indicator Ct determines whether the following reward and next history
are recorded. Set Ot = Ot−1 Ct . Dropout is absorbing. Thus Ct = 1 denotes retention here,
the opposite coding from a conventional censoring indicator. Define

                      et (a | h) = Pr(At = a | Ht = h, Ot−1 = 1),
                       gt (h, a) = Pr(Ct = 1 | Ht = h, At = a, Ot−1 = 1).

   The stipulated observation law has four substantive restrictions. First, consistency connects
the recorded actions to the defined assistance intervention. Second, conditional on Ht , action
selection is independent of subsequent potential histories under the candidate policies. Third,
conditional on Ht , At , retention is independent of the subsequent complete history; dropout
merely hides records and does not change repayment. Fourth, all histories reachable under the
candidate policies satisfy known floors et (a | h) ≥ ce > 0 for both actions and gt (h, a) ≥ cg > 0,
with ce ≤ 1/2. Infeasible actions must instead be removed from both the declared policy class
and the support requirement.
   The independent sampling unit is the whole customer history. Dependence within that
history is allowed. Independent training and evaluation cohorts follow the same complete-
history, assignment and observation law. Distinct customer identifiers do not guarantee this
condition when common calendar shocks, household links or shared staff information remain.
The construction does not qualify those sampling regimes.

   If any component interval is empty the family result is flagged incompatible. Simultaneous
coverage allows selecting a member after inspecting these intervals, provided every candidate
was fixed before evaluation and all were covered. Adding candidates after viewing evaluation
outcomes needs a new argument. For an individual paired contrast, one may instead bound
the paired score and its nuisance error directly; treating separately fitted values as independent
loses their covariance. Neither approach turns a finite candidate class into unrestricted policy
optimization.


97.6       Exact small references and the cost of conservatism
A two-decision example uses observed stress St ∈ {0, 1}, with initial probability 1/2. Historical
assistance has probability .3 + .4St . Next stress has probability .2 + .5St − .15At , retention
probability is .9−.1St −.1At , and dated contribution is 2−3St+1 −.25At . These are hypothetical
utility units; their coefficient signs express an assistance cost and an improvement in future
stress. Every path probability is rational. Retention uses a draw independent of the next
stress innovation given current state and action.
   No assistance, always assist, and assist only when stressed form a fixed finite policy
class. Exhaustive path sums under interventions are compared with a separate backward
value recursion. Summation over observed prefixes reproduces the same values with true
denominators. Perturbing action-one probabilities by +.04 and retention probabilities by −.03
verifies the fitted-law bias expression and its conservative envelope. A deliberately incorrect
current-action/retention shortcut is kept as a negative reference. The program enumerates
all paths; it draws no final evaluation sample and measures no empirical coverage. (Causal
banking research project, 2026ae)

   The common worst-case bias envelope is 1.37243 utility units, exceeding every realized bias
in the table. That conservatism is the price of using probability error bounds without favorable
outcome cancellation. These perturbations illustrate the error propagation formula; they are
not estimates learned from an executed training sample.
   Even with Bt = 2, ce = .25, cg = .65, T = 2, n = 1000 and αE = .025, the evaluation radius
alone exceeds eight utility units. The entire stipulated value range is only eight units wide. A
mathematically valid bound can therefore be unsuitable for choosing assistance. Increasing
the nominal confidence level or adding more rare history cells makes this tradeoff harder. This
is a reason to seek shorter questions, stronger information, sharper variance-sensitive bounds
or a reviewed sequential regression construction; it is not a reason to remove the error budget.
   Sequential doubly robust scores offer a substantive alternative when continuation values
are useful. They require a separate analysis of fitted action/retention models, value-function
errors and sampling dependence. The known-logger variance reduction in Jiang and Li does
not automatically establish estimated-logger finite coverage. The earlier synthetic collection
results already show that mean protection and normal-interval reliability differ. (Jiang and Li,
2016)

97.7      Observational equivalence prevents a hidden-state repair
          by prediction
Consider a single decision with no censoring. In world E, action is a fair independent coin
and Y = A with probability .8, otherwise Y = 1 − A. The intervention values are VE (1) = .8,
VE (0) = .2. In world H, private hardship U is a fair coin, Y = U , and Pr(A = 1 | U ) = .2 + .6U .
Here VH (1) = VH (0) = .5.
  Both worlds have exactly the same observed joint probabilities:

                       Observed pair (A, Y ) (0, 0) (0, 1) (1, 0) (1, 1)
                       Either world                .4      .1     .1     .4


   Even the private-state assignment in world H has overlap: its probabilities are .2 and .8.
Infinite recorded data estimate Pr(A = 1) = .5 and E[Y | A = 1] = .8 perfectly in both worlds.
Weighting by that observed probability returns .8, while the hidden world’s value is .5. No
fitted logger, extra evaluation data or language-model interpretation of these same records can
distinguish the worlds. The effect is .6 in one and zero in the other.
   Observation selection has a separate obstruction. Suppose half the cohort is observed and
half of those observed have Y = 1. One full-data world assigns zero to every missing outcome;
another assigns one. Both yield the same observed records and retention probability .5, but
their cohort means are .25 and .75. Accurately estimating the retention probability does not
recover the unobserved outcome law. The complete-case mean .5 answers neither world’s
population question.
   An additional randomized action design, a truthful relevant-state audit, or a validated prox-
y/instrument model can distinguish some such worlds. Each adds information or restrictions.
The existing proximal and partial-observation treatments explain specific routes and their rank,
exclusion and policy-information requirements; they do not authorize arbitrary hidden-state
inference. (Bennett and Kallus, 2023; Tennenholtz et al., 2020)


97.8      What the proposal can recommend after this derivation
The preserved selected17 study used truthful representative state audits and supplied action
probabilities. It passed its selected valid cells, while wrong probabilities covered only one of
512 requests and policy leakage returned no finite outputs. The present finite-table route
makes estimated-probability uncertainty explicit after restricting to sufficient observed histories.
It removes no hidden-state requirement from that predecessor and claims no new repeated-
sampling qualification. (Causal banking research project, 2026af)
   The separate proposed continuous-state servicing experiment has Gaussian innovations and
a quadratic reward. Its rewards are unbounded and its history table is not finite. Consequently
it cannot use this bounded-table theorem as written. A prospectively declared bounded utility
would define a different target; alternatively, a valid tail/moment bound and a continuous-
model estimation argument are needed. Quietly capping realized losses after evaluating the
results would answer a changed question.
  For the proposal, the executable next comparison is a declared finite-state generated
demonstration with known evaluator truth, estimated training probabilities, independent
evaluation and adverse hidden/censoring cases. Its useful-width screen must be chosen in utility
units before final draws. A broad interval or correct refusal is an informative demonstration
