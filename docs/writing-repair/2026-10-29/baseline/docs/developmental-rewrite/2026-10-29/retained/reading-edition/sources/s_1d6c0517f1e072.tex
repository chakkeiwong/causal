\section{Synthetic controls across language boundaries: solver, interface and inference contracts}
\label{sec:exec25-synthetic-crosslanguage}

A regional lending policy can be evaluated through an R worker, a Julia
estimator, a C++ numerical component or a Java service, but these choices
do not represent the same scientific or operational commitment. A repository's
dominant language may describe its generated code or service layer rather
than an exposed synthetic-control estimator. The bank must preserve the
donor population, intervention clock, fitted target, uncertainty population
and failed requests across every boundary. This comparison supplements the
R and Python analyses with selected original implementations and an explicit
decision about what each source supports.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

\subsection{Choose an interface after specifying the banking contrast}
Let $y_{1t}$ be revenue in the treated region and let $y_{jt}$ denote revenue
in eligible donor regions. A simple synthetic trajectory is
$\widehat y_{1t}(0)=\sum_j w_jy_{jt}$, with nonnegative weights summing to one.
The observed gap $y_{1t}-\widehat y_{1t}(0)$ becomes a causal contrast only
under additional counterfactual and interference restrictions. Numerical
optimality of a pretreatment loss does not supply those restrictions.
Even numerical agreement needs a target beyond the objective value.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

Consider two donors both equal to 100 before intervention, with later values
100 and 200, while the treated region has later revenue 150. Every convex
weight has zero pretreatment loss. Weights $(1,0)$, $(1/2,1/2)$ and $(0,1)$
give later counterfactuals 100, 150 and 200 and gaps 50, zero and $-50$.
These are exact stipulated values, not package outputs. An adapter comparison
must therefore specify whether it requires weight equality, fitted-value
equality, objective agreement or an admissible set of solutions. A sparse
solution and a dense solution can satisfy the same numerical optimization
while leading to different extrapolations.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

For the proposal, direct adoption means retaining a supported public
interface and its declared runtime. A worker bridge preserves that interface
behind a typed request and a versioned result. A port entails responsibility
for the algorithm, dependency substitutions, numerical criteria and inference
semantics. Reimplementing a quadratic subproblem does not reproduce the
entire estimator. These distinctions govern the choices below.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

\subsection{Julia: explicit quadratic status and less explicit inference population}
The pinned SynthControl.jl revision declares version 0.4.4 and an MIT notice.
Its module exports \texttt{SimpleSCM} and \texttt{SyntheticDiD}, reexports
TreatmentPanels, and includes the simple estimator, synthetic differences
in differences and a matrix-completion file. The retained ADH development
file is not included by this module; its existence is not evidence of an
available exported estimator. The resolved TreatmentPanels, JuMP and HiGHS
artifacts remain separate dependencies requiring inspection and qualification.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_julia}

The SimpleSCM path accepts a balanced panel with one continuously treated
unit. It fits nonnegative simplex weights to pretreatment outcomes using
a quadratic objective, requires a JuMP termination status of
\texttt{OPTIMAL}, and then extracts weights and the post-treatment gap.
Its optional donor-placebo loop also requires that status. This explicit
gate contrasts with wrappers that extract a numerical vector without
carrying its termination evidence. However, an optimum status still needs
feasibility and tolerance interpretation for the resolved optimizer;
it proves neither donor eligibility nor exchangeability.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_julia,exec25_synthcross_choice}

The synthetic differences-in-differences path permits placebo standard
errors and rejects other requested standard-error modes in the inspected
method. It may reorder the panel's outcome and treatment matrices in place
to put the treated unit last. A bridge must preserve or deliberately copy
unit identities and reconcile later consumers of the same panel object.
The fitted-state predicate tests for nonzero effect or standard-error
entries. A zero-effect result with no requested standard error can therefore
fail that predicate under its literal values, although zero is a valid
effect value. This is a conditional source concern and a proposed lifecycle
test, not a native failure observed here.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_julia}

The placebo helper also merits an explicit population oracle. With $J$
untreated rows, the inspected code sets its fitting-control count to $J-1$,
allocates that many effect entries, swaps rows for the first $J-2$ iterations,
and performs one final swap. In an independent label-only replay with four
donors initially ordered A, B, C, D, the held-out labels are A, B and D;
C is not selected. If stipulated effects for A, B, C, D are 2, 4, 6, 10,
the sample variance of the selected three is $52/3$, while that of all four
is $35/3$. Neither number is a certified causal variance. The example shows
why the realized placebo identities must be checked rather than inferred
from a method name.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_julia,exec25_synthcross_choice}

Its weight routine returns weights and an objective-history array after
either limited objective improvement or the maximum iteration count.
The selected estimator extracts weights from that result. A qualification
fixture should distinguish the stopping reason, finite history, a valid
zero effect, panel mutation, and complete placebo population. The proposed
role is a version-pinned Julia reference and conditional worker bridge;
direct adoption awaits those tests and resolved dependencies. Other Julia
leads remain uninspected, so this is not closure of the entire language cell.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_julia,exec25_synthcross_choice}

\subsection{C++ inside an R package is a component contract}
The selected coresynth 1.0.0 source exposes an R formula workflow with
RcppArmadillo numerical components and an MIT notice. Its inner synthetic
control routine forms a factored quadratic problem and returns a donor-weight
vector. The implementation tries active-set and related numerical routes,
then an accelerated projected-gradient fallback with a weight-change
tolerance and iteration cap. A later exact-solve attempt may succeed;
otherwise the path can return the current iterate. The returned vector alone
does not identify which route succeeded or provide a universal optimality
certificate. The source's conditional KKT statements must remain conditional
on the route that actually established them.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_core}

This matters in a bank comparison with nearly collinear donors. A worker
should report solver route, convergence reason, feasibility, objective and
donor identity. A direct C++ port would need to expose those quantities and
qualify the Armadillo and linear-algebra environment, as well as preserve
the R-side construction of predictors, windows, filters and estimands.
The proposal therefore retains coresynth as an R-facing reference with
inspectable C++ kernels, while deferring a standalone C++ service claim.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_core,exec25_synthcross_choice}

Its placebo inference contains useful safeguards and further denominator
obligations. The R wrapper rejects the single-donor MSPE-ratio comparison
for unsupported pooled or staggered settings, carries the original
optimizer/window choices into selected refits, and can exclude poorly
fitting placebo runs. The C++ covariate-placebo route marks failed refits
with nonfinite results; the R wrapper forms a surviving population and
reports the number used. Its rank calculation includes the treated statistic
when it is valid. These source-level features are preferable to silently
asserting that every requested donor yielded a comparable statistic, but
they do not justify ignoring the probability of failure or selection.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_core}

For an authored counting example, suppose four donor requests yield two
usable statistics below the treated statistic and two unresolved failures.
Including the treated assignment among the three observed statistics gives
$1/3$. If all five requested assignments were the intended population,
the unresolved rankings allow counts from $1/5$ to $3/5$. These are arithmetic
bounds on possible rankings, not calibrated p-values. The adapter must
report attempts, successful refits, pruning, invalid ratios and usable
statistics separately, and justify any inference conditional on retention.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

SynthHelpers provides a smaller but instructive boundary. Its dated
development version declares GPL-2 and links Rcpp and RcppArmadillo.
The retained C++ routine constructs nonnegative simplex weights through
an interior-point call. That call returns primal, dual and convergence
fields, but the exported weight wrapper extracts the primal vector while
its convergence extraction is commented out. This is not evidence that
the solver fails; it is evidence that the public wrapper does not return
all inspected diagnostics. Reusing the kernel requires preserving its
provenance and terms, specifying a diagnostic response, and qualifying
the resolved runtime. A whole-package port is deferred until that work is
justified against an existing worker bridge.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_helpers}

\subsection{Bayesian and network-aware candidates need different evidence}
The selected nasc metadata describes an R package with Stan/Rcpp components,
not a general standalone C++ estimator. Its inspected constructor requires
a spatial matrix for selected corrections and penalties and checks that
row sums are approximately one or zero. That local calculation removes
missing values before summing. A row containing a missing entry and one
can therefore satisfy this particular sum check. A complete validator must
separately establish finite entries, dimensions, unit alignment and the
scientific meaning of the network. Further caller and model inspection
is needed before claiming that such input reaches a sampler.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_nasc,exec25_synthcross_choice}

A supplied normalized matrix is not evidence of the bank's true spillover
structure. The proposal treats this candidate as a network-aware reference
requiring explicit exposure semantics, sensitivity to missing ties and
public validation of its model and MCMC behavior. The remaining Stan models,
network assumptions and estimation delegates are unqualified. Numerical
sampling success cannot settle whether a regional policy contaminates
the proposed donor portfolios.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

The bsynth source likewise describes an R/Stan workflow. Its selected draw
helper extracts separate fitted and post-treatment arrays, joins them to
row-index-derived dates and then joins observed outcomes by date. That is
an important conversion contract: unique date keys, draw identity and
the model's meaning of each array require checks before reporting an
effect distribution. The selected source does not establish calibration
or authorize a general C++ API.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_bsynth}

Artifact rights are unresolved for this pin. The package DESCRIPTION and
the inspected R helper declare Apache 2.0, while a retained Stan include
contains GPL version 3-or-later terms. Both original declarations remain
visible; no legal conclusion follows from choosing one metadata field.
The proposed decision defers distribution or porting pending a file-level
rights review and complete model/source inspection, while retaining the
workflow as a comparison candidate.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_bsynth}

\subsection{Java discovery supports a service lead, not an estimator claim}
The bounded Java title/description search returned no matching repository;
a Java README search returned additional leads, including Tencent's
fast-causal-inference. The pinned December 2025 README describes a SQL/OLAP
architecture and lists synthetic control among incubating capabilities.
Its Java SQL gateway has a Spring-based Maven descriptor and a separate
gateway description. These authentic sources support a service-layer lead;
they do not establish an available, qualified Java synthetic-control
estimator or reproduce vendor performance claims.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_tencent}

The present choice is to defer direct estimator adoption and inspect a
concrete supported method and its engine implementation before proposing
integration. A Java-facing product could instead call a qualified R,
Python or Julia worker, but that is a design option requiring a frozen
schema, row/column identities, monetary units, clock, donor population,
failure semantics and whole-request accounting. Transporting a weight
vector or a p-value alone is insufficient. Empty Scala or Kotlin search
results and unrelated control-chart hits do not prove that relevant JVM
software is absent.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

\subsection{A common public qualification package and an honest search boundary}
The proposed public tests cover zero effects, tied and nonunique optima,
treated-unit ordering, reused mutable panels, incomplete placebo loops,
solver fallback, failed and pruned donors, missing network entries,
duplicate dates and posterior-array identities. A test records requested
and realized populations and all costs; a scientific evaluation adds a
declared assignment or sampling law and counterfactual assumptions.
These are related but distinct obligations.~\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

For illustration, 15,000 USD across 500 attempts, 300 conformant outputs
and 250 useful outputs gives 30, 50 and 60 USD per respective denominator.
The figures are hypothetical. Neither local compilation nor a repository's
speed claim provides actual operating cost or banking value.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}

The retained queries and screened leads define a bounded search. Additional
Julia candidates, native interfaces, dependent artifacts, full network and
Bayesian models, commercial alternatives and other languages remain
uninspected. The comparisons in all three languages remain incomplete. Source comparison
can specify a credible next experiment, but cannot promise a universal
repair for unidentified counterfactual trajectories, arbitrary dependence
or unknown spillover truth. The packages have not been validated by execution in this comparison.
\citep{exec25_synthcross_choice}
