## SMILE and GeNIe share an engine but require separate reuse decisions

The retained official manuals describe a proprietary compiled SMILE engine,
language wrappers and the GeNIe modeling environment. Two reference decisions
cover the engine and GUI-to-service workflow without counting them as separate
qualified inference capabilities. Exact wrapper support and commercial
development, server and redistribution entitlements remain inputs to a later
integration decision. No agreement was accepted and no software was executed.
\citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license,exec24_smile_choice}

The manuals distinguish unresolved discovery patterns, DAG conversion and
uniform parameter initialization. An authored example gives two causal models
with the same observed table but effects of three fifths and zero. At 2,000
accounts, 100 USD per incremental success and 40,000 USD campaign cost, the
implied net values are 80,000 and minus 40,000 USD. Choosing a direction in
a diagram cannot resolve that evidence gap. Temporal order and utility units
likewise belong in the interface contract.
\citep{exec13_smile_pc,exec13_smile_pattern,exec13_genie_pattern,exec13_ct_smile_tutorial,exec16_cp_bayesfusion,exec24_smile_choice}

Public trials can test graph and parameter provenance, dataset permutations,
time-slice decoding, information timing and interruption before costly bank
experiments. The proposed CLI/MCP worker must retain ambiguity and typed
failure. Hypothetical expenditure of 24,000 USD over 800 submitted, 600
completed and 400 qualified requests gives 30, 40 and 60 USD respectively.
Actual prices, deployment profiles and native performance remain unmeasured.
\citep{exec24_smile_choice}
