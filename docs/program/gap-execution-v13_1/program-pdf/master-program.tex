\hypertarget{four-unresolved-scientific-arguments}{%
\subsection{Four unresolved scientific
arguments}\label{four-unresolved-scientific-arguments}}

\textbf{R02S: persistent identity, audit and calendar uncertainty.} A
valid joint region for the numerator, audit rank and calendar nuisance
can be mapped into an effect set without unsafe division. The
composition rule is known. The missing step is a feasible valid
component/joint region when these quantities are estimated under
persistent dependence, scarce dates and low audit information. A
supplied covariance is an oracle assumption. More dates do not redraw
the same audit. Next: derive the unknown-nuisance construction and its
information requirements; compare a genuinely independent-calendar/audit
design. Leave the failing regimes open if useful uncertainty needs
information that is absent.

\textbf{R03S: logging, selected policies and hidden states.} Independent
evaluation can remove reuse of evaluation outcomes for policy selection.
This does not establish sequential exchangeability, support,
logging-error control or identification of an unobserved state. Equal
observed laws can imply different intervention values. Next: specify an
identifiable observed-history class, derive a logging-error and
selection bound and identify exactly which hidden-state regimes it
excludes. Do not call this a solution for the unknown optimum.

\textbf{R04S: graphs to useful effects.} Mapping a valid joint
graph/parameter region through an effect functional preserves coverage.
What is missing is a feasible region for unknown temporal nuisance and
enough information to produce useful effects. The equal-law example X=U,
Y=bX−bU+ε has the same observational law for every b while its
intervention slope is b. This is a proved information obstruction, not
an invitation to try more graph scoring. Next: specify actual separating
measurements, interventions or defensible restrictions; prove the joint
region and an outer-bound certificate before qualification.

\textbf{R05S: dependent learning and weak information.} Orthogonal
scores can have a remainder controlled by a product of nuisance errors.
Establishing the product rate and dependence bound for the selected
learner in the failing dimension, support and misspecification regimes
is still open. Two errors merely of order n\^{}(−1/4) give an order-one
root-n remainder. Weak rank requires set inversion and may defeat useful
precision. Next: derive the learner-specific bound, pilot or
block-separation conditions, support diagnostics and inversion behavior.
A fixed-dictionary or supplied-nuisance success cannot close the broader
claim.

These are research directions with falsifiable stopping rules.
Successful general solutions are not asserted. Authentic added
information may be necessary; where it is necessary and unavailable,
retain that requirement for the next step.
