\chapter{Calibrating funding inference and accounting for its information cost}
\label{v9:chapter}

\section{Why a larger evaluation sample can leave the wrong uncertainty}
\label{v9:target}

A treasury estimate can become more precise around the wrong population
quantity when its controls are learned from a fixed, imperfect pilot. The
funding construction supplies a useful setting in which to distinguish this
problem from audit inversion, covariance estimation and computational scale.
Keep the fixed one-month price intervention, balance-stock response horizon
$H$, financial weights $w_h$ and direct repricing cost $C$ of the earlier
funding example. Its target is
\[
 V=\sum_{h=0}^{H}w_h\beta\rho^h-C.
\]
This is the value of the specified intervention; neither the reporting horizon
nor the intervention is selected to improve its reported interval.

Consider the stationary mechanism
\begin{align}
 W_t&=rW_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-r^2}\,\xi_t,\qquad
 A_t=\tfrac12W_t+\tfrac15(W_t^2-1)+e_t, \label{v9:law}\\
 B_t&=\rho B_{t-1}+\beta A_t+1.2W_t+.4(W_t^2-1)+\nu_t.
\end{align}
Here $|r|,|\rho|<1$, the $\xi_t$ are independent standard Gaussian innovations,
the action innovation $e_t$ has variance one, and it is
independent of the complete driver path, other-date action innovations and
exogenous balance shocks. Future assignment remains natural under the
intervention. A companion portfolio has coefficient $\beta_d$ and shares the
exogenous shocks. Independent origin strata $S_t\in\{0,1\}$ and complete-horizon
swaps create the two candidate outcome vectors, with correct-map probability
$p_s$ in stratum $s$. Independent representative binomial audits estimate
$p_s$. This is an origin-swap experiment; it does not inherit the persistent
customer-map sampling law merely because both examples involve identity.

For the bank, the assumptions concern assignment and observation, not just
regression fit. A source owner must substantiate noninformative swaps, an
assignment owner must justify the innovation restriction, and an audit owner
must document representative independent labels. Good predictive accuracy
does not establish those statements.

\section{An exact account of independent-pilot error}
\label{v9:pilot}

Use the fixed dictionary $x_t=(1,W_t,W_t^2-1)'$, whose stationary second-moment
matrix is $Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,2)$. Let
$c_1=.5\beta+1.2$ and $c_2=.2\beta+.4$. The stationary conditional mean of
$B_t$ has coefficients
\[
 a_0=\frac{c_1}{1-\rho r},\qquad
 b_0=\frac{c_2}{1-\rho r^2},
\]
on $W_t$ and $W_t^2-1$. The geometric sums include balances accumulated before
the origin. Natural future assignment gives, for $h\geq1$,
\[
 a_h=\rho a_{h-1}+c_1r^h,\qquad
 b_h=\rho b_{h-1}+c_2r^{2h}.
\]
Mix these focal and companion means by $p_s$ to obtain each candidate's
conditional outcome mean. The action-mean coefficients are $(0,.5,.2)'$.
An independent infinite-past sum reproduces the recursions to numerical
precision. A literal 48-branch recursion also matches the aggregate simulator:
averaging independent Gaussian branch noise replaces its standard deviation
by $\sigma_{\rm branch}/\sqrt{48}$ while preserving the common shocks.
Stationary formulas describe the population; a finite burn approximates that law.

Condition on an independent pilot of size $M$. For one stratum and one
candidate horizon, write $\delta_a$ and $\delta_y$ for the pilot's action and
outcome coefficient errors. Let $\eta$ denote the desired candidate response.
Because $E[e_tx_t]=0$ and $E[e_t^2]=1$, the population residual-regression ratio
conditional on that pilot is
\begin{equation}
 \eta_M=\frac{\eta+\delta_a'Q\delta_y}
                    {1+\delta_a'Q\delta_a},\qquad
 \eta_M-\eta=
 \frac{\delta_a'Q(\delta_y-\eta\delta_a)}
      {1+\delta_a'Q\delta_a}. \label{v9:pilot-bias}
\end{equation}
Direct Gaussian quadrature of the residual products independently verifies
this identity. It is a population ratio conditional on the pilot, not an
assertion about the expectation of a finite-sample ratio or inverse audit map.
Correct action means remove this shift even when the outcome mean is
imperfect. Knowing only the outcome mean leaves the action-error square.

The orthogonal partialling-out score and its nuisance-product requirement are
inherited ideas \citep[Section 4.1, equation (4.4), Assumption 4.1]{dml2018};
equation~\eqref{v9:pilot-bias} is the present model's explicit calculation.
The cited iid theorem is not a dependent-calendar theorem. Under a fixed
dictionary, nonsingular moments, stable mixing and sufficiently high moments,
the pilot errors have order $M^{-1/2}$ with bounded ridge penalties. The target
remainder therefore has order $M^{-1}$. Root-$T$ inference for the desired
response requires
\[
 \frac{\sqrt T}{M}\longrightarrow0.
\]
A fixed pilot does not satisfy that requirement. For a growing Bartlett
window $L$, uniform score moment bounds give the additional sufficient control
$L/\sqrt M\to0$ for fitted-score covariance. These are sufficient rates for
the specified construction, not necessary rates for every learner.

The diagnostic experiment holds evaluation draws and audits fixed within each
$T$, uses nested prefixes of an independent pilot, and replaces action and
outcome means separately by their known values. At $T=4800$, the average
conditional shift decreases from about 42,452 currency units with $M=400$ to
9,049 with $M=1600$ and 616 with $M=19200$. Known action means remove that
conditional shift; finite-ratio and covariance errors remain.
Table~\ref{v9:diagnostic-table} separates the shift from empirical bias and
compares estimated standard errors with empirical dispersion. Known nuisance
and audit information diagnose this synthetic law; the bank cannot obtain
them by labeling an estimate an oracle.

\section{Joint fixed regression and the score's covariance horizon}
\label{v9:covariance}

A distinct practical procedure estimates the prespecified fixed regression
jointly on the evaluation sample. Within each stratum regress both candidate
outcome vectors on $(x_t',A_t)'$. Frisch--Waugh residualization recovers the
same action coefficient by projecting the action on $x_t$ and regressing the
outcomes on its residual. This is a fixed-dimensional joint estimating
equation. Its argument does not authorize selecting an adaptive dictionary
from evaluation outcomes and then treating that dictionary as fixed.

Let $e_t=A_t-x_t'\alpha$, let
$q_s=E[1\{S_t=s\}e_t^2]$, and let $\epsilon_{st}$ be the population joint
regression residual for a candidate horizon. The action-coefficient influence is
\[
 \psi_{st}=\frac{1\{S_t=s\}e_t\epsilon_{st}}{q_s}.
\]
Assume nonsingular regression moments, finite $4+\delta$ moments and stationary
mixing sufficient for a score central limit theorem. The stable Gaussian law
above satisfies these conditions away from persistence one. Estimated
fixed-dimensional regression residuals consistently recover this influence.
Additional predictable outcome structure need not bias the action coefficient
when the action mean and innovation moment remain correct.

There is a stronger property specific to this mechanism. Condition on the
complete exogenous driver path and all shocks strictly before $t$. Averaging
over $e_t$, the independent origin swap and shocks through $t+H$ makes
$\psi_{st}$ mean zero: its action-response component has already been removed
by the population coefficient. Its random action and balance inputs are
measurable through $t+H$. For $u>t+H$, the earlier score is measurable before
$u$, whereas the later score has conditional expectation zero. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Cov}(\psi_t,\psi_{t+k})=0\quad(k>H),\qquad
 \Omega=\Gamma_0+\sum_{k=1}^{H}(\Gamma_k+\Gamma_k'). \label{v9:horizon}
\end{equation}
The score process need not itself be $H$-dependent. The argument requires
exogenous innovations, the correct action mean and noninformative swaps;
a finite reported horizon alone is insufficient.

For fixed $H$, untapered empirical lag products consistently estimate
\eqref{v9:horizon}; plugging in the consistent joint regression changes each
moment by $o_p(1)$. Bartlett weighting instead attenuates nonzero short-lag
covariances when its window is small. Its positive-semidefinite construction
is an inherited benefit \citep[printed p.4, Theorem 1]{newey1986}. The
untapered estimate has no such finite-sample guarantee. The implementation
qualifies only the prespecified scalar monetary contrast and returns an
unavailable result if its calendar variance is nonpositive. It does not clip
that variance or advertise a generally valid covariance matrix.

The mapping inverse remains a separate source of uncertainty. If
$\eta_{s1h},\eta_{s2h}$ are the candidate responses and each stratum has weight
one half, the monetary derivative with respect to $p_s$ is
\[
 d_s=\frac{1}{2}\sum_h w_h
       \frac{\eta_{s2h}-\eta_{s1h}}{(2p_s-1)^2}.
\]
Add $\sum_s d_s^2p_s(1-p_s)/n_s$ from the independent audits. Regular joint
asymptotics require $p_s$ away from $0,1,.5$ and finite limits for $T/n_s$.
The Wilson rank guard withholds a regular interval when an audit interval
crosses $.5$; empirical audit boundaries also remain unavailable. More dates
do not create strong identity information.

\section{What the fresh comparisons support}
\label{v9:results}

The first fresh comparison uses 600 requests in each of seven laws. Its
joint-regression horizon interval covers 550/600 at 720 dates, with Monte
Carlo interval $[.8916,.9375]$. This is a retained calibration failure.
At 1200 and 4800 dates coverage is .935 and .94; the persistent $\rho=.96$
case gives .9467. Changing the covariance does not remove all finite-regression
error. In the weak-audit law only 127/600 requests yield intervals, of which
121 cover; conditional coverage is about .953, but all-request coverage is
.202. Reporting only the conditional fraction would hide the information
shortfall. Omitting the quadratic action-mean term gives .2633 coverage;
correlating the action innovation with the structural disturbance gives zero.
These are false scientific declarations that a numerical API cannot detect.

The exact pilot calculation suggests another route: retain an independent
pilot but let $M=4T$. This satisfies the selected sufficient rate and avoids
claiming that the small joint-regression failure has been repaired.
A separately frozen fresh study compares the inherited pilot, a fourfold
pilot with Bartlett covariance and a fourfold pilot with horizon covariance.
For the latter, the fitted score converges in mean square to the true-nuisance
score, so each of its fixed-$H$ lag moments converges to the moment in
\eqref{v9:horizon}. The vanishing pilot remainder supplies the required
centering at the desired target. An imperfect pilot held fixed need not have
zero score covariance beyond $H$; the growing-pilot argument is asymptotic.
The last procedure has coverage .9600, .9417 and .9333 across the three
designs in Table~\ref{v9:followup-table}. Each Monte Carlo interval includes
.95, but the largest point estimate remains below nominal. Its mean SE is
64,431 compared with empirical SD 65,867. Relative to the same-draw inherited
baseline, the coverage changes are $4/600$, $0/600$ and $1/600$; descriptive
paired Monte Carlo intervals include zero. Clear superiority is not established.

At 720 dates the old pilot was already fourfold; only covariance differs.
The 1200/4800 designs require three/twelve times the inherited pilot history.
Mean fit-and-inference time for the horizon route is about 1.35/1.57 times the
baseline in those two cases, excluding acquisition. Thus a justified
information rate has a real requirement even where nominal calibration is
statistically compatible. The independent pilot must also represent the same
economic law. A long history drawn from a different assignment regime does not
satisfy that requirement.

\section{Fitting and covariance without retaining all scores}
\label{v9:execution}

The joint-regression route permits a stronger memory construction than merely
reducing the fitting matrix. Form each stratum's block of $[x,A,Y]$, take its QR
factor and combine the small retained triangular factor with the next block.
The final factor supplies the action projection, full regression coefficients
and residual-action sum of squares. A second pass forms the monetary score
directly. It need not materialize the horizon score matrix or retain all $T$
scalar scores.

For lag $k$, store the raw cross-product sum $R_k$, total score $S$, count $T$,
and first and last $H$ scores. If $\bar\psi=S/T$, the centered lag sum is
\[
 R_k-\bar\psi\{2S-S_{{\rm first},k}-S_{{\rm last},k}\}
       +(T-k)\bar\psi^2.
\]
Keeping the final $H$ scores of one block supplies its cross-boundary pairs
with the next. Independent dense SVD and direct centered lag calculations
agree for awkward block sizes, including blocks shorter than $H$. The
working state is bounded by block size, fixed dictionary/outcome width and
$H$; raw input storage and resident mapped pages remain real costs.

Complete fresh-process requests at 120,000 and 1,200,000 dates include imports,
immutable-input checks, fitting, prediction, inference, serialization and
read-back. At the larger size, chunked peak memory is .434--.435 of dense
memory, and elapsed time is .831--.841 of dense time. Maximum relative
output differences are below $6.0\,10^{-15}$. At the smaller size time ratios
straddle one. These measurements concern the joint-regression procedure whose
small-sample calibration failure remains visible. The growing-pilot
implementation still stores its arrays; it does not inherit these memory gains.
Neither procedure has demonstrated bank-platform service levels or analyst
labor savings.

\section{From a qualified request to an institutional decision}
\label{v9:external}

The public request names the procedure, financial target, covariance rule,
audit counts and scientific declarations. Its identity binds evaluation and
pilot bytes as well as those choices. Two local consumer examples preserve
the status, qualifications, monetary units and interval through serialization.
Unavailable results withhold monetary estimates and intervals rather than
becoming a confident zero. Interrupted unfinished fits can be retried from
immutable inputs; no partial-fit checkpoint or distributed recovery is implied.

Independent review must reconstruct the nuisance-bias and filtration arguments
and challenge the retained small-sample failure. Bank validation requires
authentic assignment, source-clock and audit evidence, an independently
invariant pilot when that route is used, and a decision-relevant precision
target. Consumer maintainers must accept the real interface; platform operators
must measure complete workloads under intended storage and faults. Workflow
evaluation must price pilot/audit acquisition, active labor and rework at matched
output quality. These are additional observations, not consequences of an
author having obtained a passing calculation or a useful local memory ratio.
