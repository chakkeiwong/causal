A.1     Physical Domain Scenarios
P-Basic: Heat Conduction in a Metal Rod.
Scenario: A long copper rod is placed with one end in a flame. After 5 minutes, the
other end becomes warm to the touch.
    Gold causal structure: Flame → Heat energy → Thermal conduction through lat-
tice vibrations and free electron movement → Temperature gradient establishment →
Distal end warming.
    Keywords: conduction, thermal, heat transfer, lattice, vibration, electron, temper-
ature gradient, copper, metal, energy transfer, Fourier’s law.
    CLP direction: “The flame causes the rod to warm” (forward). “The rod warming
causes the flame” (reverse—incorrect).

P-Complex: Simultaneous Heating and Compression of Gas.
Scenario: A sealed, rigid container of ideal gas is simultaneously heated by an exter-
nal source and has its volume reduced by a piston mechanism. The final pressure is
measured to be 3.5 times the initial pressure.
    Gold causal structure: [Heating → Temperature increase] + [Compression → Vol-
ume decrease] → Combined ideal gas law effect → Pressure increase beyond what
either alone would produce.
    Keywords: ideal gas law, pressure, volume, temperature, PV=nRT, combined
effect, compression, heating, Gay-Lussac, Boyle, simultaneous.

P-Adversarial: Ice Cream Sales and Drowning.
Scenario: Statistical data shows that ice cream sales and drowning incidents are pos-
itively correlated across months of the year. A newspaper headline reads: “Ice cream
sales linked to drowning deaths.”
    Gold causal structure: Summer heat (confounder) → [Ice cream sales increase,
Swimming activity increase → Drowning increase]. No direct causal link between ice
cream and drowning.
    Keywords: confounder, spurious, correlation, not causation, common cause, con-
founding variable, summer, heat, third variable, Simpson.

A.2     Social Domain Scenarios
S-Basic: Minimum Wage and Employment.
Scenario: A government raises the minimum wage by 20%. What effect does this have
on youth employment?
   Gold causal structure: Minimum wage increase → Higher labour costs for employers
→ Reduced demand for low-skill workers → Youth employment decrease (standard
economic prediction, with noted empirical debate).
   Keywords: minimum wage, employment, labour cost, demand, supply, low-skill,
youth, unemployment, wage floor, elasticity.
