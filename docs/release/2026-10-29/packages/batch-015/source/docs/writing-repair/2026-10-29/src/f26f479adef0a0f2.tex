\section{Native temporal and interventional engines: preserving the scientific request}
\label{exec16:cxx-temporal}

A bank considering a native discovery engine first needs to specify what is
to be learned. A graph of relationships among funding rates, balances and
market conditions is different from the response of balances to a controlled
rate change. A transition model for delinquency is different again: it can
predict the next recorded state without identifying the effect of a servicing
action. The R, Python and Java comparisons above make these distinctions at
their language boundaries. The same distinctions govern a C++ implementation,
even when its numerical kernel is fast and its graph output looks familiar.

Five families illuminate different parts of this choice. The C++ kernel in
pcalg implements search using known intervention targets through an R interface.
aGrUM supplies a native probabilistic-model engine, while the inspected dynamic
network helper is Python. Mocapy++ supplies dynamic-model inference and parameter
learning for structures supplied by the user. sDBN contains native temporal
structure-training paths. BayesNetty contributes a constrained genetic-network
search reference. Their capabilities overlap in representation and numerical
work, but their statistical experiments and result meanings differ.
The comparison uses cutoff-compatible source revisions and the technical
content of the original GIES paper; it does not treat repository search
metadata as evidence of implemented capability. \citep{exec16_ct_pcalg,exec16_ct_agrum,exec16_ct_mocapy,exec16_ct_sdbn,exec16_ct_bayesnetty,exec14_pt_paper_gies}

\subsection{A common banking question and two indistinguishable observed laws}

Let $X$ be a centered, standardized funding-rate decision variable and $Y$ a
centered retained-balance index. Consider two deliberately small causal models:
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \mathcal W_1:&X=U_X,&Y=\tfrac12X+\varepsilon_Y,\\
 \mathcal W_2:&Y=U_Y,&X=\tfrac12Y+\varepsilon_X.
 \end{array}
\]
In each world the root variable is standard normal, the error has variance
$3/4$, and root and error are independent. Both observational distributions
are centered Gaussian with covariance
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently a better fit to this observational law cannot distinguish the
two directions. Temporal labels alone do not repair this example if both
variables describe the same decision interval and their within-interval order
has not been justified.

Now replace the equation for $X$ by an independently randomized draw with
variance four. In $\mathcal W_1$, the intervention yields
$\operatorname{Var}(Y)=7/4$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=2$; in
$\mathcal W_2$, it yields variance one and covariance zero. This is an
information change, not merely an extra argument supplied to an optimizer.
The known intervention must actually replace the appropriate structural
mechanism while preserving the other stipulated mechanisms.

For a finite increase from $\operatorname{do}(X=0)$ to
$\operatorname{do}(X=1)$, suppose that one unit of $Y$ is worth USD~20 and
the action costs USD~4. The net effects are
\[
 V_1=20(1/2)-4=6,\qquad V_2=20(0)-4=-4.
\]
These are exact authored examples, not bank measurements. Their purpose is to
show why a representative graph cannot silently become the bank's selected
causal truth: the same observational fit permits opposite action decisions.
Acquiring useful intervention information may be worth considering, but its
value depends on the decision and acquisition cost, not the number of arrows
that a search happens to orient. \citep{exec16_ct_oracle}

\subsection{pcalg: a native interventional kernel with an R contract}

The selected pcalg version is 2.7-12, represented by its September 2024 CRAN
source revision. Hauser and B\"uhlmann formulate independent observations with
known intervention targets; the data need not be identically distributed
across targets. Their conservative-target condition leaves each variable
unmanipulated in at least one regime. These conditions explain the local
score: observations from regimes that intervene on node $j$ do not estimate
that node's original conditional mechanism. In the paper's DREAM4 application,
uncertain-target perturbations are excluded. This establishes a limit of that
application and formulation, not a finding that all later methods fail with
unknown targets. The paper also leaves consistency of its GIES procedure as a
question; that statement must not be turned into a claim about all subsequent
consistency literature. \citep{exec14_pt_paper_gies}

The implementation preserves this experiment through several layers. The R
constructor validates target membership and row counts, sorts rows by target
index, and constructs a nonintervened-row index for each node. The C++ entry
converts one-based R vertex and row indices to zero-based indices. The score
factory distinguishes raw Gaussian, scatter-matrix Gaussian and R callback
paths. A proposed bridge must therefore preserve row identities, target
families, labels and score configuration together. Copying a numerical matrix
and an adjacency list into a nominally equivalent native routine is not a
complete port. \citep{exec16_ct_pcalg}

For node $j$, write $N_j=\{i:j\notin I_i\}$, where $I_i$ is the known
intervention target for row $i$, and let $n_j=|N_j|$. Up to constants that do
not affect the relevant search comparison, the inspected Gaussian local score
has the form
\[
 s_j(P)=-\frac{n_j}{2}\left[1+\log\left\{
       \frac{\operatorname{RSS}_{j,P}}{n_j}\right\}\right]
       -\lambda(1+|P|).
\]
Here $P$ is the candidate parent set and RSS is the residual sum of squares
on the nonintervened rows, with the configured intercept. The default penalty
uses half the logarithm of the total row count. The likelihood expression is
not a dependent-panel uncertainty correction. Overlapping time windows and
shared calendar shocks need their own statistical justification before this
independent-row experiment is claimed.

Configuration identity matters even before any temporal extension. The
manual constructor lists \texttt{intercept=FALSE}, while the inspected R
constructor defaults to \texttt{TRUE}. For a parentless response
$(8,10,10,12)$, fitting its mean gives RSS~8, whereas fixing its mean at zero
gives RSS~408. Holding the penalty fixed, the score difference is
$2\log(51)$, approximately $7.86365$. This is an independent calculation from
the displayed score, not an observed pcalg execution. The remedy is to set and
report the intercept explicitly and test both documented and actual defaults
in a future bridge. The same contract must reject fractional identifiers
before the wrapper's integer coercion, reject zero effective row counts and
nonfinite scores, and retain diagnostics for failed factorizations. The native
Cholesky and QR paths can return NaN on failure; that must not become a valid
zero effect. \citep{exec16_ct_pcalg,exec16_ct_oracle}

GIES returns an estimated interventional essential graph and a representative
DAG. Those are different objects. Its symmetric \texttt{fixedGaps} represents
forbidden adjacency, and adaptive modes can revise the set of gaps. A banking
constraint such as ``an earlier decision cannot be caused by a later balance''
is directional; representing it as forbidden adjacency would also delete a
permitted forward relationship. A bridge should require an explicit constraint
type and validate the returned graph against the requested hard constraints.
The proposed reuse route is a versioned R bridge around the existing native
kernel. A standalone C++ port remains a separate effort because the inspected
interfaces include Rcpp objects, callback support, R preprocessing and
RcppArmadillo/BH dependencies. The GPL-at-least-2 package declaration also
requires a distribution-specific rights review; a language bridge alone does
not settle that question. \citep{exec16_ct_pcalg}

\subsection{aGrUM: unrolling a model while preserving state meaning}

The inspected aGrUM dynamic helper, at its 6 October 2026 revision, checks
time-slice suffixes and selected domain sizes, creates time-indexed variables,
reconstructs parent order, and copies conditional-probability arrays into the
unrolled model. This is valuable model-construction support. It is a Python
helper over the native engine, and the inspected path does not itself learn
a causal temporal structure. An inference engine can answer questions about
a supplied model without establishing that the supplied arrows are causal.
\citep{exec16_ct_agrum}

Equal cardinality is weaker than equal state identity. Suppose the initial
slice lists states as (current, delinquent), while the repeated slice lists
them as (delinquent, current). The intended transition has
\[
 P(D_{t+1}=1\mid D_t=0)=0.1,\qquad
 P(D_{t+1}=1\mid D_t=1)=0.8.
\]
Starting current, the correct two-step delinquency probability is
$0.9(0.1)+0.1(0.8)=0.17$. If a conversion blindly reuses the numeric
parent-state positions after the ordering changes, it instead computes
$0.9(0.8)+0.1(0.1)=0.73$. For a stipulated value $100-40p$, this changes
USD~93.2 into USD~70.8, a USD~22.4 error. This is an authored conversion
counterexample motivated by the source path; it is not an observed pyagrum
runtime result. \citep{exec16_ct_oracle}

The helper's \texttt{is2TBN} checks cardinality without checking identity of
the ordered state labels. The unrolling function's particular invalid-slice
guard combines two conditions with conjunction, so some incomplete slice
sets evade that guard. This is a static control-flow observation; it does not
establish which later operation fails for every malformed model. A proposed
adapter should validate complete slice pairing, ordered state identities,
physical period, dimensions and permitted arrows before unrolling. A
deliberate relabeling needs a probability-preserving permutation of every
affected tensor axis, followed by comparison with a small independently
enumerated model. The inspected file permits LGPL-3-or-later or MIT, supported
by the same-revision license texts; dependency and distribution closure still
need checking. Conditional adoption of supplied-model unrolling is reasonable.
Calling that adoption a newly qualified discovery method is unsupported.
\citep{exec16_ct_agrum,exec16_ct_design}

\subsection{Mocapy++: latent-state modeling is a different investment}

Mocapy++ can serve a supplied dynamic model in which an unobserved borrower
state emits recorded behavior. The inspected manual, version 1.05 from January
2010, explicitly states in Section 3.7 that structure learning is absent.
Its worked source example adds within-slice and between-slice edges before
performing expectation and maximization steps. The sequence masks distinguish
observed, hidden and missing entries. A hidden entry is not an intervention,
and an EM fit of a hidden-state model does not establish that changing a
recorded action would change the hidden state according to the fitted model.
The bounded decision is therefore to reject this version as the proposed
structure-discovery backend while retaining it as a reference for supplied
DBN inference and parameter learning. \citep{exec16_ct_mocapy}

An engineering evaluation would need to justify the legacy environment and
source custody. The inspected mirror is dated February 2019; its build uses
C, C++ and Fortran, and its instructions discuss compiler-matched Boost and
LAPACK. Moreover, the root license contains LGPL2.1 text while the inspected
DBN, EM engine and example headers specify LGPL3-or-later. These observations
require per-file clarification and cannot honestly be compressed into one
unqualified package-license label. Modernizing this code may cost more than
using another permitted inference engine with the same supplied model. The
comparison should measure the cost of preserving the model, masks, numerical
behavior and diagnostics, rather than reward a port merely because its output
has the same shape. \citep{exec16_ct_mocapy,exec16_ct_design}

\subsection{sDBN: temporal training, dependent rows and output interpretation}

The inspected September 2021 sDBN revision contains a C++14 dynamic-network
training program with stationary, nonstationary and already-rolled input
paths. Lag zero means all available time steps. The stationary loader turns
$m$ wide subject records with $T$ time points and lag $L$ into
$(T-L)m$ overlapping transition rows. The node-training code excludes future
parents and parents outside the specified lag, while allowing contemporaneous
candidates. These are concrete temporal mechanisms; they do not by themselves
identify intervention effects or validate a stationary law for irregular bank
records. \citep{exec16_ct_sdbn}

For example, three independent subjects with four observations and lag one
produce nine transition rows. Suppose each subject has one independent,
mean-zero, variance-one shock repeated through its three transition rows.
The mean of all nine rows is the mean of three shocks. Its true variance is
$1/3$, whereas an independence calculation using nine rows gives $1/9$.
An exact enumeration of eight equiprobable sign-shock worlds reproduces the
first result. Overlap can increase numerical work without increasing the
independent information count by the same factor. Likewise, dates
$(0,1,4,5)$ have adjacent gaps $(1,3,1)$: a row lag of one does not specify a
constant physical interval. Preserving original subject and calendar labels
is necessary for splitting, uncertainty and transport to a future decision.
Shared calendar shocks create an additional dependence problem even when
subjects have distinct identifiers. \citep{exec16_ct_oracle}

The output path also deserves a literal reading. The regularization stage
produces allowed directed pairs. The serializer calls this array a
\texttt{skeleton}; it adds a separate directed structure and likelihood only
when a directed-search result is supplied. With \texttt{--no-hill}, the caller
passes no such result. That array should therefore retain its candidate-edge
meaning, rather than be advertised as an acyclic causal graph, an essential
graph or a partial ancestral graph. With directed search enabled, the called
header checks paths before adding edges and restricts reversals to equal-time
nodes with a cycle check. The inspected reversal branch does not explicitly
check membership of the reversed pair in the allowed list. A future prototype
must test and enforce the intended meaning of those restrictions, especially
when starting from a supplied graph. \citep{exec16_ct_sdbn}

There is a further transport issue. The CLI uses standard output for its result
when no output file is supplied. The directed-search routine also prints
diagnostics to standard output inside the result-producing call. Static tracing
therefore predicts a mixed diagnostic/result stream for that path; no native
execution was performed here. A proposed CLI bridge should use a dedicated
result file or separate channel, validate the entire result, and return a
transport failure when parsing fails. It should not silently remove arbitrary
text until something resembling JSON remains. The existing binary hot-start
serialization also motivates an explicit version and platform contract before
checkpoints cross environments. \citep{exec16_ct_sdbn,exec16_ct_design}

Reuse remains conditional. The complete inspected tree contains no README
or license file, so public access does not establish permission to copy or
redistribute the implementation. The build depends on Eigen, LBFGS,
FlatBuffers and OpenSSL, with optional MPI/MKL paths; its release configuration
enables fast-math and contains a host-specific include directory. Resolve
terms, replace that path, freeze dependencies and compare numerical behavior
with conservative compiler settings before considering a bridge. Until then,
sDBN is a source reference and prototype candidate, not an adopted backend.
Independent generated temporal examples remain possible without copying its
implementation. \citep{exec16_ct_sdbn}

\subsection{BayesNetty: constraints and failure values need precise translation}

BayesNetty's selected June 2025 source documents genetic-network modeling,
greedy search and random or jittered restarts. Its score documentation assumes
multinomial discrete nodes and normal continuous nodes. These sources make it
a useful constrained-network reference, but do not qualify a temporal or
interventional banking estimator. The input documentation gives ordered
blacklist edges. If both directions appear in a whitelist, an adjacency is
required while either orientation is allowed. A whitelist/blacklist conflict
is documented as an error. \citep{exec16_ct_bayesnetty}

These conventions differ from pcalg's symmetric gaps. In a two-node DAG
universe, a forbidden adjacency permits only the empty graph; banning only
$A\to B$ also permits $B\to A$; requiring adjacency permits either one-edge
orientation. Two whitelist lines do not represent an unmeasured common cause
with a bidirected edge. The proposed conversion must preserve the predicate
being asserted, with endpoint semantics carried separately from input-file
conventions. \citep{exec16_ct_bayesnetty,exec16_ct_pcalg,exec16_ct_oracle}

Failure values must also retain their meaning. The inspected serial search
method returns numeric zero when its initial network has a loop. Its caller
has not been traced in this comparison, so this does not establish the final
CLI behavior. It does establish why a wrapper cannot assume that every numeric
return is a successful score. The prospective contract needs an independent
acyclicity check and an explicit failure status. The GPL3 source and documented
compiler route support a conditional reference decision, with dependency,
licensing and target semantics checked before a wider integration is enabled.
\citep{exec16_ct_bayesnetty,exec16_ct_design}

\subsection{Reuse choices and the next affordable experiment}

The resulting choices are complementary. Their common prerequisite is an
explicit scientific request, not a universal adjacency-matrix interface.

\begin{longtable}{P{0.15\textwidth}P{0.34\textwidth}P{0.43\textwidth}}
\toprule
Family & Proposed role & Condition before enabling reuse\\
\midrule
\endhead
pcalg & R bridge to the native known-target search & Explicit intercept, regimes,
indices, graph class and constraints; qualified sampling law and distribution terms.\\
aGrUM & Adopt supplied-model construction conditionally & Validate slice/state
identity and tensor conversion; distinguish inference from discovery.\\
Mocapy++ & Reference supplied DBN inference; reject as this structure learner &
Resolve mirror, file licenses and legacy numerical environment.\\
sDBN & Reference and temporal prototype candidate & Obtain reuse terms; qualify
clock, row dependence, candidate/directed outputs and transport.\\
BayesNetty & Reference constrained search & Preserve constraint predicates and
failure meaning; establish a suitable target before temporal/effect claims.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

A proposed CLI or MCP request should bind the population, independent sampling
unit, physical and availability clocks, intervention family, regime-to-row map,
state dictionaries, hard constraints, score options and intended output class.
The response should retain the selected graph class, candidate-versus-final
status, diagnostics and uncertainty scope. An unqualified regime is an
unsupported request; a failed factorization is a computation failure; an
equivalence class is unresolved orientation, not a parser failure. Neither
transport should convert one category into another. This is a proposed
interoperability contract, not a report that these external engines have
already been integrated. \citep{exec16_ct_design}

The next affordable experiment uses generated regimes and finite models with
known answers. It compares the two Gaussian worlds, intercept settings,
permuted regime/row identifiers, reordered state dictionaries, repeated subject
windows, irregular dates, contradictory constraints and malformed output.
Where reuse terms and an environment are available, a frozen upstream run can
then be compared with the independent reference on identical inputs. All
requests, failures and unavailable results remain in the accounting. Public
credit or mortgage records may supply realistic covariate ranges, missingness
patterns and calendars under their applicable terms; generated assignments
and responses must remain labeled as generated. Such observations cannot
retroactively supply authentic intervention truth.

Measure preparation, rights review, compilation, conversion, diagnostics,
scientific review and maintenance as well as numerical runtime. The USD~10
decision difference between the Gaussian worlds and USD~22.4 conversion error
show which meanings deserve early tests; they are not estimates of prototype
profitability. A costly bank experiment becomes justified only when a permitted
prototype resolves a consequential uncertainty and specifies what extra bank
information could change the decision. \citep{exec16_ct_oracle,exec16_ct_design}

The search remains bounded. The inherited scholarly query includes duplicate
and publication aliases, and the native repository queries leave relevant
families such as DBN++, EISALab/DBN and pdbntk uninspected. Name and language
metadata cannot decide their causal capability. Missing originals and rights
have concrete acquisition routes; broader search requires additional pinned
technical inspection. Useful dependent graph/effect uncertainty, latent
confounding, unknown interventions and bank transport require further
scientific work and cannot be promised by completing that acquisition.
These open branches do not prevent the proposed contract or the generated
reference experiment from progressing. \citep{exec16_ct_design}
