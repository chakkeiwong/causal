\section{Julia and MATLAB panel estimators: matching the decision before the interface}
\label{sec:exec25-panels-other}
Other-language coverage cannot be inferred from the presence of a regression
routine or a familiar estimator name. A branch-policy rollout requires a
specified adoption history, eligible comparison groups, baseline periods,
aggregation weights and uncertainty calculation. The inspected set includes
the Julia DiffinDiffs suite and its interaction-weighted implementation,
DiDInt, and two independent MATLAB repositories. Forty original files are
authenticated to selected pre-cutoff Git trees. Repository searches identify
additional candidates, but do not provide exhaustive coverage of all archives,
commercial offerings or languages. No downloaded package was executed.
\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}

\subsection{Resolve suite names, archived aliases and dependencies}
The selected DiffinDiffs repository is a suite: its short entry module
reexports DiffinDiffsBase and InteractionWeightedDIDs. The older standalone
InteractionWeightedDIDs repository explicitly points to the suite's monorepo
and says that development moved there. It should not count as a second
independent estimator. At the selected 10 April 2024 monorepo commit, the
suite declares version 0.2.0 and its interaction-weighted component declares
0.3.2. The latter names FixedEffectModels, FixedEffects and Vcov dependencies
with version ranges. The previously inspected FixedEffectModels 2.1.0 IV/FE
source is a useful separate comparator, but cannot silently replace this
component's declared 1.10 compatibility line. A resolved environment must
identify the actual delegates before native comparisons. The inspected
Julia notices are MIT-style grants; downstream data and dependency rights
remain separate.~\citep{exec25_panelother_julia,exec23_otheriv_source_0}

The treatment type declares absorbing binary treatment, allowing effects to
evolve over time. Its excluded relative periods define an omitted baseline
or other sample restriction. Separate parallel-trend types specify
never-treated or not-yet-treated comparison relations; an unspecified type
suppresses trend-specific sample alterations. These declarations organize
an analysis under assumptions. They do not prove parallel untreated trends,
absence of anticipation or absence of spillovers between branches. A service
must preserve which relation was requested and distinguish an asserted
restriction from one contradicted by the recorded treatment history.
\citep{exec25_panelother_julia,exec25_panelother_choice}

For example, a servicing regime with history $(0,1,0)$ has first adoption in
period two, but reconstructing treatment from that date as an absorbing policy
produces $(0,1,1)$. The last observation changes meaning. The adapter must
refuse that conversion or select a method that explicitly models reversals;
it cannot quietly reinterpret the policy. Similarly, dropping the period
immediately before adoption is not by itself a justification for an
anticipation restriction. The interval during which staff or customers
can respond must be part of the banking question.~\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}

\subsection{Aggregation and fixed effects are substantive choices}
The Julia interaction-weighted source constructs cohort/relative-time cells,
records their counts and sample-weight totals, estimates coefficients and
retains their covariance. Its aggregation routine constructs a weight matrix
$W$ and returns $W'\widehat\beta$ with covariance $W'\widehat V W$.
The inspected code normalizes cohort weights within each relative-time group.
Consequently the grouping variables, retained coefficient subset and returned
weight matrix are part of the estimand, not formatting details. The source
offers optional cell-level least-squares weights, which can help reconcile
why specifications disagree.~\citep{exec25_panelother_julia}

If two cohort effects are one and four units of prevented loss, equal cohort
weights give $5/2$, while weights $(3/4,1/4)$ give $7/4$. A bank allocating
equal attention to regions and a bank weighting every treated account equally
may reasonably choose different targets. Their results should not be labeled
inconsistent until the targets agree. The proposed output therefore carries
the cohort-time cells, weight basis, missing-cell exclusions, aggregation
matrix and monetary conversion alongside the estimate.~\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}

An exact six-cell example also prevents a generic two-way fixed-effects
regression from being treated as a universal staggered-adoption estimator.
Two cohorts have treatment histories $(0,1,1)$ and $(0,0,1)$, untreated
outcomes zero, and observed outcomes $(0,1,4)$ and $(0,0,1)$. Every realized
treated effect is positive, with average two. Residualizing treatment against
cohort and period effects gives a regression coefficient of $-1/2$.
The independent fraction calculation establishes the algebraic example,
not package performance. There is also no untreated comparison group left
in the final period, so a modern cohort estimator must not invent support
for that cell. Scientific identification and numerical regression output
are separate acceptance questions.~\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}

The inspected Julia default covariance is heteroskedasticity-robust; cluster
covariance is a separately selected option. Fixed-effect singleton removal
and missing cluster identifiers can change the estimation sample. The
residualization helper warns on nonconvergence and records convergence
information. The proposed wrapper should expose that state and enforce its
declared acceptance rule, rather than interpreting every returned coefficient
as a qualified solution. Cluster unit, small-cluster treatment, dependence
assumptions and the full sample mask must be retained.~\citep{exec25_panelother_julia}

\subsection{DiDInt separates covariate adjustment and inference choices}
The selected DiDInt revision of 7 June 2026 declares version 0.9.6 and Julia
1.10 compatibility. Its interface accepts treatment dates or a cohort
variable, covariates, aggregation and weighting choices, a not-yet-treated
control option, heteroskedasticity corrections, jackknife settings and
randomization repetitions. The inspected implementation constructs
state-time differences after covariate adjustment. Missing outcomes, group
identifiers, times or selected covariates are dropped with warnings. Such
filtering needs an exported sample ledger because eligibility can differ
across effects and inference procedures.~\citep{exec25_panelother_didint}

The selected least-squares helper catches solve failures and returns missing,
while the covariance helper explicitly inverts the cross-product matrix and
implements HC0--HC4 corrections. Those are distinct paths with distinct
rank and leverage conditions; a successful mean estimate does not establish
valid covariance. Randomization inference checks the availability of
required state/difference pairs, limits requested permutations to the number
of unique assignments, and permutes state and treatment-time assignments.
Exchangeability of those assignments must be justified by the design;
permutation machinery alone does not randomize a historical banking rollout.
\citep{exec25_panelother_didint,exec25_panelother_choice}

One conditional source detail warrants a focused test. In the branch for
an edge-case calculation combined with the additive covariate setting, the
code warns that the option is unavailable and evaluates
\texttt{edgecase == false}, an equality expression, rather than assigning
false. Other branches can change the flag separately. This is a static
branch-level finding, not an observed native failure. Qualification must
test the actual option combination and resulting status before deciding
whether the surrounding conditions make it reachable or consequential.
\citep{exec25_panelother_didint}

\subsection{MATLAB implementations need their own algorithm and rights audit}
The selected DiDToolbox source declares MATLAB 2021+ and the Statistics and
Machine Learning Toolbox. Its complete 103-entry tree contains no standalone
license file; no package-wide reuse permission is established by the inspected
README or source subset. A downloadable toolbox or public Git repository
does not establish rights to adopt or redistribute it. It is also an
independent repository, not evidence that its estimators are native
MathWorks capabilities or available in GNU Octave.~\citep{exec25_panelother_matlab}

The method named IW delegates to the CS engine, selects its unconditional
path and packages the returned cohort-time/event-time aggregates. The source
labels a cohort-share option as its intended IW weighting. This is a concrete
implementation route that must be compared with the target specification;
the name alone does not establish finite-sample equivalence to the Julia
regression estimator. The selected CS path defines a baseline
$g-\Delta-1$, uses not-yet-treated controls satisfying $G>t+\Delta$
while excluding the target cohort, and skips cells without the necessary
baseline, treated observations, comparison observations or positive weight.
For $g=3$, $\Delta=1$ and $t=3$, the baseline is one and a comparison cohort
adopting at four is ineligible. Output denominators must disclose such
support losses.~\citep{exec25_panelother_matlab,exec25_panelother_choice}

The selected first-treatment helper takes the earliest observed treatment
indicator. That date alone cannot establish irreversibility, and its mapping
back to rows needs an ordering fixture. The basic panel validator checks
columns and types, rather than supplying a full causal-design audit. A
separate sensitivity helper solves linear programs over smoothness or
relative-magnitude restrictions using pre-period coefficients, marginal
standard errors and an optional target standard error. Its interface does
not take a full coefficient covariance matrix. Its deterministic extrema,
pointwise intervals and selected numeric solver must not be presented as
the same inferential object as a separately qualified joint-uncertainty
procedure. Failed solver cells remain missing in its initialized output.
\citep{exec25_panelother_matlab}

The second MATLAB candidate, FDiD, has a GPLv3 notice and a selected factorial
interaction implementation. Its direct within transformation subtracts unit
and time means and adds the overall mean once. On the unbalanced rows
$(A,1,1)$, $(A,2,2)$ and $(B,1,3)$, that formula produces residuals
$(-1/2,1/2,0)$, whose time-group sums are nonzero. Thus the formula is not
a general two-way projection for arbitrary unbalanced panels. This exact
authored witness identifies a declared-design or residualization requirement;
it is not a vendor runtime observation.~\citep{exec25_panelother_matlab,exec25_panelother_choice}

There is also a conditional coefficient-label issue with two covariates.
The inspected design concatenates all $X_jZ$ columns before all $GX_jZ$
columns, while its name loop alternates those two names for each covariate.
For two covariates, positions four and five consequently receive the other
term's name in the selected construction. A pseudoinverse can address a
numerical linear system without correcting such semantic labeling or
establishing identification. The qualification suite must compare every
coefficient's column identity, rank status, finite-cluster correction and
target meaning. The paper linked by the repository is not newly admitted
as an inspected scholarly source in this software-only review.
\citep{exec25_panelother_matlab,exec25_panelother_choice}

\subsection{A matched public qualification route}
These findings support component-specific reference decisions and proposed
adapters, while leaving this language-coverage cell inconclusive. Remaining
Julia leads, gretl's primary distribution, other MATLAB packages, complete
dependency licenses and native environments still require inspection.
Zero hits from the recorded Octave or gretl repository queries establish
search limits, not absence. The MATLAB candidates' platform requirements
also cannot clear a commercial MathWorks toolbox decision.
\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}

The proposed evaluation freezes a branch adoption calendar, assignment law,
potential outcomes, anticipation and reversal policy, cohort-time target,
weights, missingness mechanism, cluster structure and scientific refusal
rules. Finite generated panels provide known truth; permitted public panels
add realistic data preparation. Compare identical admitted cells across
an independent reference, permitted native candidates and the proposed
adapter. Include unsupported final-period effects, altered row ordering,
missing clusters, singular designs, nonconverged residualization, two-covariate
labels, interrupted execution and infeasible sensitivity programs.
Scientific owners approve targets and assumptions; implementers preserve
sample and coefficient identity; platform/rights owners select environments;
evaluators own complete attempt and cost denominators. An authored USD 9,600
total gives USD 40 per 240 attempts, USD 60 per 160 completions and USD 120
per 80 admissible results. These are proposed tests and hypothetical costs,
not native benchmarks or prices.~\citep{exec25_panelother_choice}
