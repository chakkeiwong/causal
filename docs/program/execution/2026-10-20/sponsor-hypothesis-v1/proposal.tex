\section{The decision this proposal

A universal bank needs to know when a proposed action is likely to
improve a financial outcome, which assumptions make that answer
possible, and when the available information cannot distinguish
competing decisions. The proposed investment is a small reusable
causal-analysis service supporting four journeys: funding responses,
sequential servicing, intervention queries after causal discovery, and
the construction of trustworthy observations. Its immediate deliverable
is a bounded prototype and an evidence-based recommendation about what
to build, reuse or leave unsupported.

The recommendation is to develop and compare a narrow set of these
operations using public records, documented public experiments and
generated cases with known truth. Existing local research supplies
selected working constructions and substantial negative evidence. It
does not yet establish a complete product or a positive return on
deployment. Expensive bank experiments should follow a prototype result
that makes their cost worth considering. The absence of bank data,
institutional sign-off or production access does not prevent completion
of the present proposal. \citep{sp13_scope}

\hypertarget{the-reusable-product-and-its-customer}{%
\subsection{The reusable product and its
customer}\label{the-reusable-product-and-its-customer}}

The first user is an analyst who can state a banking decision and
inspect its assumptions. The reusable input records a target population,
action and comparator, information available before the action, outcome
and horizon, measurement units, observation process, causal restrictions
and sampling unit. An offer, its receipt and the later balance are
different variables. A measured association is not automatically the
effect of changing an offer.

The output keeps the target attached to its evidence. It may be an
identified formula, an estimate with justified uncertainty, a set of
compatible effects, an informative refusal or a failed computation.
Unresolved graph endpoints, unbounded confidence sets and numerical
failures require different responses. The library, command line and MCP
interface should preserve these distinctions so that a downstream
application can decide whether to act, ask for information or fall back
to its existing workflow. A callable endpoint alone does not establish
identification, useful precision or interoperability.
\citep{sp13_interfaces}

The shared layer would organize three workspaces: intervention studies,
sequential decisions and dynamic systems. The four banking journeys pass
through these workspaces, with observation quality affecting all three.
This organization avoids building a separate estimation stack for every
business team while retaining each team's population, timing and
economic account. The initial service would require analyst review of
consequential scientific choices. No autonomous bank action is proposed.

\hypertarget{a-concrete-first-slice}{%
\subsection{A concrete first slice}\label{a-concrete-first-slice}}

A useful first demonstration begins with a public or generated business
brief, constructs an explicit question and alternative diagrams, checks
the available identification route, and returns a result whose
assumptions and information limits remain visible through the library,
CLI and MCP. For a randomized offer, the first target can be the effect
of assignment. A receipt effect requires additional compliance and
exclusion assumptions. For a confounded observational table, the correct
result may be a documented ambiguity rather than a number.

The comparison must include an expert-designed template and a directly
composed existing-package workflow. The new service earns its place only
if the common representation and checks reduce consequential mistakes or
repeated effort without discarding valid alternatives. A generated
example can expose a wrong answer cheaply; it cannot establish how often
the same failure occurs at a bank.

\hypertarget{funding-and-observations}{%
\subsection{Funding and observations}\label{funding-and-observations}}

Earlier registered synthetic funding work found a passing construction
under a demanding information design: known randomized action moments,
truthful sampled links, known inclusion probabilities and independent
calendars. Its six valid cells passed the prespecified minimum-coverage
screen. False exogeneity, wrong inclusion probabilities and label errors
retained adverse findings. This supports a selected procedure
conditional on its information law, rather than a general correction for
one dependent bank panel. \citep{sp13_funding}

The newer joint-dependence argument makes that limitation more explicit.
A finite confidence-region construction can use declared nuisance bounds
and conservative projection. Its representative precision is inadequate
under the reviewed design. An exact persistence argument also shows why
an unrestricted claim cannot obtain useful shrinking uncertainty merely
by lengthening a single highly persistent series. The practical next
choices are to narrow the dependence family, obtain justified additional
information, or accept wider and possibly unbounded results. The new
derivation has not been turned into a qualified numerical projection
service. \citep{sp13_persistence}

Observation linkage is part of the scientific problem. If different
customer histories are observationally compatible but imply different
intervention values, more computation on the same summaries cannot
choose among them. Public/generated linkage cases can test whether the
prototype preserves this ambiguity. A later bank study would need
representative truth labels and an explicit inclusion mechanism; labels
with unknown selection do not establish the same result.
\citep{sp13_public,sp13_persistence}

\hypertarget{servicing-and-learned-policies}{%
\subsection{Servicing and learned
policies}\label{servicing-and-learned-policies}}

Selected earlier servicing studies support fixed-policy inference in a
restricted nonlinear family under truthful state audits, supplied
historical action probabilities and independent customer trajectories.
Their wrong-logger and information-leakage cases remain failures. They
do not establish the value of an unrestricted learned policy with hidden
borrower state. \citep{sp13_servicing}

A new argument handles estimated action and observation probabilities in
a finite, prespecified history table with bounded rewards. It separates
training from evaluation and makes the support and observation
assumptions explicit. Exact calculations check its policy-value
identities and counterexamples. Its conservative uncertainty can
nevertheless exceed the complete target range at a representative sample
size. The public servicing design uses continuous histories and
unbounded Gaussian/quadratic quantities, so this finite-table theorem
cannot simply be attached to that design. A useful next study needs a
reviewed tighter argument, a separately declared bounded target, or a
different experiment. It must preserve the failed predecessor rather
than change the target after evaluation.
\citep{sp13_servicing_successor}

\hypertarget{discovery-and-agent-support}{%
\subsection{Discovery and agent
support}\label{discovery-and-agent-support}}

Graph discovery constrains possible relationships under stated
assumptions. It may return an equivalence class whose members imply
different interventions. The proposed service must retain that class and
distinguish uncertainty in graph learning from uncertainty in an effect.
Exact latent-risk examples in the current comparison exhibit identical
observed Gaussian distributions with action effects of zero and three
fifths. They prove nonidentification within a class containing both
models; they do not enumerate the entire identified set.
\citep{exec13_latent_examples}

LLMs are proposed as aids for translating business language, finding
missing timing definitions, suggesting diagrams and alternatives, and
preparing auditable tool requests. A model-generated diagram is a
candidate assumption, not observed causal truth. The evaluation must
score false confidence, unsupported orientations, omitted alternatives
and appropriate requests for information. Deterministic task fixtures
and exact examples already support some checks; no live-model
superiority or bank decision gain is established by the new task
designs. A live comparison would require a frozen backend, held-out
tasks, reference adjudication, costs and equally informed baselines.
\citep{sp13_public}

its stipulated likelihood. A proposed deposit-diagram assistant should
preserve response frequencies, semantic categories, admitted graph
support and the evidence for adviser accuracy separately. Adopt these

A new exact servicing example separates causal attribution from
incremental value. The stipulated priority route suppresses an available
backup; either executed route succeeds. Removing priority alone restores
the backup, whereas removing both priority and backup removes recovery.
With assumed USD 200 success value, USD 40 priority cost and USD 10
executed-backup cost, priority returns USD 160 net and backup USD 190
when backup is available. Priority thus provides the factual recovery
path while adding minus USD 30 net value. If backup availability is
unknown before action and has stipulated probability \(p\), priority's
expected advantage is \(160-190p\) USD per case, positive exactly when
\(p<16/19\). This is an authored structural and monetary construction,
not a DEF-1 implementation, model trial or bank ROI.
\citep{exec18_reasoning_reference}

The prototype recommendation is to evaluate mechanism propagation,
attribution, intervention semantics and prospective value as distinct
tasks. Freeze the rubric before evaluation, adjudicate disputed
transcripts, and keep generated graph families separate from those used
to develop the rules. Public and synthetic evidence can establish
whether the proposed interfaces preserve those contracts and whether
further experiments are worthwhile. The truth of a bank's assignment and
audit processes still needs later institution-specific evidence.

workflow separates research tools, model evaluation, human simulation
and cognitive explanation, and calls for instrument validation, held-out
assessment, controlled execution and analysis that respects dependence.
It offers a design framework and illustrative code, not an empirical
validation of a banking assistant. \citep{exec20_validity}

An exact proposal example makes the investment implication visible. True
correctness is 60\% in both workflows. The first grader sensitivity and
specificity are 80\% and 90\%; for the second workflow they are 95\% and
80\%. Observed passing rises from 52\% to 65\% despite unchanged
correctness. Under an assumed value of USD 100 per additional correct
result and extra workflow cost of USD 8, the grade-based incremental
value is USD 5 while the true incremental value is USD -8. These are
imposed probabilities and planning values. Held-out calibration and its
uncertainty are necessary before correcting real scores.
\citep{exec20_validity_reference}

The proposed public evaluation would preserve whole held-out mechanisms
and scenario families, compare frozen workflows on paired scenarios,
record useful refusals and every failed request, and verify grading on
both arms. Repeated answers do not create independent scenarios;
averaging cannot remove shared environmental shocks. A fixed model
comparison supports claims about the tested versions and scenario
population, with transfer to future models and customers requiring
separate evidence. Costs should include grading and adjudication as well
as model execution. This defines a prototype evaluation to justify later
bank experiments; it does not claim that such an experiment has been
run.

\hypertarget{use-expert-guided-ensembles-to-propose-graphs-with-explicit-limits}{%
\subsection{Use expert-guided ensembles to propose graphs with explicit
limits}\label{use-expert-guided-ensembles-to-propose-graphs-with-explicit-limits}}

Tench and Demeester combine discovery algorithms by counting
connections, separating orientation, checking cycles and querying an
expert on selected disagreements. Their method is a candidate for the
proposed diagram-review workflow. Its main evidence uses synthetic
categorical networks. A narrower five-network model study reports mean
orientation accuracy of 93.3\% and existence accuracy of 66.4\%, while
existence accuracy is 43.9\% on Property. Responses are cached across
repeated seeds and sample sizes. These reported results support further
comparison, with domain dependence, shared errors and memorization
concerns retained. \citep{exec20_expert_averaging}

The proposed interface must distinguish a plausible causal path from a
direct edge and preserve unresolved graph types. Two printed algorithm
details require source inspection before reuse: rejected existence calls
appear not to increment the budget, and the exhausted orientation branch
omits the majority fallback described in the prose. These are
specification discrepancies, not demonstrated runtime defects. Physical
calls, both argument orders, retries and cache reuse must be accounted
for before estimating operating cost.
\citep{exec20_expert_averaging_reference}

A graph vote is also not a calibrated probability of an intervention
effect. An exact example gives the same observational Gaussian
distribution under two models whose effects are 0.6 and zero. With
assumed value USD 100 per outcome unit and intervention cost USD 40,
their margins are USD 20 and USD -40. Giving the first model an
unjustified 90\% probability based on its vote yields an apparent USD 14
margin. Valid temporal or experimental information could resolve the
direction; algorithm agreement alone cannot. Public prototype evaluation
should therefore compare intervention answers, useful refusal, ambiguity
and total review cost alongside graph accuracy. This retains the method
as a proposal candidate while source identity, rights and executable
conformance remain to be qualified.
\citep{exec20_expert_averaging_reference}

\hypertarget{preserve-graph-meaning-when-reusing-discovery-software}{%
\subsection{Preserve graph meaning when reusing discovery
software}\label{preserve-graph-meaning-when-reusing-discovery-software}}

The gCastle recommendation retains selected PC and linear NOTEARS
methods as references and proposes a pinned original Python worker for a
public prototype. PC can return unresolved orientations, while NOTEARS
returns weighted directions. An unresolved pair must survive conversion;
it cannot silently become two causal arrows or a claim of hidden
confounding. Explicit variable identifiers, units, restrictions and
failure states must pass unchanged through the library, CLI and MCP. The
selected source review also exposes dependency differences, limited
input guards and conditional-test support cases that the prototype must
address. \citep{exec20_gcastle_source}

Exact authored examples show why these contracts matter. Two weights of
0.01 have an acyclicity residual of \(5\times10^{-9}\), below
\(10^{-8}\), but remain a directed cycle at a deliberately chosen
threshold of 0.005; the default 0.3 threshold removes them. This is a
mathematical witness, not an observed package failure. In a separate
stipulated causal model, a coefficient of 0.3, an intervention of 100
units and a cost of USD 20 give USD 10; a mistaken graph transposition
gives USD -20. Fitted weights still need an identification argument
before carrying that economic interpretation.
\citep{exec20_gcastle_reference}

The selected upstream PC and NOTEARS test methods log caught exceptions
without asserting graph correctness. The prototype should therefore use
explicit expected graph and failure results, and measure setup,
computation, conversion and maintenance cost. Apache 2.0 source terms do
not complete a dependency rights inventory. An original worker is the
preferred experiment; a new port lacks a demonstrated platform or cost
justification. This completes the bounded reference decision while
native admission and bank usefulness remain unqualified.
\citep{exec20_gcastle_source}

\hypertarget{compare-network-effects-under-the-same-intervention}{%
\subsection{Compare network effects under the same
intervention}\label{compare-network-effects-under-the-same-intervention}}

The commercial comparison now has primary technical profiles for Stata,
SAS/ETS, ArcGIS Pro and Statsig. Spatial regressions, exposure
experiments and population switchbacks answer different questions. Stata
documents average own, spillover and total derivatives; ArcGIS documents
simple impacts with the direct impact equal to the coefficient. SAS
documents supplied-network model and covariance choices, including
default row normalization and missing weights replaced by zero. These
semantics must remain explicit in any common interface.
\citep{exec20_network_stata_spregress,exec20_network_stata_impacts,exec20_network_stata_panel,exec20_network_sas_latest_spatial,exec20_network_arcgis_method,exec20_network_arcgis_tool}

In an exact two-branch feedback example, an assumed coefficient of USD
10 produces an own response of USD 13.33 and portfolio response of USD
20. At assumed cost USD 12, the corresponding margins are USD -2, USD
1.33 and USD 8. Omitting one network tie changes the modeled portfolio
margin to USD -2. A separate covariance example gives variance 28 with
the full parameter covariance and 20 when the cross term is dropped.
These are authored references, not vendor results or bank returns.
\citep{exec20_network_reference}

Statsig's saved documentation supplies concrete alternatives: analysis
across organization/user units and switchback windows with attribution,
burn periods and bucket bootstrapping. A prototype must still qualify
cluster boundaries, exposure support, schedule randomization, carryover
and dependence. An authored time trend gives an observed contrast of 102
for a causal effect of 2; alternating treatment with carryover gives an
observed -2, an immediate effect of 2 and a sustained effect of 6.
Repeated computation does not resolve an undefined target or missing
assignment information.
\citep{exec20_network_statsig_types,exec20_network_statsig_units,exec20_network_statsig_switchback,exec20_network_reference}

Public or generated data suffice for the first contract and computation
trials. Matched commercial execution, prices and deployment rights
remain unverified. Four additional families---Optimizely, EViews, IBM
SPSS and commercial probabilistic-network tools---still need exact
target-specific originals; the acquired Optimizely route redirected to a
general homepage. These gaps keep the market comparison inconclusive
while the supported prototype routes proceed.

\hypertarget{require-the-java-engine-to-preserve-the-decision-target}{%
\subsection{Require the Java engine to preserve the decision
target}\label{require-the-java-engine-to-preserve-the-decision-target}}

The Java survey now distinguishes H2O-3 uplift forests from Tetrad's
graph-compatible slopes and stability rankings. The selected source
revisions precede the 7 October 2026 cutoff. H2O returns treated/control
response predictions and their difference; its selected ATE/ATT/ATC
metrics average predicted scores over retained rows. The selected

\citep{exec20_javaeffects_h2o,exec20_javaeffects_tetrad}

Exact authored examples show why these distinctions matter to the
proposal. A confounded population has observed response difference 0.36

can have average predictions 0.25 and 0.50. Signed graph candidates -2
and -0.5 have minimum magnitude 0.5, but treating that magnitude as
beneficial changes hypothetical margins -220/-70 into USD 30. These are
transparent constructions, not measured package or bank results.
\citep{exec20_javaeffects_reference}

The public prototype should first reconcile a documented-versus-literal
KL split expression, missing-support and root-only branches, category
mappings, and complete engine/MOJO roundtrips. The equal-probability
source expression gives -1 where the documented KL node value is zero;
no native failure is asserted. A proposed Java worker must return typed
predictions, graph candidates, rankings, numerical failures and
qualified uncertainty separately through the library, CLI and MCP.
Complementary software search, full distribution rights and held-out
validation remain open; the source review supports a concrete
experiment, not runtime admission.
\citep{exec20_javaeffects_h2o,exec20_javaeffects_tetrad,exec20_javaeffects_reference}

\hypertarget{use-literature-graphs-to-propose-testable-interventions}{%
\subsection{Use literature graphs to propose testable

Tong and colleagues offer a concrete design for combining literature
extraction, graph links and hypothesis generation. Their July 2024
article reports 43,312 psychology papers and expert ratings of generated
well-being hypotheses. Those ratings concern novelty and usefulness;
they do not experimentally validate the proposed mechanisms. Of 238
pairs labelled causal by the model, 156 received matching human causal

distinction matters when translating a business question into a causal
diagram. \citep{exec20_hypothesis_generation}

The proposal should evaluate source-supported extraction, operational
question translation, blind hypothesis quality and intervention
identification separately. A graph-similarity construction has Jaccard
score one with zero causal effect: at assumed value USD 100 and cost USD
20, treating similarity as success would suggest USD 80 while the actual
stipulated incremental margin is USD -20. These are authored examples,
not bank results. The source also contains printed ANOVA quantities that
do not reconcile under the ordinary stated model; raw analysis and the
revised supplement remain necessary before inheriting those inferential
claims. \citep{exec20_hypothesis_reference,exec20_hypothesis_generation}

A public prototype can compare retrieval-only, model-only,
graph-assisted and human-assisted pipelines with held-out document and
mechanism families. It should retain unsupported claims, reviewer
disagreement, refusals and full model/expert costs. Library, CLI and MCP
outputs should distinguish an extracted claim, a hypothesis, a reviewed
assumption and an identified query. Code adoption, exact models, graph
conventions and full rights remain open.
\citep{exec20_hypothesis_generation,exec20_hypothesis_reference}

\hypertarget{economics-and-the-next-experiment}{%
\section{Economics and the next
experiment}\label{economics-and-the-next-experiment}}

Let \(N\) be the number of attempted decision tasks in one year, \(D\)
the incremental gross monetary utility of the proposed action relative
to the chosen baseline, \(F\) fixed incremental cost, \(c\) variable
incremental cost per attempted task, and \(R\) an additional risk cost
not already counted in \(D\). A transparent planning account is \[
 V=N\operatorname{E}[D]-F-Nc-R.
\] The baseline must be a feasible alternative under the same
information. A correct refusal may leave the baseline action unchanged
while still costing review time. A misleading recommendation can have
negative utility. Loss must not be counted in both \(D\) and \(R\), and
unacceptable actions remain excluded by constraints irrespective of
their average financial payoff. \citep{sp13_economics}

\hypertarget{costs-to-measure-in-the-prototype}{%
\subsection{Costs to measure in the
prototype}\label{costs-to-measure-in-the-prototype}}

The measured account should separate data acquisition and cleaning,
labels and identity audits, model and tool calls, analyst formulation
and checking, package setup, integration and maintenance, compute and
storage, incident recovery and independent validation. Each cost needs
its unit, quantity, date/version, price source and incremental share
relative to the baseline. The current actual-price fields remain
unfilled. Public examples can reveal setup burden and local resource use
without establishing bank-scale service levels, customer outcomes or
willingness to adopt. \citep{sp13_economics}

A later bank experiment should state the decisions it distinguishes, the
uncertain assumption that matters, the target population and horizon,
expected signals, losses from wrong decisions, and the full cost of
obtaining the information. It should compare cheaper public/generated
alternatives first. If no feasible experiment has plausible positive net
information value, narrow or defer the capability. A demand for
authentic evidence becomes appropriate when that specific experiment is
justified; it is not a prerequisite for writing the current proposal.

The evidence routes are also different. We know how to complete missing

investment scenario, and request a bank experiment only for a

reliability and adoption are later decisions with their own evidence.
They remain distinct from completing a credible proposal and a useful
bounded prototype.
