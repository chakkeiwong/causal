Contrast-cases (cc): The prompt appends “Describe one scenario where the effect
WOULD occur and one where it WOULD NOT, then identify the key causal dif-
ference. Finally, answer the original question.” This strategy targets necessary and
sufficient condition reasoning [38, 39].

4.7 Formal Metric Definitions
Let 𝑅 = {𝑟 1 , 𝑟 2 , . . . , 𝑟 𝑚 } denote the set of tokens in the model response, 𝐺 =

token set.

Remark 2. CIR is a necessary but not sufficient condition for correct causal reasoning.
A model that mentions all correct causal keywords in an incoherent response will
achieve high CIR but low CLP. We deliberately include both metrics to capture this
distinction.

Causal Logic Precision (CLP).
CLP evaluates whether the response correctly identifies the causal direction. We define
three directional pattern categories:
• Forward patterns: “X causes Y”, “X leads to Y”, “X results in Y”, “Y is caused
  by X”;
• Reverse patterns: “Y causes X”, “Y leads to X” (with X and Y as per the gold
  direction);
• Ambiguous patterns: “X is associated with Y”, “X and Y are related”, “X
  correlates with Y”.
