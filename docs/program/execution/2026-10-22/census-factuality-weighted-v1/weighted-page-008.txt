2.1   Conventional Inference for Weighted Estimands
The researcher observes a vector of estimates θ̂ for the parameters θ. I model the relationship
between θ̂ and θ as follows.

Assumption 1. θ̂ ∼ N (θ,Σ), where Σ is a known positive definite covariance matrix.

    Under the normal distribution N (θ,Σ), I denote probabilities Pθ {·}, expectations Eθ [·], and
variances Varθ (·). Let Φ(z) denote the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the standard
normal distribution N (0,1) and let zα denote its α-quantile for α ∈ (0,1).
    Assumption 1 is motivated by standard large-sample asymptotic results. For example, the
central limit theorem (CLT) helps establish asymptotic normality of estimates, which motivates
the normality condition θ̂ ∼ N (θ,Σ). Similarly, the law of large numbers (LLN) helps establish
consistency of covariance matrix estimation, which motivates the condition that Σ is known.
I therefore develop my procedures under Assumption 1. In Section 5, I relax Assumption 1 and
establish the asymptotic validity of my procedures.
    For each vector of weights w ∈ W, Assumption 1 implies w′ θ̂ ∼ N (w′ θ,σw2 ), where σw2 = w′ Σw.
