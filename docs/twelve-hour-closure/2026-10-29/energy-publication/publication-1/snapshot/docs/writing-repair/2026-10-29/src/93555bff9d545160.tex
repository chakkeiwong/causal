\section{Dose-response software: the intervention, the curve and the decision}
\label{sec:exec24-dose-response-reuse}

A bank choosing the size of a credit-line increase needs more than a smooth
curve relating historical increases to revenue. Customers given large increases
may have been selected because of income, relationship value, or information
held by a manager. Another team may instead be designing randomized offer
amounts; a third may only need to summarize an already justified experiment.
These questions motivate different software contracts. Our selected comparison
distinguishes \texttt{causaldrf} 0.4.2, \texttt{CausalGPS} 0.5.1 and
\texttt{WeightIt} 2.1.0 from the curve-fitting role of \texttt{drc} 4.0-0 and
the experiment-analysis role of \texttt{DoseFinding} 1.4-1. Each is a separately
pinned CRAN mirror distribution no later than the source cutoff. The mirror is
not a complete author development history or a support guarantee. These five
candidates resolve part of the umbrella, not the multilingual or commercial
market.~\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_drc,exec24_dose_dosefinding}

\subsection{Define the causal curve before choosing a fitting routine}
Let $A$ be the offer amount in declared units, $X$ the information available
before the offer, and $Y(a)$ the outcome under a specified intervention assigning
amount $a$. The population response curve is $\mu(a)=E\{Y(a)\}$. Consistency,
conditional exchangeability for each dose, positive conditional exposure density
on the requested population and dose region, and suitable regularity justify
\[
 \mu(a)=\int E(Y\mid A=a,X=x)\,dP_X(x).
\]
For a continuous exposure, the generalized propensity score is a conditional
\emph{density}, $r(a,x)=f_{A\mid X}(a\mid x)$, not the probability of an exact
dose. Conditional means at a point require an appropriate version and
smoothness; a finite sample does not contain repeated observations at every
amount. The CausalGPS original states the causal assumptions and separates
outcome-blind design from analysis. It limits balancing to measured covariates
and refers detailed theory elsewhere. This software paper cannot alone qualify
every estimator or uncertainty procedure.~\citep{exec24_dose_original}

An authored example makes the banking consequence concrete. Suppose $X$ and
$U$ are independent standard Gaussian variables, $A=X+U$, and
$Y=2A+3X+\varepsilon$, with independent centered noise. Then
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(A)}
 =2+3\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,X)}{\operatorname{Var}(A)}=3.5,
 \qquad \frac{d\mu(a)}{da}=2.
\]
At a stipulated value of 100 USD per outcome unit and a 220 USD cost for a
one-unit increase, the historical slope suggests 130 USD, while the stipulated
causal model gives minus 20 USD. These are exact model consequences, not customer
data estimates. A curve-fitting package cannot establish exchangeability, the
information clock, interference, missingness, or intervention consistency.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

A stochastic policy instead assigns density $q(a\mid x)$ and has value
$V(q)=\int\!\int E(Y\mid A=a,X=x)q(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)$ under the
corresponding assumptions. Its ratio is $q(A\mid X)/f_{A\mid X}(A\mid X)$.
The stabilized ratio $f_A(A)/f_{A\mid X}(A\mid X)$ has a particular numerator
and cannot silently represent every customer-specific policy. A curve, an
average contrast, an inverse target dose and an optimized policy require
different output types even when they share density estimation.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{causaldrf: transparent adjustment and transformation contracts}
The inspected \texttt{hi\_est} routine fits a treatment model, constructs a GPS,
fits a user-specified linear outcome surface, and averages predictions after
replacing treatments with a requested grid value and recomputing its GPS.
Accepted families include normal, log-transformed and square-root transformed
normal, count and binary models. This is a useful transparent adjustment
reference; accepting several families does not validate covariate sufficiency.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf}

In the log-normal branch, the expression evaluates a normal density at
$\log(t)$ without the $1/t$ Jacobian of an original-scale log-normal density.
The square-root branch similarly evaluates a normal density at $\sqrt t$.
For $t=4$ and transformed-scale density $1/5$, the corresponding original-scale
log-normal density is $1/20$. At a fixed dose, multiplying all scores by the
same positive dose-only factor preserves the information in the balancing
score; transformation alone therefore does not disprove fixed-dose balancing.
However, a specified finite-dimensional outcome surface in treatment and GPS
can change, and an inverse-density or cross-dose consumer needs the actual
measure and units. A port must distinguish an original-scale density, a
transformed-scale score and an arbitrary balancing coordinate.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_contract}

The inspected \texttt{get\_ci} helper sorts bootstrap curve values separately
at each grid point and selects the ceiling of $0.975B$ and floor of $0.025B$.
It receives coefficient draws; it does not justify their sampling law. With
$B=20$ the lower index is zero, an edge condition for future qualification.
This is an index-expression witness, not a native failure report. Separate
95 percent pointwise intervals also do not imply a simultaneous 95 percent
band. In 100 equally likely authored outcomes, disjoint sets of five misses
at each of two points give 95 percent coverage at each point and 90 percent
joint coverage. Selecting an offer after searching a curve needs a stated
selection-aware or simultaneous uncertainty argument.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{CausalGPS: preserve design diagnostics and population meaning}
The 2023 original describes iterative design with hyperparameter search,
transformations and trimming. The inspected 0.5.1
\texttt{generate\_pseudo\_pop} body instead accepts a counter-weight object,
merges by identifier, computes diagnostics and returns their status. Its header
still describes an adaptive procedure; this inspected body is not itself the
historical search loop. An adapter must follow the pinned interface and record
where tuning actually occurs. Unique observation identifiers, join cardinality
and the retained population belong to its contract.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_original}

The normal GPS branch uses predicted exposure means and a common residual
standard deviation. The kernel branch estimates scale from absolute residuals,
standardizes residuals, and evaluates a kernel density on them. The inspected
residual delegate computes $(a-b)/c$; the density delegate interpolates an
estimated density with constant extrapolation outside its grid. This branch
exports the residual density value. Under a location-scale interpretation with
residual density $g$ and positive scale $s(x)$, the original-scale density is
\[
 f_{A\mid X}(a\mid x)=s(x)^{-1}
 g\!\left(\frac{a-m(x)}{s(x)}\right).
\]
The selected weighting routine divides a marginal exposure density by the
exported GPS. With stipulated marginal density $1/5$, residual density $2/5$
and scales $2$ and $1/2$, this literal ratio is $1/2$ for both observations;
the location-scale ratios are $1$ and $1/4$. This static comparison identifies
a conformance question. It assumes a valid location-scale residual model and
is not an executed benchmark or a finding about every possible GPS use.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

Units provide another exact check. Changing dollars to cents multiplies the
exposure by 100 and divides both densities by 100. The stabilized ratio stays
2 when densities change from $1/5$ and $1/10$ to $1/500$ and $1/1000$.
The reciprocal conditional density changes from 10 to 1,000. Normalizing weights
may cancel a common factor, but does not justify losing measure, scale or the
policy numerator.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

The balance routine compares a selected mean, median or maximum absolute
correlation with a threshold. Zero correlation is not independence: with equal
weights on $X\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}$ and $A=X^2$, covariance is zero while exposure
depends entirely on $X$. This deterministic example also lacks overlap.
Adding independent continuous noise with an everywhere positive density
restores conditional density support without independence, while preserving
zero covariance. Nonlinear diagnostics complement causal assumptions.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

The original includes adverse findings. Its first two illustrated matching
attempts fail a maximum-correlation threshold of 0.1, with maxima 0.198 and
0.123. A third reports 0.094 after retaining 464,194 of 580,244 observations;
its reported effective sample size is about 7,635, below its displayed
recommended 46,419. These are historical reported quantities, not reproduced
results or universal thresholds. The paper omits the detailed interval
calculation and calls the final curve illustrative.
\citep{exec24_dose_original}

The current outcome frontend rejects negative weights but replaces an all-zero
vector by equal weights. It computes plotting normalization using the weight
range, zero for equal weights. Our contract treats an empty weighted population
as unresolved, not permission to change the scientific target. Outcomes 0 and
100 with zero weights make the distinction explicit: replacement yields 50,
while the requested weighted mean has no denominator. In addition,
\texttt{trim\_gps} accesses \texttt{dataset}, whereas the inspected GPS creator
returns \texttt{.data}; exported \texttt{trim\_it} uses \texttt{.data}.
This object-field discrepancy is a static integration question, not a claim
that every supported trimming route fails.
\citep{exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{WeightIt: retain history and the actual uncertainty method}
WeightIt's selected primary text distinguishes binary and multicategory
estimands, continuous stabilized density weights and longitudinal products.
The continuous GLM delegate returns unit weights with no covariates and no
random effects, otherwise estimates a weighted residual scale, and constructs
estimating-equation components only for supported configurations. Kernel,
specified missing-data and random-effects paths are excluded from that
particular M-estimation route. Longitudinal code multiplies period
contributions and stabilization factors. Servicing analysis must supply ordered
predecision histories and censoring conventions: period weights 2 and 3 have
product 6, not sum 5.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

The outcome interface distinguishes M-estimation accounting for estimated
weights, fixed-weight sandwich covariance, and bootstrap routes re-estimating
weights. The inspected dispatcher defaults to HC0 when estimating-equation
components are absent and exposes the selected covariance type. A consumer
must preserve that field and its assumptions. Cluster options likewise require
a justified sampling unit. With 20 independent customers, 25 unit-variance
visits each and within-customer correlation 0.6, the mean variance is
$[1+24(0.6)]/500=0.0308$, versus 0.002 for independent visits. This authored
exchangeable example does not qualify arbitrary shared calendar shocks.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

The trimming interface distinguishes winsorizing from setting weights to zero
and warns that the target can change. Its implementation removes M-estimation
components after trimming; the subsequent covariance route must be reported.
Weights $(1,1,8)$ and outcomes $(0,0,100)$ give mean 80. Capping the last
weight at 2 gives mean 50 while the weight-concentration diagnostic rises from
$100/66$ to $16/6$. A larger effective sample size need not mean less causal
error. Separately, a population with 80 percent customers having effect 10 and
20 percent having effect 100 has average effect 28. Removing the second group
leaves effect 10 for a different population, not a repaired estimate of 28.
\citep{exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{drc and DoseFinding: useful roles after design justification}
The selected drc frontend accepts curve family, outcome type, weights and model
structure. Its effective-dose interface distinguishes relative and absolute
response targets, checks the relative range, and offers no interval, delta,
transformed-scale and inverse-regression routes. These fitting and inverse
response tools do not provide the causal identification argument for a banking
assignment. We retain drc as a descriptive or scientifically justified
post-design comparator and reject automatic curve-to-effect admission.
\citep{exec24_dose_drc}

DoseFinding's inspected MCPMod path tests model-based contrasts, retains
significant shapes, then fits and selects or averages models and computes a
target or effective dose. A nonsignificant result returns no model or target;
it is not a zero effect estimate. The contrast routine accepts either a
covariance matrix or compatible allocation weights and checks exclusivity.
The general route requires supplied estimates and covariance with already
valid scientific and sampling meanings; the normal route obtains them from
its fitted model. Multiplicity-adjusted testing of declared contrasts does not
automatically validate post-selection uncertainty for an optimized policy.
\citep{exec24_dose_dosefinding}

An exact illustration has means $(0,1,2)$ at three randomized doses and
independent mean variances $1/100$. Contrast $(-1,0,1)$ has signal 2, variance
$2/100$ and squared standardized signal 200. After subtracting a common
baseline from the two active means, their covariance is
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}2/100&1/100\\1/100&2/100\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Contrast $(-1,1)$ still has variance $2/100$; discarding shared-baseline
covariance gives $4/100$. A bridge must retain dose order, baseline adjustment,
covariance, degrees of freedom and candidate-model identity.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

For the authored curve $m(a)=4a/(1+a)$, $a\geq0$, an increase of one response
unit requires $a=1/3$. With cost $2a$ in matching value units, maximizing
$m(a)-2a$ instead gives $a=\sqrt2-1$. At amounts 0, 1 and 2, net values are
0, 0 and $-4/3$. The effective dose answers a different question from the net
value optimum. Its uncertainty needs response, cost and selection assumptions
together.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

\subsection{Reuse decisions and public qualification before bank experiments}
We choose reference use now: causaldrf for transparent GPS adjustment,
CausalGPS for design and balance contracts, WeightIt for point and longitudinal
weighting, drc for fitting and inverse targets, and DoseFinding for experimental
contrasts. An original R worker is a conditional future route when it preserves
objects, diagnostics and uncertainty. A whole-library port lacks a demonstrated
benefit. We reject automatic curve-to-causal and balance-to-identification
admission; native admission awaits exact distributions, dependencies, rights
and semantic evidence.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

Declarations differ. causaldrf declares MIT plus its copyright file, R at least
3.1.2 and imported/optional packages; CausalGPS declares GPL-3, R at least 3.5,
Rcpp compilation and learning/smoothing dependencies. WeightIt declares GPL
at least version 2, R at least 4.1 and method-dependent optional dependencies.
drc declares GPL-2 and R at least 4; DoseFinding declares GPL-3 and compiled
code. These are declarations, not transitive distribution clearance or a legal
conclusion. Rights, maintenance owners, installed versions and optional paths
must be resolved for the actual worker.
\citep{exec24_dose_causaldrf,exec24_dose_causalgps,exec24_dose_weightit,exec24_dose_drc,exec24_dose_dosefinding}

Public qualification can precede expensive bank access. Freeze version,
transformation, intervention density, population and dose region. Generate
known-outcome experiments covering confounding, nonlinear balance, weak overlap,
varying scales, dependence and misspecification. Compare analytical targets with
the pinned worker. Test invalid identifiers, empty/extreme weights, trimming,
missing fields, unsupported covariance types, selection, serialization and
process failure. Refit estimated stages under the justified resampling law,
retain failed requests, and distinguish pointwise from simultaneous claims.
Tuning and final assessment must not reuse the same outcomes.
\citep{exec24_dose_contract}

The scientific owner supplies causal assumptions and sampling units; the
adapter owner preserves units, measures, histories, objects and refusal states;
the rights/platform owner qualifies the distribution; the evaluator registers
independent targets and complete costs. Illustratively, 2,400 USD for 100
requests, 80 conformant outputs and 60 useful outputs gives 24, 30 and 40 USD
per respective denominator. These are not measured prices or adoption returns.
Unmeasured confounding, absent support, arbitrary dependence and policy-selection
uncertainty have no automatic software cure. Other languages, commercial
systems, uninspected delegates and dependency closures remain survey work.
This bounded reference decision admits no native capability and does not close
the broader proposal standard.~\citep{exec24_dose_contract}


The chapter's alternatives therefore turn on what can actually be changed:
an offer, a mediator distribution, an exposure amount or an assumption used
to interpret observations. Natural components, dose optima and sensitivity
thresholds are useful only when their distinct meanings reach the financial
decision. Repeated servicing adds a further requirement: an action changes
information and opportunities for later actions. The next chapter develops
that longitudinal clock and separates policy value, censoring and survival
outcomes before comparing the corresponding software.
