# Independent pilot learning and finite dependent evaluation

This is a project derivation for a restricted successor, with exact examples. It
does not qualify R05S or revise any saved experimental outcome. The proposal
operation resolves a specific argument and identifies its information needs.

## Baseline and target

R05-v2 repaired the first attempt's evaluation-driven penalty selection, full
sample standardization, covariance products across omitted dates and ignored
scenario dimensions. Those old defects are already known, not new findings.
The repaired high-dimension and larger-support cells covered 499/512 and
493/512 requests, with simultaneous lower bounds 0.949140 and 0.933851, below
the frozen 0.95 screen. Its nonlinear omitted-nuisance cell covered 0/512. The
weak-IV cell covered 512/512 but had median interval width 3.836470. These are
99% nominal intervals; comparison with the first attempt's 95% intervals does
not isolate an engineering correction. The new argument changes the experiment.

Write W=(X,Z,A,Y), where X is information available before the decision, Z an
encouragement, A the actual action and Y its subsequent USD value. The model is
Y=theta_0 A+g_0(X)+epsilon, with E[epsilon|X,Z]=0. This excludes a direct
encouragement effect after conditioning on X. Define m=E[Z|X], r=E[A|X] and
ell=E[Y|X], and residuals v_Z=Z-m, v_A=A-r, v_Y=Y-ell. Then
Q=E[v_Z v_A] and M=E[v_Z v_Y] obey M=theta_0 Q. Q unequal to zero identifies
the scalar coefficient; Q=0 generally supplies only a moment-identified set.
The interpretation is a constant structural effect under the stated model,
not an unrestricted heterogeneous ATE or LATE.

The inherited PLIV score is equation (4.8) of Chernozhukov et al. (2018), locally
saved and re-inspected in Section 4.2, equations (4.5)-(4.8), Assumption 4.2 and
Theorem 4.2. Its independent-sample, moment, nonzero-slope and nuisance-product
requirements are not a theorem for arbitrary serial panels. The finite
construction below is a project argument proved here.

## Observation law and separation

A pilot of m_P independent draws from the same marginal W law is independent
of the entire evaluation sequence. Features phi(X) in R^p are fixed before the
pilot and obey |phi_j|<=K. An intercept, if used, is a constant feature subject
to the stated penalty; this differs from the previous unpenalized intercept.
Any data-dependent feature construction requires a separate independent design
sample and conditional versions of these assumptions. There is no evaluation
tuning, rescaling or feature selection.

The evaluation sequence W_1,...,W_n is stationary and d-dependent: sigma fields
generated by sets separated by more than d dates are independent. The integer
d is supplied by the design, not inferred by looking at sample autocorrelations.
The whole vector is d-dependent, not merely each coordinate uncorrelated.
Assume |Z|<=B_Z, |A|<=B_A, |Y|<=B_Y almost surely with known bounds. Clip fitted
conditional means to those ranges; clipping predictions cannot increase their
L2 error. Do not clip observations silently: that can change the estimand.

These assumptions exclude unbounded Gaussian AR controls and unrestricted
calendar persistence from R05-v2. They describe a bounded prototype design, or
independent finite-length blocks with a defended dependence range. Extending to
unknown geometric mixing needs a valid coupling or concentration certificate
and its error allocation, rather than reusing the formulas unchanged.

## The exact conditional error that must be controlled

For a frozen pilot let delta_Z=mhat-m, delta_A=rhat-r and delta_Y=ellhat-ell.
Expanding the residual products and conditioning on X makes all linear terms
zero. Hence

\[
 M_{fit}=E[(Z-\widehat m)(Y-\widehat\ell)\mid pilot]
       =M+E[\delta_Z\delta_Y\mid pilot],\qquad
 Q_{fit}=Q+E[\delta_Z\delta_A\mid pilot].
\]

The score bias at a candidate theta is E[delta_Z(delta_Y-theta delta_A)]. On a
simultaneous event with ||delta_j||_2<=e_j, its absolute value is at most
e_Z(e_Y+|theta|e_A). Separate moment biases are bounded by b_M=e_Z e_Y and
b_Q=e_Z e_A. Their common event works for every theta at once. Errors of exact
order n^(-1/4) yield a scaled product of order one, not an ignorable remainder.
Finite bias-aware bands need not pretend that the remainder is negligible.

## A learner-specific pilot certificate

For nuisance j in {Z,A,Y}, suppose f_j(X)=phi(X)' beta_j+a_j(X), with at most
s_j nonzero beta entries and |a_j(X)|<=A_j. Write the regression residual as
epsilon_j=T_j-f_j(X), with E[epsilon_j|X]=0 and |epsilon_j|<=E_j. The pilot
fits the exact minimizer of

\[
 {1\over2m_P}\|T_j-\Phi b\|_2^2+\lambda_j\|b\|_1.
\]

For a pilot error allocation eta_j, a union of scalar bounded-mean tails gives

\[
 \|\Phi'\epsilon_j/m_P\|_\infty\le
 K E_j\sqrt{2\log(2p/\eta_j)/m_P}.
\]

Here the scalar tail follows by applying the exponential Markov inequality to
independent centered variables in an interval of width 2KE_j; their moment
generating functions are bounded by exp(t^2(2KE_j)^2/8). The deterministic
approximation contributes at most K A_j. Select lambda_j at least twice their
sum. A fixed finite penalty family whose every member meets this lower bound
is also admissible: the same event applies to all its minimizers. The rates
below additionally need the selected penalty to be at most a fixed multiple
of this threshold. Arbitrary validation-selected penalties need not qualify.

Let h=betahat-beta_j and S=support(beta_j). The basic inequality gives

\[
 \|\Phi h\|_2^2/m_P+\lambda_j\|h_{S^c}\|_1
 \le3\lambda_j\|h_S\|_1.
\]

Thus h lies in the cone ||h_{S^c}||_1<=3||h_S||_1. Suppose the empirical sparse
cone certificate ||Phi h||_2^2/m_P >= kappa_j^2 ||h_S||_2^2 holds for this
cone and every possible support of size at most s_j. It follows directly that

\[
 \|\Phi h\|_2/\sqrt{m_P}\le3\sqrt{s_j}\lambda_j/\kappa_j,
 \qquad \|h\|_1\le12s_j\lambda_j/\kappa_j^2.
\]

Another bounded-mean union bound gives, with error eta_G,

\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\|_{max}\le
 \tau=K^2\sqrt{2\log(2p^2/\eta_G)/m_P}.
\]

Since |h'(Sigma-Sigmahat)h|<=tau||h||_1^2, a population prediction certificate
is

\[
 e_j=A_j+\sqrt{9s_j\lambda_j^2/\kappa_j^2+
                 144\tau s_j^2\lambda_j^2/\kappa_j^4}.
\]

It remains valid after prediction clipping. Three nuisance events and the Gram
event fail with probability at most eta_P=eta_Z+eta_A+eta_Y+eta_G;
their dependence does not prevent the union bound. The same pilot may fit all
three nuisances. For exact approximation, bounded K,E, kappa away from zero,
and tau s/kappa^2 bounded, the norm rate is O(sqrt(s log(p)/m_P)); its square,
not its norm, is O(s log(p)/m_P). The product requirement for an ordinary
root-n approximation is sqrt(n) s log(p)/m_P ->0, with additional terms for
approximation error. The finite bands below retain the bound without claiming
that this rate is already small enough in a particular high-dimension cell.

The sparse cone certificate is a real premise, not a condition number of the
full p by p matrix when p exceeds the pilot size. Small fixed dictionaries can
use a certified lower eigenvalue; a high-dimensional design needs a justified
sparse certificate or a generated design guarantee. Failed certification must
produce a missing-bound disposition. Numerical optimization error also needs
an explicit basic-inequality allowance; a small KKT residual alone does not
prove the exact formula. The checked small example below uses an exact
soft-threshold minimizer. No production lasso has been installed by this proof.

## A feasible alternative when sparse structure cannot be certified

An independent calibration sample of k independent marginal draws can validate
a frozen clipped learner, whether sparse or not. Let L_j be its average squared
prediction loss and v_j^- a justified lower bound on E[epsilon_j^2]. The loss
lies in [0,4B_j^2], and conditional mean orthogonality yields
E[loss_j]=||delta_j||_2^2+E[epsilon_j^2]. With error delta_Cj,

\[
 ||\delta_j||_2^2\le
 L_j+4B_j^2\sqrt{\log(1/\delta_{Cj})/(2k)}-v_j^-.
\]

Take the square root of the nonnegative part and cap it by 2B_j, the deterministic
prediction-error bound. A negative uncapped expression flags an inconsistent
or rare confidence event; report that diagnostic rather than claiming perfect
learning. Use this calibration route as a predeclared alternative. Selecting
between independently valid certificates after seeing them requires their
joint error allocation. Setting v_j^-=0 is always safe, but irreducible noise
can prevent the resulting certificate from shrinking. Learning residual noise
or approximation is an information problem, not just optimizer tuning.

## Finite uncertainty for the dependent evaluation sample

Let q=d+1<=n and split dates by their residue modulo q. Each residue class is
independent internally, conditional on the independent pilot. Different
classes may be dependent. The fitted numerator product lies in
[-4B_Z B_Y,4B_Z B_Y], and the denominator product in
[-4B_Z B_A,4B_Z B_A]. With n_min=floor(n/q), define

\[
 t_j=8B_Z B_j\sqrt{\log(4q/\alpha_E)/(2n_{min})},\quad j\in\{Y,A\}.
\]

A two-sided bounded-mean tail for each of the 2q class/coordinate combinations
and a union bound imply, with probability at least 1-alpha_E, that both overall
means deviate from their fitted population means by at most their t_j. The
overall mean is a weighted average of class means, so the maximal class bound
suffices even when class sizes differ. No independence between classes or
between the two moments is assumed.

Set w_M=t_Y+e_Z e_Y, w_Q=t_A+e_Z e_A. The rectangle
C=[Mhat-w_M,Mhat+w_M] x [Qhat-w_Q,Qhat+w_Q] contains the true (M,Q) with
probability at least 1-eta_P-alpha_E for the sparse route, or
1-sum(delta_Cj)-alpha_E for the calibration route. Unverified approximation,
range, dependence or instrument assumptions invalidate the corresponding
claim. If a nuisance certificate is unavailable, either explicitly use the
unconditional e_j=2B_j bound with its often useless width, or withhold the
qualified set. Do not silently substitute empirical prediction accuracy.

If a data-dependent pilot fails the cone or numerical certificate, assigning
the whole line to that request preserves an all-request confidence statement.
Withholding a result instead is an availability failure that must stay in the
denominator. The argument bounds false exclusions across all requests; it does
not assert the same conditional coverage after selecting only requests whose
certificates pass, nor a minimum fraction of informative results.

## Weak-IV inversion, topology and usefulness

Report S={theta: there exist (M,Q) in C with M=theta Q}. Coverage follows by
inserting the true pair on the joint event. No division by Qhat, estimated
strong-instrument declaration or Gaussian covariance approximation is needed.
This is a confidence set for the moment relation under the imposed model; it
is not asserted sharp for a fuller observed distribution.

For a fixed theta, form theta times the denominator interval, reversing its
endpoints for negative theta. Membership is exactly whether that interval
intersects the numerator interval. This gives an independent oracle for an
interval-union implementation. If zero belongs to both intervals, S is the
whole real line. If the denominator straddles zero and the numerator is
strictly positive, disconnected rays result. Empty input rectangles remain
empty; a nonempty rectangle can give an empty projection when its denominator
is exactly zero and its numerator excludes zero. An empty result may reject
the assumed relation or reflect a confidence event; it does not prove a
profitable zero effect.

For positive denominator lower bound Qhat-w_Q>0, nonnegative numerator and
Qhat>0, the set is the finite interval
[(Mhat-w_M)/(Qhat+w_Q),(Mhat+w_M)/(Qhat-w_Q)]. Its width is
2(Qhat w_M+Mhat w_Q)/(Qhat^2-w_Q^2). Width therefore deteriorates without bound
as the lower denominator approaches zero. More training information can reduce
e_j; more effective evaluation information can reduce t_j; neither changes a
truly zero instrument or proves exclusion. A downstream USD decision needs its
own tolerated width and action cost. Infinite width is a valid uncertainty
answer but an inadequate action recommendation.

## Exact counterexamples and proposed checks

The repaired nonlinear generator contains h(X)=0.6(X_1^2-1). With standard
Gaussian X_1, E[h]=0, E[X_j h]=0 for the independent Gaussian coordinates and
E[h^2]=0.72. Even with infinite training, a linear nuisance dictionary leaves
h in the encouragement and outcome residuals. With instrument coefficient
k=0.8 and unit encouragement-innovation variance,
Q_linear=0.8(1+0.72), and the linear-residual ratio converges to
theta_0+0.72/[0.8(1+0.72)]=theta_0+0.523255814. This explains a persistent
target shift under the omitted term; it is not an exact formula for the saved
finite-sample lasso bias. Prespecifying the quadratic feature removes this
particular approximation error in the population model, while all estimation
and dependence conditions still need review.

For weak information, rectangles [1,2] x [-1/2,1/2], [-1,1] x [-1/2,1/2],
and [1,2] x {0} produce rays, the whole line and the empty set. A strong
illustrative rectangle [0.91,1.01] x [0.78,0.82] gives
[91/82,101/78], approximately [1.109756,1.294872]. Its radii are illustrative
inputs, not fitted pilot estimates. All examples are checked against direct
rectangle feasibility. A sign-normalized action cost of 1.15 lies inside the
last set, so this interval does not determine the decision.

Dependence cannot be learned away without information: W_t=W_1 for all t can
have bounded outcomes and arbitrarily many repeated rows, but the mean has
constant variance. No honest universal n^(-1/2) bound follows from row count.
Likewise a bound whose approximation term stays positive need not vanish even
with infinitely many new observations. These are substantive counterexamples
to unrestricted closure, not requests to stop unrelated proposal work.

## Disposition and exact next work

The argument now supplies a score-specific nuisance product, a conditional
learner certificate, a feasible calibration alternative, a finite declared
dependence construction, weak-IV topology preservation and a width criterion.
It is a known route in the bounded, independent-pilot/finite-dependence class.
It does not qualify the original Gaussian AR, unknown dependence, arbitrary
learner, nonlinear misspecification or bank-useful weak-IV regimes.

Before a final study: choose a bounded generator and independently drawn pilot,
predeclare features, approximation/range/dependence certificates, certify the
learner and numerical error, match fixed-dictionary and calibrated-pilot
alternatives, and profile total training/calibration/evaluation cost. Freeze
sample sizes, error allocations, adverse cases, exact identities, fresh draws,
all-request denominator and a usefulness threshold. Separate failure of a
certificate from failure of a claimed finite coverage rule. The old outcomes
cannot supply these new qualification data. No final successor study is
registered or implied by completing this argument operation.
