\chapter{Commercial alternatives and the capabilities still needed}
\label{exec17:s8}

\section{The procurement question is a scientific question}
\label{exec17:s8-question}

A bank does not purchase ``causal inference'' in the abstract. It purchases a
workflow for a named decision, under a named information clock, with a named
population, data owner and loss function. A lending committee might ask whether
an eligibility threshold changes repayment, a treasury desk might ask whether a
rate change changes retained balances, and a servicing team might ask whether a
sequence of contacts is worth its cost. A commercial product can be excellent
for one of these questions and irrelevant to another. A common product proposal
therefore needs a comparison at the level of targets and assumptions before it
compares commands, languages or license units.

This chapter develops the acquisition part of that comparison. It uses the inspected GeNIe,
SMILE, BayesiaLab, Bayes Server, Stata, SAS, JMP, SPSS, Mplus, Amazon and
historical Azure material already audited in the working reader. GeNIe 5.0.R2
and SMILE 2.2.4.R1 identify their manual builds; several other official pages
are mutable or were retrieved after the 7 October 2026 cutoff. Those facts are
source limitations, not evidence that an uninspected edition lacks a feature.
No paid engine was run and no price below is a vendor quotation
\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_smile_intro,exec13_bayesia_api,
exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality,exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro}.

The chapter has four contributions. First, it maps the 17 software areas to
documented commercial alternatives and to the original local probe. Second,
it identifies the semantic information that would be lost at an adapter
boundary. Third, it turns the documented license and deployment distinctions
into an executable acquisition design. Fourth, it gives exact hypothetical
cost, regret and information-value references. The monetary values are an
authored planning model. They are useful for deciding what a prototype should
measure; they are not evidence of a price, performance, or return on investment.

The acquisition argument is followed by the detailed discovery, latent-model,
temporal and dynamic-panel comparisons on which its choices depend. The
latent-model reprise makes a separate contribution: it recovers the configuration
table needed to distinguish learning from inference and turns that distinction
into a purchasing decision. Repeated examples retain their stated role as
comparisons of the same information, rather than independent evidence of a
product's performance.

The rows are not claims that every named vendor implements every operation in
the row. They are a checklist for a matched comparison. For example, GeNIe's
pattern editor preserves an equivalence class and its documented Create DAG
command randomly orients unresolved arcs. BayesiaLab EQ searches equivalence
classes and gives fixed, forbidden and temporal restrictions different
semantics. A proposed adapter that maps both products to a single
\texttt{required\_edges} field has already changed the scientific input
\citep{exec13_genie_pattern,exec13_genie_bs,exec13_bayesia_eq}.

The original probe's capability manifest exposed graph syntax review, a
supplied-DAG back-door operation, a supplied-moment residual inversion,
deterministic sensitivity and a strict observation clock. Its tested requests
did not implement general discovery, policy evaluation, survival, report
construction or job lifecycle. These observations identify that implementation
boundary; they do not erase later research or interface evidence, establish
vendor limitations, or show that the scientific methods are impossible.
\citep{exec16_commercial_runtime,exec16_commercial_matrix}

limitations.

\section{Four journeys define four different purchases}
\label{exec17:s8-journeys}

The buying unit can be written as $(q,e,d,o)$: a causal question $q$, a
specific edition and update $e$, a deployment arrangement $d$, and an output
contract $o$. Let $\mathcal A_q$ contain the units whose assumptions, operations,
rights and outputs are adequate for that question. Optimization of cost takes
place only over $\mathcal A_q$. Unknown membership is a due-diligence item;
it is not coded as either ``cheap'' or ``incapable.'' The same family can
supply an admissible analyst tool for one target and an unresolved production
service for another. The following comparisons apply that distinction to
banking decisions rather than award one aggregate vendor score
\citep{exec17_s8_paths}.

\subsection{Structural funding: what exactly changes when a rate changes?}

For treasury, distinguish a change in posted rate from a change in customer
mix and a change in the bank's pricing rule. JMP's binary-treatment report
separates ATE from ATET, while its continuous-treatment report offers a
quadratic marginal structural model and a generalized-propensity-score route.
The inspected page explicitly warns that a potential-outcome density plot
does not directly supply a causal effect. These are concrete capabilities
against which the proposal should be assessed. They can support a supplied
static comparison under their assumptions; their existence does not establish
an effect of a new dynamic pricing rule in a dependent balance system
\citep{exec13_ch_jmp_causal}.

An acquisition request for this journey should specify a monetary contrast at
two stated rates, a customer population and a balance horizon. If the business
question concerns retained balances for all eligible customers, returning an
ATET for customers who actually received the high rate changes the population.
If the rate is continuous, a binary high/low recoding changes the intervention.
A prospective adapter must retain the two evaluated doses, centering and model
form; a quadratic coefficient alone is not the requested contrast. Stata's
static treatment-effect routes offer another comparator, but their inspected
i.i.d., conditional-independence and overlap conditions cannot be silently
replaced by the bank's shared-calendar sampling law
\citep{exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro,exec13_ch_jmp_causal}.

The proposal's current supplied-moment inverse problem is a different service.
Its narrow reproducible calculation can preserve an ambiguity set, while a
commercial report may provide a developed estimation and diagnostic workflow
for a different supported model. Buying an analyst tool could therefore be
appropriate before building a general estimation interface. The proposed
common layer would have to demonstrate a further benefit: that the rate,
population, information law, uncertainty and profit mapping survive a handoff.
It cannot claim estimator superiority from returning a richer envelope around
an otherwise much narrower calculation.

The inexpensive admission case should contain two rate levels with unequal
assignment probabilities and two customer strata with unequal effects. Demand
separate ATE and treated-population outputs; change the target population
without changing the fitted outcome model and check that the aggregation
changes. Add a shared-calendar version to establish the boundary of an i.i.d.
procedure. A refusal or explicitly restricted interpretation is the intended
answer to the latter unless a justified dependence-aware route is supplied.
This is a proposed comparison of semantic behavior, not a newly executed
vendor study.

\subsection{Sequential servicing: a seat for analysis or an engine for decisions?}

Servicing combines time to resolution, competing exits, repeated actions and
cost. Stata's inspected survival treatment command excludes delayed entry and
multiple-record or time-varying-covariate data. SAS's inspected competing-risk
route has a different risk set and ignores specified options when EVENTCODE
is active. A purchase justified by a survival feature name can therefore fail
the actual request even if both products are working as documented. An
analyst may legitimately simplify a question to a fixed treatment at baseline;
the interface must make that new question explicit rather than transform
sequential records into one row without a scientific decision
\citep{exec14_survival_stata,exec14_survival_sas}.

The buying unit for a fixed horizon should name the event of interest, competing
events, censoring, entry rule, action history and net utility. If repayment and
write-off have different monetary consequences, a survival probability for
either exit is inadequate. If eligibility is learned after the first contact,
the policy cannot use it at the first contact. SMILE's influence-diagram route
is a candidate for supplied-model decision analysis, while Stata's
prespecified-policy evaluation is a candidate for its documented static
setting. They answer different questions unless the history and causal model
have deliberately been restricted to make them coincide
\citep{exec16_cp_bayesfusion,exec13_ch_stata}.

A graphical analyst seat can be useful for reviewing a supplied mechanism and
utilities without authorizing a server embedding of the same engine. Conversely,
a production license can permit an operational route whose scientific model
has not been justified. The acquisition dossier must therefore contain both
the accepted analysis and the permitted deployment. A generated two-contact
case with an explicit history clock can test feasibility before a costly
experiment. It cannot establish truthful logging probabilities in the bank's
historical records. The original dispatcher probe refused its proposed
sequential-policy identifiers. The later servicing study in
Chapter~\ref{gap29:servicing} provides bounded fitted research evidence, not
general sequential-policy runtime qualification or a commercial connector.
A licensed alternative remains a capability to investigate.

\subsection{Discovery to intervention: ownership of the unresolved graph}

Discovery procurement has two deliverables: an analyst's model-building
experience and the mathematical object consumed by an intervention query.
GeNIe documents a pattern editor and random completion to a DAG. BayesiaLab EQ
documents equivalence-class search and different treatment of fixed versus
forbidden arcs. These features make commercial editors credible alternatives
to building a new graphical front end. They also show why ``export graph''
cannot be the entire acceptance criterion. The exported object must retain
what the search did not resolve and what the analyst supplied
\citep{exec13_genie_pattern,exec13_bayesia_eq}.

In a deposit-rate example, the procurement packet should distinguish an edge
oriented by a time restriction, an edge implied by a conditional-independence
procedure, an edge asserted by an analyst, and an edge chosen only to produce
a usable DAG. A downstream supplied-model inference engine may need a DAG;
that computational need is not evidence selecting one member of a learned
class. The proposed layer can retain the class and evaluate a declared
representative as a scenario, or refuse an unqualified class-wide effect. Its
competitive value depends on making this distinction inspectable without
preventing the analyst from using a productive commercial editor.

The buying unit also changes between development and repeated scoring.
Bayesia's public API overview distinguishes modeling and learning from
inference configurations. Section~\ref{exec14:commercial-latent-choice} recovers the original table's
configuration markers and distinguishes learning from inference. That
documentary finding still does not establish installed edition parity. A development demonstration of a
learning command cannot by itself qualify a production inference entitlement
to relearn a graph after drift. Separate the learning component, retained
model, update authorization and inference component in the proposed deployment.
Request an exact feature/rights confirmation only for the narrow configuration
that survives public semantic tests \citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

\subsection{Trustworthy observations: who constructs the variables?}

The fourth journey concerns the objects delivered to every engine: rows,
identities, fields, states and times. A node called ``arrears'' can denote a
balance known before contact, a revised accounting figure published later, or
a post-contact outcome. Matching a column name to a network node does not
establish any of those meanings. A commercial inference engine can correctly
process the wrong time slice. Buying more sophisticated inference therefore
does not resolve the observation problem.

The proposed common service's strict clock is a useful narrow boundary, but
it accepts declared times and cannot authenticate them. Similarly, the record-linkage and missing-data methods considered earlier require their own
sampling and truth assumptions. This journey's purchasing unit includes a
conversion specification: customer or account identity, row inclusion rule,
state mapping, measurement units, decision time and source publication time.
The commercial feature census has not established a general engine that
reconstructs those meanings from banking extracts. That is a recorded scope
gap, not an inference that no vendor offers data preparation.

For a public/generated admission case, permute node order while preserving
labels, then permute labels while preserving numeric positions. An adapter
should accept a valid explicit permutation and reject an ambiguous one; it
should not reject all reordered data. Include a retrospectively revised field
with a publication time after the action. The same values should lead to a
different eligibility disposition when their information clock changes.
These are observable and affordable semantic tests. Later owner evidence is
needed to decide whether the recorded clock and identity are truthful.

\section{Graph discovery and model construction}
\label{exec17:s8-discovery}

\subsection{Equivalence is a deliverable}

GeNIe's PC documentation returns a pattern and describes a significance
threshold, maximum adjacency and a time-limit setting. Its Bayesian Search
route is a score-guided hill-climbing procedure with restarts and a structural
prior. The pattern editor identifies directed, unresolved and possible
hidden-common-cause arcs. Create DAG chooses directions randomly when a fully
directed network is required. These are useful capabilities, but the final
random representative is not new observational evidence. The proposed product
must store both the pattern and any selected representative, with the selection
seed and reason visible to the consumer.

SMILE makes the boundary concrete in code. Its PC call returns a
\texttt{DSL\_pattern}, can be interrupted by a progress callback, and reports
an interruption code. A time limit is checked after an iteration, so it is not
the same as a hard external wall-time guarantee. Pattern coordinates follow
the data-set variable order. A wrapper must validate an explicit permutation
and reject an ambiguous or inconsistent matrix-to-name mapping
\citep{exec13_smile_pc,exec13_smile_pattern}.

BayesiaLab's EQ description supplies a different comparison. It searches
equivalence classes, treats fixed arcs as normal arcs, and takes forbidden arcs
and temporal indices into account. Thus two fields with similar names can have
opposite effects on the search. The integration contract should carry the
restriction kind, time index and class representation separately. A license or
REST call that returns a network is not evidence that the restriction was
interpreted as intended.

The banking consequence is immediate. Suppose a deposit-rate graph has an
unresolved rate--balance orientation. One representative says that the rate
changes balances; another says that a balance shock changes the quoted rate.
Both can fit the same observational law. The proposed service should return an
equivalence class or a refusal to answer an intervention query. A commercial
editor may be the best analyst-facing component, while the shared library owns
the typed class, provenance and refusal semantics. Building another graphical
editor would need a separate business case; porting SMILE is not an
open-source option because its manual describes proprietary development and
deployment licenses
\citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license}.

\subsection{The latent and supplied-model distinction}

Bayes Server documents causal optimization, an evidence-to-abduction-to-
intervention counterfactual workflow, and criteria such as back-door, front-door
and instrumental-variable routes. Its guide says that some helper functions
are available from version 10 and that the worked calculation can be performed
from 9.4. This is strong evidence of a documented abduction workflow. It is
not a complete domain theorem for every graph, nor an execution of the local
service. The request must state whether the output is a population intervention
or an individual structural counterfactual, and which exogenous-variable model
couples the factual and alternative worlds
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality}.

BayesiaLab supplies graphical surgery, likelihood matching and contribution
analysis in the inspected pages. Stata supplies treatment effects and a
developed one-mediator workflow. These are valuable alternatives for specific
targets. Their assumptions remain part of the output: Stata's manual names
i.i.d. sampling, conditional independence and overlap; its mediation manual
addresses treatment--mediator and mediator--outcome confounding restrictions.
An adapter that returns a coefficient while dropping those conditions is a
semantic failure even if the numerical call succeeds
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesia_surgery,exec13_ci_bayesia_matching,
exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro,exec13_ci_stata_mediate}.

The buying route is therefore conditional. Reuse a commercial engine for a
supplied model when its edition exposes the required intervention or abduction
operation and its data movement and deployment rights are acceptable. Integrate
an open method when its source, license, numerical representation and target
semantics are compatible. Build the missing graph-class and assumption ledger
in the common library. Reject a direct port when the license, endpoint meaning
or hidden-state contract cannot be preserved. These are distinct decisions;
``use the package'' is not one of them.

\section{Effects, IV/RD and the limits of a command menu}
\label{exec17:s8-effects}

Stata's treatment-effect reference documents ATE, ATET and potential-outcome
means using regression adjustment, inverse-probability weighting, augmented
weighting and matching. Its caveats explain why i.i.d. sampling, conditional
independence and overlap matter. The \texttt{cate} postestimation path
evaluates a prespecified treatment-assignment policy. JMP's causal-treatment
documentation similarly defines positivity, consistency and conditional
exchangeability, while its uplift and treatment personalities address distinct
estimands. These capabilities set a high baseline for estimation. A new common
service must justify its cost by preserving target identity, evidence, clocks,
uncertainty and cross-tool failure semantics, not by adding another estimator
name \citep{exec13_ch_stata,exec13_ch_jmp_causal,exec13_ch_jmp_uplift}.

The IV/RD row is especially important for procurement. Stata's weak-instrument
postestimation describes confidence sets that can be finite, disconnected,
unbounded, whole-line or empty; its grid and simulation controls are exposed.
SAS/ETS SYSLIN supplies 2SLS, LIML, K-class, SUR and 3SLS system alternatives.
SPSS and JMP provide narrower equation or model-implied instrument paths. A
bridge that serializes only a point estimate loses exactly the result that is
most decision-relevant when the instrument is weak. The bridge should preserve
the set components, search bounds, grid, seed, covariance choice and whether an
empty set means model rejection or numerical noncoverage
\citep{exec13_ivrd_stata,exec13_ivrd_sas}.

For a credit threshold, the business request may be a local complier effect,
an all-applicant effect, or a reduced-form jump. The same columns and cutoff do
not identify all three. The proposed acquisition comparison should execute a
small public/generated fixture in which exclusion holds, then a negative control
where eligibility has a direct outcome effect. A commercial command can be the
reference implementation for the first fixture; it cannot make the second
restriction true. The cost of a licensed command is justified only if the
workflow preserves that distinction and reports the negative control.

\section{Time, panels and survival}
\label{exec17:s8-temporal}

GeNIe documents dynamic Bayesian networks and parameter learning for an existing
dynamic structure. Its parameter-learning page says that the structure is not
learned by that procedure and explains the node-and-time-slice column mapping.
SMILE's dynamic tutorial distinguishes initial from transition tables, lag order,
slice count and temporal evidence indices. SAS/ETS and Stata provide VAR,
SVAR, VARMAX and Granger-style operations, while SPSS TCM uses ``causal'' in
the Granger-predictive sense. Mplus provides intensive longitudinal and latent
dynamic models. These are substantial tools, but a predictive lag, a supplied
transition model and an intervention effect are different targets
\citep{exec13_ct_genie_dbn,exec13_ct_genie_learning,exec13_ct_smile_tutorial,
exec13_ct_stata_granger,exec13_ct_sas_causal,exec13_ct_ibm_tcm}.

The adapter must preserve sampling interval, time origin, state ordering,
calendar shocks, contemporaneous restrictions and the intervention regime. A
state array with swapped categories can pass type checks and reverse a
servicing action. A VAR test on shared monthly observations can be a useful
diagnostic while failing to identify a rate intervention. The proposal should
buy or reuse supplied-model temporal inference when it meets those fields and
build a common refusal when the question asks for an unsupported change of
regime.

Survival tools provide another concrete baseline. Stata's inspected
\texttt{stteffects} documentation excludes delayed entry, time-varying
covariates and multiple-record data in that command. SAS PHREG documents
counting-process records and competing-event output, but some options are
ignored under its EVENTCODE route. SPSS, Statistica, Minitab, JMP, NCSS and
Mplus each provide useful narrower conversion or reporting functions. The
local reference dispatcher returned completed unsupported responses for the
three authored fixed-treatment, competing-incidence and sequential-policy
identifiers. This establishes a gap for those requests in that tested revision and a
route to a matched public/generated bridge. It does not rank vendors, prove
their absence of other methods, or certify the scope of later research modules \citep{exec14_survival_stata,exec14_survival_sas,
exec14_survival_jmp,exec16_commercial_matrix,exec16_commercial_runtime}.

\section{Policy value, uplift and decision analysis}
\label{exec17:s8-policy}

Stata's \texttt{estat policyeval} evaluates a prespecified treatment-assignment
probability. GeNIe and SMILE influence diagrams compute expected utility for a
supplied model with decision nodes, information and costs. JMP uplift emphasizes
treatment differences in partitioning. Amazon Personalize documents train/test
recommendation metrics, including average rewards at rank. These capabilities
should be compared on aligned targets, not on the shared word ``policy''.

For a binary action, a prespecified static policy has value
\begin{equation}
 V(\pi)=\E\{\pi(X)Y(1)+[1-\pi(X)]Y(0)\}.
 \label{exec17:s8-static-value}
\end{equation}
For a sequential policy with history $H_t$, logging probability $b_t$ and
target policy $\pi_t$, the trajectory weight is
\begin{equation}
 W_T=\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\pi_t(A_t\mid H_t)}{b_t(A_t\mid H_t)}.
 \label{exec17:s8-sequential-value}
\end{equation}
The second object requires action histories, positivity and an outcome clock.
It cannot be obtained by placing the last action in a static treatment column.
An influence diagram answers a supplied-mechanism utility question; a
recommendation metric evaluates a predictive ranking. The service must retain
which object was requested.

Azure Personalizer is a historical comparator. Its archived documentation
describes Rank and Reward calls, contextual and action features, delayed reward,
and a service that does not maintain individual user histories. It also records
that new resources stopped in 2023 and the service retired on 25 August 2026.
The migration recommendation to an open project does not establish estimator
or feature parity. AWS metrics and a retired hosted policy service cannot be
treated as evidence of a causal-policy guarantee
\citep{exec16_cp_azure,exec16_cp_aws}.

The proposed route is to keep a static policy adapter, a sequential off-policy
research module and a supplied influence-diagram adapter separate. First use a
public or generated trajectory fixture to test clocks, logging probabilities,
costs, action support, failure denominators and independent evaluation. Only
then consider a licensed engine trial. A model that refuses a sequential target
is preferable to a static score silently substituted for it.

\subsection{Information parents can change the affordable policy}
\label{exec17:s8-information}

The SMILE influence-diagram manual supplies an unusually concrete acquisition
requirement. After inference, the returned matrices may be indexed by
unobserved decision nodes and by chance nodes that should have been observed
before a decision. It provides \texttt{GetIndexingParents} to recover those
indices. Chance-node entries are then posterior probabilities for each policy;
decision entries are expected utilities for outcomes and policies; value-node
entries are expected utilities for policies. An integration that buys access
to the matrix but exports only a scalar has discarded part of the result's
meaning \citep{exec16_cp_bayesfusion}.

Consider a generated servicing decision with equally probable histories
$h_0,h_1$ and actions 0 (no contact) and 1 (contact). Stipulate net utilities
in USD per customer:
\begin{equation}
 u(h_0,0)=u(h_1,0)=0,\qquad u(h_0,1)=10,\qquad u(h_1,1)=-30.
 \label{exec17:s8-utility-table}
\end{equation}
The histories can represent eligible and ineligible contact contexts; the
amounts already include contact costs. If the history is available before
acting, a deterministic policy is the pair $(a_0,a_1)$, with value
\begin{equation}
 V(a_0,a_1)=\tfrac12u(h_0,a_0)+\tfrac12u(h_1,a_1).
 \label{exec17:s8-indexed-value}
\end{equation}
The four values for $(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)$ are respectively 0, $-15$,
5 and $-10$. The best feasible history-dependent policy contacts only at
$h_0$ and has value 5. If history is unavailable until after the decision,
only the two constant policies are feasible, with best value 0. Randomizing
contact independently of history with probability $r$ has value $-10r$ and
cannot improve that value. Taking the largest matrix entry, 10, is neither
the best population value nor a feasible unconditional policy value. The
exact four-policy enumeration is a project derivation, not a SMILE run
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

For this stipulated utility model, purchasing a perfectly timely history
feed has gross value at most USD~5 per customer before feed, review and
deployment expenses. Buying a faster decision engine does not create this
information. A feed that becomes available after contact has zero policy
value here, even when it has the same data contents. With noisy history,
the posterior contact benefit must be positive; if $p$ is the conditional
probability of $h_0$, that benefit is $10p-30(1-p)$, so contact requires
$p>3/4$. The purchased signal needs an information clock and calibrated
meaning for $p$, not merely an accuracy percentage.

This example defines a specific acceptance case for a commercial adapter.
Export parent identifiers, state order, observed/unobserved status, decision
ordering and policy indices with the utility matrix. Reconstruct all four
policy values and the restricted two-policy value outside the engine from
that export. Then intentionally withhold the history and require the feasible
policy set to shrink. If the wrapper reports value 5 for a decision that
cannot observe history, the correct response is to reject that integration
for the requested deployment. The experiment tests the preservation of
documented information semantics; it does not rank SMILE's optimizer against
other optimizers or validate the stipulated utilities.

An alternative acquisition is a static prespecified-policy tool when the bank
can truly observe the relevant covariates before the one decision. Stata's
documented policy probability supplies that narrower comparison. A supplied
influence diagram remains useful when the bank wants to inspect mechanisms
and decisions together. A future sequential estimator is needed when the
question concerns a learned policy under repeated, uncertain assignment.
Those alternatives should compete only within their supported target and
information class. Their different purchase costs cannot compensate for a
missing predecision variable \citep{exec13_ch_stata,exec16_cp_bayesfusion}.

\section{Language, deployment and semantic loss}
\label{exec17:s8-boundary}

SMILE's C++ engine and its documented wrappers for Java, Python, R, .NET and
COM are access paths to one proprietary engine. The licensing page separates
academic and commercial development, server deployment and end-user
redistribution. Bayesia's API page separates Java modeling and inference from
REST inference, describes customer-hosted model files for one configuration and
hosted models for another, and prices production by simultaneously loaded model
instances. The phrase ``Java API'' or ``Bayesia API'' therefore does not name a
single acquisition unit \citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license,
exec13_bayesia_api}.

The request contract should include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a target and population with unit, horizon, currency and sign convention;
\item a graph or supplied model with endpoint marks, restriction types and
      unresolved-class representation;
\item treatment or action identity, assignment/logging law and observation clock;
\item estimator, uncertainty object, numerical controls, seed and refusal state;
\item artifact hashes, dependency versions, license entitlement, data location,
      interruption status and recovery identity.
\end{enumerate}

The consumer should see whether the result is documented, locally observed,
project-derived or proposed. A wrapper should preserve an interrupted SMILE
learning call as incomplete, preserve Bayes Server's intervention rather than
ordinary evidence, and preserve Stata's disconnected confidence set. It should
not coerce a vendor error or an unsupported target into a null estimate.

The recommended integration pattern is a narrow adapter plus a native semantic
ledger. For a proprietary engine, the ledger records the license, model
location, wrapper version, serialization and permitted deployment. For an
open-source package, it records the commit, license, dependency graph, source
anchors and whether the package can be adopted, wrapped, ported or rejected.
The common CLI and MCP surfaces expose the ledger and request envelope; they do
not make proprietary code open or transform a predictive metric into a causal
estimand.

\subsection{Entitlements determine the architecture that can be quoted}
\label{exec17:s8-editions}

For SMILE, the inspected license page separates development from deployment,
and server deployment from distribution of end-user programs. This creates
at least three different cost inquiries for a proposed banking product:
analysts building a prototype, a centrally hosted calculation service, and
applications distributed to consumers. The existence of a Python or R wrapper
does not collapse these distinctions. An academic development license is not
the planning assumption for a commercial bank; a server integration needs
its own applicable entitlement. No clause inspected here authorizes porting
the proprietary engine. The feasible proposed route is a licensed adapter,
with an open-source alternative considered separately
\citep{exec13_smile_license}.

Bayesia's overview describes another architecture. It distinguishes Java
deployment with model files on customer servers from a hosted REST
configuration whose model files reside on Bayesia servers in France. It also
states that the Java route validates credentials with a license server and
requires a permanent Internet connection. Those documented dependencies must
enter a deployment comparison even when all model files stay local. Model
residency and license connectivity are different properties. The public table's
initial text extraction lost visual feature markers. Their recovery in
Section~\ref{exec14:commercial-latent-choice} establishes the documented
configuration matrix, while installed edition parity and contractual guarantees
remain unresolved. The evidence supports inquiring about model residency,
network dependence, loaded-instance capacity and learning versus inference \citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

Stata illustrates why concurrent users and concurrent engines must not share
one field. Its license-options page defines an identified single user and a
network license for a specified number of concurrent users within a single
site. It describes annual and perpetual arrangements. This is evidence about
the published user-license categories, not confirmation that unattended
service accounts, remote MCP clients, geographic replication or redistribution
are covered. The proposal should use a licensed analyst workflow as a
candidate reference and keep an unattended service route pending the relevant
terms. Counting API workers as ``users'' would be an unsupported legal and
operational assumption \citep{exec13_ivrd_license}.

These distinctions change the quote request. It should state the legal
deployment entity, exact edition/update, development users, runtime users or
instances as applicable, sites, model residency, permitted data movement,
offline needs, recovery replicas, consumer access and expected model churn.
An edition list without those quantities does not price the workflow. The
public originals establish which questions matter; they do not provide binding
permission or numeric prices. This is why the financial examples below use
transparent hypothetical terms rather than convert an undocumented seat price
into a server cost.

\subsection{Version replacement is a scientific change}

An upgrade can add a needed helper without preserving an operational contract.
Bayes Server's inspected example says that its counterfactual calculation is
possible from version 9.4, while the illustrated abduction helpers arrive in
version 10. A prototype that manually constructs the older calculation and a
workflow that invokes the later helper have different reproducibility and
review costs, even if both answer the same supplied structural question.
The acquisition dossier should preserve the structural coupling, evidence
removal and intervention steps, then test whether a replacement produces the
same declared result. Matching a button name is insufficient
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality}.

Retirement has a similar consequence at the service boundary. The inspected
Azure Personalizer pages record retirement on 25 August 2026, before the
literature cutoff. They describe Rank/Reward and explicitly say that the service
does not maintain individual user information or history. A migration candidate
must therefore reconstruct whatever external history the old workflow used,
its reward delay and assignment logging. A replacement recommendation engine
can preserve a ranking interface while changing the evaluation problem. Treat
the old service as a historical design comparator, and price migration,
requalification and export retention separately from a recurring engine fee
\citep{exec16_cp_azure}.

For SAS EVENTCODE, the ignored-option list makes the upgrade/translation test
particularly observable: parse the actual effective procedure and covariance
settings, then compare them with the request. If an integration asserts that
an ignored RANDOM statement supplied a random-effects fit, the problem lies
in the integration's claim. Preserving the original request alongside the
effective invocation enables a reviewer to see that mismatch. A successful
numeric result does not authorize relabeling the effective model
\citep{exec14_survival_sas}.

\section{Scheduling a licensed engine}
\label{exec17:s8-scheduling}

Bayesia's production description makes simultaneous loaded model instances a
license quantity. A simple planning model is useful before a quote arrives.
Let job $i$ have supplied load-and-inference duration $d_i$, and let $m$ be the
number of permitted instances. For independent jobs all available at time zero and a nonpreemptive
schedule, the makespan is
\begin{equation}
 T_m=\min_{\text{assignment to }m\text{ instances}}
       \max_{j\leq m}\sum_{i\in j}d_i.
 \label{exec17:s8-makespan}
\end{equation}
This is a workload assumption, not a measured vendor rate. Three planning jobs
with durations 7, 5 and 4 take 16 units on one instance and 9 units on two
instances by placing the 5 and 4 jobs together. The calculation tells the
prototype to measure model-load time, queueing and failure recovery. It does
not establish that a production license permits that schedule or that the
engine scales linearly.

There is a simple lower-bound check on this reference: every schedule needs
at least the longest job time, 7, and at least the average assigned load,
$16/m$. For two instances, a makespan below 9 would require each instance to
carry exactly 8 units because the durations are integers and total 16. No
subset of $\{7,5,4\}$ sums to 8. The partition $\{7\},\{5,4\}$ attains 9,
proving optimality for this three-job instance. Three instances attain 7.
A deadline of 8 therefore requires three under these assumptions; a deadline
of 9 needs two. This is a useful prospective quote distinction even though
none of these durations is an observed product timing
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

The vendor's loaded-model language also exposes a limitation of the simple
job formula. A warm model that is idle between requests can still consume a
licensed instance. Let $I_k(t)$ indicate that model instance $k$ is loaded at
time $t$. The proposed entitlement constraint is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_k I_k(t)\leq m\quad\hbox{for every }t.
 \label{exec17:s8-occupancy}
\end{equation}
This counts simultaneously loaded instances, not daily request volume or
physical servers. In the simple three-job reference each job loads, computes
and unloads within its stated duration. If models are retained between jobs,
their occupancy intervals must be extended. If loading and unloading are
costly, the policy can trade repeated startup against scarce instance slots.
The correct evidence is a trace of load, readiness, request start, completion
and unload events, with license failures retained. A thread count alone cannot
recover this trace. Exact concurrent-query behavior within an instance remains
an edition/runtime question; it is not assumed by the model
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

For example, retaining one model throughout a quiet period can leave only
$m-1$ slots for the other models even while that first model performs no
inference. A benchmark that reports only CPU utilization can miss this binding
constraint. Conversely, sequential loading may satisfy a one-instance license
while violating the decision deadline. A bank choosing between those routes
should measure the requested deadline and refusal/fallback outcome together,
then ask what capacity is needed for the same supported workload. A larger
license has no demonstrated value merely because more cores are available.

SMILE's time-limit semantics introduce a different scheduling issue. The
manual checks elapsed search time after an iteration completes. Without a
bound on the remaining iteration, setting the nominal limit to the bank's
deadline does not prove that the result arrives by that deadline. An external
wrapper may enforce a wall-time stop, but it must mark the learning result
as interrupted and retain the last valid artifact separately from a completed
graph. Killing the process cannot convert a partial search into an accepted
scientific answer. The prototype should include a deadline at an iteration
boundary and one during an iteration; the latter is precisely the case a
simple elapsed-time setting can fail to resolve \citep{exec13_smile_pc}.

The same ledger must include model transfer, data residency, license-server
availability, retries, operator review and retirement. A cheaper per-instance
license can be dominated by a long load time or by the cost of converting a
pattern, state ordering or uncertainty object. Those terms belong in the
workflow cost, not in an unqualified engine benchmark.

\subsection{Buying capacity when models stay resident}
\label{exec17:s8-residency}

The production-instance description has a further implication that a throughput
table misses. Requests can revisit an already loaded model, while retaining
that model consumes a slot between requests. An acquisition decision therefore
depends on the model-retention policy as well as the number of licenses. The
Bayesia overview explicitly allows sequential loading and unloading to reduce
the simultaneous-instance requirement. It does not specify the latency of
these operations, a caching policy, or a guarantee that one model instance can
serve concurrent requests. Those are quantities to measure. The following
reference isolates residency while keeping actual product behavior unresolved
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api,exec17_s8_residency}.

Consider a single worker receiving requests for immutable model versions from
a finite set. Loading a model consumes five abstract time units, inference
consumes one, and eviction is instantaneous. No model is loaded initially.
Requests are processed in their declared order, with no prefetch and no overlap
of loading and inference. The bank buys capacity for $m$ resident models,
including idle ones. These assumptions deliberately exclude parallel speedup:
the question is whether buying an additional slot saves reloads. A model name
includes its parameter and schema version; a revised balance-response network
is a different object even when an analyst gives it the same display name.

Write the ordered model requests as $x_1,\ldots,x_K$, and let $S$ be the
resident set just before a request. A hit occurs if $x_t\in S$. A miss requires
loading $x_t$ and, if $|S|=m$, selecting an eviction. For a known batch, let
$J_t(S)$ denote the smallest number of remaining loads from time $t$. Retaining
unused models is free apart from capacity, so an optimum can delay an eviction
until the slot is needed. The recurrence is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 J_{K+1}(S)&=0,\\
 J_t(S)&=J_{t+1}(S),\qquad x_t\in S,\\
 J_t(S)&=1+\min_{S'\in\mathcal E_m(S,x_t)}J_{t+1}(S'),\qquad x_t\notin S,
\end{split}
\label{exec17:s8-resident-bellman}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E_m(S,x)$ is $\{S\cup\{x\}\}$ if $|S|<m$, and is
$\{(S\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}:y\in S\}$ if $|S|=m$.
Thus every candidate next set contains the requested model and respects the
purchased capacity. The recurrence is exact by conditioning on the next
eviction and then optimizing the remaining suffix. It is not an estimator of
a future workload. Under the stated serial model, a schedule with $N$ loads
has completion time
\begin{equation}
 T=K+5N.
 \label{exec17:s8-resident-time}
\end{equation}
Changing the load or inference duration replaces those coefficients; overlapping
operations or concurrent calls require a different state and time model.

Take the six-request batch $(A,B,C,A,B,C)$. One slot requires six loads and
finishes at 36. Three slots load each distinct model once and finish at 21.
With two slots, the following resident sets attain four loads:
\begin{equation}
 \{A\},\quad\{A,B\},\quad\{A,C\},\quad\{A,C\},\quad\{B,C\},\quad\{B,C\}.
 \label{exec17:s8-resident-witness}
\end{equation}
Load at requests 1, 2, 3 and 5; requests 4 and 6 are hits. Completion time is
26. At least four loads are necessary: the first three distinct requests each
require a load, and loading $C$ in the third request with capacity two evicts
either $A$ or $B$. Both are requested again, so at least one further load is
unavoidable. This lower bound and the witness establish the exact two-slot
optimum. A separate enumeration of all feasible resident-set paths gives the
same result, including paths that voluntarily evict early
\citep{exec17_s8_residency}.

The offline optimum needs information about the remaining batch. A familiar
online rule evicts the least recently used resident model. For the same
two-slot sequence its resident sets are $\{A\},\{A,B\},\{B,C\},\{C,A\},
\{A,B\},\{B,C\}$. Every request is a miss, so it takes 36, exactly the
one-slot time. The extra license has supplied storage capacity but this policy
has not converted it into a time saving. The distinction is commercially
material: a demonstration that knows the complete batch cannot support a
streaming service-level promise for an unspecified eviction policy.

Reordering requests to group identical models would answer a different
scheduling problem. It may be legitimate for independent overnight reports;
it may be impossible when the next servicing request depends on an earlier
decision or when each request has a deadline. The acquisition case must say
whether such reordering is permitted. Likewise, preloading all three models
before starting the stopwatch both consumes capacity and incurs work. Excluding
that preparation would favor the larger resident configuration for the wrong
reason. The reference starts with an empty resident set so the full preparation
cost is visible.

\subsection{A workload law and a deadline produce different purchases}
\label{exec17:s8-resident-workload}

An expected-cost quote needs a workload law. Suppose, only for this example,
that each of the six requests independently chooses $A$, $B$ or $C$ with
equal probability. There are $3^6=729$ equally likely batches. This is an
authored finite law, not an inferred distribution of bank demand. It permits
both complete enumeration and an independent probability calculation for the
online least-recently-used rule.

For one slot, the first request always loads and each later request differs
from its predecessor with probability $2/3$. For three slots, loads equal the
number of distinct requested models. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \E N_1=1+5(2/3)=\frac{13}{3},\qquad
 \E N_3=3\{1-(2/3)^6\}=\frac{665}{243}.
 \label{exec17:s8-resident-means13}
\end{equation}
With two slots, before request $t\geq2$ there is only one resident model
exactly when all the preceding requests are equal. That event has probability
$3^{2-t}$. Conditional on it, the next request misses with probability $2/3$;
otherwise there are two resident models and the next independent request
misses with probability $1/3$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \E N_2=1+\sum_{t=2}^{6}\left\{\frac13+\frac13\,3^{2-t}\right\}
       =\frac{769}{243}.
 \label{exec17:s8-resident-mean2}
\end{equation}
The independence of the next request from the resident state is essential to
this calculation. Changing model frequencies or making the next model depend
on previous outputs changes the law. Exhaustive evaluation of all 729 batches
matches these three rational expectations exactly; it is not Monte Carlo
evidence with an estimated standard error \citep{exec17_s8_residency}.

Now assign an illustrative allocated capacity charge of USD~0, 120 and 300
per six-request planning batch to one, two and three slots. These are
incremental accounting assumptions, not a vendor's billing units or quotes.
Price a time unit at USD~20, including the stipulated common preparation and
waiting cost. For a known batch, the objective is the charge plus $20T_m$;
for the specified random online workload, it is the charge plus $20\E T_m$.
The comparison is
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
 \text{resident slots}&1&2&3\\ \hline
 \text{known-batch optimum time}&36&26&21\\
 \text{known-batch total USD}&720&640&720\\
 \text{online mean total USD}&1660/3&135220/243&168560/243
\end{array}.
\label{exec17:s8-resident-prices}
\end{equation}
Thus the known batch favors two slots; the random online workload favors one,
with expected cost approximately USD~553.33 against USD~556.46 for two and
USD~693.66 for three. The small expected difference between one and two is a
property of these supplied numbers. It would be irresponsible to regard it
as a measured procurement margin without observing the workload and costs.

A hard deadline is a separate constraint. Over the finite support of six-request
sequences, one slot and two-slot least-recently-used scheduling both have
worst completion time 36. Three slots have worst completion time 21, because
there are at most three distinct models. A requirement to finish every supported
sequence within 30 units therefore selects three among these online
configurations, despite its higher mean cost. For the known batch, the exact
two-slot plan finishes at 26 and is eligible. The facts available before
purchase decide whether that cheaper plan is a relevant comparator.

This is also a limitation of any deterministic online two-slot policy under
an unrestricted adaptive arrival model. An adversary observing the resident
set can always request a missing one of the three models, forcing a load at
each of the six requests. That argument does not assert that bank arrivals are
adversarial. It shows why a worst-case claim cannot be inferred from an average
or a prescient batch schedule. If requests are random and independent, use
that declared law. If the bank requires a deadline only with a specified
probability, estimate that tail under an appropriate workload model and
report the failed fraction. Do not silently reinterpret ``every request'' as
``the average request.''

The example has deliberately held scientific acceptance fixed across slots:
all model versions and outputs are identical and no inference is skipped.
An apparent speedup obtained by retaining an old model after an approved
parameter update changes that condition. If $A$ becomes $A'$ midway through
a batch, the residency identity and load count must change. If the policy
changes the model selected after observing an output, future requests are
endogenous to the earlier computation and the independent uniform calculation
no longer applies. A prototype should retain those version and decision events
so that the capacity estimate corresponds to the actual approved analysis.

\subsection{What the capacity prototype must reveal before a quote}

The loaded-instance model identifies a small set of observations with direct
acquisition consequences. For each permitted configuration, measure loading,
license validation, readiness, inference and unload times separately; keep
resident versions and model-file locations throughout the trace. An engine
that runs quickly after loading can still be unsuitable for frequent model
changes. A route with customer-resident model files can still depend on the
documented license-server connection. An interrupted or unlicensed load must
remain in the assigned workload, with its actual fallback or unresolved
deadline; it is not a zero-time successful request
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

Two comparisons should then be reported. A known-batch reference measures
what a sufficiently informed scheduler could attain under the same capacity
and ordering constraints. A deployable online policy measures what the
proposed service can attain using only information available at each request.
The gap between them is a scheduling opportunity, not evidence that the vendor
engine is inefficient. Original loaded-model traces allow the bank to ask
whether an additional instance or a better retention policy would address the
observed bottleneck. A mean CPU time without such traces cannot distinguish
those purchases.

License and runtime failures require a further state. The finite time formula
assumes every required load is permitted and finishes in its stipulated time.
If validation is unavailable with no known upper delay, this model supplies
no finite deadline guarantee. The response is to keep that operational input
open, evaluate an allowed fallback and avoid quoting unsupported availability.
The same separation applies to a SMILE search interrupted during an iteration:
extra licensed capacity does not turn an incomplete graph into a completed
scientific result. A capacity proposal should preserve the completed/incomplete
state while measuring its operational cost \citep{exec13_smile_pc}.

Finally, allocate recurring capacity charges to a planning workload only once.
A license shared by funding and servicing analyses is a common fixed resource;
charging its full annual fee independently to each journey overstates total
cost, while omitting one journey's occupancy understates contention. The
prototype should report joint resident intervals and the rule for allocating
their expense. A price negotiation can then use both a scientifically matched
output requirement and a concrete instance quantity. The proposal's immediate
deliverable is this testable purchasing model. Actual durations, permitted
concurrency, deployment rights, prices and bank workload remain observations
to obtain after the narrow prototype shows merit.

\subsection{Extra instances do not remove a shared licensing dependency}
\label{exec17:s8-availability}

The inspected Bayesia API description separates model-file location from
license validation: a Java configuration can retain model files on customer
servers while validating credentials with the vendor's license server when
launching an instance. It states a permanent connection requirement. This
has a specific acquisition consequence. A proposal for several local replicas
must explain which dependencies those replicas share. Local data residency
does not, by itself, imply independent recovery or offline operation
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

Consider a deliberately narrow recovery experiment. All replicas are initially
unloaded, and a required model must become ready during a stated recovery
window. Suppose validation must be available in that window, and at least one
local worker must be able to load the model. Let $C$ indicate common validation
availability and $R_j$ local readiness for replica $j$. Write
$\Prob(C=0)=q$ and $\Prob(R_j=0)=p$. In this authored model the $R_j$ are
mutually independent and independent of $C$. The success event is
\begin{equation}
 \{C=1\}\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^m\{R_j=1\},\qquad
 A_m=(1-q)(1-p^m).
 \label{exec17:s8-common-recovery}
\end{equation}
The formula follows by taking the complement of all local workers failing,
then applying the stated independence from validation. It concerns a cold
recovery request. It is not a statement that disconnecting the license server
terminates a currently loaded engine or makes every ordinary query fail;
that behavior is not established by the inspected page.

With stipulated $p=1/10$ and $q=1/50$, one, two and three replicas have success
probabilities .882, .9702 and .97902. Ignoring the shared validation gate would
report .9, .99 and .999 instead. The difference matters to a capacity purchase:
a two-replica calculation that drops the common dependency appears to meet a
.99 target but actually does not meet it in this very model. Increasing $m$
cannot solve that problem, since
\begin{equation}
 A_m\leq1-q=.98<.99.
 \label{exec17:s8-recovery-ceiling}
\end{equation}
The probability calculation is reproduced by summing every common/local
readiness combination for each of the three capacities
\citep{exec17_s8_availability}.

For a general desired probability $a$, the same model requires
$q\leq1-a$ before replica count can be sufficient. When $q<1-a$ and
$0<p<1$, a finite sufficient count satisfies
\begin{equation}
 p^m\leq1-\frac{a}{1-q}.
 \label{exec17:s8-recovery-condition}
\end{equation}
At $q=1-a$, the right side is zero, so no finite count with $p>0$ attains
the boundary. These conditions do not estimate $p$ or $q$; they identify which
measurement or architectural change could alter a purchase decision. A second
local machine cannot resolve an unavailable common credential path. A different
permitted validation arrangement, a genuinely ready standby or an alternative
engine may change the event structure, but each requires its own documented
rights, scientific equivalence and operating evidence.

The standby distinction is especially easy to obscure in a benchmark.
Keeping a model already loaded changes the recovery experiment and can consume
a licensed instance during the quiet period. Starting the standby only after
failure changes its readiness time and can expose the shared validation path.
Neither arrangement is universally cheaper or more reliable from the public
description alone. A quote should specify whether the required capacity
includes retained standby models, when validation occurs and what recovery
behavior the vendor actually supports. A performance comparison should count
the preparation and occupancy of that exact arrangement.

The independence assumption also needs scrutiny. Replicas on one host can
share a process supervisor, memory pressure and storage. Replicas on different
hosts can still share a network route, model store or license service. If local
readiness is perfectly common rather than independent, adding replicas leaves
local success at $1-p$, making the cold-recovery probability $(1-q)(1-p)$
for every $m$. The appropriate input is a joint failure model, not a list of
machines. This is a practical reason to preserve configuration and dependency
identity alongside observed failure counts.

The inexpensive prototype can first exercise the common layer against a
declared unavailable-validation response and an interrupted model load. It
should retain the recovery request, each attempt, elapsed time and the eventual
fallback or unresolved result. Such fixtures can demonstrate correct accounting
and refusal before a paid engine is available. They cannot establish actual
license-server uptime or vendor failover behavior. A later permitted trial
should inspect those behaviors using the exact contracted edition and recovery
arrangement. A broad availability claim waits for observations from the relevant
workload and time horizon.

This dependency calculation belongs in the commercial comparison because it
can change the item being purchased. A capacity-only quotation may offer more
instances without addressing the binding common recovery condition. A proposed
open or commercial alternative must preserve the same causal outputs before
its different recovery architecture is economically comparable. The useful
procurement conclusion is conditional and specific: price the supported
configuration that can satisfy the required recovery experiment, and record
when the current evidence cannot identify such a configuration.

\section{Exact hypothetical cost and route choice}
\label{exec17:s8-cost}

To make the acquisition decision reviewable, consider three routes for $n$
assigned requests in one planning year. The values below are deliberately
round and hypothetical:
\begin{align}
 C_B(n)&=420000+1.8n, &\text{build the common service},\\
 C_L(n)&=190000+5.6n, &\text{licensed adapter and conversion},\\
 C_H(n)&=285000+3n, &\text{hybrid: common ledger plus licensed engine}.
 \label{exec17:s8-costs}
\end{align}
The fixed terms include engineering, review and deployment preparation. The
variable terms include request preparation and operation. They do not include
an invented vendor quote. The exact pairwise crossings are
\begin{equation}
 n_{LB}=\frac{230000}{3.8}=\frac{1150000}{19},\qquad
 n_{LH}=\frac{95000}{2.6}=\frac{475000}{13},\qquad
 n_{HB}=\frac{135000}{1.2}=112500.
 \label{exec17:s8-crossings}
\end{equation}
Thus the licensed route is cheapest at low volume under this model, the
hybrid route is cheapest in the middle, and building the common service is
cheapest at sufficiently high volume. The ordering is a consequence of the
assumed costs, not a recommendation to purchase any named product.

At volumes 10,000, 50,000 and 150,000, the exact costs in USD are respectively
$(246000,315000,438000)$ for licensed, hybrid and build at the first volume;
$(470000,435000,510000)$ at the second; and $(1030000,735000,690000)$ at the
third. The route minimizing maximum regret over these three scenarios is hybrid:
its regret is USD~69,000, compared with USD~340,000 for licensed and USD~192,000 for
build. This is a minimax decision over a declared volume set, not an expected
bank cost. Minimizing the largest absolute cost instead selects build: its
largest cost is USD~690,000, against USD~735,000 for hybrid and USD~1,030,000
for licensed. A committee must choose its objective before using the table.
The exact enumeration is reproduced in the accompanying S8 calculation record
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

Semantic admissibility comes first. If a licensed route cannot preserve a
disconnected IV set, a graph class, or a sequential policy's logging law, its
cost is not comparable to a build route that can. The route is then rejected
for that target regardless of its nominal price. The prototype should measure
the fraction of requests that require conversion, refusal or human review and
replace the planning terms with observed quantities.

\subsection{A quote ceiling for a genuinely comparable configuration}

Suppose the hypothetical hybrid envelope excludes a new annual deployment
charge $z\geq0$, with all other expenses held fixed. It remains competitive
with both alternatives exactly when
\begin{equation}
 z\leq z_{\max}(n)=\min\{135000-1.2n,\,-95000+2.6n\}.
 \label{exec17:s8-quote-ceiling}
\end{equation}
At 50,000 requests the admissible increment is at most USD~35,000. Across
continuous nonnegative volume, the largest such ceiling occurs where the two
terms agree, $n=1150000/19$, and equals USD~$1185000/19$, approximately
62,368.42. Outside the interval from $475000/13$ to 112500 requests, hybrid
is already more costly than another route even with $z=0$. At integer request
counts the continuous maximum is an upper bound; it need not be attained.
These are affordable-price ceilings under assumed equal service, not
estimates of a vendor's willingness to sell \citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

A quote for an analyst seat cannot be inserted as $z$ for a server deployment.
Nor can an instance fee be multiplied by customer count when the contract
counts loaded models. First choose the configuration and feasible schedule,
then enter its charge in the correct fixed or occupancy-dependent term.
If the quote changes the route's permitted geographic use, history retention
or model-learning operations, reassess admissibility before comparing cost.
The cost surface makes this negotiation specific: it identifies the required
entitlement and the largest incremental price consistent with the stated
volume and alternative routes.

Shared costs need the same discipline. If the bank already operates a source
and assumption ledger required by every route, its sunk acquisition cost
does not become a saving unique to hybrid. If building that ledger is still
necessary in every route, include its incremental cost in every relevant
envelope. Likewise, a manual fallback needed after an unsupported request
must be charged to the assigned population. Earlier cost accounting develops
these principles generally; here their role is to prevent a favorable
commercial comparison obtained by assigning common scientific review to only
one alternative. The proposal needs observed workflow accounts before any
of the displayed cost inequalities can support a procurement decision.

\section{The value of a compatibility signal}
\label{exec17:s8-signal}

The uncertain acquisition fact may be compatibility with the required
information and uncertainty contract. At 50,000 assigned requests, suppose
that a common licensed engine has a good or bad compatibility state. Both
licensed routes share that state. Their annual costs, in USD, are
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|rr}
 & \text{good} & \text{bad}\\ \hline
 B&510000&510000\\
 L&470000&720000\\
 H&435000&535000
\end{array}.
\label{exec17:s8-signal-cost-table}
\end{equation}
The bad-state additions pay for conversion repair, replacement or fallback
that restores the same scientific acceptance. A route with an uncorrected
scientific error is inadmissible; these are not prices for tolerating wrong
causal answers. Build is compatible in both states by assumption. This is an
acquisition scenario, not a measured advantage of our present prototype.

Let the prior good-state probability be $3/5$. Expected costs are USD~510,000,
570,000 and 475,000 for $B,L,H$ respectively, so the no-test choice is hybrid.
A proposed fixture has sensitivity $19/20$ and false-positive probability
$1/20$ for the common compatibility state. The four joint probabilities for
good-positive, bad-positive, good-negative and bad-negative are .57, .02, .03
and .38. Bayes' rule therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \Prob(+)=\frac{59}{100},\quad
 \Prob(\mathrm{good}\mid +)=\frac{57}{59},\quad
 \Prob(\mathrm{good}\mid -)=\frac{3}{41}.
 \label{exec17:s8-signal-posterior}
\end{equation}
For a posterior probability $p$, the route costs are
$510000$, $720000-250000p$ and $535000-100000p$.
Licensed is dominated by hybrid throughout $0\leq p\leq1$, since their
difference is $185000-150000p>0$. Hybrid beats build exactly when $p>1/4$.
Thus a positive signal retains hybrid and a negative signal selects build.
Using the joint probabilities avoids rounding the conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{signal}}=.57(435000)+.02(535000)+.41(510000)=467750.
 \label{exec17:s8-signal-value}
\end{equation}
Gross information value is USD~7,250. A USD~5,000 test has net value
USD~2,250; a USD~15,000 test has net value minus USD~7,750. Perfect state
information would select hybrid in the good state and build in the bad state,
for expected cost USD~465,000 and value USD~10,000. This upper bound rules out
any USD~15,000 experiment justified only by this one annual decision. Separate enumeration of all nine
signal-contingent pairs of routes confirms this optimum \citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

This example connects the commercial architecture to experiment design. Testing
the R and Python wrappers of the same SMILE core does not supply two independent
observations of a core semantic defect. Wrapper-specific serialization defects
need separate states or conditional error laws. Similarly, a public fixture
with known answers can reveal whether graph marks or utility indices survive
conversion, while it cannot authenticate deployment rights or estimate a
bank's future mixture of requests. The probabilities above are stipulated
properties of a signal about one acquisition state. They are not a claim that
95\% sensitivity on generated examples transfers to a bank.

A prospective measurement must distinguish failure at the engine, wrapper,
licensing, data-conversion and human-review levels. Count every assigned
fixture, including unavailable and unsupported cases. Before increasing the
budget, recompute the choice with every feasible alternative, including doing
the narrow task with an existing analyst tool. The experiment is valuable only
through the decision it can change, at the scale and time horizon to which its
information applies.

\section{Adoption decisions and remaining competitive gaps}
\label{exec17:s8-decision}

The comparison yields six concrete decisions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reuse a commercial editor} for analyst graph construction when
      the bank accepts the edition, licensing and model-export boundary. Keep
      the unresolved class and source evidence in the common ledger.
\item \textbf{Integrate a licensed engine} only for a named supplied-model,
      intervention or inference profile whose wrapper, error and deployment
      behavior pass a matched public/generated fixture.
\item \textbf{Adopt open-source components} when the original source, license,
      dependency rights, coordinate convention and uncertainty object can be
      preserved. A package's presence in a catalog is not a runtime result.
\item \textbf{Port selectively} only for a narrow algorithm whose semantics,
      numerical tolerances and adverse cases can be reproduced. Proprietary
      SMILE or hosted vendor implementations are not porting candidates.
\item \textbf{Build the semantic ledger and refusal layer} because it spans
      graph classes, target clocks, uncertainty, artifacts and licenses rather
      than one estimator. This is the proposed product's defensible common
      capability, still unqualified at bank scale.
\item \textbf{Reject a route for a target} when it changes the estimand,
      drops unresolved structure, cannot preserve an uncertainty set or lacks
      a lawful deployment path. Rejection is a successful proposal outcome.
\end{enumerate}

The gaps remain substantial. The local service has no general discovery or
sequential-policy runtime; no commercial engine has been executed here; exact
edition parity, paid prices, embedding rights and support terms remain unknown
for many families; and arbitrary latent graphs, dependent learners, authentic
assignment truth and bank economics remain open. A complete census across all
17 areas requires additional primary manuals and owner inputs. The present
chapter narrows what should be measured and purchased; it does not claim that
the competitive survey is closed.

\section{A prototype-to-purchase protocol}
\label{exec17:s8-protocol}

The next public/generated comparison should freeze one target from each of four
journeys: a graph-class intervention query for discovery, a weak-IV funding set, a
competing-event servicing conversion and an observation identity/clock map.
Include a fixed-history policy value as an additional servicing case. For each target, freeze the
input schema, unit, clock, graph/model, method, uncertainty object, license
mode, seed, resource budget and stopping rule. Run the common ledger, a selected
open-source reference and a documented commercial route when accessible. Report
semantic equivalence, refusal/unsupported counts, numerical error, uncertainty
shape, preparation and review time, memory, queueing, recovery and full cost.

The study must include negative controls: a randomly oriented graph, a
transposed node matrix, an unsupported delayed-entry survival record, a
nonpositive logging probability, a direct-effect IV violation and a recommendation
metric presented as though it were a causal value. A system passes only when it
preserves the refusal or identifies the changed target. The full denominator
includes unavailable originals, failed conversions and manual reviews.

An accepted public/generated result would justify a narrow paid trial. It would
not justify a bank deployment. The paid trial would bind the exact edition,
entitlement, data residency, service account, operating platform and publication
rights, then repeat the frozen matched protocol. Authentic bank assignment,
audit truth, accepted loss and institutional economics remain later inputs.
The prospective cases and their source-specific acceptance conditions are
recorded separately from the exact arithmetic \citep{exec17_s8_design}.

\label{exec17:s8-end}

Commercial alternatives already provide meaningful graph, model, estimator,

\section{Commercial discovery: compare the retained graph before the interface}
\label{exec13:commercial}

Commercial Bayesian-network systems already address substantial parts of the
workflow that a new causal product would have to build. The relevant comparison
includes interactive model construction, structure and parameter learning,
background restrictions, model exchange and programmatic inference. The
proposal should specify which of these services it would reuse and which
scientific contract it would add. A common command-line or MCP interface
alone would not establish an advantage over an existing modeling environment.

The following comparison uses inspected vendor technical documentation.
The GeNIe manual declares version 5.0.R2, built on 23 November 2024; the
SMILE programmer's manual declares version 2.2.4.R1, built on 27 April 2024.
The public URLs were retrieved after the 7 October 2026 literature cutoff,
so those headers identify the declared manuals without proving their exact
historical bytes at the cutoff. The inspected BayesiaLab pages are mutable
and do not consistently pin the technical discussion to a numbered release.
Their findings are consequently dated documentation observations. No vendor
executable, commercial trial or paid service was run for this comparison
\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_smile_intro,exec13_bayesia_structure}.

GeNIe exposes both a PC route and a Bayesian Search route. The PC manual
documents a default significance level of 0.05, maximum adjacency of eight,
and no time limit by default. It allows continuous data under a multivariate
Gaussian assumption and returns a pattern for further inspection. The
manual's suggested histogram and scatterplot checks are useful exploratory
diagnostics; they cannot establish the full joint distribution or the causal
assumptions. Its Bayesian Search discussion instead describes score-guided
hill climbing with restarts, a BDeu-related sample-size parameter and a
structural prior. A seed of zero uses the processor clock, so it is not a
fixed reproducibility seed. These are different procedures to configure
and compare, even though they share a graphical application
\citep{exec13_genie_pc,exec13_genie_bs}.

The pattern editor is a particularly important capability. Its documentation
explicitly discusses an equivalence class and permits inspection of unresolved
arcs. It also documents a \emph{Create DAG} command that randomly chooses
directions for unresolved arcs while producing an acyclic graph. That command
can be useful for constructing a representative Bayesian network, but the
choice does not add observational evidence for the selected directions.
The proposed product must preserve the pre-conversion pattern and label any
selected representative. It should not describe GeNIe as necessarily forcing
a single graph: the documented pattern workflow is itself a useful alternative
\citep{exec13_genie_pattern}.

Consider the funding law in Section~\ref{exec13:survey}. Its three Gaussian
models share the same covariance but imply effects of either zero or 0.48
thousand currency units per percentage point. A proposed interface that keeps
only a randomly chosen representative could report either zero or 120 currency
units per customer for the 25 basis point action. The difference would come
from the extra orientation choice. This is a project counterexample to a
lossy integration design; it is not an observed GeNIe estimate or a claim
that the vendor's documented interface conceals that choice. A matched trial
should inspect the pattern, the conversion and the returned effect separately
\citep{exec13_examples}.

BayesiaLab supplies another relevant alternative. Its structural-learning
overview describes heuristic search and an MDL score used to compare learned
networks. The EQ page explicitly describes search over equivalence classes.
That makes class-aware search a documented competitor capability, rather
than a novelty the proposed library can claim simply by retaining ambiguous
graphs. A score optimized over graph structures still does not validate
the banking assignment mechanism or supply an intervention-effect confidence
region. Those are additional scientific questions
\citep{exec13_bayesia_structure,exec13_bayesia_eq}.

The EQ page also documents a consequential distinction among restrictions:
fixed arcs are treated as ordinary arcs, whereas forbidden arcs and temporal
indices are taken into account. A workflow that marks a bank-supplied edge
as fixed must therefore inspect the selected algorithm's actual meaning for
that setting. A generic \texttt{required\_edges} field cannot be mapped to a
similarly named graphical control without a behavioral check. The same page's
description of efficiency is a vendor claim, not a measured runtime result
or a proof of finding the global optimum in this proposal
\citep{exec13_bayesia_eq}.

\section{What a commercial integration must retain}

SMILE is the proprietary C++ engine underlying GeNIe. Its programmer's manual
describes compiled platform distributions and wrappers for Java, Python, R,
.NET and COM. These are access paths to an engine, not independent discoveries
of five competing causal implementations. Their individual interoperability
still needs review. The C++ manual does not, by itself, verify that every
wrapper exposes every endpoint type and error state
\citep{exec13_smile_intro}.

The \texttt{DSL\_pc::Learn} reference returns a \texttt{DSL\_pattern} and a
success or error code. A progress callback can interrupt learning, in which
case the documented return is \texttt{DSL\_INTERRUPTED}. The time limit is
checked after an iteration, so the documented setting is not evidence of a
strict external wall-time guarantee. A resource-bounded wrapper should retain
interruption as an incomplete computation and enforce its own lifecycle
contract if a hard deadline is required. Stopping the process cannot make
the unfinished graph a completed scientific answer
\citep{exec13_smile_pc}.

Graph conversion also requires care. The \texttt{DSL\_pattern} reference
declares absent, undirected and directed edge types, indexed by the dataset's
variable order. Its conversion to a network assumes that the dataset and
pattern coordinates correspond. GeNIe's graphical pattern documentation
uses a richer visual description, including colored and double-headed arcs.
These pages alone do not establish a complete equivalence between every
graphical mark and the C++ representation, or a standard CPDAG/PAG
serialization. Before reuse, the adapter needs named-node fixtures that
distinguish a collider, an unresolved path, a deliberately transposed
matrix and a permuted dataset. It should preserve any unresolved mapping
as an unsupported conversion
\citep{exec13_smile_pattern,exec13_genie_pattern}.

Bayesia's programmatic offering needs a different distinction. Its API overview
separates development and production configurations, and it describes a
stateless REST service for inference with an existing Bayesian network.
That description does not establish that the same REST service performs
structural discovery. The overview also describes Java configurations and
production licensing by the number of simultaneously loaded model instances.
Its table distinguishes customer-hosted Java models from the described REST
service's hosted model location. An integration proposal must select the
actual interface and operation before estimating deployment cost or data
movement. A label such as ``Bayesia API'' is too broad for either conclusion
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

\section{Commercial latent-variable methods and the banking

A relationship manager can observe borrower risk that is absent from the

is therefore a relevant alternative, but that phrase covers several
different services. An analyst may specify a measurement model and
estimate its parameters; a clustering procedure may construct unobserved
customer classes; a discovery procedure may retain causal relationships
compatible with hidden common causes; or a randomized experiment may
identify an effect for an unobserved compliance class. The proposed
product needs to choose among these services using the decision and
information available to the bank.

This comparison examines the declared GeNIe manual family 5.0.R2,
mutable BayesiaLab and Netica help, the Stata latent-class example
carrying a 2025 copyright and release-19 data reference, SAS/STAT 14.1
CALIS documentation, and Chapter 7 of the guide linked by Mplus as
Version 8. These are specific documentation observations. The public
copies were acquired after the 7 October 2026 literature cutoff; their
headers and links identify editions without establishing every page's
exact bytes at that cutoff. No commercial executable was run, and
documentation of one path cannot establish the presence or absence of
every other method in the vendor's ecosystem.
\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_latent_genie_parameters,
exec13_latent_bayesia_data,exec13_latent_netica_structure,
exec13_latent_stata_lca,exec13_latent_sas_calis,exec13_latent_mplus}

\hypertarget{the-object-that-must-survive-a-discovery-interface}{%
\subsection{The object that must survive a discovery
interface}\label{the-object-that-must-survive-a-discovery-interface}}

For causal discovery with unmeasured common causes, a learned latent
class is different from an equivalence class of causal graphs. In the
no-selection ancestral-graph construction reviewed by
\citet[Section 3]{zhang2008}, a maximal ancestral graph represents
marginal independence and ancestral relations among observed variables.
A directed edge records ancestry in the underlying causal DAG; it need
not exclude additional hidden confounding. A bidirected edge records the
absence of ancestry in either direction and the presence of a latent
common-cause path. It does not identify a particular borrower-risk
factor, its dimension, or its measurement equation.

A partial ancestral graph, or PAG, represents a Markov equivalence class
of these graphs. Its invariant tails and arrowheads are shared across
the class, while a circle records an unresolved endpoint. Under the
stated Markov, faithfulness and graph assumptions,
conditional-independence information can constrain this object. A fitted
mixture component or a posterior probability of customer membership is a
different mathematical output. Neither can replace a circle endpoint
merely because its label contains the word ``latent.'\,' The cited
construction excludes selection; Zhang explicitly notes that selection
requires a broader ancestral-graph class and additional rules. A
loan-book analysis conditional on approval must retain that extra
question. \citep[pp.1442--1445]{zhang2008}

The proposed library consequently needs separate representations for a
supplied latent-variable model, a fitted measurement or mixture model, a
discovered graph class and an identified intervention query. Parameters,
class probabilities and graph endpoints have different meanings. The
separation also determines the correct baseline: a commercial mixture
fit should be compared with the same mixture specification, while a
proposed PAG operation should be compared with a discovery method under
the same latent and selection assumptions. The presence of a diagram is
insufficient to make these comparisons interchangeable.

\hypertarget{a-good-observational-fit-can-leave-the-lending-effect-unresolved}{%
\subsection{A good observational fit can leave the lending effect
unresolved}\label{a-good-observational-fit-can-leave-the-lending-effect-unresolved}}

Let \(A\) be a centered concession and \(Y\) a centered subsequent loss.
Consider two Gaussian structural models with independent disturbances.
In the first, \[
 A=\varepsilon_A,\qquad
 Y=\frac35 A+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 \operatorname{var}(\varepsilon_A)=1,\quad
 \operatorname{var}(\varepsilon_Y)=\frac{16}{25}.
\] In the second, \[
 A=U+\eta_A,\qquad Y=U+\eta_Y,\qquad
 \operatorname{var}(U)=\frac35,\quad
 \operatorname{var}(\eta_A)=\operatorname{var}(\eta_Y)=\frac25.
\] The unobserved \(U\) represents a stipulated risk component. Both
models have exactly the same observed covariance matrix, \[
 \Sigma_{(A,Y)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&3/5\\3/5&1\end{pmatrix},
\] and therefore the same centered bivariate Gaussian distribution.
Nevertheless, replacing the equation for \(A\) changes the mean of \(Y\)
by \(3/5\) per unit in the first model and by zero in the second. The
second model's observational regression slope remains \(3/5\); it
describes risk-related assignment, not an intervention response. These
two models establish nonidentification within any proposed model class
containing both. They do not enumerate every compatible model or
establish that the full identified set contains only two effects.
\citep{exec13_latent_examples}

For an explicitly illustrative monetary scale, let one concession unit
equal one percentage point and one centered-loss unit equal one thousand
currency units per borrower. A 25 basis point action then changes
expected loss by 150 currency units under the first model and zero under
the second. The equality of the observed likelihoods cannot choose
between those recommendations. Increasing the observational sample or
selecting the better optimizer cannot resolve an equality of
distributions. The calculation is exact project algebra with stipulated
units, not a fitted bank estimate or a vendor result.
\citep{exec13_latent_examples}

A risk score or inferred segment also needs a measurement argument
before it can close that gap. In a separate Gaussian example, take
independent unit-variance \(U,\varepsilon_A,\varepsilon_Y\), and let \[
 A=U+\varepsilon_A,\qquad Y=U+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 W=U+\varepsilon_W,
\] with independent proxy noise of variance \(s\). There is still no
causal effect of \(A\) on \(Y\). Given \(W\), the residual variance of
risk is \(v=s/(1+s)\). Consequently, \[
 \frac{\operatorname{cov}(A,Y\mid W)}
      {\operatorname{var}(A\mid W)}=
 \frac{v}{1+v}.
\] With \(s=1\), adjustment reduces the observational slope from \(1/2\)
to \(1/3\), but the causal effect remains zero. Only the stipulated
error-free limit removes this particular source of confounding. This
example does not rule out identification from appropriately designed
multiple proxies or a justified measurement model; it shows why the mere
availability of an inferred score is insufficient. If the score is
trained using post-offer outcomes, its information clock needs an
additional review. \citep{exec13_latent_examples}

\hypertarget{existing-engines-for-supplied-models-and-latent-classes}{%
\subsection{Existing engines for supplied models and latent
classes}\label{existing-engines-for-supplied-models-and-latent-classes}}

GeNIe's parameter-learning documentation starts with an existing network
whose structure is already defined. Its EM procedure can learn
parameters with missing observations and explicitly discusses random
initialization when latent variables are present. The source states that
parameter learning leaves structure fixed. It also makes a consequential
distinction: its continuous multivariate-Gaussian EM implementation
described on that page does not allow missing values. The proposal
cannot infer that every data-type/missingness combination is supported
from the general EM label. \citep{exec13_latent_genie_parameters}

The structure-learning introduction describes a separate interface over
selected data columns. In that declared manual, the routines accept
categorical variables; PC additionally accepts jointly Gaussian
continuous variables. Mixed categorical/continuous structure learning
requires discretization, and the manual states a missing-record
restriction with an exception for the Naive Bayes routine that
constructs its prescribed structure. These are documented restrictions
of the selected interface, not a claim about all current BayesFusion
products. An initial reuse route is therefore a supplied discrete latent
network with an explicit observation model and parameter-fitting
contract. Its fitted parameters must not be advertised as a discovery
result that has resolved hidden confounding.
\citep{exec13_latent_genie_structure,exec13_latent_genie_parameters}

BayesiaLab supplies substantive latent-representation alternatives. Its
Data Clustering documentation creates a new factor node whose states are
clusters. The ordinary construction uses a Naive Bayes structure with
the factor as parent of the manifest variables, random initialization,
and EM updates based on posterior class probabilities. Conditional
independence of the manifest variables given the class is part of that
representation. The documented Multinet alternative combines a Naive
structure with class-specific maximum-weight spanning trees and hard
class assignments during its alternating procedure. Thus the vendor
offers more than a single generic clustering option; the representation
and assignment policy belong in a matched comparison.
\citep{exec13_latent_bayesia_data}

Its heterogeneity option changes the objective toward segmentations that
increase a stated mutual-information index with the target. That can be
useful for describing different customer groups. An increase in that
index is neither an estimate of treatment-effect heterogeneity nor
evidence that the induced factor blocks every confounding path. The
adjacent Variable Clustering tool groups nodes using Arc Force, offers a
dendrogram, and can feed Multiple Clustering to construct synthetic
variables. It groups variables rather than immediately identifying a
customer's hidden risk. A proposed integration must retain which of
these operations was performed, its variables and target, soft versus
hard assignments, and the data used to construct the representation.
\citep{exec13_latent_bayesia_data,exec13_latent_bayesia_variables}

Netica's Learning Algorithms help describes counting, EM and conjugate
gradient methods for conditional probability tables. Counting uses cases
with definite values for the node and its parents; the page directs
latent or incomplete-data applications toward EM or gradient methods and
notes local optima. Its adjacent Structure Learning page describes TAN
learning around a selected diagnostic or predictive target, expressly
motivating outgoing target links by predictive dependence. This is a
useful modeling and prediction alternative. It does not supply a causal
interpretation of those target-to-predictor arrows. The structure page
refers to the version 5.00 introduction of TAN; that historical feature
description is not a verified inventory of a current Netica release.
\citep{exec13_latent_netica_algorithms,exec13_latent_netica_structure}

The Netica discussion writes an objective proportional to
\(p(D\mid N)p(N)\), where \(N\) denotes the candidate network and its
parameters. When the prior varies across candidates, this is a posterior
criterion, even though the accompanying discussion also uses
maximum-likelihood language. A matched adapter comparison must therefore
record the prior and existing probability tables as well as the reported
fit statistic. The product should compare the actual configured
objective; a shared method name does not establish numerical
equivalence. \citep{exec13_latent_netica_algorithms}

Stata's worked latent-class example is more explicit about the fitted
quantity. It supplies a two-class categorical latent variable through
\texttt{lclass(C 2)} and estimates class-specific intercepts for four
binary responses using \texttt{gsem}. The example reports marginal class
probabilities, posterior membership probabilities and hard assignments
obtained by thresholding those probabilities. These are distinct
summaries: the reported marginal proportions need not equal the
fractions of people assigned by the threshold rule. Neither summary is
automatically an observed pre-offer covariate or a randomized treatment
class. \citep[pp.2--6]{exec13_latent_stata_lca}

The same Stata example shows an EM initialization reaching its iteration
limit before full-model fitting converges. That is not evidence that the
final fit failed; it demonstrates why an integration should preserve
stage-specific diagnostics instead of interpreting the first convergence
message as the entire result. A prototype can reuse this supplied-model
workflow as a latent-class comparator while retaining the chosen class
count, starts, fitted probabilities, uncertainty and final convergence
status. No Stata execution is reported here.
\citep[pp.3--5]{exec13_latent_stata_lca}

SAS/STAT 14.1 CALIS provides another substantial alternative for
specified latent models. Its overview describes factor,
measurement-error, covariance, path and other structural-equation
specifications, several modeling languages, parameter constraints, fit
diagnostics and reusable output data sets. ML and GLS carry
multivariate-normal assumptions in the documented formulation. FIML
handles missing data under the stated missing-at-random assumption. The
source distinguishes Satorra--Bentler scaled inference from the separate
robust procedure that downweights outliers. These choices address
different statistical problems; selecting one does not validate the
causal assignment or make nonrandom observation ignorable. The new
product currently lacks this breadth of fitted-model workflow and should
justify any replacement rather than treating a new interface as
equivalent functionality. \citep{exec13_latent_sas_calis}

\hypertarget{mplus-is-also-a-causal-effect-comparator}{%
\subsection{Mplus is also a causal-effect
comparator}\label{mplus-is-also-a-causal-effect-comparator}}

The Mplus mixture chapter distinguishes measurement and structural
models, supports categorical latent classes and extensions with
continuous latent variables, and describes multiple starts for local
solutions. It documents options for missing data and complex sampling,
including strata, clusters and weights. These declarations identify
specific modeling alternatives; they do not imply that any chosen
default handles the bank's sampling or missingness law. A comparison
should reproduce the selected model and settings before measuring
resource use or decision quality.
\citep[pp.165--171]{exec13_latent_mplus}

More importantly for this proposal, Examples 7.23 and 7.24 describe
complier-average causal-effect estimation in randomized trials. In
Example 7.23, compliance class is observed in the treatment arm and
latent in the control arm. Training indicators constrain membership, the
noncomplier treatment slope is fixed at zero, and the complier slope is
estimated. The source identifies maximum likelihood with robust standard
errors as the default for this example. This is an existing documented
causal-effect use of a latent-class model. The proposal cannot claim
novelty merely from combining a latent variable with an intervention
target. \citep[pp.203--205]{exec13_latent_mplus}

A banking interpretation is a randomized assistance offer. In this
example, \(Y\) denotes net customer value in thousands of currency
units, so a positive effect is beneficial. Let \(Z\) denote the offer,
\(D\) receipt of assistance, and \(C\) a pre-existing compliance type.
For a declared one-sided design, suppose \(D=ZC\), \(Z\) is randomized
independently of potential outcomes and \(C\), and the offer has no
direct effect on the outcome apart from receipt. Consistency and a
positive first stage are also required. Under these restrictions, with
\(p_c=\Pr(C=1)\), the offer contrasts satisfy \[
 \Delta_D=p_c,\qquad
 \Delta_Y=p_c\,\operatorname{E}\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid C=1\}.
\] The ratio identifies an effect for compliers, rather than the effect
of offering assistance to every eligible borrower. The experimental
design and the exclusion restriction supply the interpretation; a fitted
class alone does not.

An exact four-cell project law illustrates the distinction. Set
\(p_c=2/5\), assign the offer with probability \(1/2\), and define
\(Y=2+C+3D\) in thousands of currency units. The first stage is \(2/5\),
the offer effect is \(6/5\), and the receipt effect for compliers is
three. If an additional direct offer effect of \(1/5\) is present, the
offer effect becomes \(7/5\) and the same ratio becomes \(7/2\),
although the effect of receipt holding the offer fixed remains three.
Communication or reassurance accompanying an offer can therefore matter
to the model. The exact enumeration is a proposed-design diagnostic, not
an Mplus replication or an empirical estimate.
\citep{exec13_latent_examples}

Even a documented encoding deserves a numerical check. Example 7.24 sets
binary-indicator logit thresholds to \(+15\) and \(-15\) while
describing perfect class measurement. The smaller implied probability is
\(1/(1+e^{15})\), approximately \(3.0590\times10^{-7}\), rather than an
exact structural zero. This observation does not demonstrate an
empirical failure of Mplus. It means that a proposed adapter must
distinguish a deliberately near-deterministic measurement specification
from a hard membership constraint, including in comparisons with Example
7.23's training-variable route. \citep[pp.205--207]{exec13_latent_mplus,
exec13_latent_examples}

\hypertarget{choosing-a-commercial-latent-model-component}{%
\section{Choosing a commercial latent-model
component}\label{choosing-a-commercial-latent-model-component}}

\label{exec14:commercial-latent-choice}

The practical choice for a bank is a component that answers a specified
question under an explicit information law. A supplied borrower-risk
model, an induced customer segmentation, a discovered class of causal
graphs and an intervention effect are different purchases. Their outputs
can support one another only through an additional modeling argument.
This distinction also prevents the proposed library from taking credit
for functionality that an established commercial system already
documents while our canonical interface still reports an unsupported
operation.

Consider a lending team that wants to assess a concession before the
next credit-loss period. A documented latent-class model can describe
unobserved heterogeneity in repayment patterns. It need not determine
whether a concession caused a change in loss, because the same private
information can influence both the concession and the outcome. The
observational-equivalence and noisy-proxy examples in the preceding
commercial discussion continue to apply. A proposal to combine an
existing fitter with explicit causal assumptions may be useful; it must
not advertise the fitter as a solution to the missing information.

\hypertarget{six-concrete-alternatives-and-five-unresolved-comparisons}{%
\subsection{Six concrete alternatives and five unresolved

The inspected GeNIe manual identifies its family as 5.0.R2. Its separate

identities, not evidence that a particular pair of installed binaries

network, including initialization, latent variables and discrete
missing-data cases. It explicitly keeps the structure fixed. The stated
continuous multivariate-Gaussian implementation does not accept missing
values on that parameter-learning page. The structure-learning interface
has its own data restrictions. A reuse decision should therefore begin
with a fixed discrete model and its explicit observation law, rather

\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_smile_intro,exec13_latent_genie_structure,exec13_latent_genie_parameters}

simultaneously loaded model instances. Thus a bank that serves many
models concurrently may face a different license requirement from one
that loads models sequentially, even on the same physical machine. This
is a licensing observation, not a claim that either arrangement meets
the bank's latency requirements. The preserved API table links a USD
twelve-month price list but supplies no numeric fee in its inspected
cells. No numerical amount or binding quotation is available for the
compared deployment.
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api,exec13_bayesia_professional}

For the other selected engines, the technical examples do not establish
the needed edition-specific license, redistribution rights or full
deployment price. The comparison therefore distinguishes a
vendor-requested quotation, a public price-list link whose amount has
not been verified, and an unavailable price in the inspected sources.
None means zero cost. A proposed experiment can still use generated data
and an available open-source baseline while price and license evidence
for a commercial trial are acquired later if justified.

Let L be the license expense allocated to the intended analysis period,
I the integration and maintenance allocation, D the data-preparation
cost, H the human review cost, C the computation/storage cost, and O the
monitoring/recovery cost. For a candidate workflow, compare \[
 K=L+I+D+H+C+O
\] at the same accepted output quality. If m valid decisions are
produced, K/m is an accounting rate only when failed requests, manual
corrections and retries remain in K and the definition of a valid
decision is held fixed. It is not a causal return-on-investment
estimate. Faster inference on a supplied model cannot be compared
directly with a workflow that also constructs, reviews and fits that
model. The present evidence supplies some license categories and
configuration constraints; it supplies neither actual vendor workflow
timings nor observed bank prices.

\hypertarget{the-bounded-proposal-and-a-useful-next-comparison}{%
\subsection{The bounded proposal and a useful next
comparison}\label{the-bounded-proposal-and-a-useful-next-comparison}}

The observed local canonical service preserves supplied graph syntax,
declared context, selected simple effect/set operations and explicit
unsupported results. It does not expose a commercial latent fitter or a
general latent-discovery engine. The proposal is to add evidence-linked
formulation, distinct graph and measurement objects, selected adapters
and a decision interface that preserves ambiguity and uncertainty. Those
proposed controls are a reason to investigate an integration; they are
not an observed superiority claim against the vendors.

The matched comparisons answer different investment questions.
First, a supplied finite latent measurement model can compare fitting
and convergence under the same observation law, class count, prior and
initialization budget. Second, a representation task can compare class
probabilities and predictive use while preserving the distinction from
causal adjustment. Third, a graph discovery task needs explicit
latent/selection assumptions, equivalence-class outputs and an effect
query. A fitter or TAN predictor cannot enter the third comparison as if
it produced the required graph class. A randomized-compliance comparison
is a fourth, separate route when the actual design supports it.

For each route, freeze generated observed inputs and reserve hidden
truth for evaluation. Preserve nonidentification, empty cells, label
permutations, failed optimization, weak separation and alternative
missingness mechanisms in the denominator. Compare numerical outputs
only after checking that they represent the same object. Record full
workflow resources and unpriced components, then ask which additional
information or implementation step could change the recommendation. A
successful generated comparison can justify a scoped later licensed or
bank experiment. It cannot retroactively authenticate a vendor version,
create a missing identification assumption or confer institutional
acceptance.

\hypertarget{commercial-temporal-models-and-intervention-discovery}{%
\section{Commercial temporal models and intervention
discovery}\label{commercial-temporal-models-and-intervention-discovery}}

\label{exec13:commercial-temporal}

\hypertarget{the-commercial-comparison-starts-with-the-decision-clock}{%
\subsection{The commercial comparison starts with the decision
clock}\label{the-commercial-comparison-starts-with-the-decision-clock}}

A funding committee asks whether raising a quoted deposit rate by 25
basis points for one month will increase retained balances enough to
cover the additional expense. A forecasting team asks whether past rates
improve its balance forecast. A risk team asks whether the same
relationship survives a change in the historical pricing policy. All
three questions use time series, but they require different outputs. A
predictive lag coefficient can answer the second question without
identifying either of the other two.

The commercial landscape already provides substantial functionality
relevant to these questions. GeNIe and SMILE represent supplied dynamic
Bayesian networks, update beliefs over time, and learn parameters in a
declared structure. IBM SPSS offers automatic selection of predictive
inputs in temporal models. SAS and Stata provide established
multivariate time-series interfaces, while Stata separately exposes
structural restrictions and external-instrument identification. Mplus
supplies intensive-longitudinal models with random effects, latent
decompositions and explicit time-series syntax. BayesiaLab documents the
distinction between observation and intervention and provides
graph-based causal-analysis workflows. The proposal should explain how
these capabilities can be used and where its own implementation is less
mature; their existence rules out a claim that commercial software
offers only correlation tables.

This comparison is bounded to inspected official technical text. GeNIe's
manual identifies itself as 5.0.R2, built in November 2024; the SMILE
manual identifies itself as 2.2.4.R1, built in April 2024. Other anchors
are SAS/ETS 14.1, the Mplus 8.1 language addendum, IBM's SPSS Statistics
31.0.0 documentation route, and the retrieved Stata command manuals.
Mutable pages were acquired on 13 October 2026, after the 7 October
literature cutoff. Edition labels identify what was inspected; they do
not independently authenticate the exact bytes served on the cutoff
date. No vendor engine or paid deployment was executed for this
comparison.
\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_smile_intro,exec13_ct_genie_learning,
exec13_ct_sas_causal,exec13_ct_mplus81,exec13_ct_ibm_tcm,exec13_ct_stata_granger}

The proposed request should state five things before selecting a
package. The sampling interval identifies the meaning of a lag. The
information clock specifies which balances, quotes and macro releases
were available at each decision. The contemporaneous model specifies
whether a within-month response can precede another measured variable.
The intervention states whether the bank replaces an action once,
maintains a policy, or responds to a structural shock. Finally, the
sampling law distinguishes a long calendar, independent experimental
units and branches sharing common shocks. These fields determine which
commercial result can be interpreted as an answer, and which can serve
only as a diagnostic or candidate model.

\hypertarget{dynamic-bayesian-networks-a-strong-supplied-model-baseline}{%
\subsection{Dynamic Bayesian networks: a strong supplied-model
baseline}\label{dynamic-bayesian-networks-a-strong-supplied-model-baseline}}

The GeNIe dynamic-model overview defines a dynamic Bayesian network as a
discrete-time model of a stochastic process. It explicitly says that the
temporal extension does not itself mean that the structure or parameters
change over time: its described process is stationary. It permits
temporal observations and discusses both discrete and continuous
examples. Those statements concern the representation and evaluation of
a supplied model; they are not a guarantee that the model's scientific
mechanisms are stable under a bank
intervention.~\citep{exec13_ct_genie_dbn}

The parameter-learning page resolves an otherwise consequential
ambiguity. It states that GeNIe's structure-learning algorithms do not
learn the structure of dynamic models, while the parameters of an
existing DBN can be learned from time series. The network structure must
already be defined. Data columns use a node identifier followed by an
underscore and time-step number, except at time zero. The interface
matches model nodes and states to those columns and then applies EM; the
structure remains unchanged and existing network evidence is ignored
during parameter learning. \citep{exec13_ct_genie_learning}

This is a valuable comparator for a servicing or funding prototype. A
bank can supply a hypothesized state-transition graph, use the tool to
fit its parameters and inspect temporal predictions. It cannot infer
from a successful fit that the same software discovered that graph,
identified a rate intervention, or selected between hidden-information
alternatives. Nor does the discrete-DBN parameter-learning page
establish parameter learning for every continuous model mentioned
elsewhere in the manual.

SMILE's C++ tutorial makes the integration contract unusually concrete.
It uses \texttt{SetTemporalType} for plate nodes,
\texttt{AddTemporalArc} with an explicit lag order, separate initial and
temporal conditional probability tables, and \texttt{SetNumberOfSlices}
for unrolling. \texttt{SetTemporalEvidence} receives a time index and an
integer outcome index. Temporal beliefs are returned in a matrix whose
adjacent entries correspond to outcomes within a slice. An adapter must
preserve those outcome indices, the distinction between initial and
transition distributions, lag order and the number of slices. A swapped
state ordering would change the meaning of the result while leaving a
superficially valid array.~\citep{exec13_ct_smile_tutorial}

The maintained C++ library and its documented Java, Python and R
wrappers are plausible integration routes. They are not evidence that
every wrapper exposes the same temporal operations or numerical
behavior. The inspected license distinguishes development from
deployment, including server and distributed-program deployment.
Academic use has stated eligibility and purpose restrictions. The
appropriate proposal decision is to consider a licensed local adapter
for supplied-model inference, with wrapper parity and deployment terms
checked for the chosen configuration. Porting its proprietary engine is
not an available open-source reuse strategy.
\citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license}

\hypertarget{automated-temporal-prediction-and-the-meaning-of-causal}{%
\subsection{Automated temporal prediction and the meaning of
``causal''}\label{automated-temporal-prediction-and-the-meaning-of-causal}}

IBM's Temporal Causal Models page says that the procedure constructs an
autoregressive model for each target and selects inputs from candidate
series. It then explicitly defines ``causal'' in this context as Granger
causality: past values of another series improve prediction beyond the
target's own past. It supports column-based series and a
multidimensional representation in which categorical dimensions
distinguish series. The GUI can produce \texttt{TCM\ MODEL} syntax.
These are substantive workflow capabilities that the proposed local
causal library does not presently reproduce. \citep{exec13_ct_ibm_tcm}

The correct comparison is with automated predictive model selection.
Representing many branch series does not by itself establish the
sampling law for a policy-effect interval, and selecting a controllable
predictor does not establish that changing it will change the target.
The inspected overview links time-interval, aggregation, missing-value
and model-build options, but the retrieved detailed routes did not
return their technical content. Defaults for lag selection,
transformation and aggregation therefore remain unverified here. The
overview is sufficient to establish the procedure's stated Granger
meaning; it is insufficient for a full configuration or performance
comparison.

Stata's \texttt{vargranger} is similarly explicit. It performs
equation-specific Wald tests that the coefficients on another endogenous
variable's lags are jointly zero. When it follows a structural VAR
command, these tests still concern the underlying reduced-form VAR. A
structural command name does not change the null hypothesis of the
postestimation test. \citep{exec13_ct_stata_granger}

The SAS/ETS 14.1 \texttt{CAUSAL} statement also documents Granger tests
based on a VAR of the variables listed in \texttt{GROUP1} and
\texttt{GROUP2}, with the order supplied by \texttt{MODEL}. It is not
applicable with order zero. Its example treats the group declarations as
material to the fitted test. An integration must therefore record the
actual series in each test and its lag specification, including whether
a contextual variable was excluded. A request for ``causality'' cannot
be translated merely by matching it to a command named
\texttt{CAUSAL}.~\citep{exec13_ct_sas_causal}

Consider an exact project counterexample. Let an unobserved liquidity
factor follow

\[
 U_t=\tfrac45U_{t-1}+e_t,\qquad
 \operatorname{var}(U_t)=1,\quad \operatorname{var}(e_t)=\tfrac9{25},
\]

with stationary Gaussian initialization. Set the observed rate deviation
and next balance deviation to

\[
 A_t=U_t+\nu_t,\qquad Y_{t+1}=U_t+\eta_{t+1},
\]

where all measurement innovations are independent, centered, and have
variance one; they are also independent of the factor innovations. Rates
are measured in percentage points and balances in USD millions. This is
a stipulated law, not an estimated banking relationship. Conditional on
the single balance lag \(Y_t\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{cov}(Y_{t+1},A_t\mid Y_t)&=1-(4/5)^2/2=17/25,\\
 \operatorname{var}(A_t\mid Y_t)&=2-(4/5)^2/2=42/25.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The population predictive coefficient is \(17/42\). Adding the observed
rate reduces prediction mean squared error from \(42/25\) to \(59/42\).
Thus it is a useful predictor relative to this declared one-lag
baseline. Replacing only the equation for \(A_t\) changes neither
\(U_t\) nor the equation for \(Y_{t+1}\); the rate intervention effect
is zero. Treating the predictive coefficient as an effect would
attribute \(\tfrac14(17/42)=17/168\) million dollars of balances to a
25-basis-point action in this example. No such causal increase occurs.
\citep{exec13_ct_examples}

This calculation neither reports a vendor false positive nor proves a
failure of its documented prediction task. It shows why a bank action
requires an additional identifying argument. It also keeps the
conditioning history explicit: the displayed coefficient is for the
stated one-lag predictor comparison, not an unproved statement about
every possible infinite-history test.

\hypertarget{structural-restrictions-and-external-instruments-deserve-separate-credit}{%
\subsection{Structural restrictions and external instruments deserve
separate
credit}\label{structural-restrictions-and-external-instruments-deserve-separate-credit}}

Stata's \texttt{svar} is an intervention-relevant comparator beyond a
Granger test. Its manual allows user-specified short-run or long-run
restrictions on structural impulse responses and explains their economic
motivation. The interface records lag choices, exogenous variables and
constraint matrices. It also documents a local-identification check and
warns that a model that is not structurally identified can nevertheless
converge. Disabling that check must not be interpreted by an adapter as
scientific approval. \citep{exec13_ct_stata_svar}

\texttt{ivsvar} supplies another route. The inspected manual describes a
GMM estimator for a single target shock and a minimum-distance estimator
for multiple target shocks. An instrument is correlated with the target
shock and uncorrelated with nontarget shocks. The additional information
identifies the relevant impact column up to its normalization; it does
not identify all other shocks. Multiple-target designs require further
restrictions. The GMM interface exposes robust, cluster and HAC variance
options and an impact scale. These are documented capabilities, not a
claim that a noisy bank announcement or an arbitrary policy date
satisfies the instrument conditions.~\citep{exec13_ct_stata_ivsvar}

The distinction can be developed without relying on a software run.
Suppose a two-variable monthly reduced-form process for rate and balance
deviations has

\[
 X_t=FX_{t-1}+u_t,\qquad
 F=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1/5&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \Sigma_u=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its eigenvalues are both \(1/2\), so a stationary Gaussian law exists.
Two structural representations have coefficient matrices

\[
 D_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

covariances are respectively \(\operatorname{diag}(1,3/4)\) and
\(\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1)\). Both yield exactly the same \(F\),
\(\Sigma_u\) and stationary observed process. They disagree about the
contemporaneous ordering: the first permits a within-month rate effect
on balances; the second orders the balance before the rate. The
structural lag matrices also differ, as required to preserve the same
reduced form.

first structure and \((\delta,0)'\) under the second. Subsequent
expected differences propagate by \(F^h\). The resulting balance

\hypertarget{intensive-longitudinal-models-and-intervention-analysis}{%
\subsection{Intensive longitudinal models and intervention
analysis}\label{intensive-longitudinal-models-and-intervention-analysis}}

Mplus's version 8.1 addendum distinguishes DSEM, which regresses an
outcome on its past, from RDSEM, which places autoregression on the
residual after contemporaneous predictors. RDSEM is documented for a
single series and two-level models. Its syntax examples use
\texttt{CLUSTER}, \texttt{LAGGED}, \texttt{TYPE=TWOLEVEL\ RANDOM} and
\texttt{ESTIMATOR=BAYES}, with random slopes and within/between
components. The addendum also includes linear-trend examples and lagged
categorical outcomes. These are valuable alternatives for heterogeneous
customer histories and measurement processes. \citep{exec13_ct_mplus81}

The time grid matters to the observation law. The addendum warns that a
covariate missing at one or more time points can exclude an observation,
including missingness arising with \texttt{TINTERVAL}. It illustrates
bringing that covariate into the model through an autoregression so
missing-data theory can be used. This changes the modeled system; it is
not a universal permission to fill irregular bank records. The analyst
still needs a meaningful time unit, a missingness argument, and
assumptions about the mechanisms and random effects.

For the proposal, Mplus is a model-fitting and heterogeneity comparator,
with richer longitudinal syntax than the current narrow local prototype.
The inspected addendum does not establish automatic discovery of a
causal equivalence class, identification of an observational policy
intervention, or calibrated uncertainty over unknown causal structure. A
posterior for a supplied dynamic model and a confidence set covering
alternative intervention structures answer different questions. Regime
changes and nonstationary mechanisms must be modeled and checked
explicitly; a trend example is not evidence that arbitrary regime drift
is supported.

BayesiaLab should also receive explicit causal credit. Its feature page
distinguishes hard and uncertain evidence from intervention analysis and
names Graph Surgery and Likelihood Matching as causal-estimation
workflows. Its technical e-book section distinguishes identification
from estimation: identification requires a causal model or partial
knowledge of data generation, with the chapter expressing the
assumptions in a DAG. The text states that more observations cannot
compensate for absent causal
assumptions.~\citep{exec13_ct_bayesia_inference,exec13_ct_bayesia_identification}

These sources support an intervention-analysis comparator for an
asserted model. They do not, in the pages inspected here, establish a
temporal discovery algorithm that learns lags, latent confounding,
contemporaneous equivalence classes and regime changes together. A
candidate integration would first check the exact edition/API operation,
graph semantics, supported identification route, and representation of
an unavailable effect. Commercial capability must not be inferred from a
feature label, but the proposal must equally avoid claiming graph-based
intervention analysis as its exclusive innovation.

\hypertarget{frequency-common-calendars-and-uncertainty-belong-in-the-interface}{%
\subsection{Frequency, common calendars and uncertainty belong in the
interface}\label{frequency-common-calendars-and-uncertainty-belong-in-the-interface}}

The transition coefficient changes with the sampling interval. For the
constructed monthly process, observing quarter-end stocks gives

\[
 X_{3k}=F^3X_{3k-3}+u_{3k}+Fu_{3k-1}+F^2u_{3k-2},\qquad
 F^3=\begin{pmatrix}1/8&0\\3/20&1/8\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The monthly cross-lag coefficient is \(1/5\), while the quarter-end
coefficient is \(3/20\), not three times the monthly coefficient. The
quarterly innovation covariance is
\(\Sigma_u+F\Sigma_uF'+F^2\Sigma_u(F^2)'\). Quarterly average balances
or cash-flow totals would require a different observation operator. The
exact example checks this covariance both by recursion and by a block
covariance calculation.~\citep{exec13_ct_examples}

Consequently, an adapter must preserve whether a field is a stock, a
flow, an average or an end-of-period observation. It must also preserve
timezone, release lag and policy availability. Merely converting a
monthly timestamp to a quarterly label can change both the statistical
model and the banking target. A proposed regime intervention must state
which equations are invariant and which may change, instead of assuming
that a historical forecast carries over.

Common calendars create a separate uncertainty issue. Suppose a single
date's errors across twenty branches are \(C+\epsilon_i\), with common
variance \(1/2\), independent branch variance \(1/2\), and independence
between the two components. The variance of the branch mean is
\(1/2+1/40=21/40\). Incorrectly treating all twenty errors as
independent gives \(1/20\), understating variance by a factor of
\(21/2\). This is a specified example, not an estimated correction for
bank data. \citep{exec13_ct_examples}

A package can expose cluster or HAC options, as the inspected Stata
instrumental-SVAR interface does, while the analyst still has to justify
the chosen asymptotic units and dependence assumptions. A collection of
branch models is not automatically a collection of independent
calendars. Neither a Granger-test level nor a posterior interval in one
supplied graph becomes a simultaneous uncertainty guarantee over
searched lags, selected models, alternative graphs and intervention
effects.

\subsection{Commercial dynamic panels: instruments, horizons and the effective calendar}
\label{exec19:dynamic-scope}

A funding team may observe hundreds of branches but only a few policy dates.
Its balance equation can contain lagged balances, branch-specific conditions,
quoted rates, common liquidity shocks and delayed measurements. The software
comparison must separate four questions: forecasting balances, estimating a
dynamic panel coefficient under instrument restrictions, tracing a supplied
structural shock, and evaluating a policy that changes the pricing rule.
These questions share data structures but do not share an automatic causal
interpretation. Commercial products already provide substantial estimation,
diagnostic and reporting capabilities. The proposed common interface needs
to preserve their scientific premises before it can compare or compose them.

The additional inspected originals are Stata's \texttt{xtabond},
\texttt{xtdpdsys} and \texttt{lpirf} manuals and EViews' panel-estimation
chapter. The Stata PDFs carry July 2026 creation metadata; the EViews page
displays a March 2025 update. All were acquired on 19 October 2026. These
dates identify the inspected material but do not authenticate the exact bytes
available at the 7 October cutoff or every feature in a licensed edition.
The comparison is therefore documentary and version-limited. It does not
report a paid-engine trial or a complete commercial-market census.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_stata_ab,exec19_dynamic_stata_system,
exec19_dynamic_stata_lp,exec19_dynamic_eviews_panel}

\paragraph{Difference and system estimation answer conditional moment questions.}
Write a scalar dynamic panel model as
\begin{equation}
 B_{it}=\rho B_{i,t-1}+\beta A_{it}+\nu_i+\epsilon_{it},
 \label{exec19:dynamic-model}
\end{equation}
where $B_{it}$ is a balance measure, $A_{it}$ a rate or action variable,
$\nu_i$ a branch component and $\epsilon_{it}$ an idiosyncratic disturbance.
Differencing removes $\nu_i$, but does not make the differenced lagged
outcome exogenous. Lagged levels can supply moment conditions only under
appropriate timing and error restrictions. A rate chosen in response to
current liquidity news must not be classified as strictly exogenous merely
because its database column precedes the outcome column. Predetermined,
endogenous and strictly exogenous declarations change the admissible
instrument lags and the resulting estimator.

The inspected \texttt{xtabond} manual explicitly targets many panels and
few periods and requires absence of autocorrelation in the idiosyncratic
errors. It distinguishes one-step estimation, two-step estimation and their
variance estimators. Its robust two-step option uses a Windmeijer correction;
that correction addresses the declared variance calculation, not the truth
of the instrument moments. The manual also warns that instrument counts grow
quadratically with the number of dates and that too many overidentifying
restrictions can perform poorly in small samples. It supplies lag limits
and serial-correlation diagnostics. A diagnostic nonrejection is evidence
about a test under its assumptions, not proof that a bank's assignment
mechanism satisfies every required restriction.~\citep{exec19_dynamic_stata_ab}

System estimation adds equations in levels with lagged differences as
instruments. The \texttt{xtdpdsys} manual motivates these moments when
lagged-level instruments become weak under persistence, but states an
additional initial-condition orthogonality restriction. Extra equations do
not supply extra valid information unless that restriction is defensible.
The source retains the many-panel/few-period setting and idiosyncratic-error
conditions. A common service should consequently record which moments were
used and why they are valid, rather than translate a request for a more
persistent model directly into a stronger estimator label.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_stata_system}

An exact authored example isolates the initial-condition issue. Set the
action term to zero in \eqref{exec19:dynamic-model}, take $\rho=1/2$, and
let $\nu_i,\eta_i,\epsilon_{i2},\epsilon_{i3}$ be independent fair
signs. Under initialization $B_{i1}=\nu_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta B_{i2}=\tfrac12\nu_i+\epsilon_{i2},\qquad
 E[\Delta B_{i2}(\nu_i+\epsilon_{i3})]=\tfrac12.
 \label{exec19:dynamic-invalid}
\end{equation}
The proposed level-equation moment at date three is nonzero. By contrast,
if $B_{i1}=\nu_i/(1-\rho)+\eta_i=2\nu_i+\eta_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta B_{i2}=-\tfrac12\eta_i+\epsilon_{i2},\qquad
 E[\Delta B_{i2}(\nu_i+\epsilon_{i3})]=0.
 \label{exec19:dynamic-valid}
\end{equation}
Both identities follow by expanding the products and using the independence
and zero means of the signs. The reference enumerates all sixteen states
for each initialization. This is an assumption check, not a reproduction of
a commercial estimator's error or a proof that the second initialization
qualifies every system-GMM moment. Increasing the number of branches cannot
turn the nonzero population moment in \eqref{exec19:dynamic-invalid} into
zero. A covariance correction cannot do so either.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_reference}

\paragraph{Instrument construction is part of the output contract.}
EViews' inspected chapter exposes first differences and orthogonal deviations
for removing individual effects. Its \texttt{@DYN} syntax expands
period-specific lag instruments; \texttt{@LEV} preserves an instrument in
levels within a transformed equation. These choices are substantively
different from leaving a regressor unchanged or treating a lag as universally
valid. For one endogenous series with unrestricted period-specific levels
from lag two backward, a balanced history of $T$ dates produces
\begin{equation}
 K(T)=\sum_{t=3}^{T}(t-2)=\frac{(T-1)(T-2)}{2}.
 \label{exec19:dynamic-instruments}
\end{equation}
At $T=20$ this is $171$, matching the illustrative count in the inspected
EViews chapter. The formula describes this particular uncollapsed instrument
construction, not every GMM configuration or rank after missingness. The
adapter should return the actual columns, lags, count and rank, along with
the equation transformation and one-step or two-step weighting choices.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_eviews_panel,exec19_dynamic_reference}

The same EViews page distinguishes covariance methods labeled for period
clustering, individual clustering and two-way clustering. It also states
that some GMM weights cease to be available after differencing or orthogonal
deviation. One displayed weight label appears alongside a description of
two-step or multistep use that requires edition-specific clarification; the
proposal should not resolve that ambiguity by silently choosing its own
interpretation. Nor should it infer that every covariance option described
for a panel equation is available for every transformed GMM model. The
selected executable specification must bind the actual permitted combination.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_eviews_panel}

\paragraph{A branch count is not a count of independent policy dates.}
Consider an authored descriptive law for a branch-level funding response,
\begin{equation}
 Z_{it}=\theta+U_t+E_{it},
 \label{exec19:dynamic-calendar}
\end{equation}
with independent mean-zero, unit-variance common-date shocks $U_t$ and
idiosyncratic shocks $E_{it}$. Distinct dates are independent in this example.
There are $N$ branches and $T$ dates, with all rows observed. The overall mean
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{var}(\bar Z)=\frac1T+\frac1{NT},
 \qquad
 V_{\mathrm{row}}=\frac2{NT},
 \label{exec19:dynamic-variance}
\end{equation}
where $V_{\mathrm{row}}$ is the value obtained by incorrectly declaring
all $NT$ rows independent. The first identity follows by writing the mean as
$\theta+T^{-1}\sum_t U_t+(NT)^{-1}\sum_{i,t}E_{it}$. The common
shock averages over dates and does not disappear as more branches are added.
At $N=3,T=2$, enumeration of all $2^{2+6}=256$ fair-sign histories gives
variance $2/3$, while the false row-independent calculation gives $1/3$.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_reference}

For $N=100,T=25$, the variances are $101/2500$ and $1/1250$, a ratio of
$101/2=50.5$. If one response unit is stipulated to mean USD~10,000 per
branch-period, the corresponding standard deviations of the mean are
approximately USD~2,010 and USD~283. These are exact population-variance
consequences of the assumed law, with square roots rounded for presentation.
They are not confidence intervals, estimated bank losses or a report that
any named package uses the incorrect calculation. A software option for
clustering by date is useful, but twenty-five dates do not automatically
justify its finite-sample calibration. Persistent shocks, unequal loadings,
missing dates and selection require further analysis beyond this example.
\citep{exec19_dynamic_reference}

For a bank-wide rate policy that changes only by calendar date, adding
saturated date effects can also remove the very rate variation used to
identify its coefficient. Time controls and common-shock handling must be
designed with the target, not appended as a generic repair. Branch-specific
exposure variation, a justified external instrument, a different policy
design or a longer independent calendar may supply useful information, but
each changes the assumptions or the estimand. The proposal cannot promise
that a larger table alone closes the information gap.

\paragraph{Local projections and structural shocks remain distinct.}
Stata's \texttt{lpirf} manual describes joint multistep regressions, dynamic
multipliers for declared exogenous variables, horizon and lag controls, and
OLS, robust and HAC covariance choices. Its \texttt{dfk} option changes
the residual covariance divisor and \texttt{small} changes the reference
statistics. Long horizons consume trailing observations and can greatly
reduce the usable sample. These are material comparison dimensions. They
do not establish that a pricing innovation is independent of liquidity news
or that a pointwise interval is a simultaneous guarantee over every reported
horizon.~\citep{exec19_dynamic_stata_lp}

The previously inspected Stata structural-VAR manual requires supplied
short-run or long-run restrictions and explicitly warns that convergence
can occur without structural identification. Its external-instrument VAR
manual distinguishes a single target shock from multiple shocks and states
relevance, exclusion and normalization requirements. Those originals remain
the appropriate evidence for their bounded claims. A local projection, a
restricted VAR and an instrumented shock response should be compared at the
same shock units, information clock and horizons before their numerical
responses are compared.~\citep{exec13_ct_stata_svar,exec13_ct_stata_ivsvar}

Other commercial families provide useful alternatives with different current
evidence. The inspected SAS VARMAX text describes Granger tests and a
lower-triangular dynamic representation; it does not establish the full
dynamic-panel procedure or every equation omitted by HTML text extraction.
IBM's inspected TCM overview explicitly defines its use of causal in
predictive Granger terms. Mplus' selected DSEM/RDSEM addendum describes
supplied longitudinal models, random effects and missing-covariate limits.
None should be relabeled as an equivalent dynamic-panel policy estimator
without a more specific contract.~\citep{exec13_ct_sas_causal,
exec13_ct_sas_structural,exec13_ct_ibm_tcm,exec13_ct_mplus81}

\paragraph{What a public comparison can establish next.}
The current product proposal should offer typed requests for a chosen
scientific target, instrument design, sampling calendar and uncertainty
statement. Commercial engines remain possible licensed comparators; this
review supplies no entitlement, current price or right to embed them.
MathWorks' selected official IRF route was inaccessible, earlier OxMetrics
manual routes remain unresolved, and a hosted open-source frontend still
needs a concrete vendor, edition and numerical backend. These are specific
evidence omissions, not findings that the products lack relevant features.

A public or generated benchmark can first freeze a valid short-panel law and
an intentionally invalid initial-condition law, a common-date law, and
weak-instrument or rank-deficient cases. Each engine must receive the same
rows, lags, transformation, normalization and retained horizons. Record
instrument counts, effective dates, convergence, diagnostic limitations and
all failed attempts. A refusal should retain its reason rather than become
a zero effect. Compare point estimates only when their targets agree and
compare uncertainty only under a stated joint or pointwise criterion. This
work can justify whether a later licensed prototype and bank experiment is
worth funding while leaving universal dependence, hidden-confounding and
institutional acceptance claims open.

\section{A purchasing recommendation must retain the scientific comparison}

The detailed studies give the acquisition argument a concrete basis. Existing
systems provide graph exploration, supplied-model fitting, latent-class
analysis, trial-specific effects and temporal estimators that the proposed
product should compare seriously. Their outputs answer different questions.
The observationally equivalent lending models, imperfect proxy, compliance
example, predictive lag and alternative structural orderings show why fit or
interface convenience cannot supply the missing identification information.
The panel examples add instrument validity, initialization and the effective
calendar to that same requirement.

The cost and capacity derivations apply only after those scientific objects
match. Model residency, online information, deadlines and shared validation
dependencies can change which licensed configuration is useful. Hypothetical
cost crossings and compatibility signals identify measurements that could
change a purchase; they do not establish actual savings. The defensible
recommendation is conditional reuse with a preserving interface and a matched
comparison of the complete workflow. The next chapter develops the
identification and counterfactual distinctions that such an interface must
retain.
