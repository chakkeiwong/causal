Relationship to Bias-Aware CIs. My robust CIs have a similar structure to the bias-aware
CIs advanced in Armstrong and Kolesár (2018, 2020, 2021a,b), but there are important dif-
ferences in model setup and inferential objective. The bias-aware CIs are designed to provide
valid inference for a fixed target estimand λ′ θ subject to bounds on the parameter space for
θ, such as bounds on heterogeneity—see Kwon and Sun (2025) for results of this flavor. By
contrast, my robust CIs are designed to provide valid inference for a class of target estimands
{λ′ θ : λ ∈ Λ} and impose no bound on heterogeneity, opting instead to infer the heterogeneity
using η̂1−β . A downside of bounding heterogeneity is the potential for undercoverage when the
bound is incorrect, while a downside of inferring heterogeneity is the additional error level β
in the uniform coverage statement (20).

5     Uniform Asymptotic Validity
The foregoing results are developed under normally distributed estimates θ̂ ∼ N (θ,Σ), a known
covariance matrix Σ, known baseline weights w, and a known class of alternative weights Λ.
In this section, I establish the uniform asymptotic validity of my procedures under asymptot-
ically normal estimates, consistent covariance matrix estimators, consistent estimators for the
baseline weights, and consistent estimators for the class of alternative weights. Sections 5.1
and 5.2 formalize the asymptotic setup and assumptions. Sections 5.3, 5.4, and 5.5 establish
asymptotic results for the class of alternative weights, maximum distance, and robust estimator.
Sections 5.6 and 5.7 establish asymptotic results for the heterogeneity UCB and robust CI. For
self-contained practical implementations of my inference procedures, see Section 6.
