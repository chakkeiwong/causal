Chapter 99

Learning with an explicit error
budget before a dependent
evaluation

Consider an encouragement to take a bank offer. The bank observes information X before
the encouragement Z, the eventual action A, and its subsequent value Y . Flexible prediction
is useful because accounts differ in their pre-existing circumstances. It also creates a second
estimation problem: removing predictable variation imperfectly changes the instrument moment.
When the residual response to the encouragement is small, that change can be large relative
to the information identifying the effect. A narrow interval computed after fitting a good
predictor need not describe the uncertainty in the bank’s causal decision.
   This chapter develops a restricted alternative. Learn on an independent pilot, obtain
an explicit bound on each prediction error, and carry that bound into a joint region for
two observable moments. Evaluate those moments on a bounded sequence with a known
dependence range. Project the region into the effect, retaining disconnected and unbounded
answers. The construction is a project derivation with exact numerical references, rather than
an implemented general learner or a new repeated-sampling qualification (Causal banking
research project, 2026c). Its restrictions are substantial; explaining them is part of deciding
whether the proposed product merits a larger experiment. After the derivation, a design
analysis asks whether the bound can support a decision at plausible information levels. The
final comparison considers geometric and hierarchical causal models as substantive alternatives:
they change the dependence restrictions or intervention target and require their own estimation
and uncertainty arguments.


99.1     What the repaired predecessor did and did not establish
The preceding sparse-learning experiment already has a repaired version. Its first version
selected penalties using evaluation outcomes, standardized with all observations, included
covariance products across an omitted calendar gap, and ignored some scenario dimensions.
The repaired version uses training-only nested selection, training-specific scaling, within-fold
Bartlett covariance, and the actual registered dimension and support. The original results
remain exploratory. They do not establish the performance of that repaired procedure.
   For the repaired learner, the high-dimension and larger-support scenarios covered respectively
499/512 and 493/512 requests. Their simultaneous lower coverage bounds, 0.949140 and
0.933851, fell below the frozen 0.95 threshold. The nonlinear omitted-nuisance scenario covered

99.2      The structural target and its observable moment
The imposed partially linear model is

                           Y = θ0 A + g0 (X) + ϵ,       E[ϵ | X, Z] = 0.                     (99.1)

The conditional moment excludes a remaining direct effect of the encouragement and unmea-
sured causes associated with its residual after conditioning on X. It is a scientific assumption.
A prediction fit or source hash cannot verify it. Here θ0 is a constant structural coefficient,
expressed in outcome units per unit of action. Interpreting it as an unrestricted heterogeneous
average effect would require a different argument.
  Let m(X) = E[Z | X], r(X) = E[A | X] and ℓ(X) = E[Y | X]. Define residuals
vZ = Z − m(X), vA = A − r(X) and vY = Y − ℓ(X), and moments

                                M = E[vZ vY ],       Q = E[vZ vA ].                          (99.2)

Equation (99.1) implies M = θ0 Q. A nonzero Q identifies the scalar coefficient from this
relation. If Q = 0, the relation can be uninformative even when the observations themselves
are measured exactly.
  The residualized score (Z − m(X)){Y − ℓ(X) − θ(A − r(X))} is the inherited partially linear
IV score in Chernozhukov et al. (2018, Section 4.2, equation (4.8)). That source states its
moment, nuisance-product and nonzero-slope conditions. Its independent-sample theorem
does not supply a result for arbitrary serially dependent bank rows. The following finite
construction instead proves its own statement for a declared observation law.


99.3      An observation law that makes separation meaningful
The pilot contains mP independent draws from the marginal law of W = (X, Z, A, Y ) and is
independent of the entire evaluation sequence. The evaluation observations W1 , . . . , Wn are
stationary and d-dependent: collections separated by more than d dates generate independent
sigma fields. The integer d is supplied and justified by the design. Uncorrelated residuals or a
small sample autocorrelation are insufficient to establish this property.
   Assume known almost-sure ranges |Z| ≤ BZ , |A| ≤ BA , and |Y | ≤ BY . Clip predicted
conditional means into the respective ranges. Since the true conditional means lie there,
clipping predictions cannot increase their squared error. Clipping the observed outcome is
different: it can change the target and cannot be introduced silently to make a bound available.
   The features ϕ(X) ∈ Rp are fixed before the pilot, with |ϕk (X)| ≤ K. A feature learned
from data needs a separate design sample and conditional versions of these assumptions. A
constant feature can represent an intercept, but the derivation below penalizes its coefficient

99.10      The proposed next experiment and its limits
A useful bounded prototype would compare a small fixed dictionary, the certified sparse pilot,
and the independent calibration route on the same declared target. Its full resource record must
include feature preparation, training, tuning, certificate construction, calibration, dependent
evaluation, set projection and retained refusals. A faster optimizer is of little value if the
required pilot or confidence width makes the decision uneconomical.
   Before final draws, choose a bounded mechanism with an explicit dependence range and a
separate pilot, freeze the dictionary and approximation bounds, review the cone and numerical
certificates, and profile complete costs. Then freeze sample sizes, error allocations, adverse
cases, alternatives, fresh draws, stopping rule and useful-width criterion. The old failed
outcomes cannot become the new evaluation data. No final successor study is registered by
the argument in this chapter.
   The route is now explicit for this restricted family. General unbounded dependent learning,
unknown approximation error, unverified instruments, arbitrary learners and useful weak-IV
precision remain open. Where the certificate is too costly or too wide, narrowing the question
or changing the information design is a substantive product recommendation.

99.12      Structured causal models: restrictions, computation and
           the target
A universal bank observes accounts within branches, repeated decisions within customers and
exposures connected through markets or payment networks. The observations are neither
an arbitrary collection of independent rows nor automatically interchangeable. Two recent
primary contributions clarify different parts of this problem. Geometric causal models use
specified symmetries to organize dependence and identification. A hierarchical causal pipeline
translates distributional interventions into symbolic expressions and fitted numerical factors.

99.12.1     A symmetry is a scientific restriction

In Weinstein and Blei’s geometric causal model, an endogenous variable xv is a structured
collection (xvω )ω∈Ω , where Ω indexes locations, times, nodes or other positions. A group G acts
on these collections. A causal mechanism f v is equivariant when applying a transformation
before evaluating the mechanism is equivalent to transforming its output:
                                                                   
                      f v (ϕ(xpa(v) ), ϕ(ϵv )) = ϕ f v (xpa(v) , ϵv ) ,       ϕ ∈ G.                (99.24)

Here pa(v) denotes the parents of variable v in a specified directed acyclic graph and ϵv
is exogenous noise. The model uses equivariant mechanisms to create dependence among
endogenous positions from the stipulated noise structure. A hard intervention replaces the
selected mechanism with a supplied value. Permutation, spatial-shift and array symmetries
express different models; they are not different software optimizations of one unrestricted
model. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)
   For a banking application, translation of a branch-level model across locations would
assert that the relevant mechanism survives that transformation after the declared context is
represented. A policy change, regulatory boundary or market break may make that assertion
implausible. The proposal should therefore ask which transformations preserve the decision
mechanism, what variables encode exceptions, and what evidence could falsify the proposed
symmetry. These are proposed elicitation questions. The paper assumes the causal graph
and symmetry group are known and leaves their discovery to future work. An LLM may help
formulate candidate restrictions and collect supporting documents; this paper supplies no
experiment establishing that an LLM can validate them. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)


99.12.2     Identification and consistency retain their assumptions

The paper separates learning an observational law from one structured realization, identifying
an intervention from that law, and estimating it from finitely observed coordinates. Its mixing-
action condition requires countably infinite index and transformation sets, with transformations
that eventually move any finite set away from itself. Under the stated model, ergodicity then
makes learning from an expanding observation domain possible. The do-calculus soundness
argument uses the graph’s Markov decomposition. For groups consisting of index transforma-
tions, the completeness argument embeds conventional unrestricted causal models into the
geometric class. Stronger symmetries can add restrictions: the orthogonal-group instrumental
variable example invokes linear-Gaussian structure, a nonconstant relevant instrument and
finite interference. These conclusions have different premises and should not be compressed
into a claim that symmetry identifies any latent graph. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)
   The finite-data consistency result is likewise conditional. It assumes finite interference, local
information under the transformations, an amenable group with a tempered Følner sequence,
finite state spaces and positivity of the required finite observational configurations. Informally,
averages over the permitted transformations estimate finite marginal laws; the identified

99.12.3     Hierarchical interventions need an explicit weighting target

Aiad and coauthors develop a complementary pipeline for hierarchical structural causal models.
Its unit-level variables represent distributions or conditional mechanisms of subunits. For
example, Qai describes the treatment distribution within unit i. The queried contrast can be
written as a difference between expected outcomes under two interventions on that distribution,
do(Qa = q1 ) and do(Qa = q0 ). The pipeline collapses the hierarchical graph, augments it
where required and marginalizes variables before sending the transformed graph to pyAgrum

99.12.4     An auditable expression tree must preserve the evaluated functional

The hierarchical pipeline’s abstract syntax tree records products, marginalizations and fitted
density keys. This can expose which conditional models are required and which computations
can be evaluated separately. That is useful for a library/CLI/MCP interface: the declared
estimand, identified symbolic expression, selected numerical factors and resulting value can
remain separately inspectable. The paper reports parametric, mixture, categorical and
nonparametric fitting options and parallel evaluation. Those descriptions are primary paper
claims. The linked repository, dependency versions, factor fallbacks and implementation have
not been newly inspected or executed in this review. (Aiad et al., 2026)
   Crucially, the paper explicitly acknowledges that normalizing unstable multiplicative factors
changes the numerical functional. A constant representation change that cancels exactly is
different from a nonconstant alteration of relative weights. To see the latter in a transparent
construction, take weights p = (1/4, 3/4) and effects τ = (20, −4). Their weighted contrast is
USD 2. Multiplying the two weights by (2, 1) and renormalizing gives (2/5, 3/5), with contrast
USD 28/5 = 5.60. The nominal dependency graph did not change, but the functional did.
This constructed modification is not a reconstruction of the paper’s normalization code. It
shows why the exact transformation must be declared and either proved invariant or reported
as a different functional with its own uncertainty argument. (Aiad et al., 2026; Causal banking
proposal project, 2026cl)
   The STAR application makes the distinction concrete without providing a matched nor-
malization experiment. Its reported mathematics row has 2402.75 − 2365.95 = 36.80 points.
The normalized reading diagnostic has 1788.10 − 1766.47 = 21.63 points. These rows concern
different outcomes; their difference cannot isolate the effect of factor normalization. The
selected ExactBIC graph is described as plausible, with a substantive orientation and added
latent class context, rather than uniquely recovered. The paper also distinguishes student-level
OLS, an IV class-size coefficient and the distributional class intervention. A larger number
across these targets is not, by itself, evidence of a more accurate causal method. (Aiad et al.,
2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026cl)
   The stated analysis uses 322 classes with ten sampled students per class, and identifies multi-
parent factor estimation as a bottleneck. Its conclusion leaves deeper hierarchies, uncertainty
for stabilized functionals and analysis across plausible graphs open. The reported 3.11-second
sequential and 1.9-second four-process evaluations imply a ratio of about 1.64, but this is a
check of the paper’s arithmetic, not a local timing result or a bank service-level claim. These
limits should remain part of the proposed product comparison. (Aiad et al., 2026; Causal
banking proposal project, 2026cl)

The two contributions suggest an orderly prototype route. First specify the banking unit,
target, information clock and intervention law. Then justify a particular dependence restriction
or hierarchical representation, retain the assumed graph or plausible graph family, and derive
the identified functional. Preserve every numerical factor and any alteration introduced for
