   Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models for
Biomedical Causal Discovery and Clinical Decision Support
                                          Author: James

Abstract

The explosion of biomedical data has opened up new possibilities in terms of advancing
biomedical research, diagnosing diseases, and supporting decision-making in clinical practice.
But the tremendous quantity of diverse data produced through scientific publications, electronic
medical records, genomics, medical images, and clinical trials imposes serious challenges in
terms of knowledge integration and causal reasoning. Knowledge Graphs (KGs) and Large
Language Models (LLMs) are two complementary Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools that have
shown promise in revolutionizing biomedical causal discovery by combining structured and
unstructured data into knowledge representations. Knowledge Graphs consist of interconnected
semantic networks of biomedical entities like genes, proteins, diseases, drugs, biological
pathways, and clinical outcomes, whereas Large Language Models offer superior natural
language processing and evidence synthesis abilities. The current review discusses recent
advances in Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models integration for biomedical causality
analysis and health care-related applications. The study used systematic narrative review method
for analysis of peer-reviewed papers published between 2020 and 2025. It was found that the
hybrid Artificial Intelligence methods based on transformers with biomedical knowledge graphs
provide better results for causality analysis, biomedical relation extraction, evidence synthesis,
and clinical recommendation systems than traditional machine learning algorithms. However,
there are some problems associated with incomplete biomedical knowledge graphs, language
models' hallucinations, data privacy issues, computational expenses, difficulties in achieving
interoperability, and lack of explainability. Further research will likely concentrate on such areas
as explainable artificial intelligence, retrieval-augmented generation, knowledge integration in
multimodal format, federated learning, and trustworthy AI systems that can assist personalized
medicine and evidence-based clinical decision making.


Keywords: Knowledge Graphs, Large Language Models, Biomedical Artificial Intelligence,
Clinical Decision Support, Causal Discovery, Biomedical Informatics, Precision Medicine,
Natural Language Processing.

Introduction
In the healthcare industry, there has been tremendous growth in the amount of biomedical data
produced over the last two decades. Millions of research papers, clinical reports, genomic
databases, pharmaceutical research, EHRs, and imaging data are produced each year throughout
the world. This increase in the amount of biomedical knowledge provides a lot of opportunity for
better disease diagnosis, drug development, precision medicine, and patient care. The increased
diversity and complexity of such data sources, however, make it very challenging to discover
meaningful cause-and-effect relations between them through traditional approaches. Biomedical
causal discovery is the process of discovering the causality between biological entities, diseases,
environmental factors, treatment options, and clinical outcomes. Causality is vital in evidence-
based medicine since the process of making healthcare decisions involves more than just finding
a correlation. Healthcare decisions rely on an understanding of how certain events lead to
diseases and what mechanisms are behind their treatments. Conventional statistics usually
uncovers correlations without being able to determine if there is a real causation or if a
correlation is driven by some other factor. AI technology has now become indispensable for
dealing with these problems. The most impactful AI technologies include Knowledge Graphs
(KGs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) that have proven highly successful in biomedical
information processing tasks. While these two technologies have been initially created separately
from each other, nowadays more research is being conducted regarding their integration to
enhance biomedical causal discovery and clinical decision support. There are various biomedical
knowledge graphs that have been developed using information acquired from the scientific
literature, public biomedical databases, and clinical databases. Some of the resources for
biomedical data include DrugBank, Gene Ontology, Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), Unified
Medical Language System (UMLS), SNOMED CT, and Disease Ontology. These knowledge
resources allow the AI systems to uncover relationships that can exist between different
biomedical entities but cannot be discovered through traditional database queries. Large
Language Models constitute yet another great leap forward in artificial intelligence in the
biomedical domain. They leverage the power of the transformer architecture by training them on
large amounts of textual data and thus giving them the ability to understand natural language,
synthesize scientific evidence, solve biomedical questions, generate hypotheses, and engage in
clinical reasoning. BioGPT, BioMedLM, Med-PaLM, ClinicalBERT, and PubMedBERT have
shown remarkable performance in biomedical NLP tasks.Knowledge Graphs can be defined as
structured semantic networks that reflect connections between real-world entities in graph form.
As for the biomedical area, nodes in the graph usually refer to various concepts like diseases,
genes, proteins, drugs, biological pathways, symptoms, biomarkers, and clinical interventions
whereas the edges denote connections between these entities like "causes," "treats," "interacts
with" or "associated with." In other words, the use of graphs helps to incorporate heterogeneous
biomedical information from different sources into a unified knowledge base. In spite of their
advanced skills, individual LLMs have significant drawbacks. In particular, they can create
hallucinations, offer old information, or suggest recommendations not proven scientifically.
Because of high requirements for precision in health care, LLMs integration with verified
biomedical Knowledge Graphs is becoming an interesting research trend. Knowledge Graphs
ensure verifiable data and reduce LLMs' hallucinations, increasing the level of facts' validity and
interpretation. Integration of Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models resulted in
development of hybrid systems, able to use both logical reasoning and deep language
comprehension. While Knowledge Graphs are used to represent structured biomedical
connections, Language Models analyze unstructured scientific texts and generate explanations in
human understandable way. These technologies allow to automate such processes as causal
discovery, literature synthesis, drug repurposing, adverse event prediction, and personalized
treatment. One of the fields where such hybrid technologies can prove their worth is represented
by Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS). In the contemporary world, CDSS help medical
specialists in analyzing patients' data, extracting relevant clinical evidence, making diagnoses,
suggesting treatment options, and forecasting patient outcomes. Thanks to the integration of
causal knowledge extracted from biomedical knowledge graphs with Large Language Models
reasoning powers, it becomes possible for such technologies to provide evidence-based
recommendations for individual patients. Another area where Knowledge Graph-enhanced
LLMs become more and more relevant is related to precision medicine. It is becoming
increasingly clear for modern medicine that patients have different responses to the same
therapies because of their genetics, environmental factors, lifestyle, and nature of disease. Due to
the possibility of integrating genomic data, proteomics, metabolomics, EHR, and biomedical
literature into graph-based knowledge structures, AI models can identify unique causal pathways
for therapy decisions. Drug discovery has also made great strides through these advancements in
technology. The development of pharmaceutical drugs has become an ever-increasingly common
application of knowledge graphs, which help identify novel interactions between genes, proteins,
diseases, and chemicals. Large Language Models help to complement these efforts through the
examination of large amounts of biomedical literature to summarize evidence and hypothesize
new targets for therapy. This approach has helped speed up the repurposing of already existing
drugs during emerging global health crises, such as the one brought on by COVID-19.
Notwithstanding the significant achievements, many problems still persist. Biomedical
knowledge graphs often include incomplete, inconsistent, or even obsolete information because
of the constant advancements of science. Large Language Models still suffer from problems
connected to explainability, computational difficulty, domain adaptation, and ethical issues
connected with patient confidentiality and algorithmic biases. Moreover, the combination of
structured knowledge with neural language models needs an advanced architecture. In light of
these advancements, a systematic review of the existing literature on the topic will be necessary
to gain insight into the potential and constraints of using Knowledge Graphs and Large Language
Models in the context of biomedical causality discovery. This paper aims to analyze the
developments that have been made so far in terms of methodology and applications in medicine.


Methodology
The current study used a systematic narrative review approach to review the latest developments
in combining Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models for biomedical causal discovery
and clinical decision support. PRISMA guidelines were followed for selecting literature to make
sure of the transparency and structure of the process.
Search Strategy of Literature
Researches were identified using comprehensive searches of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science,
IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar from
2020 to 2025. The search terms included: Knowledge Graphs, Large Language Models,
Biomedical Artificial Intelligence, Clinical Decision Support, Causal Discovery, BioGPT,
PubMedBERT, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Biomedical Knowledge Graphs, and Precision
Medicine.
Inclusion Criteria
Articles were selected if:
 ➢ Published in peer-reviewed journals or conferences.
 ➢ Considered Knowledge Graphs, Large Language Models, or integration of these
   technologies in biomedical context.
 ➢ Focused on causal discovery, biomedical reasoning, or clinical decision support.
 ➢ Provided empirical studies, methods and innovations, or reviews.
 ➢ Published in English from 2020 to 2025.
Exclusion Criteria
 ➢   Studies were excluded if they were:
 ➢   Editorials, opinion articles, or conference abstracts not having full articles.
 ➢   Devoted to non-biomedical applications only.
 ➢   Lacked in methodological transparency or experimental detail.
 ➢   Duplicate entries among databases searching.
Data Extraction and Synthesis
Information collected from the selected studies includes details on the publication, AI technology
used, knowledge graph sources, language model architecture, domain of biomedical application,
evaluation techniques used, strengths and limitations of the study, and future directions of the
research. Qualitative thematic synthesis of the results was performed due to methodological
diversity of the selected studies, rather than performing quantitative meta-analysis.


Results
The reviewed literature indicates fast progress in the development of integration between
Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models for better biomedical causal discovery and
clinical decision-making. In all the chosen works, hybrid architectures proved superior to pure
language models and traditional machine learning approaches in terms of knowledge retrieval,
biomedical reasoning, relation extraction, and evidence synthesis. Knowledge Graphs provided
great advantages in biomedical information integration because they linked heterogeneous
biomedical data containing genomics data, disease ontologies, drug interactions, biological
pathways, and clinical data. This made it possible for the AI systems to use semantic reasoning
with decreased inconsistencies related to data isolation. Large Language Models based on
Transformers exhibited outstanding performance in comprehension of biomedical literature and
extraction of clinically valuable data. Models such as PubMedBERT, BioGPT, Med-PaLM, and
BioMedLM were capable of interpreting complex biomedical terms and generating consistent
summaries of scientific information. However, their performance increased greatly with
grounding in biomedical knowledge graphs using retrieval-augmented generation approaches. A
number of papers demonstrated that causal discovery benefits from integration of graph
reasoning and language modeling. Hybrid systems effectively discovered new gene-disease
relationships, predicted adverse effects of medications, offered individualized treatment
suggestions, and formed biomedical hypotheses supported by evidence. Knowledge Graphs also
decreased the occurrence of hallucinations and increased factual consistency in answering
clinical questions. The Clinical Decision Support Systems employing Knowledge Graphs and
Large Language Models were found to be better at diagnostics than traditional rule-based
decision support systems. Context-aware recommendations were formed based on patient data
and knowledge obtained from scientific literature and structured knowledge sources.

In conclusion, the discussed research provides significant evidence that Knowledge Graphs and
Large Language Models integrated together greatly improve biomedical causal discovery,

Discussion
The results obtained in this review prove that the integration of Knowledge Graphs and LLMs is
one of the most important advancements in biomedical artificial intelligence. Both approaches
are already highly effective in biomedical data analysis by themselves; however, together they
offer a more advanced way of conducting causal discovery and providing support for clinical
decisions. While Knowledge Graphs bring biomedical knowledge and explicit semantic
relations, Large Language Models offer advanced natural language understanding, reasoning,
and synthesis of information. In combination, these tools create intelligent systems that are able
to perform evidence-based medicine. One of the main outcomes of the research is that
Knowledge Graphs considerably improve biomedical knowledge integration. Modern biomedical
data come from various sources: electronic health records, genomic sequencing, proteomics,
metabolomics, clinical trials, pharmaceutics databases, and biomedical literature. The
aforementioned data is typically available in different formats, which makes their processing
rather difficult. Knowledge Graphs offer a solution to this problem as these entities are
represented as interconnected nodes in a graph structure, each with their semantic relationship.
As a result, a researcher can incorporate different pieces of fragmented biomedical information
into a more structured and interconnected knowledge network. Large Language Models are
useful in processing huge amounts of biomedical unstructured data collected in scientific
researches, clinical notes, and healthcare reports. Models such as BioGPT, PubMedBERT,
BioMedLM, ClinicalBERT, and Med-PaLM show excellent results in biomedical Natural
Language Processing in various tasks such as named entity recognition, biomedical question
answering, literature summarization, relation extraction, and clinical text understanding. Unlike
traditional methods of biomedical Natural Language Processing that depend much on manually
engineered linguistic features, transformer-based models learn context-aware representations
directly from very big biomedical datasets. Nevertheless, there are some limitations associated
with the use of standalone Large Language Models. The most common disadvantage is the so-
called hallucination – generation of accurate, fluent but unsubstantiated information. In the case
of healthcare, the use of inaccurate information can affect the results of clinical decision-making
if generated information is used without verification. To reduce this limitation, Knowledge
Graphs provide structured and verified biomedical knowledge as the base for responses
generated by the language model. Besides, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models
make this process more efficient by retrieving verified biomedical knowledge before generation.
Another essential finding is the increasing use of hybrid KG–LLM systems in biomedical causal
discovery. Statistical techniques that are commonly used to analyze biomedical data usually
detect associations between variables rather than reveal the causal relationships between them.
The use of hybrid systems provides an opportunity to analyze various biomedical data sources
and find causal relations between variables using context-driven graph-based reasoning. It is
especially useful in genomics, systems biology, epidemiology, and precision medicine because
of the complexity of the interaction between different types of factors involved in the
development of diseases. Another two application domains where integration of Knowledge
Graphs and Large Language Models would be highly beneficial are drug discovery and
pharmaceutical research. Development of pharmaceutical drugs normally entails a lot of lab
experiments and significant costs. Integration of hybrid AI systems helps to speed up this process
due to identification of previously unrecognised relations between various diseases, genes,
proteins, biological pathways, and medicines. Knowledge Graphs find indirect connections
between those elements, while Large Language Models fast aggregate information from
thousands of biomedical papers. Such an integration of technologies turned out to be very
effective in case of drug repurposing when already developed medications are re-examined for
their therapeutic use cases. It is worth mentioning that Clinical Decision Support Systems
(CDSS) have been greatly affected by the integration of Knowledge Graphs and LLMs.
Contemporary CDSS help doctors and other healthcare workers in making decisions by offering
diagnoses, treatment suggestions, prognoses, drug interaction warnings, and personalising
treatment. Traditional decision support systems based on rules normally have difficulties coping
with constant update of medical knowledge. Meanwhile, hybrid AI systems integrate the newest
biomedical evidence from scientific literature and keep the reasoning structured with Knowledge
Graphs. In addition, the review also points out the application of these technologies within the
realm of precision medicine. The goal of precision medicine is to deliver personalized treatments
for patients by taking into account their unique genetic, environmental, lifestyle, and medical
histories. By integrating genomic data, electronic medical records, test results, imaging
techniques, and scientific articles, hybrid AI models can uncover personalized causal factors
affecting treatment effectiveness. A recent use case for these technologies is that of public health
monitoring and infectious disease surveillance. In times of global health crises, such as the
COVID-19 pandemic, scientists needed to sift through an ever-growing body of scientific
literature in order to understand the transmission vectors, possible treatments, the efficacy of
vaccines, and new strains of the virus. Despite the clear advancements that the reviewed
literature demonstrates, there are still some challenges that should be taken into account. The
first important limitation is associated with the quality and consistency of biomedical Knowledge
Graphs. Given that biomedical knowledge constantly changes, many knowledge graphs become
obsolete unless they are constantly updated through new research publications. Inconsistent or
incomplete structure of knowledge graphs might result in lower reasoning precision and affect
the quality of artificial intelligence applications based on such graphs. The computational
complexity is one more challenge. Large Language Models have billions of parameters and
demand considerable computational power both for training and for implementing purposes.
Combining such models with large-scale biomedical Knowledge Graphs increases the memory
and computational cost. Such infrastructure demands might restrict the implementation of the
technology in developing countries. There is also the question of explainability, which continues
to be very significant. Medical practitioners need clear explanations in support of the suggestions
made by the AI for the application of the recommendations into practice. The Knowledge Graphs
offer a very good solution in this respect because there is explicit reasoning between the
biomedical entities, but the neural reasoning behind Large Language Models can be quite hard to
understand. Therefore, in the future, research should concentrate on developing Explainable
Artificial Intelligence (XAI) with high performance and transparent reasoning capabilities.
Another key factor that must be taken into consideration is ethics. There are a number of ethical
considerations associated with the use of electronic health records, genomic data and patient-
generated data in the training of AI systems. In addition, AI models trained on biased data can
actually create new biases in health care provision to different demographic groups. Another
problem is related to the interoperability of biomedical information systems. Different coding
schemes, terminologies, and databases used by healthcare organizations can prevent
interoperability at multiple levels in hospitals, laboratories, and research facilities.
Standardization frameworks, such as SNOMED CT, ICD, HL7 FHIR, and UMLS, will remain
crucial to ensure semantic interoperability and facilitate Knowledge Graph creation. Research
directions for the future stated in the reviewed literature highlight the importance of multimodal
biomedical intelligence. Future systems should not be limited only to the processing of textual
data. Instead, they should integrate information from medical imaging, genomic and proteomics
data, metabolomics, sensors in wearables, lab results, and patient reports within the framework
of a Knowledge Graph. Together with Retrieval-Augmented Generation, federated learning, and
explainable reasoning, these technologies could lead to the development of reliable AI systems
that would enable personalized medical decisions. In conclusion, it is evident from the evidence
reviewed in this paper that the use of Knowledge Graphs in conjunction with Large Language
Models considerably improves biomedical causal discovery and clinical decision support.
Despite the presence of some challenges associated with methodology, computation, and ethical
considerations, further developments in the field of artificial intelligence are expected to help
with managing medical knowledge, speeding up biomedical discoveries, improving precision
medicine, and enhancing clinical practice.


Conclusion
Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models have proved to be a breakthrough combination
in biomedical causal discovery and intelligent clinical decision support. Thanks to the
combination of structured biomedical knowledge and advanced natural language processing and
reasoning, hybrid artificial intelligence systems eliminate a lot of drawbacks inherent in
traditional statistical analysis and pure machine learning models. The review proves the value of
Knowledge Graph-enhanced Large Language Models in relation extraction, causal inference,
literature review, drug discovery, personalized medicine, and clinical recommendations.
Knowledge Graphs offer clear semantic representations of complicated biomedical relations,
whereas Large Language Models allow for the efficient analysis of large amounts of
unstructured biomedical literature. The combination of both allows for better detection of causal
pathways, hypothesis formulation, biomedical knowledge search, and the mitigation of the risk
of AI-generated unsubstantiated answers by utilizing techniques such as knowledge grounding
and Retrieval-Augmented Generation.


However, there are still several challenges that need to be overcome, including incomplete
biomedical knowledge bases, computational challenges, model hallucinations, poor
explainability, interoperability, and ethical issues related to privacy and algorithmic fairness. It
will take constant cooperation between biomedical researchers, clinicians, data scientists, AI
specialists, and healthcare policy-makers to address all these limitations. Future research in the
field needs to pay attention to explainable artificial intelligence, multimodal data integration,
federated learning, up-to-date biomedical knowledge graphs, and trusted clinical AI models
capable of helping to practice precision medicine. As biomedical information increases rapidly,
the combination of Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models will become increasingly
vital for advancing evidence-based medicine and healthcare delivery.
