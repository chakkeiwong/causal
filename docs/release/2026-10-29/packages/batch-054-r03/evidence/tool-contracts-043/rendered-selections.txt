72.6     Recovery includes an unknown outcome
Local pure computations and external effects have different recovery contracts. A deterministic
calculation can be rerun with pinned inputs and environment when recomputation is allowed.
A paid model request may have completed and been billed even if its response was lost. A
proposed journal distinguishes prepared, submitted, acknowledged, completed, failed and
outcome-unknown states. A timeout after submission should not be treated as proof that the
provider did no work. The availability of provider lookup or idempotency must be established
for the selected service. (Causal banking proposal, 2026z)
   There is a simple indistinguishability argument. Let history h0 lose a request before the
external service receives it, and let history h1 lose the response after the service completes
the work. If the local journal in each case contains only submitted and the same timeout, its
observable state is identical. A rule depending only on that state takes the same action in
both histories. Resubmission can duplicate work under h1 ; refusing to resubmit can leave
the task unfinished under h0 . Randomization changes the probabilities but cannot guarantee
both no duplication and eventual completion from this information alone. A service lookup,
idempotent external operation or an explicitly accepted duplication policy adds information or
changes the requirement. This finite argument concerns the stated observation model, not all
possible distributed systems. (Causal banking proposal, 2026aa)
   For a causal assistant, the appropriate response may be to retain an unknown provider
outcome while continuing independent deterministic checks or another task. Cancellation
similarly needs a scope: stopping future local work does not prove that an already submitted
remote request was cancelled or its charge reversed. The budget ledger should retain any
reservation whose financial outcome is unknown until reconciliation. The scientific report can
remain incomplete with an explicit reason; filling the missing answer from memory would sever
its evidence chain.

72.19.3     A servicing route with backup: attribution versus value

Consider a deliberately stipulated servicing mechanism. Let A ∈ {0, 1} denote availability
of a backup contact, and C ∈ {0, 1} activation of a priority service. At the next stage, B
records whether the backup actually runs and D whether the priority route delivers. Recovery
Y follows the Boolean equations

                       B = A(1 − C),         D = C,       Y = max{B, D}.                   (72.9)

These are authored assumptions, not estimated account behavior. They encode perfect success
of an executed route and suppression of the backup while priority is active. In the factual
state A = C = 1, the realised path is D = 1, B = 0, Y = 1. A faithful narrative can identify
the executed priority route. Under an intervention setting only C = 0, however, the structural
equation for B is still active: B = 1 and Y = 1. Removing priority alone does not remove
recovery in this state. (Causal banking proposal project, 2026y)
 If a different comparison also forces the backup to remain off, the outcome is instead Y = 0.
With the subscript describing the intervention,

                          Ydo(C=0) A=1 = 1,       Ydo(C=0,B=0) A=1 = 0.                   (72.10)

The second expression changes two mechanisms. It may be relevant to a declared attribution
question, but it is not the outcome of disabling only the priority service. A result object must
retain which comparison was requested. Replacing an intervention by a compound action
that holds a downstream route fixed silently changes the scientific question. Conversely, a
zero effect of disabling one route does not erase the fact that the route was executed. The
explanatory and intervention answers can differ without logical contradiction.
  To make the distinction economic, suppose a recovery is worth USD 200, priority costs
USD 40 when activated, and an executed backup costs USD 10. The stipulated net value per
case is
                            N = 200Y − 40C − 10B USD.                            (72.11)
At A = 1, priority produces USD 160 net and the backup-only route USD 190. Priority’s
incremental net value is therefore minus USD 30 although it provides the factual recovery
path. When A = 0, priority gives USD 160 while no priority gives zero. If backup availability
is not known before action but its stipulated probability is p, averaging these two states gives

                     E[Ndo(C=1) − Ndo(C=0) ] = 160 − 190p USD per case.                   (72.12)

Under these assumptions priority has positive incremental value precisely when p < 16/19,
with indifference at equality. If availability is actually observed before action, the appropriate
conditional decision differs from this population decision. The information clock must therefore
accompany the value calculation. Exact enumeration of all four (A, C) states and rational checks

recovers the entire stipulated target. The stronger distinction is information-theoretic. Let
S and U be independent fair bits, with Z1 = U and Z2 = S ⊕ U . Enumeration of the four

                      H(S | Z1 ) = H(S | Z2 ) = 1,        H(S | Z1 , Z2 ) = 0.               (72.16)

Each isolated channel leaves a full bit of uncertainty; the pair determines S. These are project

authors. If a stipulated bank decision must choose the bit correctly and an error costs USD
200, either isolated channel leaves expected loss USD 100, while their combination permits
zero. This is a designed value-of-information illustration, not a measured privacy cost, price or
banking result. An approved reconstruction observer and its allowed side information must
accompany a disclosure claim. (Yu et al., 2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026eu)

report no leak when the reply instead discloses entity B’s value. If B also belongs to the forget

correctly. The paper acknowledges this limitation and reports that counting other forget
entities changes the retrieval results for its input-corruption reference. A bank evaluation
should report queried-entity, any-authorized-forget-entity, third-party and joint-reconstruction

policy. Synthetic linked entities and deliberately split values suffice to test the initial contract.

72.24.3     Prompt selection is learning and needs held-out evaluation

The survey covers selecting uncertain questions for annotation and optimizing prompts using
feedback, search and learned rewriting. It also reports that different tasks, models and metrics
limit comparisons, and that automated prompting can fail to improve on simple baselines.
These are source-level observations, not reproduced rankings. An optimized prompt is fitted
to information: developer trials and expert edits are part of training even when model weights
stay fixed. (Chen et al., 2025a)
  Consider a constructed null example with K = 20 candidate prompts and n = 4 develop-
ment tasks. Every prompt has true success probability 1/2. Assume success indicators are
independent across prompts and tasks. For prompt j, let Sj be its development successes.

Nevertheless, a fresh test independent of selection still has expected accuracy 1/2 for the

  The banking protocol should therefore reserve unseen tasks or task families, record the
complete search and repair budget, and separate development examples from scoring evidence.
Frozen model and retrieval versions make a comparison interpretable but do not themselves
guarantee generalization. Where tasks share a template, customer history or policy source,
that clustering affects the unit of evaluation. Repeated paraphrases of one causal problem
must not be counted as independent scientific problems without justification.
   The survey’s discussion of supplied solution chains is particularly relevant: it acknowledges
that supplying ground-truth intermediate solutions limits what a high solve rate demonstrates.
Our evaluation should label an oracle-assisted condition separately from unaided translation
or identification. Likewise, zero-shot describes the declared examples in a prompt, not verified
absence of the problem from a model’s pretraining data. Neither a polished explanation nor
agreement among human judges independently establishes causal correctness. (Chen et al.,
2025a)
