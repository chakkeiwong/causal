\chapter{Learning funding responses from verified histories}
\label{integrated:linked_funding}

\begin{quote}\small
Correctness counts can leave an individual funding response unidentified when
two candidate histories mix equally. An audit that resolves the actual focal
history supplies a different observation. We derive a sampled-outcome score
for the existing continuous-action, common-calendar funding target, including
its additional audit variance and independent nuisance pilot. A registered
4,800-request comparison finds finite intervals in every linked-outcome
request. At exact identity rank one, the augmented method has median width
about USD 527,000, while the count-based set is whole-line in all 600 requests.
Its coverage there is 559/600, so finite availability is not general
calibration. False exogeneity, incorrect inclusion probabilities and noisy
truth labels produce severe failures. The result identifies useful new
information to seek from a bank; its authenticity, price and acceptable
decision precision remain external requirements.
\end{quote}

\section{The same bank decision, with a richer observation}

A deposit team considers a one-month increase of ten basis points in its
offered rate. Let $F_t=(F_{t0},\ldots,F_{tH})'$ denote the focal portfolio's
balance response history at origin $t$, with $H=6$. Two candidate vectors
$Y_{t1},Y_{t2}$ contain the focal and companion histories in an uncertain
order. An origin's assignment applies to all seven horizons. The two strata
$S_t\in\{0,1\}$ each have fixed target weight one half. If $\beta_{sh}$ is
the focal balance response to the rate intervention, define
\begin{equation}
 \theta_s=\sum_{h=0}^{H}w_h\beta_{sh},\qquad
 V=\tfrac12(\theta_0+\theta_1)-C.\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:value}
\end{equation}
The inherited synthetic account has 48 branches, USD one million per balance
unit, monthly margin .002 and discount .99. Its weights are
$w_h=48\times10^6\{.002(.99)^h-(.001/12)1\{h=0\}\}$ and
$C=48\times100\times10^6(.001/12)=400{,}000$ USD. The immediate induced
balance also pays the increased rate; future margins are discounted once.
With focal response $2(.82)^h$, the target is USD 375,149, or USD 751,749
when persistence is .96. These are reproducible synthetic accounts, not
measured bank profits.

Chapters~\ref{integrated:weak_audit_projection} and~\ref{integrated:weak_audit_sharpening} infer $V$ from candidate responses and independent correctness counts. If the probability $p_s$ of the first candidate being
focal equals one half, the mean mixing equations cannot separate focal and
companion effects. Better interval algebra retains that ambiguity. Consider
instead an audit indicator $R_t$ with known probability $q_t>0$ that, when
selected, reveals \emph{which stored candidate history is focal}. The bank
must provide this information in both evaluation and independent pilot
histories. One audit resolves an origin's vector, not seven independent
audits. Unselected labels are unavailable to the estimator.

This is an extension of the monograph's independent-pair audit argument to
the selected dependent funding calendar. It does not identify persistent
maps across arbitrary customer pairs, arbitrary shared audit errors or
many-to-many ownership. Here the inherited candidate swaps are independent
by origin and the new audit draws are conditionally independent across
origins given the complete histories and their supplied probabilities.

\section{Identification from sampled linked outcomes}

Let $B_t=(Y_{t1}+Y_{t2})/2$ be the observed candidate average and let
$\mathcal H$ contain all complete focal/companion histories, assignments and
actions before audit randomization. Define the vector pseudo-outcome
\begin{equation}
 Z_t=B_t+\frac{R_t}{q_t}(F_t-B_t),\qquad
 \E[R_t\mid\mathcal H]=q_t.\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:pseudo}
\end{equation}
Only the selected term requires the focal identity. Conditional expectation
gives $\E[Z_t\mid\mathcal H]=F_t$ exactly. No inverse $1/(2p_s-1)$ appears;
indeed swapping the candidate names and complementing the selected audit
labels leaves $Z_t$ unchanged. The alternative $Z_t^{I}=R_tF_t/q_t$ has the
same conditional expectation, but uses no observed average as augmentation.
There is no unconditional theorem that either has lower variance for every
possible outcome law.

Write $A_t=m_s(X_t)+v_t$ for the observed action, where $X_t$ contains the
fixed driver dictionary and stratum. Let
$\ell_s(X_t)=\E[w'Z_t\mid X_t,S_t=s]$.
Under the inherited exogenous-innovation response law,
\begin{equation}
 \E\!\left[1\{S_t=s\}v_t
   \{w'Z_t-\ell_s(X_t)-\theta_sv_t\}\right]=0,
 \qquad Q_s=\E[1\{S_t=s\}v_t^2]>0.\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:score}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{integrated:linked_funding:eq:pseudo} reduces this observed moment to the corresponding
focal-outcome moment. Thus the same intervention target is identified even
at $p_s=1/2$, provided the new audit law and the causal action restriction
are true. The conditional expectation identity does not establish exogeneity.

An independent 4,800-origin pilot selects among the fixed penalties
$0,1,10,100$ using first-half fitting and second-half standardized prediction
loss, then refits on its whole history. Its outcomes are the same audited
pseudo-outcomes, so its labels are part of the information bill. On the
1,200 evaluation origins, set $\widehat v_t=A_t-\widehat m_s(X_t)$ and
$\widehat u_t=w'Z_t-\widehat\ell_s(X_t)$. The estimator is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\theta_s=
 \frac{\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}\widehat v_t\widehat u_t}
      {\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}\widehat v_t^2},\qquad
 \widehat V=\tfrac12(\widehat\theta_0+\widehat\theta_1)-C.
 \label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:estimate}
\end{equation}
The partialling-out orthogonality and joint nuisance-product/action-square
rate premise appear in the inspected DML original \cite{dml2018}.
That paper's iid result does not by itself prove the dependent result here.
For this fixed stable Gaussian dictionary, fixed $q\ge q_{\min}>0$ and
independent invariant pilot, coefficient errors of order $M^{-1/2}$ give
product remainders $O_p(M^{-1})=o_p(T^{-1/2})$ when $M=4T$. This is a
pointwise growing-history statement, not a uniform guarantee when audit
probability tends to zero or persistence tends to one. A falsely supplied
probability changes the population moment before any rate argument applies.

\section{Audit variance on a shared calendar}

Define the oracle scalar influence for $V$ using focal outcomes as $\psi_t^F$.
With $c_t=\tfrac12\sum_s1\{S_t=s\}v_t/Q_s$, the pseudo-outcome influence is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_t^Z=\psi_t^F+a_t,
 \qquad a_t=c_t(R_t/q_t-1)w'(F_t-B_t).\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:decompose}
\end{equation}
Conditionally on $\mathcal H$, $\E a_t=0$. Independent audit randomization
also gives $\E[a_ta_u\mid\mathcal H]=0$ for $u\ne t$, and every $a_t$ is
uncorrelated with any complete-history score $\psi_u^F$. Its variance is
\begin{equation}
 \E a_t^2=\E\!\left[c_t^2(1/q_t-1)\{w'(F_t-B_t)\}^2\right].
 \label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:auditvar}
\end{equation}
The added audit term therefore changes lag zero only. This conclusion uses
independent audit draws \emph{conditional on the complete histories}; a
persistent audit mechanism or correlated label errors needs a new argument.

For the latent focal score, the monograph's innovation filtration condition
averages the current exogenous action innovation and shocks through $t+H$
to zero, conditional on the full exogenous driver path and shocks before $t$.
The score is measurable through $t+H$. A later score more than $H$ dates away
has conditional mean zero given the earlier one, giving zero covariance
beyond $H$. The scores need not be $H$-dependent. Stable roots, finite higher
moments and stationary mixing supply the score CLT; the finite horizon by
itself is insufficient. This condition fails under the deliberately endogenous
innovation law.

Using the fitted scalar score
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\psi_t=\frac{T}{2}
 \sum_s\frac{1\{S_t=s\}\widehat v_t
   (\widehat u_t-\widehat\theta_s\widehat v_t)}
  {\sum_j1\{S_j=s\}\widehat v_j^2},
\end{equation}
center it by its sample mean and write the centered scores as $d_t$.
The selected variance estimate and normal interval are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat s^2=T^{-2}\!\left\{\sum_t d_t^2+
       2\sum_{k=1}^{H}\sum_{t=k+1}^{T}d_td_{t-k}\right\},
 \qquad \widehat V\pm z_{.975}\widehat s.\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:variance}
\end{equation}
The empirical scores include audit noise, so adding a second independent
``audit variance'' would double count it. We refuse nonpositive/nonfinite
variance, absent sampled strata and deterministic scores at the numerical
roundoff floor. We do not clip a negative estimate into an interval.
The inspected Newey--West original proves positive semidefiniteness for its
Bartlett weighting \cite{newey1986}; this untapered, fixed-horizon scalar
construction is different and inherits no such guarantee. Its advantage
within the stated filtration law is avoiding attenuation of nonzero short-lag
covariances.

\section{Fresh comparison and retained adverse evidence}

The eight designs and three procedures were fixed before their final draws.
Each design has 600 independent requests, the same evaluation/pilot histories
across methods, and distinct random streams for count audits and outcome-linked
audits. The count method uses 1,600 correctness labels per stratum and the
previous half-sum/half-difference rectangular projection. The linked methods
use $q=.25$ in both histories, except the $q=.10$ low-audit cell. Near rank
means $(p_0,p_1)=(.55,.515)$; exact rank means $(.5,.5)$; the baseline is
$(.9,.85)$. The persistent cell changes the balance root from .82 to .96.
Three adverse cells separately violate exogeneity, actual inclusion or
label truth. Neither the reported probabilities nor the declarations test
their own truth.

All 4,800 requests produced computed sets for all three procedures. The
linked intervals were finite in every request. Table~\ref{integrated:linked_funding:tab:coverage}
reports all-request coverage and availability, and
Table~\ref{integrated:linked_funding:tab:diagnostics} retains bias, empirical dispersion and labels.
Exact-binomial Monte Carlo intervals are saved for each coverage frequency,
with Bonferroni simultaneous control across all 24 design/method combinations.
They describe Monte Carlo uncertainty under these selected laws only.

In the near-rank cell, the count set was whole-line in 552/600 requests;
its 48 finite sets had median width USD 17.396 million. The augmented
interval was finite in all 600 with median width USD 528,513 and coverage
572/600. In all 48 paired finite requests it was narrower. At exact rank,
the count set was whole-line in all 600 requests, while the augmented median
width was USD 526,663. This is new outcome information resolving an old
mean ambiguity; it is not an improvement obtained from the old count data
alone. There is no paired finite width ratio at exact rank because the
baseline has no finite set there.

Finite precision does not establish 95\% calibration. Exact-rank augmented
coverage is $559/600=.9317$, with mean SE/empirical SD .943. Its individual
Monte Carlo interval is approximately $[.9084,.9505]$ and simultaneous
interval $[.8942,.9593]$. Baseline augmented coverage is \mbox{$564/600=.94$}.
These finite samples neither prove a general failure nor qualify uniform
nominal coverage. Under the persistent law, augmented median width is USD
1.413 million against synthetic value USD .752 million. The low-audit
design uses about 601 linked labels across both histories and has median
width USD 599,027. The ordinary $q=.25$ designs use about 1,500 labels.
Neither label count is an observed market price.

The augmented interval was narrower than inverse weighting in 4,793 of
4,800 matched requests and wider in seven wrong-inclusion requests. This
descriptive result includes invalid-law cells and has no universal ordering
interpretation. Even on the valid laws, reducing the augmented width relative
to the count box trades the box's strong conservatism for a normal-approximation
interval; one must assess coverage and precision together.

\section{Why truthful audits and exogeneity remain essential}

The false-exogeneity cell has innovation correlation .5 with a balance shock
of scale 2. The inherited observational-equivalence argument shifts the
observed action coefficient by one. The financial shift is therefore
$\sum_h w_h(.82)^h=387{,}575$ USD. The observed augmented bias is USD 388,119,
and coverage is only 148/600. Resolving focal identity does not resolve action
confounding; the wide count set's 600/600 coverage in this cell supplies no
causal validation.

If actual inclusion is $q_a$ but the caller supplies $q_d$, then
\begin{equation}
 \E[Z_t\mid\mathcal H]
  =B_t+\frac{q_a}{q_d}(F_t-B_t).\label{integrated:linked_funding:eq:wrongq}
\end{equation}
With $q_a=.10$, $q_d=.25$, focal coefficient 2 and companion .5, the
augmented coefficient is 1.55, not 2. Its population monetary bias is about
USD $-174{,}409$, compared with the saved bias USD $-179{,}157$. Coverage
is 378/600. Pure inverse weighting instead targets coefficient .8, explaining
its larger negative bias and 159/600 coverage. This failure happens even
with independent selection; more complicated unknown selection laws add
further obligations.

If selected labels independently flip with probability $e$, the apparent
focal mean becomes $(1-e)F_t+eG_t$, where $G_t$ is the companion. For $e=.15$
the coefficient shifts by $.15(.5-2)=-.225$, producing population monetary
bias about USD $-87{,}204$. The augmented observed bias is USD $-84{,}726$
and coverage 528/600. Label-error correction would need external gold truth
and its sampling uncertainty; pretending that the label is exact has not
performed such a correction. These population identities and the numerical
audit-variance decomposition are project checks, not source-derived bank
guarantees.

\section{A design calculation before buying labels}

For a constant, truthful $q$, write
$K=\E[c_t^2\{w'(F_t-B_t)\}^2]$ and let $\Omega_F$ be the latent focal
long-run variance. Equation~\eqref{integrated:linked_funding:eq:auditvar} gives
$\Omega_Z=\Omega_F+(1/q-1)K$. If an external bank decision specifies a
maximum interval width $w_\star$, its first-order planning inequality is
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_F+(1/q-1)K\le
 L,\qquad L=T\left(\frac{w_\star}{2z_{.975}}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
When $K>0$ and $L\ge\Omega_F$, this gives
$q\ge K/(L-\Omega_F+K)$; if $L<\Omega_F$, even complete audit cannot meet
the width target at that calendar size. If $K=0$, audits add no variance in
this specific moment, but the caller still needs a valid information law.
This algebra is a planning approximation, not a finite-sample coverage
certificate. Values of $K$ and $\Omega_F$ used for a new design must come from
an independent design pilot and include their estimation uncertainty.

For the registered $M=4T$ pilot, expected linked-label demand is $q(T+M)=5qT$.
An economic comparison also needs history acquisition, truthful resolution,
pilot fitting, complete evaluation and review costs. Count labels and linked
histories are different services; 1,500 linked histories cannot be declared
cheaper than 3,200 correctness labels without actual prices. The bank has
supplied neither those prices nor $w_\star$. A fixed precision target and
authentic audit design are the next inputs required to turn this construction
into an investment or deployment decision.

\section*{Evidence, scope and continuation}

Independent enumeration checks the audit expectation and added diagonal
variance. Dense fitting and direct lag products agree with the implementation
across chunk boundaries to about $1.9\times10^{-14}$ relative error. Complete
audits reduce to observed focal histories; unsampled labels, invalid
probabilities and degenerate variance are rejected. The first 600 raw requests
were preserved as unqualified after an interval-serialization mismatch stopped
summary generation. The shared-format repair and round-trip check preceded a
fresh registered seed; none of those first requests is part of the 4,800 final
comparison. The saved journal reconstructs every summary and interval; eight
fixed requests were separately regenerated. These are author checks, not an
independent scientific review.

Resolving the actual customer history supplies information that
correctness counts alone can miss. Finite intervals at exact identity rank
one are demonstrated for the specified funding model; their calibration and
useful precision for a bank remain uncertain. Valid assignment, known audit
inclusion and accurate labels are essential. Independent origin audits do
not establish inference under arbitrary persistence of customer identity and
calendar shocks. Nonlinear policies, temporal graphs and other learners
require their own arguments. Bank adoption also needs independent review,
relevant operating trials and measured costs. The following comparison asks what stronger assignment and sampling information can achieve across funding, servicing, discovery and learning. It retains this chapter's distinction between finite availability, calibrated uncertainty and worthwhile financial precision; the stronger information has its own acquisition and feasibility costs.

The experiment preserves its specification, code, completed results
and the failed partial run. These records distinguish the reported evidence
from unperformed work. The literature cutoff remains 7 October 2026. No new exhaustive source search
was performed; the two inherited primary copies were reinspected for the
limited roles cited above.
