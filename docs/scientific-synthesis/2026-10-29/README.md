2. The first generated study runs 3,000 matched replications. With n=2,000 and
   treatment propensity 0.1 in the high-benefit stratum, a constant residual
   projection averages 26.447 while the population benefit is 50. The interval
   covers its own target in 97.2% of replications and the population ATE in none.
   At the illustrative cost of 40, misinterpreting it rejects a beneficial
   offer in all 500 replications. The alternative averages 50.011, but fails
   under sparse support in another scenario; those failures remain counted.
3. The independently specified extension runs 6,000 new replications. A pooled
   Student interval has 94.7% conditional coverage under equal normal variances
   and 52.2% when the rare treated cell has higher variance. Cellwise Student
   intervals combined by a union bound retain coverage but are extremely wide.
   This is evidence for improving treatment support, not a universal interval
   remedy. These are generated Gaussian examples, not external-package trials.

   adjustment, information-date checks and refusal of an unsupported dependent
   sampling design. No sibling repository or live model was invoked.
5. The conditional investment recommendation favours a narrow comparative
   prototype over a broad replacement. Its cost-benefit equation explicitly
   leaves unknown costs and measured banking benefits unfilled.

6. A new public-data experiment fits a fixed logistic baseline on 41,188 UCI
   archived contact records. Adding unavailable call duration raises later-
   archive AUC from 0.630 to 0.793 and random-split AUC from 0.693 to 0.883.
   Those gains cannot justify pre-call targeting. Earlier/later subscription
   frequencies differ sharply; the fitted predictions establish no causal effect.
