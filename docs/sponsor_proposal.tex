\chapter*{The proposed investment}
Build a common scientific library and an independent workbench for bank
policy and time-series analysis, repeated decisions under partial observation,
and temporal causal discovery with automatic data organization. The product
should preserve what was known at each decision, what intervention is being
compared, what identifies its effect, and which financial outcome it changes.
Other applications can use the same operations through an SDK and evidence
exports.

The recommended first commitment is a six-month construction and bank-design
tranche, illustratively USD\,1.14 million plus bank-owner, independent-validation
and security participation still to be priced. All three scientific workspaces
start in this tranche. Their release claims differ: a qualified policy effect,
a conditional latent-model scenario and an ambiguous causal graph are useful
but distinct outputs. The team should implement a narrow supported path in
each workspace and measure whether their common foundation actually saves work.

A large shared product is not automatically the least expensive choice.
Under the proposal's explicit equal-output assumptions, workflow savings
alone require roughly 27 completed equivalent studies annually. Low reuse
and delayed adoption favor a capable team composing existing tools.
Further investment should depend on measured reuse or additional decision
value, with that expert team as the comparator.

\section*{The banking problem}
A promotion can raise deposit balances while reducing funding contribution
because the higher rate reprices the existing book. A collections action can
change next month's eligibility and the distribution of future arrears.
A regression on loan growth can confuse borrower demand with bank supply.
A predictive hidden-state model can fit historical observations while
misvaluing a new policy if staff used private information when taking actions.
These are different economic questions requiring different designs.

Banking literature provides concrete precedents: local deposit-market
structure and monetary transmission \citep{drechsler2017}; comparisons of
differently affected banks lending to the same firm \citep{mian2006};
refinancing eligibility distinguished from self-selected take-up
\citep{agarwal2020}; and demand/supply models for market-wide mortgage
regulation \citep{benetton2021}. Their coefficients are not ready-made
estimates for a new bank campaign. Their designs explain which variation,
measurements and assumptions a product must preserve.

\section*{What the product delivers}
The policy and transmission workspace evaluates customer interventions,
staggered branch changes, bank-firm supply shocks and dynamic response paths.
It preserves comparison groups, target weights, common economic shocks and
financial covariance. The sequential workspace compares explicit strategies,
fits controlled latent-state models and carries action selection and
observation uncertainty into conditional policy values. The discovery and
data workspace reconstructs historical information, proposes semantic
mappings, preserves uncertain entity links and investigates graph structure
under declared restrictions.

An analyst moves from a bank decision to a reconstructible study. A product
owner receives contribution, customer outcomes, uncertainty and unresolved
conditions. A reviewer can inspect the original account and estimator. A
developer consumes the same typed result rather than reinterpreting an
unlabeled effect array. The proposed standalone application deploys within
the institution's controlled environment; no named banking group is an
existing client or endorser of this proposal.

The common library should build temporal semantics, study orchestration,
financial reconciliation, score/inference contracts and evidence export.
It should reuse established estimators and planning/discovery engines.
The survey covers \SoftwareCount{} open-source projects, with preserved
license and documentation observations. Examples include DoWhy,
DoubleML/EconML, panel and R longitudinal methods, Tigramite and POMDP/state-space
ecosystems \citep{sw_dowhy,sw_doubleml,sw_econml,sw_did,sw_lmtp,
sw_tigramite,sw_dynamax,sw_pomdps}.
These are source assessments; they are not claims that all packages have
been runtime-qualified together.

\section*{What has actually been constructed}
The research package includes a technical monograph, 87 scholarly PDFs,
three official guidance PDFs, traceable sources and material claims, fitted
synthetic banking studies and four JSON contracts. Two narrow Python
components implement historical event-revision selection and mergeable
crossed-cluster score reduction. The full compiler, service, workspaces
and sibling adapters remain a proposed product.

The studies go beyond favorable demonstrations. A 96-branch deposit rollout
has positive balance effects but estimated monthly contribution near negative
USD\,5,219 per treated branch. The collections study compares direct,
importance-weighted and doubly robust policy estimates; a plausible
current-action weighting shortcut values the wrong sustained policy.
Repeated samples retain nominal-interval coverage failures.

An unknown-emission hidden-state study estimates initial probabilities,
transitions and observations from signals/actions alone. On observational
data, omitting hidden action selection reverses the model's policy-gain
sign; a longer convergence check preserves the failure. Modeling that
selection improves the stated experiment but leaves substantial policy-value
uncertainty. It does not establish generic observational identification.

The bank-firm example separates supply from demand through borrower effects.
A 1,200-panel experiment shows how common shocks invalidate row-independent
uncertainty. Two structural timing assumptions generate different impulse
responses with the same observed Gaussian VAR law. Exact short-path checks
independently verify selected likelihood and sequential-score identities.
All are synthetic research evidence, not measured bank uplift.

The ten-million-row component measurement takes about 1.2 seconds and
123 MiB peak resident memory in this environment. It generates and reduces
precomputed two-dimensional scores. It excludes source reads, temporal
reconstruction, nuisance fitting, POMDP fitting and graph search. It supports
a component construction choice, not an enterprise-scale service promise.

\chapter*{Cost, execution and acceptance}
\section*{A fair construction comparator}
Both alternatives must deliver the same completed studies: a frozen
population, credible identifying design, suitable reference comparison,
economic account and reconstructible review evidence. Expert composition
uses more study-specific code; the shared product automates recurring work.
The cost model does not give the expert team weaker scientific obligations.

The illustrative three-year comparison uses a 10\% discount rate.
Expert composition has USD\,1 million initial shared work, USD\,0.6 million
annual common operation and USD\,150,000 per completed study. The product
has USD\,4 million initial work, USD\,1.8 million annual operation and
USD\,60,000 per study. These are assumptions, not quotes or measured savings.
The USD\,1.14 million first tranche is included within the product build.

Amounts are present costs in USD millions. The first three scenarios have
8, 24 and 36 studies every year; the ramp has 4, 16 and 30. A positive
saving favors the product. The 24-study case needs approximately
USD\,246,000 additional constant annual decision benefit to cover its cost
premium. The ramp needs about USD\,980,000. Only value beyond what the expert
team would achieve belongs in this account.

The assumed USD\,90,000 saving per study equals 3.6 person-months at the
planning staffing rate. That needs evidence. If the saving halves, the
cost-only reuse threshold doubles to roughly 53 studies annually. Customer
and country-specific semantic work, validation and support may substantially
reduce reuse. Count failed studies and outcome-maturity delays in both
alternatives. Do not add analyst savings twice or treat synthetic policy
gains as commercial revenue.

\section*{The first six months}
Staff one product/economics lead, two causal/econometric scientists, two
data/platform engineers and one application engineer. At an assumed
USD\,25,000 loaded person-month, 36 person-months cost USD\,900,000.
Adding USD\,50,000 compute/tooling and 20\% contingency gives the
USD\,1.14 million illustrative tranche. The bank-specific estimate must
also price business/data owners, independent validation and security.

In the first month, select a funding rollout, a repeated-decision history
and a temporal mechanism question. Inspect action logs, historical
availability, ownership and outcome maturity. Agree the equivalent-work
baseline and required evidence. During months two and three, construct the
common snapshot/evidence path, qualify a limited panel/longitudinal path,
and implement finite-state estimation and a temporal graph adapter with
explicit scientific status.

During months four through six, complete bounded workspaces and have a
second analyst reconstruct their studies. Compare active analyst/reviewer
hours, calendar delay, unresolved material findings and economic relevance
with expert composition. A proposed 30\% effort reduction is a target to
test. Bank-scale storage, full estimator training and retry behavior require
separate workload qualification; a fast arithmetic kernel does not satisfy it.

Subsequent tranches expand supported histories, measurement models,
discovery assumptions, distributed execution and independent-product support
only when the scientific and cost evidence warrants them. The roadmap does
not postpone POMDP/discovery work to an undefined later product, nor promise
that all three workspaces can authorize operational customer policies in
six months.

\section*{Reuse across the existing repositories}
FinMarketing can export assignment episodes and group contribution components,
then consume policy effects with their target and covariance. dataml can
supply masks, recovery assumptions and uncertainty draws. BayesFilter can
supply numerical filtering/estimation components while the causal library
owns the action model and intervention interpretation. MacroFinance can
exchange shock restrictions and response paths; cardnpv requires explicit
contrast-sign conversion. These are proposed integrations based on inspected
local documentation \citep{localrepos}, with concrete acceptance journeys
in the technical monograph. No sibling repository was changed by this work.

\section*{What would stop or narrow the investment}
Narrow the target when action support or data coverage is inadequate.
Return conditional scenarios or bounds when historical policy value is
unidentified. Keep graph ambiguity visible. Stop a statistical release when
numerical stability or relevant interval calibration fails. Stop common
platform expansion if most applications require bespoke work and the
measured savings do not cover shared cost. Useful data and study components
can survive a negative result for another module.

For deployment, the bank needs actual access authorization, independent
model validation, operational ownership and qualified dependencies. The
April 2026 US guidance supersedes SR11-7 and SR21-8 and has specified
scope, including an exclusion for generative/agentic models
\citep{fed2026}. PRA SS1/23 has a revised April 2026 version with specified
institutional applicability \citep{pra2026}. The product supplies evidence
for the applicable review process; the proposal does not confer approval.
