69.30.6     What the available software can contribute

The public repository linked by the thesis is a PyWhy-LLM adaptation. Its 2 February
2026 revision contains the language suggestion and retrieval components, pairwise notebooks,
declared dependencies and an MIT notice carrying the PyWhy copyright. The complete file
inventory and two inspected notebooks do not expose the thesis’s HallBayes integration, Monte
Carlo graph construction or causal-flow training pipeline. This is a limit of the available
reproduction evidence, not proof that no private or differently named implementation exists.
They support reuse assessment of the front end, not independent reproduction of all reported
results. Its retrieval helper also creates its own language-model client and falls back to an
answer without retrieved evidence when no match is found. A bank adapter should pass the
selected model explicitly and retain the absence of evidence as an observable condition. The
front-end ideas therefore belong behind a small, versioned interface. An upstream component
that supplies the required operation with maintained interfaces is a serious alternative to
maintaining this adaptation. The inspected repository establishes no performance advantage
for a wholesale port. Its MIT notice is distinct from the permissions needed for dependencies,
retrieval data and models. (Molero Pérez and PyWhy contributors, 2026)
   The HallBayes repository at the inspected June 2026 revision presents Berry, an evidence-
support service with command-line and MCP interfaces. Its README distinguishes cited-
evidence support from world truth, and the inspected core accepts claims and cited spans
as the scoring inputs. That distinction fits a useful role in checking an assistant’s attributed
claims. The June 2026 revision is not the December 2025 implementation cited by the thesis.
A date-bounded query of the current default-branch history returned no commit for that
earlier date, while a retained version-one tag points to February 2026. This does not prove the
older software never existed; it prevents silently treating the current service as the thesis’s
exact dependency. No downloaded source was executed in this comparison. (Hassana Labs
et al., 2026)
   The software comparison reinforces the separation between evidence retrieval, graph con-
struction and intervention calculation. A quotation check can test whether a cited passage
supports a claim under a declared predicate without establishing that claim’s causal truth.
Similarly, an information ceiling does not supply calibrated edge probabilities, and the spread
of fitted-model effects does not acquire confidence-interval coverage from repetition alone. A
prototype could support stronger claims only with an authentic complete implementation, a
declared joint graph law or retention procedure, relevant calibration evidence, and public or
synthetic experiments with known interventions and matched information across comparators.

69.31       Domain knowledge as evidence for a causal graph
A collections manager considering a payment holiday needs to distinguish a plausible story
from an intervention that improves customer outcomes. A language model may suggest that
financial strain affects delinquency, that servicing changes financial strain, and that earlier
delinquency affects the servicing offer. Each statement needs a population, a time index,
a variable definition and an evidential basis. Joining the three statements without those
qualifications can create a cycle whose meaning is unclear. A retrieval system can help locate
the underlying evidence, but a retrieved sentence does not by itself establish an invariant
structural mechanism. Two recent clinical studies make the promise and the limitations of
this assistance concrete.


69.31.1     Graph elicitation and the meaning of completeness

Aziz and Brookhart study a hypothetical comparison of statins with no treatment for preventing
cardiovascular disease. They run four prompting strategies on GPT-4o and a zero-shot strategy
on GPT-o1, with three separate conversations per configuration. Two author reviewers assess
consistency, acyclicity, source accuracy, completeness and prompt adherence. This is a study
of fifteen outputs in one familiar clinical scenario, rather than an estimate of performance over
a population of banking questions. The original was published on 30 October 2025. Aziz and
Brookhart (2025)
   Its completeness criterion asks whether the graph contains seven groups of variables drawn
from an ASCVD risk score. This is a useful check for conspicuous omissions, but it does not
validate the graph’s arrows or establish that its adjustment set identifies the statin effect.
Predictors can have different causal roles: a pretreatment common cause, a mediator and a
selection variable cannot be treated interchangeably simply because each predicts the outcome.
In a bank, a strong arrears predictor measured after a servicing offer may be an outcome of
that offer. Including it in a list is different from correctly deciding whether to adjust for it.
   Table 1 and the article’s accompanying text report fourteen acyclic graphs and seven graphs
containing six of the seven criteria. The seven-of-fifteen count does not support the abstract’s
“more than half” wording. Race is absent from all fifteen outputs. That is a reproducible
observation about the study’s criterion; it does not establish a universal requirement to insert a
particular attribute into every causal model. A social attribute’s role needs an explicit account
of the processes it represents and the target population. Blindly adding a variable to improve
a checklist score could conceal the same scientific problem as omitting it. Aziz and Brookhart
(2025); Causal banking proposal project (2026bg)

   The study also separates producing citations from producing reliable evidence. One-shot
outputs supply sources but include nonexistent references and incorrect titles, dates or author
details. Chain-of-thought outputs give explanations without sources in all three runs. The
GPT-o1 zero-shot configuration consistently supplies references and code, yet one code output
needs manual repair. Its third run is described as supplying nine sources and then assessed
using denominators of six; the original outputs are needed before aggregating that run’s
citation accuracy. The source assessments described in the paper use title and abstract review,
which is weaker than checking that primary methods and results support a particular causal
arrow. Aziz and Brookhart (2025)
   One instruction-based output contains arrows in both directions between statin use and
cholesterol. The authors correctly identify the need for temporally indexed nodes if feedback is
intended. The banking counterpart is Dt → At → Dt+1 , where Dt is delinquency information
available before action At . Collapsing Dt and Dt+1 into a single “delinquency” node loses
the information clock. A graph assistant should request that missing distinction rather than
silently reverse an arrow to satisfy a syntactic check. The study supports a brainstorming and
source-checking role; it supplies no validation of a bank effect estimator.


69.31.2     Retrieval helps under a particular reference and query

Lin and colleagues combine language models, retrieval and observational discovery for chronic
low back pain. Their February 2026 medRxiv original is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed result.
They use baseline observations from 1,868 participants and a fourteen-variable expert reference.
Crucially, the authors explicitly state that this reference contains cycles and is not a DAG.
The reference was developed from clinical expertise and a literature synthesis; it is a declared
comparison target, not observed intervention truth. Lin et al. (2026)
   The three knowledge systems share Mistral 7B-Instruct v0.3. Ordinary retrieval supplies
document chunks selected by vector similarity. Knowledge-graph retrieval extracts entities
and relations, merges entities, forms communities and retrieves entities, attached relations,
community summaries and supporting text. The paper describes 600-token chunks with 100-
token overlap, an 8,000-token retrieval budget, and a pipeline involving constrained extraction,
a graph database and community clustering. These are descriptions of the authors’ system;
they do not establish that its dependencies are pinned, licensed for the proposed distribution
or operationally integrated here. Lin et al. (2026)
   Both retrieval systems use the same source articles that the experts used to construct the
comparison graph. This is an informative test of reconstructing an aligned body of knowledge.
It does not measure performance when the literature is incomplete, contradictory, selected
by a different team, or independent of the reference adjudicators. The authors discuss this
alignment explicitly. Their argument that the model predates publication of the expert graph
also does not by itself authenticate the model’s training corpus or exclude related earlier
content. The appropriate extension is a deliberately held-out reference and document design,
not a declaration that contamination has been ruled out.
   For the data component, the paper describes hill climbing with local generalized linear models,
BIC scores and 500 bootstrap samples. A thresholded consensus graph forms the initial model.
The system then combines its edges with the knowledge system’s proposals and asks a further
direction question when both directions are suggested. Plausibility, association, temporality
and a direct causal query are separate prompt conditions. Their relative performance matters
as much as the strongest result. Lin et al. (2026)
