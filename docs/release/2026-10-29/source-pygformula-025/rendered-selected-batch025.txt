47.4      Modifying a natural action and redrawing an action
A dynamic servicing policy may modify the action that would naturally be chosen: shorten a
delay, reduce a proposed payment, or add support when the proposed contact intensity lies in
a specified range. This is more specific than drawing a new action from a distribution with the
same conditional marginal frequencies. At later times the natural action is evaluated under
the history produced by earlier interventions. Unobserved features of the action mechanism

can connect that later natural action to an earlier one, even when there is no unmeasured
confounding of the ordinary joint treatment regime and outcome.
   The longitudinal modified treatment policy analysis distinguishes a natural action policy
from its stochastic-intervention counterpart. Under its structural model, standard sequential
randomization separates a current treatment innovation from later covariate and outcome
innovations. The stronger condition also separates it from future treatment innovations.
Theorem 1 identifies the redrawn stochastic intervention under the standard condition and the
natural modified policy under the stronger condition, together with support. The two targets
must therefore be named before reusing the same observed-data recursion. (Díaz et al., 2023)

independent fair bits, with no measured covariates. In both worlds A1 = U and the structural

actions can matter to a policy that modifies those proposals. Observing all outcome confounders
for ordinary assigned regimes does not automatically reveal that proposal mechanism. This
interpretation is a possible mechanism, not an assertion about a bank’s data. A proposal should
ask whether the natural recommendation can be recorded, whether its history-dependent
generation can be justified, and whether a randomized redraw is operationally acceptable. If
the business instead intends a fresh randomized allocation at every time, the stochastic target
may be exactly the intended one.

47.20.4     Deterministic histories must change the fitting population

A simulator can enforce a structural rule while estimating the stochastic part of that rule on the
wrong records. The selected pygformula documentation describes a covariate restriction as a
known value outside specified histories. It directs the fitting step to exclude those deterministic
histories, then directs simulation to assign their known value. For a bank, eligibility may make
a service-completion indicator identically zero: the completion model then describes eligible
accounts, rather than an average that includes accounts unable to receive the service. This is
a distinction between two conditional distributions, even before any causal effect is considered.
(pygformula contributors, 2026b)
   The documented input is a list of restrictions, each beginning with the covariate’s name. In
the selected fitting helper, the list used to identify restricted covariates repeats the whole first
restriction instead of extracting each name. Its subsequent membership test compares a name
with those lists, so the documented input does not enter the intended filtering branch. The
simulation helper does extract the names and assigns deterministic values on the corresponding
histories. The constructor forwards the supplied restrictions directly. Thus the two steps
implement different populations on this selected path; this conclusion follows from inspected
source, not from an executed package experiment. It is specific to this revision and schema,
and does not assert that every fitted model changes numerically. (pygformula contributors,
2026b)
  An exact constructed likelihood shows when the distinction matters. Let E indicate eligibility,
Z completion and X a binary predictor. At a post-baseline fitting time, each value of X has
ten eligible records, eight with Z = 1 and two with Z = 0, and ten ineligible records with the
known value Z = 0. Use the logistic model

                             logit Pr(Z = 1 | X, E = 1) = β0 + β1 X.

Restricting estimation to eligible records gives fitted probability 4/5 in both predictor cells.
Pooling the deterministic zeros gives 2/5 in both. For either fit, the intercept and slope scores
vanish at the corresponding constant probability; both cells contain successes and failures, and
the information matrix is positive definite. These are finite, unique likelihood solutions, not
an appeal to an intercept-only formula that the input parser might handle differently. The
predictor is deliberately uninformative within eligibility; no claim is made about a model that
separately represents every eligibility history. (Causal Banking Research Project, 2026c)
   If half the simulated accounts are eligible and the other half are correctly set to zero,
the completion mean is 2/5 under restricted estimation but 1/5 under the pooled fit. A
stipulated USD 100 receipt contingent on completion and USD 30 cost per account give
expected margins of USD 10 and USD −10. This is a contract-payoff calculation under an
invented law, not an estimated causal service benefit. Increasing simulation size converges
more precisely to the respective fitted laws; it cannot repair their different fitting populations.
The proposed worker should therefore retain the exact fitting row set for each transition, verify
deterministic assignments separately, and withhold this restriction profile until a corrected
native path reproduces the declared law. An explicitly fitted stochastic-subpopulation model
is a substantive alternative, provided that its conditioning histories and standardization
population remain visible. (Causal Banking Research Project, 2026c; pygformula contributors,
2026b)

47.20.5     Censoring labels and competing risks define different denominators

The input clock places covariates on interval k and the outcome on interval k + 1. The selected
outcome helper uses nonmissing outcomes at nonnegative times; with explicit competing
events it also requires no current competing event. A censoring-name argument is not passed
to that helper. The censoring model instead supports the observed natural-course inverse
probability weighted comparator. Supplying that model therefore does not automatically
select the outcome likelihood rows. The documentation permits missing or zero outcomes on
a survival censoring row under its stated fine-interval qualification; for an end-of-follow-up
outcome it requests a missing value. These conventions concern which event information is
observed, not merely how a missing field is displayed. (pygformula contributors, 2026b)
   For a deliberately coarse interval, suppose 100 accounts supply eight observed defaults,
72 observed nondefaults and 20 outcomes unknown after censoring. A constant outcome
likelihood over observed outcomes gives 8/80 = 1/10; coding the unknown outcomes zero
gives 8/100 = 2/25. A stipulated contract paying USD 10,000 on default and charging USD
900 has model-based margins USD 100 and USD −100, respectively. Neither choice alone
identifies the complete finite-population risk: the 20 unknown outcomes permit a proportion
between 2/25 and 7/25. Interpreting the observed-outcome fit as a target risk additionally
requires an appropriate conditional independent-censoring assumption, support and model
specification. This construction does not contradict a small-interval approximation; it shows
why that approximation must be justified before applying the convention to coarse monthly
data. (Causal Banking Research Project, 2026c; pygformula contributors, 2026b)
