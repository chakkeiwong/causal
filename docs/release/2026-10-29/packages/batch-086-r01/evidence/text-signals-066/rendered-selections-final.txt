Assistance should be selected by the task it improves. A system that answers questions about a
supplied causal mechanism has not thereby shown that it can discover a defensible mechanism

27.1      Mechanisms, timing and the interpretation of a diagram
A retention request can mean an invitation, acceptance, a price change or a repeated contact
rule. The distinction determines the population and intervention before any graph is evaluated.
Question clarification and graph critique are plausible assisted tasks in the inspected literature.
(Kıcıman et al., 2023; Zheng, 2026)
   Each proposed edge needs an economic interpretation, an information date and supporting
evidence. A graph with unresolved directions can already guide a decision when every credible
member implies the same action. Conversely, one wrong orientation can reverse a financial
sign even when most edges are correct. The mixed LACR retrieval results support testing the
retrieval choice itself. (Zhang et al., 2024b)
   Formal reasoning under a supplied mechanism and eliciting the mechanism of a new bank are
different tasks. CLadder and causal-learn+ motivate different comparisons on that distinction.
In a repeated policy, later observations cannot silently become pretreatment adjustment
variables. An unsupported calculation calls for an explanation of the missing information or a
revised question. (Jin et al., 2023; Zheng, 2026)

27.4     What repeated readings add to measurement
A bank may ask an assistant to recover financial distress from customer notes before assessing
the effect of a servicing action. Reading the same notes several times is attractive because
disagreement is easy to observe. Agreement can be less informative: several readers may share
the same omission. The distinction affects whether text can help control confounding. The
literature on text-based causal adjustment identifies measurement error, collider information
and post-treatment content as separate problems. (Keith et al., 2020)

27.6      What causal reasoning benchmarks establish
A banking analyst asks several different questions of an assistant. Can it translate a request for
higher deposits into an intervention and a measurable target? Can it distinguish information
known before an offer from information created by the offer? Can it explain which arrows in a
diagram are assumptions, and recognise when several diagrams fit the same observations? A
correct answer to a narrative puzzle is useful evidence about some of these abilities, but it
does not establish all of them.


27.6.1    Explanations deserve their own test

Wang and Shen examine three constructed scenarios: coin operations, interrupted file downloads
and a hypothetical vaccination calculation. Four variants of each scenario and three prompting
approaches give 36 prompt conditions. Their scoring distinguishes a correct result and
explanation, a flawed explanation, and an incorrect result. This is valuable because a correct
number can accompany a mistaken economic argument. The study reports mixed changes
from examples and guided reasoning, rather than a uniformly helpful prompting intervention.
(Wang and Shen, 2024)
   The terms used in a benchmark also need scrutiny. The paper calls reasoning from an
observed effect to a cause actual causality. That task description does not, by itself, specify a
formal criterion for an individual’s actual cause. Its smallpox case asks which source produces
more deaths in a stipulated population. With the given numbers, vaccination reactions
produce 990,000(0.01)(0.01) = 99 deaths, while disease among unvaccinated people produces
10,000(0.02)(0.20) = 40. Those population counts differ from the per-person risks, 0.0001
and 0.004. Confusing a total with a risk is an estimand error; the arithmetic alone does not
establish a general Simpson-reversal mechanism. These are deductions from the paper’s stated
example, not new medical evidence.
   The banking analogue is direct. A large customer group can contribute more defaults
while offering a smaller default risk per account. A deposit offer can increase gross balances
without increasing net contribution. An assistant should state the denominator and economic
target before presenting the answer. The paper’s prompts provide useful stress cases for that
distinction. The small scenario set and model configurations do not support a current ranking
of assistants for banking work. We have inspected the published methods and results; we have
not reproduced its model calls or inspected every supplementary response.

27.6.2    Scale does not remove uncertainty about the answer key

MDC-Bench broadens the comparison to 8,422 questions across seven disciplines, including
business, economics and management. Its tasks distinguish reasoning, intervention, decision
and counterfactual questions. Questions are constructed from causal narratives extracted from
preprints, with model-generated alternatives and a manual sampling check. The published
protocol identifies model versions, prompt templates, temperature and output limits. (Wang
et al., 2026, Sections 2–3 and Appendix A)
   Its harder condition requires two ranked answers instead of one. For example, the reported
Claude thinking configuration has average accuracy 84.56% in the basic condition and 59.25% in
the harder condition. These are different response requirements on the same benchmark; their
difference is not a measured deterioration in one fixed banking task. The benchmark is useful
for comparing errors by causal structure and question type, provided its answer-generation
assumptions remain visible.
   The detailed prompts reveal an important distinction. Reverse reasoning may use necessity
and sufficiency, or otherwise select an abductive explanation judged probable from background
knowledge. Intervention questions construct an altered causal narrative; the answer alternatives
are ranked by their judged plausibility. These procedures do not supply a known structural
equation system, a randomised treatment effect or an identified posterior over causal diagrams.
Appendix A’s sampling review helps detect poor questions but does not turn model-generated
causal statements into independent economic truth. The authors also discuss weak long-chain
relations and distractors that may be too easy. (Wang et al., 2026, Limitations; Tables 28–29
and 39–40)


27.6.3    An agent architecture still needs an identified effect

Chen combines language models, diffusion models and interacting agents in a study of climate-
policy sentiment. Section 6 describes 43,943 tweets and comparisons with topic, language
and diffusion baselines. It reports F1, AUC, causal-chain precision, counterfactual error and
simulated intervention outcomes. Table 4 removes individual components to examine their
contribution. These are reported results of the paper; the model was not reproduced locally.
(Chen, 2025, Sections 5.6 and 6; Tables 1–4)
   The causal interpretation needs more than those comparisons. Equation (16) factorises a

reported architecture is a research lead. Its stated improvements are not evidence that the
proposed banking service already delivers causal or commercial benefits.

27.6.4    A useful banking comparison

The proposal should use such benchmarks to diagnose formulation and reasoning failures, then
add tasks whose consequences can be checked independently. A compact questionnaire and an
executable calculation are necessary comparators. For a deposit question, all methods should
receive the same decision date, eligibility rule, alternative offer and contribution definition.
For servicing, all should see the same observed history and reasons for dropout. For a diagram,
an admissible set should remain admissible when the observations cannot orient an edge.
   The resulting assessment has distinct outcomes: whether the target is correct, whether a
proposed adjustment is justified, whether an unknown effect remains unknown, whether the
numerical calculation is correct, and how much analyst time the assistance saves. Independent
adjudication is needed for open-ended economic formulations. Generated cases with known
mechanisms can test specific errors before an expensive bank exercise. The public experimental,
servicing and linkage studies in this volume provide numerical cases of that kind, but no live
assistant has yet been evaluated on them.
  The inspected papers therefore sharpen the proposed experiment rather than establishing
a benefit from the proposed service. Repeated agreement among prompts is especially weak
evidence when their errors share a source, as the repeated-measurement calculation shows.
The useful next comparison measures consequential mistakes and time on held-out tasks, with
human adjudication and a fixed cost allowance.


27.7     A positive workflow result and its limits
The retained ORCA study gives a more direct reason to test assistance in a completed
workflow. Twelve participants used a counterbalanced within-subjects design; the baseline
assistant already had tools, step gates and minimal state. The paper’s Figure 5 reports total
task durations of 1,395 seconds for the baseline and 1,107 seconds for ORCA, a reported
reduction of about 21%. Table 1 reports completion rates 0.75 and 0.83. This is positive
published evidence for a structured workflow on the study’s relational, generated causal tasks.
It is not a measured benefit for this banking proposal. (Chung et al., 2026, pp. 3–5)
   The interpretation changes when the outcome definitions and all attempted analyses are
retained. Completion means producing an ATE, regardless of accuracy; discovery success
requires the treatment and outcome nodes to be present. The paper’s confidence-interval
metric is described as an estimated ATE falling within a ground-truth interval, so its reported
0.083 and 0.50 must not be presented as conventional repeated-sample confidence coverage.
The full-data condition comparison for absolute ATE error has p = 0.321; after excluding
configuration-error outliers, the reported OLS comparison has p = 0.020. Both belong in an
assessment of completed work because configuration errors are among the failures a service is
supposed to avoid. The abstract’s claimed 42-percentage-point completion gain also differs
from the displayed 0.75-to-0.83 comparison; this synthesis uses the stated table comparison
rather than reconciling that discrepancy by invention. (Chung et al., 2026, pp. 1, 5, 17–18)
   The proposal should therefore test whether shared intermediate results and question clari-
fication save full effort while preserving scientific quality. It should retain all configuration
failures and report separately the time spent by the analyst, tool execution and correction. The
external study supports that experiment’s plausibility. Its small participant sample, specified
tasks, outcome definitions and analysis exclusions prevent a claim that the same benefit is
established across bank studies.

formal procedure, requiring the assistant to preserve both the answer and its conditions. An
appropriate refusal must explain the missing restriction, not simply return an empty result.
The benchmark literature and the retained mechanism-clustering critiques give reasons to keep
failed cases and uncertainty denominators in the evaluation.
  For execution, the existing local interface study shows equality for four constructed requests,
not model-driven analysis quality. A scripted workflow is the credible baseline. Compare any
assistant on request construction, faithful tool use, errors detected and correction effort. Do
not give it credit for a calculation performed by the formal tool while omitting the costs of
choosing or correcting the request.
   For reporting, a deterministic template is a strong comparator because it can already state
the estimand, assumptions, interval and scope. The assistant’s proposed value is explaining
why the result changes a decision or why it cannot yet support one. Check that explanation

a positive recommendation fails even if its prose is persuasive. No local live-model evaluation
establishes an incremental benefit for this reporting task.

66.11      Linguistic measurement and executable interventions in
           banking
A language-assisted banking decision begins with an intervention that a bank can actually
implement. A complaint team may decide whether to rewrite an acknowledgement, request
clarification or escalate a case. An analyst may call the treatment politeness, yet this word
can denote the writer’s intention, the customer’s perception, a classifier score or a particular
editing procedure. These objects need not coincide. Their separation is necessary before an
agent translates the business question into a graph, proposes an adjustment set or calls an
estimator. A well-formed causal query about the wrong object remains the wrong business
answer.


66.11.1     Intent, perception, labels and actions

The inspected preprint of Pryzant et al. (2020) distinguishes writer intent T , reader perception
Te, a proxy label Tb and text W . Its first identification result assumes that W blocks the
relevant backdoor paths and that intent agrees with perception, T = Te. These are substantive
assumptions. A bank may intend reassurance while a distressed customer perceives evasion;
a classifier may score deferential wording highly even though the letter makes no useful
commitment. Neither a fluent explanation nor high predictive accuracy establishes agreement
between these variables.
   An operational treatment should identify an editing rule, the eligible message population, the
permissible information and the distribution of text versions produced by the rule. For example,
a rule might replace a discretionary closing paragraph with a fixed, reviewed clarification of the
next servicing step. It must specify whether facts, deadlines and promised remedies can change.
If these attributes vary together with tone, the resulting policy effect concerns the entire
rewriting procedure. Calling it an isolated effect of politeness requires further restrictions; the
proposed product should not silently impose them.
   The same distinction guides diagram construction. Pre-existing case severity can influence
both message selection and subsequent resolution. A customer response following the edited
message is downstream of that action, even if an agent can later extract it into a numerical
feature. Putting that response into a baseline adjustment set changes the question and can
invalidate identification. The assistant should retain a variable’s event time, observation time,
decision availability and semantic definition alongside its name. It can suggest candidate
arrows and competing explanations, while an explicit causal model states which claims are
assumed and what evidence could contradict them.
   The source’s linguistic formulation provides a conceptual foundation rather than a certificate
for an arbitrary embedding. Appendix A discusses the difficulty that a representation can
perfectly predict a proxy treatment, leaving no overlap for a within-representation comparison.

66.11.4     A conditional route from text to adjustment

experiments test the proposed representations under stated generators. Its consistency result
also requires sufficiently accurate nuisance models and a well-behaved estimator, with bounded
outcomes and overlap given as sufficient conditions in the discussion. Predictive language
performance alone does not verify these requirements in a bank.

66.11.5    Public complaints as a prototype resource

Returning to the text-rewriting question, the study by Pryzant et al. (2020) supplies two
distinct kinds of evaluation. Its Amazon setting constructs semi-synthetic outcomes, permitting
comparison within the stipulated outcome model. Its CFPB application uses observational
complaints and a fast-response indicator defined by a response in fewer than 15 days. It
matches complaints with opposite outcomes, selects 4,000 similar pairs and retains the upper
and lower quartiles of a politeness score, producing 4,000 selected complaints. These choices
define a selected study population, not the entire customer population. (Pryzant et al., 2020)
  The reported effect estimates should not be relabelled as known causal truth. Matching on
outcomes and restricting to score extremes require an explicit account of selection and of the
population to which a result applies. An agent-generated summary must preserve those choices
rather than quote a favourable estimate as a verified benefit of adopting a rewriting service.
Generalization to unselected complaints, contemporary servicing and repeated interactions
requires additional justification.
   For a proposal-stage prototype, public or generated data can establish useful facts at much
lower cost than a bank experiment. A frozen public-data snapshot can exercise event-time
parsing, missing-text handling, semantic label provenance and the distinction between response
time and substantive resolution. A generated outcome construction can test whether an
estimator recovers an effect under an explicit assignment and measurement law. An observed
complaint archive provides realistic input structure, while a stipulated generator supplies
known potential outcomes only within its model. Neither supplies otherwise unobserved

66.12      From business phrases to causal variables and defensible
           diagrams
A useful causal assistant must decide what the variables mean before it can reason about arrows.
In a servicing discussion, current borrower, delinquent borrower and financially distressed
borrower may denote values of different operational definitions, overlapping classifications or
measurements taken at different times. Automatically drawing one node for each phrase can
turn a vocabulary mistake into a scientific model. The first task is to propose a coherent
representation and preserve alternatives that cannot yet be resolved.

66.12.2    What the evaluation actually establishes

The source uses a within-subjects design in which seven named language models generate
examples and evaluate examples from themselves and other models. It sets temperature to
zero and uses the same English prompts. Its domain tables contain eight domains, including
finance. Task 1 uses 560 positive and 560 negative examples; Task 2 uses 559 positive and 560
negative examples. These are reported evaluation denominators, not independently audited
bank expert labels. (Razouk et al., 2024)
   The results illustrate why a single model ranking is insufficient. In the source’s Task 1
table, GPT-4-turbo has reported precision 97%, recall 41% and kappa 40%; rankings differ for
the second task. These historical labels and reported numbers describe that generated-data
evaluation. They are not a recommendation for a current provider, a service guarantee or
evidence that 97% of suggested banking variables are correct. High precision with low recall
can leave important distinctions unresolved. The cost of an omitted variable may differ greatly
from that of a false proposed merge. (Razouk et al., 2024)
   The paper explicitly states that its numbers derive from generated data and need comparison
with domain-specific expertise. Agreement with generation-derived labels can reward shared
conventions or mistakes. Cross-model evaluation broadens the comparison but does not
produce independently established causal truth. Temperature zero supplies neither calibrated
uncertainty nor an immutable service implementation. Domain dependence is a reason to
stratify later assessment by banking journey, variable type, language and ambiguity rather
than transport an overall score to every workflow.
