Chapter 1

From a confident answer to
uncertainty about a lending effect

A credit committee can ask an assistant to propose a causal diagram, attach probabilities to
its arrows and simulate a change in lending terms. Each step looks familiar. Together they
can create an assurance that none of the steps has earned: a narrow interval for the financial
consequence of a policy. The issue is not whether a language model can help an analyst. It is
whether the meaning of uncertainty survives the passage from an answer about an arrow to a
distribution over diagrams and then to an intervention effect.
   Molero Pérez’s master’s thesis supplies a concrete, unusually complete description of this
proposed passage. It combines language-assisted discovery, retrieval, three measures of answer
confidence, probabilistic graphs and causal normalizing flows. It evaluates pairwise direction
judgments on the Tübingen cause–effect pairs and a seven-variable medical application using
Framingham observations. The work is valuable as a workflow comparator: it asks how a
proposed graph changes the eventual intervention calculation. Its probability assignments and
evaluation target nevertheless need separate justification before a bank could use the resulting
intervals. (Molero Pérez, 2026, Sections 4.1–4.4)


1.1     What the complete workflow actually evaluates
The thesis compares self-reported confidence, answer-token probabilities and an information-
based score called HallBayes. In its final procedure, the last score supplies a quantity called
risk of hallucination, rij . An accepted arrow is assigned probability 1 − rij ; a rejected arrow
is assigned probability rij . Three runs are averaged. Each possible arrow is then drawn
independently as a Bernoulli variable, producing 100 candidate graphs. A topological sort
is used to obtain an ordering for a causal flow. (Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 44–50, equations
(14)–(19), Appendix D.3)
   For the medical example, the reference diagram is an expert-constructed diagram drawn
from the clinical literature. The thesis explicitly recognizes that the true graph of a complex
medical population is generally unknown. It trains 100 models for each of four arrangements:
the reference graph, the language-derived graphs, the reference ordering without adjacency
restrictions, and random orderings without adjacency restrictions. For each intervention–
outcome pair, it calculates effects from generated populations and takes the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles across the 100 models. The principal comparison is overlap between these intervals
and intervals from the fitted reference-graph models. (Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 52, 58–62)
  This design measures agreement with a specified fitted reference. It does not observe the

   The reported mean interval-overlap scores are 0.723 for estimated graphs, 0.712 for the
reference ordering alone, and 0.301 for random orderings. The first two differ by 0.011. These
are reported source results, not a replication in this proposal. The table does not supply a
paired uncertainty analysis establishing that the small difference is a general advantage of
language-derived graphs. Nor is the PC comparison a comparison of identical information:
the language procedure receives semantic and retrieved knowledge, while PC works from
observations. The thesis specifies chi-square independence tests while favouring continuous
clinical variables; the public implementation examined here does not reconstruct that full
comparison. Accordingly, the proposal retains the workflow and its measured target, without
importing a claim of general causal or commercial superiority. (Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 54–55,
77–79, Tables 10–11)
  The thesis itself recognizes consequential limitations. It reports that the HallBayes calibration
curve is not sufficiently good, describes the loss of valid arrows during refinement, and
acknowledges the unaddressed possibility of hidden common causes. Those admissions support
a measured reading of its contribution. The analysis below makes the corresponding probability
and decision requirements explicit rather than attributing an unconditional guarantee to every
part of the source. (Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 68–69 and 79)


1.2     A ceiling on success is not a probability of success
Write p for the probability of a correct answer to a specified binary question, q for a reference
probability, and
                                     p              1−p
                     d(p, q) = p log + (1 − p) log        ,     0 < q < 1,                   (1.1)
                                     q              1−q
using the usual continuous interpretation at p = 0 and p = 1. All logarithms in this treatment
are natural, so information is measured in nats. Suppose, for the moment, that a valid
information bound supplies d(p, q) ≤ ∆. Define

                           pmax (∆, q) = sup{u ∈ [0, 1] : d(u, q) ≤ ∆}.                       (1.2)

Then p ≤ pmax and hence
                                       1 − p ≥ 1 − pmax .                                     (1.3)
The complement is a lower bound on error. It is neither an upper bound on error nor an
estimate of the error probability. On printed page 47, following equation (16), the thesis calls
this complement an upper bound. That direction is inconsistent with its definition of pmax as
the highest feasible success probability. The inconsistency is visible in the original page; it is
not a text-extraction artefact. (Molero Pérez, 2026, p. 47, equations (15)–(16))
   For example, q = 0.1 and ∆ = 0.5 give pmax = 0.49505075. A success probability of 0.1
satisfies the inequality because d(0.1, 0.1) = 0. Its error probability is 0.9, which is above the
reported complement 0.50494925. Substituting the cap as the probability of an arrow would
turn a feasible upper limit into a point belief without additional evidence. The numerical root
was checked with both bisection and an independent bracketing solver.

   There is a second issue before even this direction argument applies. The thesis’s equation (14)
averages clipped positive log-probability changes for the selected answer after masking parts of
its prompt. The original information inequality concerns an expectation of divergence between
full distributions, with a specified event and reference. A selected answer’s log-probability
change is not that expectation merely because both quantities are measured in nats. Clipping,
selection of the answer and the meaning of the correctness event all need to be accounted for.
(Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 46–47)


1.3     What the original information inequality does supply
The first version of Chlon and colleagues’ paper appeared in September 2025 under the title
Predictable Compression Failures: Why Language Models Actually Hallucinate. Its June 2026
third version has a narrower title and an explicit emphasis on evidence-grounded binary
adjudication. Both versions are available before the survey cutoff. Their substantive differences
must be retained: the later version requires a specified verification primitive or calibrated
verifier, and places unrestricted generation without one outside its stated regime. (Chlon et al.,
2025, 2026)

The first inequality follows from convexity in the second argument. The second follows by
reducing the outcome to the indicator of A. Equivalently, partition the likelihood ratio over A
and its complement and apply the log-sum inequality on each part. This supplies a necessary
information requirement for a given event probability. Equality in the second step needs no
remaining distinction within either part. Equality in the full chain also needs equality in the
mixture step; an exponential tilt relative to S alone does not remove variation among the Sk .
(Chlon et al., 2025, 2026, Theorem 5 and Appendix A.4)

outcome space, take

                S = (0.05, 0.05, 0.001, 0.899),    P = (0.05, 0.05, 0.899, 0.001),           (1.5)

The first quantity exceeds the proposed information requirement for 95% success by a factor
of about 3.06, yet success remains 10%. The information distinguishes outcomes within failure;
it does not establish success. This is a project counterexample to treating the necessary
full-distribution budget as a sufficient guarantee. It is not a re-analysis of the paper’s reported
experiments.
  The third version computes a Bernoulli divergence for a verifier-relative predicate. This
avoids the particular within-failure variation in (1.5). Direction still matters: d(0.05, 0.5) =
d(0.95, 0.5), so a minimum-divergence threshold alone cannot distinguish 5% from 95% success.
Restricting p to the increasing branch p ≥ q, using an exact binary divergence and knowing
what the reference probability means would remove this particular ambiguity. These are real
conditions, not consequences of a confident answer. A calibrated verifier would itself require

validation for the relevant predicate and population. (Chlon et al., 2026, Sections 3.5–3.6,
Algorithm 1 and Appendix A.5)
   For a bank, verifying that a document contains a statement is different from verifying that
the statement identifies a causal arrow. A successful test of quotation or logical consistency can
support the former without supplying the latter. The practical recommendation is to use such
scores as disclosed diagnostics for evidence review, and to evaluate any decision threshold on a
separate, adjudicated set of the actual questions it will handle. A numerical threshold should
not be passed downstream as a calibrated graph probability unless that stronger interpretation
has been established.


1.4     Independent arrows can describe an impossible joint belief
Even defensible marginal probabilities need not define a distribution over legal causal diagrams.
Consider only the possible arrows A → B, B → C and C → A, each assigned probability 4/5
and sampled independently. The probability of drawing the complete directed cycle is

                             Pr(cycle) = (4/5)3 = 64/125 = 0.512.                             (1.7)

Topological sorting detects this cycle; it does not make the graph acyclic. If cyclic draws are
discarded, the marginal probability of any one retained arrow becomes

               Pr(A → B | acyclic) =           3
                                                   =    ≃ 0.59016,       p = 4/5.             (1.8)
                                         1−p         61
Thus rejection changes the probabilities. Deleting an arrow from each cycle also changes the
distribution and requires a specified rule.
   More fundamentally, no distribution supported on DAGs can retain all three marginals
at 0.8. Every acyclic realization contains at most two of these arrows. Taking expectations
therefore gives
                        Pr(A → B) + Pr(B → C) + Pr(C → A) ≤ 2,                         (1.9)
whereas the proposed sum is 2.4. Enumeration of all eight arrow subsets verifies both the
conditional probabilities and this bound. This construction shows what a general graph-
sampling design must handle. It does not assert that the thesis’s seven-variable example
actually generated a cycle. Its stated topological procedure checks whether all vertices are
ordered; the inspected description does not establish a general repair that preserves the original
arrow probabilities. (Molero Pérez, 2026, pp. 49–50)

To prove this, if the true graph is retained and its own set covers the effect, their union covers
it. Failure is contained in the union of those two failure events. No independence between
them is required.
   This elementary guarantee identifies the work that a prototype must do. An interval justified
only for a graph fixed before looking at the data does not automatically satisfy the effect
condition after graph selection. One route uses a simultaneous family of intervals over a fixed
candidate class; another uses an independent sample for effect estimation, subject to the
relevant assumptions. Sample splitting does not repair a false causal model. If a retained graph
does not identify the effect, its Cg must represent the justified bounds or remain uninformative.
Dropping that graph because it widens the answer would spend unaccounted graph error.
   In the two-world calculation, the class is fixed and contains both graphs, so αG = 0. The
reverse graph gives the exact set {0}; the forward graph gives the ordinary slope interval. This
explains the second row of Table 1.1. In a real application the hard part is establishing the
retention and estimation conditions. A language score alone supplies neither. The method is
therefore a concrete proposal route with explicit research obligations, not a claim that general
calibrated language-assisted causal inference has been solved.


1.7     What to adopt, what to test and what to decline
The public repository linked by the thesis is a PyWhy-LLM adaptation. Its 2 February
2026 revision contains the language suggestion and retrieval components, pairwise notebooks,
declared dependencies and an MIT notice carrying the PyWhy copyright. The inspected
complete tree and selected notebooks do not contain the thesis’s HallBayes integration, Monte
Carlo graph construction or causal-flow training pipeline. They support reuse assessment
of the front end, not independent reproduction of all reported results. Its retrieval helper
also creates its own language-model client and falls back to an answer without retrieved
evidence when no match is found. A bank adapter should pass the selected model explicitly

and retain the absence of evidence as an observable condition. The proposal should integrate
the front-end ideas through a small, versioned interface and prefer maintained upstream
components where their scope matches; porting the incomplete repository wholesale offers
no demonstrated advantage. Preserve applicable notices and review dependencies and data
permissions separately. (Molero Pérez, Alba María and PyWhy contributors, 2026)
   The HallBayes repository at the latest inspected pre-cutoff revision presents Berry, an
evidence-support service with command-line and MCP interfaces. Its README and source
distinguish cited-evidence support from world truth. That distinction fits a useful role in
checking an assistant’s attributed claims. The June 2026 revision is not the December 2025
implementation cited by the thesis. A date-bounded query of the current default-branch history
returned no commit for that earlier date, while a retained version-one tag points to February
2026. This does not prove the older software never existed; it prevents silently treating the
current service as the thesis’s exact dependency. No downloaded source was executed in this
comparison. (Hassana Labs and contributors, 2026)
   The decision is consequently specific. Adopt the separation between evidence retrieval,
graph construction and intervention calculation. Evaluate quotation and evidence-support
checks for their own stated predicates. Decline the direct conversion of an information ceiling
into calibrated edge probabilities, and decline unqualified confidence-interval language for
the spread of fitted-model effects. Before a prototype makes stronger claims, it needs an
authentic complete implementation, a declared joint graph law or retention procedure, the
relevant calibration evidence, and public or synthetic experiments with known interventions and
matched information across comparators. Bank validation can follow when those experiments
justify its cost.


1.8     A survey source that cannot support the proposed com-
        parison
A separate institutional thesis, Large Language Models for Causal Inference, was recovered
from the University of Alcalá. Its identity is clear, but its survey and productivity estimates
cannot presently support the proposal. It reports both ten and eighty included studies in
different places. Two arXiv identifiers assigned to causal-language studies in Appendix A
resolve to papers on Toeplitz determinants and a smart-building security testbed. The named
reproducibility repository currently returns HTTP 404. These are specific, independently
checkable discrepancies; they do not establish intent or prove that a repository never existed.
(Ait Lhouss, 2025, Section 4.1, Tables 5–6 and 9, Section 5.4.3, Appendix A)
  The source is retained for identity and exclusion transparency. Its reported accuracy, time
savings and independent trials are not used as evidence. An authentic corrected study inventory
and the underlying trial records could change that disposition. This exclusion also illustrates a
practical requirement for a causal research assistant: locating a plausible citation is not enough.
The linked original must support the particular claim before that claim enters a commercial
comparison or an investment case.
