## C++ acceleration must retain panel semantics

The C++ comparison follows selected fixest kernels together with their R
callers and a dated xhdfe development profile. Residualization, rank, row
ordering, covariance scaling and unavailable inference are part of the
contract. A port that copies only a numerical kernel can lose those meanings.
The proposed initial fixest route remains a typed worker preserving the
original package's semantics. [@exec25_panelcxx_fixest; @exec20_fixest_source]

xhdfe supplies explicit cluster, convergence, selection and covariance-repair
fields, which make it a useful public-profile candidate. Static inspection
also motivates a full-matrix covariance test: projecting a full matrix and
then replacing a separately projected slope block is not generally guaranteed
to preserve positive semidefiniteness. Our exact counterexample has determinant
-3/4 and a contrast variance of -3. It does not prove that this output is
attainable in the package, and no native failure was observed. Reachability,
scientific coverage and cost remain separate tests.
[@exec25_panelcxx_xhdfe; @exec25_panelcxx_reference]

Four further leads are retained separately: SDID, capybara, gfe and NNRPanel.
Their dated scope and dependency declarations differ; implementation and
underlying scholarly claims remain to be inspected. The gfe license-field
placeholder conflicts with the root MIT text and needs resolution. Root
licenses do not clear full distributions. [@exec25_panelcxx_sdid; @exec25_panelcxx_capybara; @exec25_panelcxx_gfe; @exec25_panelcxx_nnr]

The next public trials use known weighted projections, unsupported treatment
targets, panel gaps, cluster limits and covariance contrasts. An illustrative
USD 3,600 costs USD 30 per 120 submissions or USD 45 per 80 usable results.
No runtime, price or investment superiority is claimed from repository
documentation. [@exec25_panelcxx_reference]
