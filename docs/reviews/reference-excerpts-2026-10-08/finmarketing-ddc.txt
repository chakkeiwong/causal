A logit probability can resemble a softmax exploration policy without having
the same economic interpretation. Here its randomness comes from the agent's
utility shocks; an exploration policy instead randomizes the learner's action.
An entropy-regularized control problem can reproduce the integrated operator
when reward, scale and information agree, but applying softmax to an ordinary
unregularized $Q$ does not establish that correspondence.

Neither field is defined by state dimension or function approximator. Finite
MDPs can use tabular values; structural models can use flexible approximations.
Model-based RL may estimate transitions, while model-free methods may estimate
action values from samples. Strategic responses by several decision makers
require the appropriate game or interaction model in either vocabulary.
Household utility and bank contribution remain separate objectives, even when
they depend on the same transactions.

\section{NFXP: model solution inside likelihood estimation}
\label{sec:ddc-nfxp}

Nested fixed-point estimation is associated with the empirical dynamic-choice
work of \citet{rust1987optimal}. Its computational formulation associates a
solved decision model with each trial parameter vector.
\citet[Section~2.2, equations~2.5--2.8]{rust2000nfxpmanual} describes the
likelihood and fixed-point relation in his implementation manual. In the
centered logit case,
hold the trial parameters fixed and solve the customer's problem.
Replace the old continuation values by updated ones until they agree to
the chosen tolerance. This inner iteration is
\begin{equation}
 \bar V^{(k+1)}(s)=\tau\log\sum_a\exp\left\{
 \frac{u(a,s;\theta)+\beta\int\bar V^{(k)}(s')\,dF(s'\mid a,s;\theta)}{\tau}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:ddc-vi}
\end{equation}
The outer calculation maximizes the appropriate likelihood using the implied
choice probabilities. Solver error, statistical identification and likelihood
optimization are separate diagnostic questions.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Contraction under the declared primitives]
On a finite state and action space with a stochastic transition matrix and
fixed $0<\beta<1$, the operator in \cref{eq:ddc-vi} is a contraction of
modulus $\beta$ in the supremum norm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If two value arrays differ by at most $d$, every action's discounted
continuation differs by at most $\beta d$. The log-sum-exp function is
monotone and shifts by $c$ when every input shifts by $c$. Bounding all
inputs above and below by that common shift bounds the resulting values by
$\beta d$. The same reasoning applies to the deterministic maximum.
\end{proof}

An action-value calculation fixes the current action, then chooses the
best future action at each possible next state. Integrating averages those
future values over the transition law. For deterministic rewards and no
omitted shocks, the action-value operator is
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal TQ)(s,a)=u(a,s;\theta)
       +\beta\int\max_{a'}Q(s',a')\,dF(s'\mid a,s;\theta).
 \label{eq:ddc-q-operator}
\end{equation}
In the integrated logit model, replace the maximum by
$\tau\log\sum_{a'}\exp\{Q(s',a')/\tau\}$. Full model-based iteration
computes the transition expectation. Tabular Q-learning instead uses sampled
transitions and stochastic-approximation updates to selected entries, with
its own step-size and visitation conditions. Shared fixed-point structure
does not make NFXP identical to Q-learning. Its outer objective remains
likelihood, not policy return.

\section{CCP estimation and the information in choice probabilities}
\label{sec:ddc-ccp}

Conditional-choice-probability methods use first-stage choice probabilities
within structural estimation \citep{hotz1993ccp}. Under the declared logit
model and positive probabilities, the common logit denominator cancels
in a probability ratio. Taking logs then reveals the utility-value difference,
in units set by $\tau$. Taking their ratio gives
\begin{equation}
 v(a,s)-v(a_0,s)=\tau\{\log P(a\mid s)-\log P(a_0\mid s)\}.
 \label{eq:ddc-hm-inversion}
\end{equation}
This is a difference in action values. Adding a common constant to every
$v(a,s)$ leaves the probabilities unchanged, so inversion alone cannot
recover the value level, current utility, or a discount factor.

For a finite state space, the extra continuation calculation can be written
explicitly. Average transitions and flow utility using the choice
probabilities. A further term accounts for the utility shocks attached to
the choices actually selected. Set $F_P(s,s')=\sum_a P(a\mid s)F(s'\mid a,s)$,
$u_P(s)=\sum_a P(a\mid s)u(a,s)$, and
$H(P_s)=-\sum_a P(a\mid s)\log P(a\mid s)$. Substituting the logit formula
into $\bar V$ gives a familiar present-value system: current expected
utility, including the selected shock, plus discounted continuation under
the choice-induced transition matrix. Bringing continuation to the left gives
\begin{equation}
 (I-\beta F_P)\bar V=u_P+\tau H(P).
 \label{eq:ddc-ccp-evaluation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\bar V=\sum_aP(a\mid s)v(a,s)+\tau H(P_s)$, and substituting
the continuation expression for $v$ yields the equation. The inverse exists
under the finite-state discounted assumptions because
$(I-\beta F_P)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}\beta^kF_P^k$.

The entropy term represents the expected selected shock under the centering
convention. Omitting it produces the value of a different decision problem.
For fixed transitions, probabilities and discounting, this calculation is
linear in utility coefficients when utility is linear in those coefficients.
General nonlinear utility, unknown transitions or discounting, normalizations
and identification restrictions still require their own estimation argument.
CCP inversion is not a generic linear least-squares recovery of all parameters.

Sparse cells, probabilities near zero, misspecified shocks, omitted states,
and error in estimated transitions can make the inversion unreliable.
First-stage estimation uncertainty enters the structural uncertainty. A
non-logit shock law requires its appropriate invertibility argument and
calculation; a closed-form logit inverse is not a universal requirement of
all CCP methods. Their computational advantage must be assessed for the
actual model and data rather than assigned a fixed runtime claim.

\begin{bankbox}{What choice probabilities identify}
Suppose a bank observes customer choices sufficiently well to estimate their
log odds. Those odds can constrain relative household action values under
the maintained shock model. They still leave a common utility level
unresolved and do not supply the bank's net contribution. The fitted finite example in \cref{sec:structural-worked} makes the
customer-choice and bank-contribution distinction numerical. The linear
calculation in \cref{eq:ddc-ccp-evaluation} provides a separate way to
reconstruct an integrated value under the same primitives. More precise choice frequencies cannot repair
an omitted financial target or utility normalization.
\end{bankbox}
