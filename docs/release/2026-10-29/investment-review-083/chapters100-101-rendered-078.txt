Chapter 100

Banking decisions, review effort and
the value of assistance

The preceding studies identify what information and computation can establish. This chapter
asks what those capabilities are worth under an explicit decision rule. It develops information
value, complete workflow cost and evaluation design before examining selective automation,
retry mechanisms, ambiguity, staged investment and transport to a bank’s requested cases.
The numerical accounts are stipulated examples that expose the measurements needed for an
actual investment decision.


100.1       Value belongs to a decision and an information set
The value of an agent is the change it makes to a feasible decision after including its information,
review and operating costs. Producing a longer report, drawing more edges or executing a
notebook faster can help that decision, but none is the economic target itself. The banking
application must specify the action, baseline workflow, affected population, financial horizon
and loss before comparing systems.
   Let a range over feasible actions and let V (a, θ) be their monetary value under a causal
state θ. An information set I might contain observed data, authenticated design records and
unresolved alternatives. If a justified probability law for θ is available, an expected-value
decision solves
                               a∗ (I) ∈ arg max E{V (a, θ) | I}.
                                               a

This expression is a decision rule conditional on a probability model. A language model’s
self-reported certainty, the frequency of repeated answers, or a fraction of retrieved documents
is not automatically that probability. The distinction matters most when a small change in
the assumed probability changes a lending, servicing or funding action.
   If the evidence instead supports a set Θ(I) without probabilities, the bank needs an explicit
ambiguity policy. For example, a robust rule can maximize inf θ∈Θ(I) V (a, θ). Expected value
and this robust criterion answer different decision questions. The software must preserve the
information needed for either and require the policy to be named. It must not manufacture
a distribution by assigning equal weight to whichever graphs an algorithm happened to
enumerate.

100.2      An exact example of useful and costly information
Consider a stipulated one-customer intervention whose incremental gross return is either
CNY 0 or CNY 400, with intervention cost CNY 300. Suppose the two states have equal prior
probability. These are project assumptions for an arithmetic example, not bank estimates,
elicited probabilities or measured model performance. Acting has expected net return 200 −
300 = −100, so the unassisted expected-value decision is to do nothing, with value zero.
   Perfect information would permit action only in the CNY 400 state. Its expected gross
value relative to doing nothing is therefore

                         2 max(0, 0 − 300) + 2 max(0, 400 − 300) = 50.
                         1                   1


For this action set and loss, no information service can justify an expected payment above
CNY 50 per decision solely through improved action selection. The bound would change if the
service had other validated benefits or changed the feasible actions; neither is assumed here.
   Now stipulate a binary signal that reports “positive” with probability 0.9 in the high-return
state and 0.1 in the low-return state. Each signal outcome has marginal probability 0.5. Bayes’
rule gives posterior high-state probabilities 0.9 after a positive signal and 0.1 after a negative
signal. The conditional net returns from acting are respectively

                       0.9(400) − 300 = 60,            0.1(400) − 300 = −260.

The optimal rule acts only after a positive signal. Its gross expected value is 0.5(60) = 30. An
independent calculation keeps the loss from false positives visible:

                               0.5(0.9)(100) − 0.5(0.1)(300) = 30.
                               |     {z       }    |       {z       }
                              true-positive gain   false-positive loss

The information value is below the perfect-information bound, as it should be.
   Suppose obtaining and checking the signal costs CNY 2 in computation and CNY 8 in
review for every request, including negative ones. The incremental net value is then CNY 20.
Under the additional simplifying assumptions of identical expected value, linear costs and no
discounting, a CNY 50,000 fixed development cost breaks even at 50,000/20 = 2,500 decisions.
These figures are reproduced with exact rational arithmetic. They are a transparent planning
construction, not a price quotation or an ROI estimate for the implemented prototype.
   The threshold for acting is a high-state probability above 300/400 = 0.75. An assistant
that says “90% confident” can cross that threshold without having a calibrated probability.
Review must establish the meaning and reliability of the signal. A syntactically valid report, a
correct posterior formula and an accurate multiplication cannot compensate for an invented
likelihood.


100.3      Refusal can preserve value, but it also consumes re-
           sources
If all that is defensible is the set Θ = {0, 400}, robust net value from acting is −300, while doing
nothing guarantees zero incremental value. The robust rule therefore declines the intervention.
This conclusion differs from a claim that the true effect is zero. It expresses how the bank
chooses under unresolved uncertainty and the stated loss.
  A service that exposes the ambiguity can be valuable if the incumbent workflow would act
on a falsely precise estimate. The evaluation must include that incumbent behavior explicitly.

Against an already cautious baseline, the same refusal may add little decision value while still
costing analyst time. A refusal is neither automatically a failure nor automatically a benefit.
  The inspected observational workflow illustrates this problem through critic declines under
poor overlap (Chou et al., 2026b). The denominator must include the declined requests, their
review cost and what happened next. Did a reviewer obtain a better design, narrow the
population with explicit authorization, commission an experiment, or simply lose time? Each
path has a different economic implication. Changing to a selected population must also change
the target rather than make the original request appear successful.


100.4      Four banking journeys require different evidence
For a funding decision, assistance can assemble the rate action, balance units, margin horizon
and policy constraints. It can also flag a mismatch between calendar covariance and an
assumed independent sampling unit. Value arises only if these corrections improve a decision
or avert an error relative to the baseline. A conservative interval wider than the bank’s action
threshold may be scientifically defensible and economically inconclusive.
   For sequential servicing, the central object is a policy adapted to information available at
each decision. The total bill includes state measurement, assignment logging, intervention
delivery and customer outcomes, as well as model calls. An agent can help describe a dynamic
regime, but a static ATE translator cannot evaluate it by substituting a single treatment
indicator. The economic study must compare genuinely feasible policies under common
information and count unsuccessful or unavailable evaluations.
   For discovery followed by intervention, a visually simpler graph can conceal alternative
effects. The useful information is whether additional evidence changes the set of financially
acceptable actions. More accurate recovery of irrelevant edges may have little value for the
selected decision. Conversely, one mistaken orientation near the treatment or outcome can
reverse a policy recommendation. Graph accuracy and decision loss should both be reported,
with neither used as a proxy for the other.
   For trustworthy observations, a language system can reconcile field names and suggest
ownership or inclusion questions. The bank must still establish actual linkage, label and audit
truth. Reviewing a source dictionary may prevent a semantic error; it does not supply sampled
identity labels or independent calendars. The cost of obtaining those scientific inputs belongs
in the information budget, and their absence remains a legitimate stop condition for an effect
calculation.

For request i, a prospective accounting identity is
           Ci = Ci,model + Ci,retrieval + Ci,numeric + Ci,review + Ci,data + Ci,operation .
The terms represent distinct resource uses, not independently measured values in the current
project. Model cost includes all calls and retries. Retrieval includes acquisition and any
permitted indexing. Numerical cost includes the complete fit, uncertainty calculation and
serialization. Review includes time spent on wrong, refused and unsupported answers. Data
cost includes audits and additional information. Operations include storage, recovery and
support.
 A natural-workflow comparison measures the quality and cost of complete workflows. A

   The experimental unit must also match the cost and outcome calculation. Repeated prompts

100.6      What can be concluded before a live evaluation
The local controller demonstrates preservation of declarations, alternatives, refusals and
numerical set topology in selected authored examples. It makes zero live model calls. Its
measured interface behavior cannot reveal a provider’s accuracy, token use, price or review
burden on unfamiliar questions. Those fields remain unavailable rather than being filled with
zeros.
   Published benchmarks support the design of a prospective evaluation. They cannot be
combined across different model revisions, task distributions and information budgets into
a bank ROI estimate. CausalDS in particular reports divergence between symbolic success,
interval quality, abstention and cost, which supports measuring them separately (Leban and
Sun, 2026b).
   The next empirical step requires a specified backend and custody arrangement, actual prices
and budget, independent held-out mechanisms, adjudicators and qualified numerical baselines.
For bank adoption it additionally requires feasible actions, authentic records and accepted
loss thresholds. Until those inputs exist, exact decision examples can check the accounting
and expose threshold errors. They cannot establish that assistance crosses an institutional
investment threshold. The following cost calculation makes the review and error-loss tradeoff
explicit, before the selective-automation examples show why cost and quality must be evaluated
over every requested decision.


100.7      Full cost and the investment threshold
Cost is an accounting identity before it is a performance result. For request i, retain input,
output and cache tokens with their actual dated prices; numerical compute; retrieval and
storage; tool retries; analyst review and rework; escalation; and any failed-run charges. Add
fixed integration, security and maintenance costs separately. Unmeasured components remain
missing. Token counts alone cannot establish either dollar savings or cost per usable causal
decision.
   An explicitly illustrative price scenario makes the arithmetic concrete. An ordinary review
takes 30 minutes at USD 60/hour plus USD 1 compute, for USD 31/request. Assisted review
takes 10 minutes at the same rate, USD 2 tools, 30,000 input tokens at USD 2/million and
5,000 output tokens at USD 8/million, USD 0.20 retrieval and USD 0.20 expected retry charges.
The assumed token cost is USD 0.10. If 20% of requests additionally require a 30-minute
escalation, expected escalation cost is USD 6 and total assisted cost is USD 18.50. These
prices, times and escalation frequencies are scenario assumptions, not provider quotations or
measured productivity.
  The resulting variable saving is USD 12.50/request before incremental error losses and fixed

costs. With USD 12,500 fixed cost, 1,000 requests reach arithmetic break-even only if all
those assumptions hold and decision quality is otherwise unchanged. If the assisted method
increases a USD 10,000-loss error by 0.1 percentage points per request, the added expected loss
is USD 10 and net saving falls to USD 2.50, requiring 5,000 requests to recover the same fixed
cost. At a 0.2 percentage-point increase, variable net value is already negative. With all other
inputs fixed and no added error loss, escalation probability must be below 37/60 ≈ 61.67% for
positive variable savings. These sensitivities explain why an inexpensive-looking model call is
not a business case. (Causal banking proposal, 2026p)
   The prospective analysis should compare paired decision loss plus cost per requested case,
as well as cost per valid result. The latter ratio needs its numerator and denominator reported;
selective refusal can improve the ratio while reducing service. In bank data the relevant error
loss may be unknown, so the proposal can report break-even surfaces and identify which
uncertainty a prototype should resolve. It must not assign invented observed losses to satisfy
an ROI gate.


100.7.1     A study that can be registered after a prototype

The proposed evaluation protocol is a reviewed prospective design, not a registered or executed
study. Its 400 mechanisms, four label/evidence conditions and three repetitions are a planning
allocation. They would generate 4,800 requests per workflow arm, but the independent unit
is the mechanism and repetitions/conditions are nested. For a single Bernoulli proportion,
400 independent observations give an approximate worst-case 95% normal half-width of 0.049.
For a paired difference bounded in [−1, 1], the worst-case normal half-width can be 0.098.
An elementary distribution-free bounded-mean calculation at half-width 0.05 and failure
probability 0.05 requires at least 2,952 independent mechanisms. To see this, the second
derivative of the log moment-generating function of a variable in [−1, 1] is a tilted variance
at most one. Integrating twice gives log E exp{λ(D − ED)} ≤ λ2 /2. Independence and the
exponential Markov inequality then give P (|D̄ − E D̄| ≥ ϵ) ≤ 2 exp(−nϵ2 /2). Solving for n
yields the stated conservative count. This calculation does not choose a final sample size or
justify normal inference in every stratum. (Causal banking proposal, 2026p)
   First use public, development-only examples to measure feasibility and costs for a numerical
baseline, an ordinary analyst workflow, a single assistant, retrieval assistance and a hybrid
numerical assistant. Add debate only as a separate ablation. Cross unfamiliar labels with
held-out mechanism families, misleading retrieval, clock violations, proxy measurement and
nonidentification. Keep an equal-information comparison separate from an equal-resource
comparison; a system receiving a scientific source or more numerical fitting has a different
information or compute budget. For a human study, assign cases and workflow order to avoid
the learning/selection confounding illustrated by CausalChat, and include review, adjudication
and rework time.
   Use the development pilot to choose decision-relevant tolerances, feasible budgets and a
justified final allocation. Before a final run, bind provider/model revision, actual settings, pack-
age/container identities, independent holdout and truth, retrieval corpus, human adjudication
protocol, prices, stopping rules and the analysis script. Freeze paired mechanism-level analyses
and the multiplicity policy. Retain every planned attempt, including malformed, unsupported,
timed-out and unavailable requests. Quality thresholds concern target/clock errors, unjustified
causal claims, calibration where eligible, justified refusal, decision loss and full cost; a single
composite score cannot establish all of them. These are future prototype/experiment inputs,
not reasons to block this literature-and-design proposal.
  The immediate investment is a narrow prototype using public data and stipulated mecha-

100.8      Selective automation must be valued over requested
           decisions
An assisted workflow chooses both an answer and whether to act on it. These choices change
the population on which accuracy is reported. Let Ri = 1 mean that request i receives an
automated recommendation and Ri = 0 mean that it is referred to a fallback. Let ai and
bi denote the respective actions, θi the decision-relevant state, and ℓ(a, θ) the loss relative
to an explicitly specified feasible comparator. If Ci includes all work before referral, Hi the
additional fallback cost and Di the loss caused by delay, a complete per-request accounting
variable is
                     Zi = Ci + Ri ℓ(ai , θi ) + (1 − Ri ){Hi + Di + ℓ(bi , θi )}.
Loss is monetary here only because the example stipulates a monetary mapping. Legal
feasibility, access to assistance and other constraints can instead restrict the allowed actions;
they should not be silently converted into an invented dollar penalty. A comparison estimates
the change in Zi over the same requested population, including requests on which either
workflow fails. An unanswered request has a subsequent action or an unresolved consequence,
not a disappearing row. (Causal banking proposal, 2026ag)
   Consider 100 generated offer decisions: 80 with a USD 100 loss for a wrong action and
20 with a USD 10,000 loss. Workflow A answers all 80 low-consequence requests and ten
high-consequence requests. It makes one error in each group. Workflow B answers 60 low-
consequence and ten high-consequence requests, making three errors in the first group and
none in the second. A’s conditional answer accuracy is 88/90, higher than B’s 67/70. These
are invented counts that define the example, not estimates of either a model or a bank’s error
rate.
  Assume A costs USD 2 and B costs USD 4 for every requested decision. Each referral costs
another USD 40 and resolves the action correctly in this particular construction. Delay costs
USD 5 for a low-consequence referral and USD 50 for a high-consequence referral. The full
accounting is
                      ZAtotal
                              = 200 + 10,100 + 400 + 500 = 11,200,
                           total
                          ZB     = 400 + 300 + 1,200 + 600 = 2,500.
B is less accurate among its answers and automates fewer requests, yet has USD 8,700 less
total loss and cost in the constructed portfolio. The fallback is assumed correct, so these
totals are optimistic about review; allowing review errors adds their losses through the same

   A missing identification method, inconsistent units, inaccessible evidence or an ambiguous
causal target can behave like the permanent component. A new prompt does not necessarily
change the missing information. Independent retry arithmetic would substantially understate
terminal workload in that case. The proposed agent should classify the failure, retain the failed
request and route to an appropriate remedy: repair a format, select a supported method, seek
evidence or return an unresolved decision. A retry budget limits spending; it does not establish
that repeated reasoning supplies independent evidence. The classification itself should be
checked because a mistaken “transient” label can generate costly repetition.
   CausalDS reports marked differences in tool-use strategy and total tokens, alongside tool-
output-management failures and distinct content and abstention outcomes. Its reported ATE
interval coverage uses only seven eligible cases per model, or five for one model, in that
particular exam. These scoped results support retaining trajectories and component outcomes.
They do not supply the permanent-failure fraction of a banking workflow, and total tokens do
not determine dollars without dated input/output/cache prices and the relevant billing rules.
The prototype should measure the actual sequence of attempts, which input changed between
them, and the eventual fallback. No named service price or bank runtime is assumed here.
(Leban and Sun, 2026b)


100.10       Causal ambiguity needs a declared decision rule
The funding example in Section 80.17 maps a supplied response set through a stated profit
function and obtains net values from minus USD 625 to plus USD 1,625. Holding the rate fixed
has zero incremental value in that example. To examine the decision rule, temporarily treat
those endpoints as a declared ambiguity set containing the true state; this is a conditional
decision construction. If the set came from a confidence procedure, its coverage uncertainty
remains an additional issue. It is not a posterior probability law over the possible profits.
  A robust net-value rule holds because the worst net value from acting is negative. A
deterministic minimax-regret rule acts. To see the difference, define regret in state θ as

                             R(a, θ) =      max         V (b, θ) − V (a, θ).
                                         b∈{hold,act}

Acting has worst regret USD 625, whereas holding has worst regret USD 1,625. Robust value
protects the attained payoff; minimax regret protects the shortfall relative to what could have
been chosen with state knowledge. Neither rule is deduced from the graph or interval alone.
They express different preferences for the same scientifically unresolved funding question.
   If randomizing the entire action were an admissible policy, choose action with probability

100.11       A public prototype buys an option to make a later
             investment
A proposal need not know bank ROI before it can justify a small prototype. It does need an
account of which later decision the prototype could change. Let G be the eventual net value
of constructing a selected capability, after all subsequent implementation and operating costs
in a declared horizon. The choice today is to build or decline. A pilot supplies a signal S,
costs K today and delays the later value by a discount factor d ∈ (0, 1]. Under an explicitly
stipulated joint probability model, the two option values are
                W0 = max{0, EG},         Wpilot = −K + d E[max{0, E(G | S)}].
The maximum inside the expectation represents the option to decline after an unfavorable
signal. It must correspond to a feasible stop decision: costs already committed before the
signal cannot be removed by a retrospective choice. Pilot cost is excluded from G because it
is subtracted separately. The law of S describes how the prototype informs the commercial
and scientific state; a benchmark score is not automatically such a signal. (Causal banking
proposal, 2026ag)

This assumption includes task difficulty, information availability, feasible actions, review and

USD 2 advantage, while a deployment mix with w = 3/5 has a USD 14 disadvantage. The
threshold is w = 1/4. Knowing only that w ∈ [.1, .4] gives a value range from minus USD 6

the routing policy and fallback outcomes must be part of the transported object. (Causal

   This is especially relevant when business translation is the proposed benefit. A public
dataset with clean treatment and outcome names can test numerical orchestration yet supply

A difficult-case stratum should be defined by the causal task and information obstacle, not
merely by a model’s low confidence. Language assistance can suggest the strata and identify
missing fields, but their prevalence and consequences need separate evidence. Feng et al.’s

   Prices also need a transport argument. A prototype’s observed analyst minutes are resource

101.3      The value of obtaining additional evidence

101.3.1     The evidence needed before investing

The investment decision requires a comparison of feasible alternatives, their costs and the
evidence for their expected benefits. Reproducible public and simulated studies can support
that comparison within their stated assumptions. A restricted result may justify a narrow
prototype while leaving broader research questions unresolved.
   Public evidence informs three choices: which existing methods to reuse, where language
assistance is useful, and how analysts should conduct a reproducible study. A dataset can
reveal a timing error or support a controlled comparison without settling every question about
those choices.
   Later bank qualification asks a different question: do the assumptions, precision, workflows
and economics hold for a specific institution? That stage may need genuine offer histories,
logging information, independent labels, owner-reviewed semantics, production constraints
and prospective outcomes. The relevant input should be requested only for the capability and
target population under test. An internal bank pilot is not required to demonstrate that the
proposal has a reproducible public baseline and a sensible experiment design.

small bank study costing 20,000 could avert an unsuitable 200,000 build in 20% of scenarios,

of scenarios, the same gross value is 4,000 and it is not justified on that ground. These are

101.3.3     Conditions for moving to expensive evidence

An actionable next-experiment recommendation specifies the proposed intervention, target
population, candidate baseline, decision-loss threshold and the smallest information addition
that could alter the decision. It also states an explicit stop or redesign outcome. A bank
experiment should not be recommended merely to fill a missing-evidence cell.
   For funding, the missing addition may be time-stamped offers and an assignment mechanism,
not more aggregate deposits. For servicing, it may be feasible-action and hardship information
at each decision time. For discovery, it may be one feasible intervention that distinguishes
competing graphs. For observation construction, it may be a representative independently
resolved sample of uncertain identities with known inclusion probabilities. Public data help
design each measurement and estimate its computational burden, while the bank supplies the
later population-specific truth.
  Authentic institutional approval, independent acceptance, actual workflow prices and pro-
duction service levels remain future qualification requirements. A proposal can be complete
without them. It cannot honestly claim those outcomes without obtaining them.

The public-data portfolio supports specific analyses, including the earnings experiment and the

101.4      The cost of a useful answer
A bank does not buy words by the thousand because it needs more words. It commissions a
study to decide whether a cheaper funding mix, a different servicing policy or an additional
piece of information is worth having. The relevant unit of production is therefore a useful,
independently checked answer to a specified question. An answer may recommend no change.
It may also conclude, usefully, that the available evidence cannot distinguish the alternatives.
A fluent report that misses the question is wasted production, however little electricity it
consumes.
   This distinction changes the investment calculation. A small model may generate text
cheaply but require more correction. A large batch may lower energy per request while making
the analyst wait. A power meter may report a precise number without measuring the intended
period or attributing the consumption to the intended study. We examine these three problems
in turn. The argument uses inspected public sources, exact calculations and generated arrival
times. It does not report measured energy use by the proposed bank service.


101.4.1     What the public comparisons establish

Argerich and Patiño Martínez (2024) measure CPU, memory, graphics processor and storage
consumption on a specified Linux machine. Their experiments compare model families, model
sizes, batching and numerical precision. The machine has an Intel i9-9900K processor, 32 GB
of memory and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 graphics processor. Their short-prompt experiment
repeats the same input ten times; their question-answering application separately examines
preparation and querying. These choices make the measurement boundaries visible. They do
not resemble a complete causal investigation with document retrieval, alternative diagrams,
identification checks, numerical estimation and human review.
   Two qualifications matter for a purchaser. First, the paper explicitly sets accuracy aside
in Section VII. Its reported energy reductions therefore cannot establish a lower cost per
correct causal conclusion. Second, the quantization experiments depend on the software used
to execute the model. Reducing numerical precision can save memory without proportionately
reducing latency or energy. The paper’s findings do not warrant choosing a bank model from
its parameter count or nominal precision alone.
   A seemingly closer comparator is the joint accuracy, energy and latency study by Husom
et al. (2025). We inspected its April 2025 preprint and public supporting code. The experiment
uses a Raspberry Pi 4, a hardware power meter and five general-purpose task collections.
The prompts request answers without explanations. This is already a different product from
a causal study whose assumptions and reasoning must be examined. More seriously, the
publicly supplied code introduces an unresolved correctness problem. We exclude the source’s
accuracy and joint quality–energy rankings from our recommendation pending reconstruction;
the citation here explains that exclusion.
   The code for the 164 programming tasks passes each stored test to a general evaluation
routine. Every stored test defines a function named check(candidate), but none invokes
it. The caller supplies the test definition without adding the call for the task’s named entry
point. The inspected evaluation routine concatenates the candidate program and the supplied
reference, then reports whether this combined program terminates without an error. Defining a
test function does not execute its assertions. The relevant evaluator and task files are identical
in the inspected September 2026 revision and a February 2025 revision preceding the preprint
(Husom, 2026; Hugging Face, 2022).
  An authored example makes the consequence concrete. Suppose a function must return

returns zero. Both terminate normally when followed only by the definition of two test
assertions. Calling the test function then accepts the first and rejects the second. This checks

recompute each task’s result from preserved outputs. The static evidence does not establish

101.4.3                    The waiting cost of batching

A batch of questions prepared overnight can be available before computation begins. An
analyst asking the first question of the morning is in a different position. If the service waits
for enough further requests to fill a batch, an improvement in processing efficiency can be
purchased with the analyst’s time. Energy per processed token does not record that price.
   An exact calculation separates collection delay from processing time. Assume requests arrive
as a Poisson process of rate λ per second. Group consecutive arrivals in batches of b, and
release a batch as soon as its last request arrives. Ignore service capacity and any backlog for
this calculation. Let Xj be the time between the jth and (j + 1)st requests within a batch.
The Xj are independent exponentials with mean 1/λ and variance 1/λ2 . The average waiting
time in a batch is

Each gap contributes to the j earlier requests that are waiting through it. Summing these
contributions proves the formula. The first request waits (b − 1)/λ on average, twice the

of collection waiting per customer, and 19 minutes for its first request. At 60 requests per
minute the corresponding averages are 9.5 and 19 seconds. The same batch size therefore has
quite different commercial implications for a quiet specialist service and a heavily used shared
service.

101.4.4     Price the whole attempt, then divide by useful output

Define an attempt as a complete, bounded study under a declared procedure. Its energy

and an explicitly allocated share of idle consumption. Let Ai equal one when independent
assessment finds a useful answer to the intended question, and zero otherwise. The acceptance
rule is fixed before comparing configurations. Under comparable repeated tasks and a law of
large numbers, if p = E(Ai ) > 0 and e = E(Ei ) < ∞,

nonduplicated expense Ki , define Ci = rE Ei + rH Hi + Ki . Then E(Ci )/p is the corresponding

cloud charges and electricity must be treated consistently to avoid counting the same cost
twice. Waiting is an opportunity cost only when it delays useful work or a decision; elapsed
seconds alone are not a salary bill. No tariff, staff price, model price or return on investment
is imputed here.
   Useful answers also differ in consequence. A justified refusal to estimate an unidentified
effect is valuable protection, but should be reported separately from a delivered effect estimate.
An unsupported estimate that passes a superficial format check should count as neither. A
later trial should therefore retain both the frequencies and decision consequences of correct
answers, useful refusals, correction, abandonment and misleading answers. Averaging them
into a single convenient success rate would conceal the very risk the service is supposed to
reduce.


101.4.5     A bounded investment recommendation

The available evidence supports a proposal for measurement and comparison, not a claim that
a particular model minimizes a bank’s cost. EnergyMeter is a plausible component after its
time boundaries, missing readings and attribution rules are repaired and tested. The inspected
edge-benchmark scorer should not be adopted unchanged; its missing test invocation is a
specific repairable defect, and redistribution rights for that repository also need clarification.
The comparator’s published accuracy rankings remain excluded until its actual results can be
reconstructed.
   For the proposed causal service, the first matched comparison should use the same questions,
evidence cutoff, task mix and independent acceptance criteria for the shared service and the
established analyst alternative. Record every complete attempt and its corrections, including
justified negative findings. Measure full-workflow expense and energy, and show waiting
separately. This makes the eventual prototype experiment worth funding: it can test whether
a saving survives the costs of producing an answer that a decision-maker can actually use.
   The information studies clarify what the first comparison must recognize as a useful
result. Section 14.4 shows how random recovery of the fixed historical outcome can repair
an observation problem; its precision improvement depends on the information and effort
purchased. Section 14.5 then separates random invitation, successful completion and a stable
historical outcome. The bank can buy more replies without resolving the effect, or obtain a
precise answer about the wrong date. In the bounded example, even a positive effect set may
not cover the offer cost.
   The graph studies make the parallel distinction between more computation and more
identifying information. Completing the oracle search does not qualify the finite-sample
1846                                                         Causal analysis for a universal bank


selection procedure in Section 20.15. The two compatible mechanisms in Section 69.30 still
imply different financial actions until new information excludes one. A shared service earns its
place if it helps the analyst recognize these distinctions, choose an informative next study or
avoid an unsupported recommendation at lower full cost. Producing more estimates is not by
itself that benefit.


101.5      A spending sequence with real alternatives
The first expenditure buys a matched analyst comparison using the constructed and public
cases, with independently assessed scientific quality and recorded full effort. It asks whether
the small integration changes decisions or reduces work enough to pay for its ongoing cost. A
bank-data pilot follows only if that evidence is favourable and the bank supplies a defensible
target, data access, assignment information and a meaningful precision requirement. An
operating trial then tests the relevant interfaces and platform under its actual conditions.
   Several observations would reverse or narrow the recommendation. Equal quality and effort
favour the established workflow. A commercial entitlement supplying equivalent outputs at
lower full cost favours that route. Frequent lack of treatment support or reliable observation
truth favours better study design and data collection. A repeated need for unsupported
dependent inference limits the offer route rather than justifying a broad claim. Evidence
of consequential, reproducibly avoided errors or lower full effort can justify extending the
integration.
   This is a conditional proposal with a selected next experiment. It preserves the larger
scientific and software programme without treating its unfinished questions as already solved.
The detailed comparisons and operating evidence developed in the book allow a reader to
inspect those restrictions and alternatives before funding the next step.
