Proposition 5. Consider the GCM in Figure 5. Assume the instrument z is not constant, i.e.
p(z) is not a delta function, and the instrument is relevant, meaning it has a non-zero effect on

The proof is in Section B.5. The idea is that under O, the ergodic distributions must be i.i.d.
Gaussian (Freedman’s theorem), and the equivariant mechanisms must be linear [Freedman, 1962,
1963, Orbanz and Roy, 2015]. Causal effects are identified in linear-Gaussian IV models, when the
instrument is relevant and not constant [Bowden and Turkington, 1985, Pearl, 2009].
    This result illustrates how symmetry can give rise to causal identification. The infinite orthogonal
group encompasses the permutation group as a subgroup, S ⊂ O. The causal effect is not identified
under S, but is identified under O. So using the same causal graph but stronger symmetries, i.e.,
larger groups G, can enable causal identification.
