    Proposition 5 establishes optimality of the minimax-bias weights w∗ from two perspectives:
first, at the observed data, w∗ minimizes the bias Bb w̄β (Λ) that is inferred ex post; and second,
across hypothetical data realizations, w∗ minimizes the bias that is possible ex ante. Conve-
niently, the maximum bias from either perspective is proportional to the maximum distance:
w∗ does not depend on the estimated heterogeneity η̂1−β or on the heterogeneity bound η. In
the above setup, the optimality of w∗ among weights w̄ ∈ W ∗ is equivalent to optimality of the
robust estimator τ̂ ∗ among weighted estimators τ̂w̄ .
    By default, I take the consideration set to be the class of alternative weights: W ∗ = Λ. In this
case, w∗ can be interpreted as the weights that minimize worst-case disagreement across the
class of alternative weights w̄ ∈ Λ. Moreover, since the minimax-bias weights depend only on
the covariance geometry and the class of alternatives—rather than on estimated heterogeneity
or a heterogeneity bound—they provide a natural default for researchers facing ambiguity over
their initial choice of baseline weights. I now analyze the structure and interpretation of the
