\chapter{Reliability when identities, calendars and models are uncertain}
\label{r8:reliability}

A deposit intervention can affect many customers on the same date, while one
customer's uncertain identity persists across dates. These are two different
sources of repeated information. Auditing the same identity every month does
not produce independent identity evidence, and adding customers on one calendar
does not remove uncertainty about a common funding shock. The scientific
question is therefore not simply whether a database has enough rows. It is
which independent dimensions inform the intervention target, what information
the audits buy, and whether the resulting interval is precise enough to support
the decision. This chapter develops selected extensions with those distinctions
intact. Its new arguments are project derivations; its known-truth examples are
synthetic. They do not establish a generally validated bank product.

\section{Persistent identity audits on a shared calendar}
\label{r8:joint}

Consider $N$ independently sampled persistent customer pairs observed on $T$
common dates. Let $A_{it}\in\{-1,1\}$ be a randomized signed action, with two
candidate outcomes $Y_{i0t},Y_{i1t}$. Randomization, the outcome model and the
fixed outcome units identify a response moment $U_{it}=A_{it}Y_{i,\mathrm{focal},t}$.
This identification premise is separate from the identity-audit calculation.
For example, if $Y=(\theta_i+C_t)A+e$ with $E[Ae]=0$, the moment averages the
customer response and common shock; multiplying it by a declared monetary
scale gives currency per signed action. An observational bank action cannot
inherit this interpretation merely by satisfying the input shape.

Write $V_{ikt}=A_{it}Y_{ikt}$ and $H_{it}=(V_{i0t}+V_{i1t})/2$.
A persistent audit $R_i$ has known conditional inclusion probability $q_i$,
bounded below by a positive constant. The accurate binary identity $M_i=1$
selects candidate zero. If $U_{it}$ is revealed by the audit, the augmented
moment is
\begin{equation}
 Z_{it}=H_{it}+\frac{R_i}{q_i}(U_{it}-H_{it}),\qquad
 E_R[Z_{it}\mid\text{complete pair history}]=U_{it}.
 \label{r8:audit-moment}
\end{equation}
The expectation follows by substituting $E_RR_i=q_i$. The full history shares
one audit indicator. Summing this identity over dates does not change the audit
sampling unit. In particular, correctness counts alone can be singular when
each alias is correct half the time, while actual audited outcomes can still
identify the response. The new information changes the experiment.

Suppose the observed audit label $L_i$ is an independently flipped version of
$M_i$, with symmetric error probability $\epsilon<1/2$. The corrected moment is
\begin{equation}
 U^*_{it}(\epsilon)=H_{it}
  +\frac{(2L_i-1)(V_{i0t}-V_{i1t})}{2(1-2\epsilon)}.
 \label{r8:label-correction}
\end{equation}
Conditional expectation over the flip replaces $2L_i-1$ by
$(1-2\epsilon)(2M_i-1)$, recovering the correctly selected moment. Substitute
$U^*$ into \eqref{r8:audit-moment}. This argument needs the flip law conditional
on the pair history and audit design, not just an average error frequency.
Selected or asymmetric errors can violate it. A shape validator cannot discover
such a scientific violation from supplied histories.

\subsection{A selected joint limit, rather than an arbitrary cluster rule}

Let the corrected score have the representation
$Z_{it}=h(W_i,C_t,E_{it})$. The persistent attributes $W_i$, including map,
audit and label randomization, are iid. The stationary calendar $C_t$ is
independent of those attributes and strongly mixing. Conditional innovations
$E_{it}$ are independent across cells. Assume a $2+\delta$ moment envelope and
summable mixing/covariance bounds sufficient for the calendar central limit
and the residual covariance sums below; for instance
$\sum_k\alpha_C(k)^{\delta/(2+\delta)}<\infty$ with a suitable uniform envelope.
Define $\theta=EZ_{it}$ and the projections
\[
 a(W_i)=E[Z_{it}\mid W_i]-\theta,\qquad
 b(C_t)=E[Z_{it}\mid C_t]-\theta,\qquad
 r_{it}=Z_{it}-\theta-a(W_i)-b(C_t).
\]
Both conditional projections of $r$ vanish. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \bar Z-\theta=\frac1N\sum_i a(W_i)+\frac1T\sum_t b(C_t)
                  +\frac1{NT}\sum_{i,t}r_{it}.
 \label{r8:joint-projection}
\end{equation}
Cross-pair residual covariances vanish conditionally on the calendar. Within a
pair, the assumed summable residual covariance bound gives variance of its
calendar sum of order $T$. Consequently the last term has variance
$O((NT)^{-1})$. The first two sums are independent, and the iid and mixing
central limits give leading variance
\begin{equation}
 V_{NT}=\frac{\sigma_a^2}{N}+\frac{\Omega_b}{T},\quad
 \sigma_a^2=\operatorname{Var}(a(W)),\quad
 \Omega_b=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Cov}(b(C_0),b(C_k)).
 \label{r8:joint-variance}
\end{equation}
This is a first-order argument under comparable $N,T$ growth, with at least one
nonzero leading projection and nonzero total leading variance. Degenerate
interaction-only regimes need a different limit. The reference operation
requires a supplied nondegeneracy declaration rather than pretending to test
that population condition from a finite sample.

The independent multiway sampling framework of \citet{cluster2021} separates
exchangeability and dissociation. A serially dependent calendar does not
automatically satisfy those premises. Equation \eqref{r8:joint-projection}
uses a different selected representation, and the calendar limit is supplied
by its own assumptions. This distinction prevents a convenient two-way API
from substituting for a sampling theorem.

Estimate the pair term by the sample variance of pair means divided by $N$,
and the calendar term by the Bartlett long-run variance of date means divided
by $T$. For centered date means $x_t$, the latter numerator is
\[
 \widehat\Omega=\frac1T\left[\sum_t x_t^2+
 2\sum_{k=1}^{L}\left(1-\frac{k}{L+1}\right)
       \sum_{t=k+1}^{T}x_tx_{t-k}\right].
\]
The positive-semidefinite Bartlett construction is inherited from
\citet{newey1986}; consistency here also needs the new score's joint sampling
conditions. Sufficient selected rates include $L\to\infty$ and
$L^2/\min(N,T)\to0$, with summable covariance tails and moment bounds.
Pair and calendar estimates can both contain interaction noise. Its population
order is $1/(NT)$, so retaining both is compatible with the leading limit;
it is not an exact finite-sample conservative confidence guarantee. Truncated
serial covariance and other finite-sample errors can still cause undercoverage.

\subsection{The third independent sample}

Let $M$ independently sampled gold labels estimate $\epsilon$ by the observed
error fraction $\hat\epsilon$. Gold truth must be accurately adjudicated and
representative of the same symmetric flip law. Differentiate the score:
\[
 \partial_\epsilon Z_{it}
   =\frac{R_i(2L_i-1)(V_{i0t}-V_{i1t})}
          {q_i(1-2\epsilon)^2},\qquad
 g=E\partial_\epsilon Z_{it}.
\]
With $\epsilon$ bounded away from one half, and $M$ comparable to $N,T$, a
Taylor expansion adds $g(\hat\epsilon-\epsilon)$ to the leading error. Its
independent variance contribution is $g^2\epsilon(1-\epsilon)/M$.
The quadratic Taylor remainder is negligible at the stated joint rate.
Plugging in the grand-mean derivative and the gold error fraction gives the
third variance component. A gold-label confidence region reaching one half
prevents regular inversion and yields an explicit unqualified result. This
is neither a bound for arbitrary label contamination nor semantic proof that
the recorded customer is the economic unit of interest.

\section{What audit spending can and cannot buy}
\label{r8:audit-design}

Suppose a complete pair supplies an audit correction difference $D$. Conditional
on the complete history, the audit-randomization variance is
$(q^{-1}-1)D^2$. For strata with population proportions $\pi_s$, per-audit
relative costs $c_s>0$ and second moments $d_s=E[D^2\mid s]$, minimizing this
contribution at expected budget $\sum_s\pi_sc_sq_s=B$ gives the Lagrangian
first-order condition
\[
 -d_s/q_s^2+\lambda c_s=0,\qquad
 q_s=\min\{1,\max\{q_{\min},\kappa\sqrt{d_s/c_s}\}\}.
\]
The objective is convex for positive $q_s$, so the clipped rule with a budget
multiplier solves the stated finite-stratum objective. An independent fully
audited pilot estimates $d_s$ and freezes the allocation before evaluation.
It does not certify the unknown population optimum, and it must not use the
final coverage outcomes to choose the design.

In the synthetic two-stratum example, costs are $1$ and $3$ relative units,
proportions are one half and budget is $.6$ per pair. The independent pilot has
4,000 fully audited pairs, costing 7,936 relative audit units under its realized
strata. Conditioning on the supplied symmetric error probability $.15$, it
produces inclusions $.2282$ and $.3239$, against uniform inclusion $.3$ at the
same expected budget. Final evaluation uses new histories and independent
gold labels. The gold sample has 1,200 labels per request; its adjudication
price and the history-acquisition cost are additional, unmeasured economic
inputs. They are not assigned a zero price.

The realized mean audit costs are 144.50 and 144.76 per 240-pair evaluation for
uniform and pilot allocation, respectively. Their expected costs are matched
by design, not forced to match every random audit count. Mean joint standard
errors are 204.9 and 203.8 currency units, a small change which does not by
itself justify the pilot's expense. The optimization theorem concerns an audit
variance component; it does not guarantee an attractive whole-program return.

A common-calendar floor survives even perfect identity information. Under the
declared AR calendar with marginal standard deviation $.8$ and persistence $.65$,
the asymptotic calendar variance is
$.8^2(1+.65)/((1-.65)T)$. At $T=180$ and monetary scale $1,000$, its nominal
95\% halfwidth is about $253.75$. Halfwidth targets of $100$ or $200$ therefore
cannot be attained by purchasing more pair audits alone. A target of $400$
clears this calendar floor, but still has pair and validation uncertainty.
Changing the calendar, the target or a scientifically justified model is a
different experiment; the audit optimizer cannot make this limitation vanish.

\section{Calibrating the dynamic funding inverse}
\label{r8:calibration}

The earlier deposit construction transforms two candidate responses in each
stratum into sum/difference primitives $m$ and covariance $\widehat V$.
With correctness probabilities $p_0,p_1$, the financial response before direct
price cost is $c(p)^\top m$, where
\[
 c(p)=\left(\tfrac12,\frac1{2(2p_0-1)},
                  \tfrac12,\frac1{2(2p_1-1)}\right)^\top.
\]
The primitives are half-sums and half-differences of the horizon-weighted
candidate responses. This factor matters for both currency units and variance.
Independent binomial audits contribute the delta variance through
$j_s=-m_{2s+1}/(2p_s-1)^2$ and $p_s(1-p_s)/n_s$.
More audits stabilize this inverse when its rank is separated from zero;
they do not repair a truncated calendar covariance estimate or a wrong outcome
model. The weak-rank confidence-set alternatives remain available separately.

Fresh original-design replications retain 720 dates, 2,880 independent pilot
dates, 200 audits per stratum and correctness probabilities $.95,.8$.
Two larger designs have 1,200 or 4,800 dates, a 1,600-date pilot, 1,600 audits per
stratum and probabilities $.9,.85$. These changes alter several information
dimensions, so a favorable larger-design result is not a unique diagnosis of
the historical undercoverage. Within each design, prespecified Bartlett windows
with multipliers $2,5,10$ are compared on the same fresh samples.

Known-audit arms remove calibration uncertainty only for diagnosis. A separate
1,000-draw synthetic training sample estimates the primitive response covariance
for each design, including independently retrained nuisance fits. Its estimated
covariance is frozen before final evaluation. A Student multiplier acknowledges
finite reference training but is not an exact theorem for these non-Gaussian
estimated responses. These arms can expose a variance-estimation contribution;
they are not deployable oracle procedures and do not retroactively qualify the
earlier intervals. Finite coverage, bias, precision and the remaining inverse
assumptions must be assessed together.

The fresh original-design interval covers $468/500=.936$; the 1,200-date
design covers $474/500=.948$, whereas the 4,800-date design covers only
$459/500=.918$. Its pilot remains at 1,600 dates. Larger windows do not
consistently improve these arms. The independently trained covariance raises
the corresponding frequencies to $.954,.968,.930$, but still leaves the
largest design short of nominal coverage. Its empirical response standard
deviation is about $71,401$ currency units, versus mean default standard error
$64,424$, with positive mean error about $11,718$. Known-audit comparisons
likewise retain a shortfall. Thus audit-count uncertainty is not the sole
observed limitation. Finite-pilot and calendar-variance contributions need
separate further investigation; these comparisons do not uniquely identify
their causes or supply a generally calibrated replacement.

\section{Sharper confidence in a finite latent information class}
\label{r8:graphs}

Suppose independent Gaussian episodes have ordered variables $(Z,A,Y)$.
The information class is supplied scientifically. Under observed sufficiency,
the coefficient of $A$ in $E[Y\mid Z,A]$ is the causal target throughout the
declared finite class. Gaussian linear regression with intercept gives an exact
conditional $t_{n-3}$ interval when the design has rank three. This uses causal
invariance across the class, rather than selecting a graph and then ignoring
its uncertainty. A broad latent observational class still supports every real
effect through the earlier same-law witness. More precise computation cannot
remove that lack of information.

Under valid randomized encouragement and exclusion, the true null effect
$\theta$ makes $Y-\theta A$ have zero $Z$ regression slope. Its Gaussian
reduced-form error is independent of $Z$ even when the treatment residual is
confounded with outcome error. The exact null-test approach is the Gaussian
specialization of the weak-IV principle discussed by \citet{staiger1994}; no
lower bound on the first-stage coefficient is needed.

Center all three variables and write
$k=Z^\top Z$, $a=Z^\top A/k$, $y=Z^\top Y/k$. Let $e_A=A-aZ$,
$e_Y=Y-yZ$ and $S_{uv}=e_u^\top e_v$. Inverting the two-sided null test gives
\begin{equation}
 (y-\theta a)^2\leq
 \frac{t_{n-2,1-\alpha/2}^2}{(n-2)k}
       (S_{YY}-2\theta S_{AY}+\theta^2S_{AA}).
 \label{r8:weak-iv-inversion}
\end{equation}
To verify the pivot, regress $Y-\theta A$ on $(1,Z)$: its slope is $y-\theta a$,
residual sum of squares is the expression in parentheses, and residual degrees
of freedom are $n-2$. At the true null, normal slope and independent scaled
residual variance give the Student distribution. Membership of the true effect
therefore has probability $1-\alpha$, including at zero first-stage rank.
The confidence set can nevertheless be practically uninformative.

Equation \eqref{r8:weak-iv-inversion} is a quadratic sublevel set. A positive
leading coefficient gives the interval between real roots; a negative one can
give two rays; absent roots give the empty or whole-line answer according to
sign. Linear and constant cases must also survive. Stable root formulas,
extended-precision discriminants and outward endpoint rounding reduce numerical
error without providing a complete floating-point certificate. These are new
prespecified confidence procedures, not intersections with the earlier 95\%
covariance rectangle. Intersecting independent-looking 95\% answers would
require its own simultaneous confidence argument.

In 2,000 fresh strong-encouragement episodes, the new set is bounded throughout,
with median width $.2079$ in outcome units per action and coverage $.9525$.
Weak encouragement often yields whole-line or disconnected sets, while exact
zero first stage does not invalidate true-null coverage. Supplied sufficiency
has coverage $.954$ in its valid law. False exclusion has zero encouragement-set
coverage, and falsely supplied sufficiency under hidden confounding likewise
fails. An exact statistical pivot cannot make an incorrect causal restriction
true. General PAG confidence and dependent histories remain distinct problems.

\section{An audited action-dependent nonlinear transition variance}
\label{r8:nonlinear}

Extend the scalar private-logger model by
\[
 S_{t+1}=\rho S_t+\gamma\tanh(S_t)+\beta A_t+\sqrt{q(A_t)}\,\xi_{t+1},
 \quad q(A)=q_0(1-A)+q_1A,
\]
with $A\in\{0,1\}$, independent standard normal $\xi$, and
$|\rho|+|\gamma|<1$. Observation remains $O_t=S_t+\sqrt{o}\,\zeta_t$;
the private action probability is
$.05+.90\operatorname{expit}(\lambda_0+\lambda_sS_t+\lambda_oO_t)$.
Reward has conditional mean
$\eta_0+\eta_sS_t+\eta_aA_t+\eta_{sa}S_tA_t$ and independent normal noise.
The Gaussian initial distribution and all observation, logging and reward
parameters remain in the likelihood. Adding $\log q_1$ produces 16 parameters;
silently omitting logging probabilities would change the observed experiment.

Representative accurate state audits reveal complete trajectories. Conditional
transition means are identified by a full-rank regression on
$(S_t,\tanh S_t,A_t)$ over their support. Residual second moments in both action
groups identify $q_0$ and $q_1$. Full support and an open state range distinguish
the linear and nonlinear features. Observation variance, logger parameters,
reward coefficients/variance and initial moments follow from their corresponding
labelled conditional laws with full-rank designs. Without representative state
truth or both action groups this argument does not supply identification.

Main trajectories integrate states using the same model factors; audited
trajectories evaluate them directly. Their independent log-likelihood sums
share one parameter. The recursive predictive-likelihood principle is inherited
from \citet{sarkka2013}, while this audit/model identification and uncertainty
construction are project arguments. Two constrained local starts use the stable
domain $|\rho|+|\gamma|\leq .95$ and explicit parameter bounds. For an interior
fit, Hessian $H$ and separately centered main/audit score matrices give
$\widehat\Sigma=H^{-1}(\sum\tilde s\tilde s^\top)H^{-1}$.
The gradient of the fixed policy value maps this covariance into value
uncertainty. Both independently sampled trajectory counts enter; audits are
not treated as free parameter knowledge.

The policy uses time-specific observable-signal thresholds trained independently
in the earlier study, frozen here as $(-1,-1,0)$. Its value integrates the
observation-triggered action probability and each action's transition kernel,
discounting rewards by $.97$ per step and converting by $1,000$ currency units.
It is not an optimal full belief-history policy. The registered three-step
mechanism has $q_0=.35$, $q_1=.9$, 80 main and 640 audited trajectories.
Every available fitted interval passes likelihood, score and value refinement
from 61 nodes on $[-7,7]$ to 101 on $[-10,10]$. Independent checks include all
16 likelihood and value derivative directions, direct labelled likelihood,
equal-variance reduction and the exact Gaussian limit requiring both
$\gamma=0$ and $\lambda_s=0$. Agreement is evidence about these calculations,
not a uniform numerical-error certificate or global optimizer proof.

All 120 correct-model requests supply intervals and 115 cover the truth;
the Monte Carlo 95\% interval is $[.9054,.9863]$. A homogeneous-variance challenger
also covers 115 of 120; this selected target does not establish a material
value-inference advantage for the added parameter. The extension supplies an
explicitly identified model capability, not an empirically proven superiority.
Adding an omitted nonlinear transition term causes all 120 fits in each arm
to become unqualified. Retaining unavailable outcomes is more informative than
computing coverage only among favorable fits. General nonlinear state models,
uniform boundaries and global policy optimization remain open.

\section{A richer dependent learner that fits in chunks}
\label{r8:learning}

The partial-linear IV score retains the Robinson form of
\citet{dml2018}, with nuisance conditional means $(\ell,r,m)$:
\[
 \psi_t(\theta)=(Z_t-m(X_t))
       \{Y_t-\ell(X_t)-\theta(D_t-r(X_t))\}.
\]
The source's iid inference theorem is not a theorem for an arbitrary temporal
learner. Here the dictionary is fixed as $(1,X,X_0X_1)$, observations are bounded
and geometrically mixing, the population Gram and IV slope are separated from
singularity, and all three nuisance means lie in the dictionary. Fixed-dimensional
OLS then has root-sample error under these selected conditions. Orthogonality
leaves a product error of order $n^{-1}$; a growing temporal gap makes training
and evaluation coupling negligible, and a slowly growing Bartlett window with
the corresponding score-error rate supplies the covariance argument.

Two held-out blocks are separated by gap 100. Each trains on the other; windows
and feature availability are checked before fitting. The whole declared origin
is the sampling unit. Inference globally centers retained scores and computes
lag products within each evaluation block. Concatenating separated blocks and
forming lags across their missing calendar gap would create nonexistent adjacent
observations. The declared population mixing rate is supplied, not estimated
from a successful fit.

For chunked fitting, form each block of the augmented matrix
$[X_{\mathrm{dict}},Y,D,Z]$ and take its QR factor. Stack the small retained
$R$ with the next augmented block and repeat QR. Orthogonal transformations
preserve the least-squares objective; the final leading $R_{XX}$ and target
columns solve the same three nuisance problems as dense least squares.
Predictions use the identical fixed dictionary, folds and envelope clipping.
Rank loss is refused. This construction avoids the full expanded fitting
matrix; it still retains input storage and $O(n)$ residuals/scores. It does not
make arbitrary adaptive dictionaries or learners safe to fit out of core.

Fresh synthetic laws add the previously omitted interaction to outcome and
instrument conditional means. The old linear dictionary has zero coverage in
300 draws. The richer QR and independent dense SVD routes agree to floating-point
tolerance and remove that large bias, yet their original-window coverage is
only $271/300=.9033$. A separately registered larger 16,000-date design reaches
$282/300=.94$ with both the original and a longer window; its Monte Carlo interval
is $[.9068,.9641]$. Equal coverage counts do not show that the longer window is
unnecessary in general. A further omitted quadratic term gives zero richer-
dictionary coverage. Representation, numerical algebra and calibrated
uncertainty are separate achievements.

\section{Evidence required before institutional use}
\label{r8:external}

Independent scientific review must reconstruct the target, assumptions,
identification, uncertainty and limitations, including negative comparisons.
Authentic bank validation then needs assignment and source-clock owners,
representative independent audit truth, known selection probabilities, a frozen
question and held-out evidence. An unknown-truth bank study cannot simply
reproduce synthetic truth-coverage counts. It needs assignment diagnostics,
placebos, audit sensitivity and financial reconciliation appropriate to its
design, together with a decision-relevant precision requirement.

Application acceptance requires consumer maintainers to preserve qualifications,
unbounded/disconnected sets and refusals under their actual interfaces. Workflow
economics require matched complete analyst tasks and quality, active labor,
rework, audits and infrastructure costs. Multi-host qualification requires the
target platform and declared fault/recovery and service objectives. Local
serialization, mapped-input fitting and restart checks do not supply these
external observations. The investment argument therefore continues with
bounded scientific capabilities and explicit dependencies, rather than an
assumption that completing the author program has completed adoption.
