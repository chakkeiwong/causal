\chapter{Learning a causal model from observational data}
A discovery method can narrow the plausible economic relationships without
settling the effect of a proposed action. The comparisons here follow that
remaining question through specific implementation choices. A supplied graph
can support a checked adjustment candidate; a learned structural model adds
assumptions about functions and disturbances; hidden borrower circumstances
change the graph information that must be retained. The practical choice
depends on those assumptions as well as the computation.

The first studies compare a restricted pgmpy adjustment route, causal-learn
search and the different maintenance and numerical obligations of CausalNex,
DAGMA and Causica. Native and other-language studies then examine graph
representations, latent-variable methods and the meaning of their scores.
The final bnlearn and LiNGAM comparisons carry the argument into intervention,
counterfactual and bootstrap summaries. Each answers a different part of the
same banking request; successful graph construction alone does not complete
that request. The detailed DAGMA study includes bounded execution of a saved
source module, while the other passages retain their stated source-inspection
and exact-reference evidence scopes.

\section{A restricted pgmpy integration: verify the candidate, preserve the question}
\label{exec13:pgmpy-reuse}

The proposal selects a narrow pgmpy integration for generating an adjustment
candidate from a supplied, fully observed causal DAG. The project must then
verify that candidate independently before reporting it. This choice reuses
a permissively licensed graph library while preserving a scientific boundary:
a successful method call does not prove that the supplied graph describes
the bank, that the requested adjustment identifies the intended effect, or
that a numerical estimator has valid uncertainty. The selection authorizes
development of this limited prototype path; it is not evidence that an
adapter has been implemented or executed
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary,exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The source is pinned to the repository revision beginning
\texttt{1f6296fc2606}, committed on 5 October 2026 and selected by the
cutoff-bounded history query. Its project metadata declares version 1.1.2.
Fifteen inspected source, guide, test and licensing files match the selected
Git tree, in addition to their local SHA-256 identities. Source acquisition
occurred after the 7 October cutoff. The pin establishes which implementation
is discussed; neither a late repository snapshot nor a recent commit measures
maintainer responsiveness or long-term support
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

\subsection{A guide, a graph type and an implemented method are separate evidence}

The identification guide presents minimal, variance-optimal and all-set
adjustment routes. The pinned implementation gives a more qualified picture.
Its minimal-variance branch raises \texttt{NotImplementedError}. The minimal
branch explicitly requires a single exposure and outcome, constructs a
proper backdoor graph, then obtains a minimal separator. The all-set branch
enumerates subsets of an ancestor candidate set after removing specified
causal-path and latent variables. These operations cannot be combined into
a claim that every documented graph class and every advertised adjustment
variant is implemented with the same semantics
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

The distinction between minimal and minimum matters even without statistical
estimation. A set is inclusion-minimal when no proper subset remains valid.
It is minimum-cardinality when no valid set has fewer elements. The inspected
separator routine greedily removes dispensable members of an initial set;
that does not establish a global cardinality minimum. Nor does cardinality
alone determine estimator variance, data acquisition cost or missingness.
A front end should not translate the option name into the promise
``the cheapest or most precise set of controls''
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

An exact graph illustrates the difference. Let $C$ cause $A$, $B$ and $Y$;
let $A$ and $B$ cause $X$; and include $X\to Y$. The two inclusion-minimal
backdoor sets are $\{C\}$ and $\{A,B\}$. Conditioning on $C$ blocks both
forks; without $C$, both $A$ and $B$ are needed. A project reference that
deletes dispensable variables from $\{A,B,C\}$ obtains either answer
depending on deletion order. That computation describes the semantics of
greedy minimality; it is not a measured pgmpy output. For a lending example,
one verified common driver may be cheaper to collect than two separate
proxies, but its actual measurement quality and availability still determine
whether the smaller set is useful \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The package's all-set path is also a bounded algorithmic choice. Enumerating
subsets can require work exponential in the candidate count. Restricting
the candidates to ancestors is meaningful, but a consumer must retain that
domain rather than describe the output as all imaginable observed-variable
sets. Variables irrelevant to the target, descendant restrictions and
generalized adjustment criteria introduce different enumeration questions.
The first proposed wrapper therefore excludes both all-set enumeration and
the unimplemented minimum-variance option. It asks for one candidate and
checks its stated sufficient criterion
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

\subsection{A total-effect validator must reject the wrong question}

The inspected public validation call first checks the supported graph type
and invokes a graph-role check. The latter checks the presence of exposure
and outcome roles. The adjustment validator then tests whether parents of
the exposure remain connected to the outcome conditional on the exposure
and proposed adjustment variables. This particular call path does not
independently exclude descendants of the exposure
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

Consider the supplied DAG $X\to M\to Y$ with $X$ a root. Its exposure
has no parents, so the inspected parent's-connection predicate is an empty
conjunction and evaluates to true, even if the proposed adjustment variable
is the mediator $M$. The role-presence check does not reject that assignment.
This is a static trace through authentic source, not an installed-engine
experiment. It suffices to show why the project cannot adopt that validation
call alone as its total-effect admission rule. The finding concerns this
specific validator and pin; it does not assert that every identification
method returns an invalid set
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary,exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The financial significance can be derived exactly. Let independent,
zero-mean, unit-variance errors generate
\[
 X=U_X,\qquad M=2X+U_M,\qquad Y=3M+U_Y.
\]
Under an intervention on $X$, the derivative of expected $Y$ is $6$.
The covariance entries are
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X)=1,\quad \operatorname{Var}(M)=5,\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,M)=2,\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=6,\quad \operatorname{Cov}(M,Y)=15.
\]
The coefficient of $X$ in the population regression on $(X,M)$ is
\[
 \frac{6\cdot5-2\cdot15}{1\cdot5-2^2}=0.
\]
Conditioning on the mediator has removed the mediated path. Reporting that
coefficient as the total effect would change the causal question. If $X$
is a one-unit service intensity, $Y$ is dollars over the agreed horizon and
the common linear law is stipulated for $1{,}000$ accounts, the authored
gross response scenario is $6{,}000$ dollars, compared with zero under the
misreported coefficient. This is generated arithmetic, not bank ROI, a
binary-treatment guarantee or a measured customer response
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The proposed first path consequently requires a conservative pre-action
backdoor contract. A candidate set must contain observed variables distinct
from exposure and outcome, include no descendants of the exposure, and
d-separate exposure and outcome in the graph with outgoing exposure edges
removed. This is a sufficient contract for the selected DAG setting. It
is intentionally narrower than generalized adjustment over all supported
graph classes. An otherwise useful generalized set outside the contract
receives an unsupported-scope disposition; it is not declared scientifically
impossible. The separation between a restricted sufficient criterion and
complete identification is part of the result's meaning.

Two project-owned graph calculations support the proposed verification
boundary. One tests separation after ancestral moralization. The other
enumerates simple skeleton paths, checks noncollider conditioning and checks
whether colliders have conditioned descendants. They agree on $26{,}064$
queries across all $543$ labeled DAGs on four nodes, including every ordered
exposure--outcome pair and every subset of the remaining nodes. The check
helps detect mistakes in the small reference implementation; it is not a
proof for arbitrary graph size, an upstream pgmpy test run or a statement
about statistical discovery from finite samples
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

\subsection{A selected DAG can conceal unresolved direction or a failed extension}

Static discovery remains a separate reuse decision. The pinned PC class
supports original, stable and parallel variants; its defaults include
parallel execution, test level $.01$, a maximum conditioning-set size of
five and a partially directed return. In the fitted method, the names
\texttt{pdag} and \texttt{cpdag} follow the same return branch.
The \texttt{dag} option calls a conversion selecting one orientation.
The option spelling alone cannot establish a complete equivalence class,
whole-graph confidence or causal identification under hidden confounding.
The source declares causal sufficiency, acyclicity and faithfulness;
particular conditional-independence tests add their own assumptions
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

The default conditioning cap can stop search before all possible separating
sets have been considered. Test selection, data types and expert constraints
must therefore be retained alongside the graph. A per-test threshold of
$.01$ is not a $.99$ confidence level for every edge or the downstream
effect. Required and forbidden edges introduce external information;
their provenance must survive any business-language or agent-assisted
formulation step. A graph's attractive rendering cannot supply that
provenance \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

The PDAG implementation contains a particularly clear conversion boundary.
Its \texttt{to\_dag} routine warns when it cannot find a consistent
extension preserving directed edges and v-structures, then orients remaining
edges arbitrarily while avoiding cycles. The result can therefore be an
acyclic graph with different v-structures. The \texttt{to\_cpdag} route
first obtains an extension when undirected edges exist, so it cannot be
used as an independent certificate that the input represented a valid
equivalence class \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

A four-node chordless undirected cycle supplies an exact refusal fixture.
There are sixteen orientations of its four edges; two form directed cycles
and fourteen are acyclic. Every acyclic orientation has a sink with two
nonadjacent parents and therefore introduces an unshielded collider.
Consequently none preserves the undirected graph's absence of specified
colliders. The project enumerates all fourteen acyclic cases to check this
small argument. An arbitrary DAG returned after a warning would answer a
different graphical question; simply confirming acyclicity would miss the
change \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The initial integration therefore consumes an already supplied DAG rather
than an automatically completed discovered graph. Future discovery
integration must preserve typed directed and undirected edges, isolates,
separating sets, constraints, warnings and the exact selection procedure.
Named-edge conversion is preferable to guessing direction from an unlabeled
binary matrix. An incomplete or nonextendible graph must remain an explicit
scientific or computational limitation, with no fallback to a conveniently
oriented causal story.

\subsection{The permitted prototype path and its failure contract}

The proposed operation accepts one exposure and one outcome in a fully
observed acyclic graph, with unique string node identifiers and an explicit
node list. It initially caps the graph at twelve nodes as a conservative
wrapper design choice; that number is not measured engine capacity.
Latent, bidirected, selection, undirected and ambiguous-edge inputs are
outside this path. It constructs a fresh pinned DAG with explicit roles
and requests the minimal adjustment candidate. The original graph remains
available for comparison \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

After a successful return, the wrapper must verify unchanged nodes, edges,
exposure and outcome roles; validate candidate membership and timing;
exclude exposure descendants; and run the independent graphical check.
Success then means a candidate satisfying the specified criterion relative
to the supplied graph. It carries no numerical effect, confidence interval
or certification of the graph's real-world truth. An empty valid set is
a meaningful result, whereas a null or failed return is a different
disposition. A failure to find a set by this method must not become a
claim of global causal nonidentifiability
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

Exceptions, warnings, unsupported variants, altered graph structure and
resource limits are retained with their causes. They do not become an
empty adjustment set or zero effect. The same graph, target identity and
disposition must cross library, CLI and MCP boundaries. Implementation
acceptance will require actual pinned-engine calls on named confounder,
mediator, collider, isolate and permutation fixtures, together with invalid
graph and failure cases. The exact references developed here provide
oracles for that work; no pgmpy runtime or adapter call was performed
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_oracle}.

The minimal path's attraction is limited but practical: pgmpy can propose
a graph-based candidate while the project's independent sufficient check
guards its meaning. It is not necessary to copy the whole library or
port its graph stack to another language to obtain this benefit. A
reference-only role remains appropriate for presently unverified discovery
and estimation branches. Minimum-variance adjustment is rejected at this
pin because the branch is unimplemented. Generalized adjustment, latent
graphs and data-driven direction selection are deferred to separate
source and semantic reviews \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

\subsection{Licensing, dependencies and ownership make reuse concrete}

The pinned MIT license permits use, modification and distribution subject
to preservation of its copyright and permission notice and contains a
warranty disclaimer. That permission does not replace dependency-license
review. Project metadata requires Python from 3.10 up to but excluding
3.15 and declares twelve mandatory dependencies, including NumPy,
SciPy, NetworkX, pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels, scikit-base and
Hugging Face Hub. Optional groups separately include Torch, graphical
tools and LiteLLM. Declared version floors are not a resolved reproducible
environment \citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

The proposed graph service should use an isolated, locked environment and
only the necessary path. Owners must retain source and dependency identities,
notices, conversion fixtures and resource measurements. An upgrade requires
rechecking API names, graph representations and failure behavior as well as
rerunning accepted fixtures. The inspected revision includes adjustment
tests for observed, latent and M-graph cases, which are useful maintenance
evidence; their presence does not imply that those tests were executed here
or that all targets and graph classes are qualified
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

The LLM pairwise class is also a separate capability within the same
repository. Its description states that it uses variable names and text
descriptions, not the observed data values, and its source includes an
optional provider-call path. Such suggestions can inform a reviewable
business formulation but do not inherit the evidence of a CI-based
discovery algorithm. This selected graph operation has no live-model
dependency. A future language-model path would need its own custody,
ambiguity, source-grounding and held-out evaluation contract
\citep{exec13_pgmpy_reuse_primary}.

Costs therefore include graph elicitation, controls and timing review,
dependency maintenance, integration fixtures and adjudication of failures,
even though the selected source license has no purchase price. No monetary
maintenance estimate is supplied without workload evidence. Research owners
must justify the assumed graph and target; adapter owners must enforce
the conversion and failure contract; reviewers must verify executed
results before runtime qualification. The concrete revisit trigger is
a new pin, graph class or method path, followed by source inspection and
matched behavioral tests. This division gives pgmpy a useful, limited
place in the proposal while keeping the unimplemented and scientifically
unsupported extensions visible.
\label{exec13:pgmpy-reuse-end}

\section{A scoped causal-learn reuse decision}
\label{exec14:causallearn-decision}

For the first Python discovery comparison, direct scoped integration of PC from
causal-learn is preferable to porting its search and orientation rules into a
new implementation. FCI remains a separately reviewed profile. The package name
\texttt{causal-learn}, import namespace \texttt{causallearn}, Tetrad's Python
bridge and the similarly named online service are distinct identities. The
selected source revision declares 0.1.4.8 and an MIT root license; six selected
source and license files match the same pinned Git tree. The inspected setup
file declares twelve direct dependencies without version bounds. That is
sufficient for a concrete reuse recommendation, but insufficient for an admitted
executable adapter \citep{exec14_causallearn_reuse}.

The first proposed PC profile assumes complete IID Gaussian observations,
causal sufficiency, acyclicity, faithfulness and no unmodeled selection. Its
output is a graph-equivalence object, not an effect or a confidence level. The
adapter must preserve the node bijection, both endpoint marks and isolated
nodes. A conflict mark produced by PC cannot be interpreted as an FCI latent
common cause. Singular Fisher-Z inverses, invalid effective degrees of freedom
and nonfinite p-values must produce an unavailable numerical query rather than
an independence assertion. Alpha belongs to the named CI procedure and is not
whole-graph or effect coverage \citep{exec14_causallearn_reuse}.

A complete implementation needs a locked executable environment, dependency
rights inventory, method-specific conversion, failure handling and a matched
engine comparison. Porting is deferred because no measured embedding constraint
currently justifies duplicating maintenance. Exposing every algorithm through
one generic operation is rejected for the initial adapter because their
assumptions and output meanings differ. R pcalg and Java Tetrad remain useful
comparators with separate contracts. Authentic bank validation is not a
prerequisite to this source-based proposal decision; it is a later condition
for a bank-specific scientific or operational claim
\citep{exec14_causallearn_reuse}.

\section{CausalNex: maintenance, graph semantics and reuse}
\label{exec14:causalnex}

A graph-learning package enters a bank's proposal through two different
questions: whether its mathematical output answers the intended question, and
whether someone can own its continuing operation. CausalNex illustrates why
both questions matter. Its canonical source at revision
\texttt{b87ab2059987}, dated 31 August 2026, declares version 0.12.2. The README
explicitly states that the project reached end of life on 24 June 2026 and
will receive no further updates, bug fixes, security patches or issue and
pull-request review. The later source commit does not rescind that notice.
This is affirmative maintenance evidence from the recovered repository; the
earlier failure to retrieve its historical namespace was only an access
failure.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

The proposal therefore rejects CausalNex as a default new dependency while
retaining its design as a historical reference. A narrow archival comparison
could still be justified by a specific reproducibility need. It would require
an accountable maintainer, an isolated environment and a declared endpoint;
the old package's existence supplies none of these obligations automatically.
The root notice declares Apache 2.0 and separately excludes rights to the
QuantumBlack trademarks. That notice supports a licensing route, but it is
not a complete inventory of transitive and bundled rights. The packaging
requires Python at least 3.8 and below 3.11, NumPy below 1.24, pandas below 2.0
and pgmpy below 0.1.20, together with other dependencies and Python-specific
scikit-learn requirements. These declarations motivate environment isolation;
they do not establish that a resolved environment is impossible, vulnerable
or inexpensive to maintain.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

\subsection{A learned structure and an intervention target}

Consider a servicing analyst who records account covariates, an offer and a
subsequent recovery amount. The inspected continuous-data NOTEARS entrypoints
take an $n\times d$ matrix $X$, with accounts in rows and variables in columns.
With $W_{ij}$ representing a coefficient from variable $i$ to variable $j$,
the unpenalized data term and acyclicity function are
\[
 L(W;X)=\frac{1}{2n}\|X(I-W)\|_F^2,
 \qquad h(W)=\operatorname{tr}\{\exp(W\circ W)\}-d.
\]
Here $\circ$ denotes elementwise multiplication, $\exp$ is the matrix
exponential and $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the square root of the sum of squared matrix
entries. The augmented objective adds $\rho h(W)^2/2+\alpha h(W)$, with
penalty $\rho$ and multiplier $\alpha$. The lasso path represents a coefficient
as the difference of nonnegative parts and penalizes their sum. Tabu edges and
tabu parent or child variables become optimizer bounds. These are concrete
source-level computations.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

Even an acyclic solution supplies a candidate structure under that loss and
those restrictions. An intervention contrast such as expected recovery under
an offer versus no offer additionally needs a causal interpretation, an
eligible population, an outcome horizon and assumptions about confounding and
the observation clock. A post-offer delinquency measurement cannot become a
baseline variable because the optimizer accepts its column. The selected
entrypoints return no causal graph confidence region or treatment-effect
interval. The package also advertises Bayesian-network fitting and
intervention facilities; this selected source review does not qualify those
separate implementations or establish their appropriateness for longitudinal
servicing.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

The graph class makes another boundary explicit. A \texttt{StructureModel}
can contain directed cycles. Edge origins take the values unknown, learned
or expert; the pandas wrapper preserves column names and marks learned
edges, whereas the selected array-created graph uses the class default.
Origin records who or what supplied an edge. It does not certify its truth.
An assistant can preserve that provenance and ask which temporal or business
restriction supports an expert edge, but should not convert the label
``expert'' into verified causal evidence.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

\subsection{Preprocessing and graph repair change the reported object}

The selected objective does not center $X$ automatically. To see the
consequence without fitting the upstream package, impose two observations
$(-1,-2)$ and $(1,2)$ for variables $(A,B)$ and restrict the direction to
$A\to B$. With no intercept the least-squares coefficient is
$\sum_i A_iB_i/\sum_i A_i^2=2$, and the full two-variable loss above is $1/2$.
Change the origin of the first variable to $A'=A+1$. The observations become
$(0,-2)$ and $(2,2)$, and their centered second-moment matrix remains
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Yet the no-intercept coefficient is now $1$. Holding the old coefficient at
$2$ yields loss $3$; refitting within the fixed direction yields loss $2$.
These are exact calculations for an imposed example, not measurements of
which graph the upstream optimizer would select. They show why a caller must
declare centering, the population used to fit preprocessing and the treatment
of intercepts.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_oracle}

Scaling also affects a subsequent graph operation. The method
\texttt{threshold\_till\_dag} repeatedly deletes the edge with smallest
absolute weight until a DAG remains; its own docstring warns about weights
with different units.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}
For a stipulated two-cycle, let the coefficients be $A\to B=0.2$ and
$B\to A=0.5$. This rule deletes $A\to B$. Express the same coefficient
relations using $B'=10B$: the weights become $2$ and $0.05$, so the rule
deletes $B\to A$. Algebraically, a change of units with diagonal scale matrix
$D$ gives $W'=D^{-1}WD$. In this example the cycle product remains $0.1$ and
both acyclicity values equal $2\cosh(0.1)-2>0$. The cycle has not disappeared
through a change of units; the pruning choice has changed.
\citep{exec14_causalnex_oracle}

In a banking model, two opposite surviving arrows can lead to different
adjustment sets and intervention stories. Deleting a weak edge is thus a
scientific modeling choice, even when it also solves a software input
requirement. A wrapper would need to retain the original graph, the units,
the declared pruning procedure and the full deletion trace. Standardizing
variables may define a more explicit comparison, but cannot prove the
resulting arrows causal or remove observational ambiguity.

Threshold boundaries also deserve an explicit contract. At the inspected
revision the unpenalized path zeros weights satisfying
$|w|\leq t$, while the lasso path zeros weights satisfying $|w|<t$.
For nonzero weights $w=\pm0.2$ and threshold $t=0.2$, the first path removes
both edges and the second retains them. A common adapter that applies a
single threshold convention would silently change one method's output.
Independent rational arithmetic reproduces these boundaries; testing the
actual wrapper would remain a separate implementation task.
\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary,exec14_causalnex_oracle}

\subsection{A conditional archival route and its alternatives}

The inspected optimizer consumes the returned solution vector and can issue a
nonconvergence warning before returning a graph. It does not promote optimizer
success into a structured scientific guarantee. A prospective archival
wrapper would capture termination and warnings, test finite output and
acyclicity, and retain an unqualified candidate when these checks fail. It
would preserve column identities, transforms and tabu directions, and report
timeouts or environment failures as unavailable results. An edge weight would
never be repurposed as a confidence level. These requirements follow from
the selected source and from the meaning of the proposed output; no CausalNex
runtime qualification is claimed here.~\citep{exec14_causalnex_primary}

Direct adoption would transfer unsupported lifecycle obligations to the
product. Porting the optimizer would additionally transfer numerical behavior,
dependency adaptation and testing to the local team; there is no demonstrated
embedding requirement that warrants this ownership. An isolated historical
benchmark is the narrower route when a concrete comparison requires it.
Other discovery engines discussed in this survey need their own pinned
decisions, because constraint tests, score searches and latent-variable
representations answer different questions. Their names are not aliases for
CausalNex, and a separately reviewed pgmpy version does not qualify the older
dependency used here.

For a small banking prototype, an explicit expert DAG and an exactly
enumerated reference model provide useful comparators: they expose assumptions
and intervention arithmetic while leaving discovery accuracy open. The cost
comparison should include retained environments, license and dependency
review, preprocessing, failed requests, analyst correction, future fixes and
retirement. No observed price or maintenance-cost estimate is available from
the present static review. Reconsideration of CausalNex would require a unique
archival need, a named owner, a permissible resolved environment and executed
conversion and failure checks. Public or generated examples can support
those initial checks without purchasing access to bank data.
\label{exec14:causalnex-end}

\section{Reusing DAGMA without changing the scientific question}
\label{exec16:dagma-reuse}

A bank considering a new servicing action may first want to learn which
observed variables could mediate its response. Continuous structure learning
offers a computational route to candidate graphs, but its output must survive
several distinct interpretations: numerical optimization, graph topology,
structural assumptions and the intervention being contemplated. DAGMA is a
useful case because the original Python implementation exposes a compact
linear optimizer and a nonlinear alternative, while its acyclicity argument
has a precise domain. The proposed reuse decision is to retain this original
implementation as a reference and a candidate for a later isolated adapter.
The current evidence does not justify wholesale adoption or a port into the
core library. The selected source revision dates from January 2024; it is
distinct from translations of the method in other software.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary}

\subsection{A domain theorem is not a numerical acceptance test}

Write $W_{ij}$ for the weight from parent $i$ to child $j$, and let
$S=W\circ W$ denote its entrywise square. The inherited characterization
uses
\begin{equation}
 h_s(W)=-\log\det(sI-S)+d\log s,
 \qquad s>\rho(S),
 \label{exec16:dagma-domain}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the number of variables and $\rho$ is the spectral radius.
Within this nonsingular M-matrix domain, $h_s(W)$ is nonnegative and vanishes
exactly for a DAG. The primary paper separately assumes that the chosen
statistical model identifies the graph. Its central-path result concerns
the limiting optimization scheme, not every finite sequence of approximate
gradient steps. Neither statement supplies an effect confidence region for
a graph selected from a finite banking sample.
\citep[Section 2, Theorem 1 and Lemma 6]{exec13_pydisc_dagma2022}

An independent two-variable calculation provides a useful behavioral oracle.
For $W_{12}=a$, $W_{21}=b$ and zero diagonal,
\begin{equation}
 h_s(W)=-\log\{1-(ab/s)^2\},\qquad |ab|<s.
\end{equation}
Differentiating this scalar expression, or using
$\nabla h_s(W)=2W\circ(sI-S)^{-\mathsf T}$, gives an independent check of
the original module's derivative. At $a=0.2$, $b=0.3$ and $s=1.3$, its value
is approximately $0.00213244957$. The inspected original module agrees with
the scalar expression; central finite differences at step $10^{-6}$ agree
with its derivative to a maximum absolute discrepancy below $4\times10^{-11}$
in this example. This is a numerical identity check at a declared point,
not an estimate of graph recovery or a uniform numerical guarantee.
\citep{exec16_dagma_exact}

The domain cannot be omitted from that oracle. For
$W=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2})$ and $s=1$, the determinant is
one, although the graph contains two self-loops and $\rho(S)=2$.
The original helper returns a value near zero outside its theorem domain.
For a two-cycle with $a=2$, $b=1$ and $s=1$, the determinant is $-3$;
the helper's log-absolute-determinant expression gives $-\log3$.
These are direct calls to the numerical helper. They do not show that the
optimizer admitted those matrices during fitting: the optimization loop
contains separate inverse-matrix domain checks. They show why a consumer
cannot certify a DAG by accepting an isolated small residual. A future
adapter must inspect domain validity and the returned graph's actual support,
including self-loops and directed cycles.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_exact}

\subsection{What the executed source module requires of an adapter}

The bounded execution used the unchanged saved linear module with Python
3.11.15, NumPy 2.1.3, SciPy 1.17.1 and tqdm 4.70.0. It did not install the
complete DAGMA distribution. Seven deterministic input cases exercised the
least-squares path with four inner iterations and one outer stage. These
small budgets deliberately expose interface boundaries; the resulting
observations are not a performance or accuracy study.
\citep{exec16_dagma_runtime}

The first boundary is ownership of observations. In the least-squares path,
the module centers the caller's array in place. Integer input raises a
NumPy casting exception. A service that retains the original observations
for another estimator therefore needs its own floating-point copy, a record
of the removed means and a stable mapping between columns and variable
identities. Copying data is also a real memory and transfer cost. It should
appear in a matched workflow comparison rather than disappear outside the
timed optimizer.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_runtime}

The second boundary concerns scientific constraints. The signature describes
edge collections as optional lists, while the inspected implementation
recognizes tuples of tuples. For the same four-row input, excluding edge
$(0,1)$ as \texttt{((0,1),)} produced a zero in that matrix entry. Supplying
\texttt{[(0,1)]} instead left an entry approximately $0.039991$.
The invalid branch constructs a \texttt{ValueError} without raising it.
An empty tuple raises an indexing exception. A requested included edge also
remained absent on a zero-signal input, consistent with the upstream warning
that inclusion is not guaranteed. These findings call for an explicit
distinction between hard exclusions, soft inclusion preferences and hard
required edges. A scientific constraint cannot silently become a preference
because a serialization library changed a tuple to a list.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_runtime}

The third boundary is the identity of the diagnosed result. With no weight
threshold, the deliberately short run returned a cyclic matrix, including
self-loops. With a threshold of $0.3$, the same optimization returned the
empty graph. The saved prethreshold residual remained approximately
$0.003200116$, although the returned graph's residual was zero. The stored
least-squares score was approximately $2.693174$, while evaluation on the
returned graph gave $3.125$. Neither value is intrinsically misleading if
its matrix is named. A wrapper becomes misleading when it attaches one
matrix's diagnostics to another matrix without saying so.
\citep{exec16_dagma_runtime}

A proposed adapter should retain the raw and thresholded matrices, the
original and effective optimization settings, the observation transform and
diagnostics recomputed on each reported object. Its runtime bound must cover
the upstream retry loop as well as the nominal iteration counts. Malformed
inputs, singular solves, nonfinite values, exhausted budgets and cyclic
outputs need distinct error or incomplete dispositions. Silently deleting
an edge to obtain a DAG changes the reported procedure; any such repair
would require its own explicit rule and qualification. These are concrete
requirements for a future wrapper, not capabilities established by the
reference module's execution.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_runtime}

\subsection{Units and nonlinear weights can reverse a banking interpretation}

For a stipulated linear structural model written with row observations,
$X=XW+E$, a change of measurement units $Z=XD^{-1}$ gives
$W_Z=DW D^{-1}$, where $D$ is a positive diagonal scale matrix. In a DAG,
the total directed-path coefficient matrix is
\begin{equation}
 K=(I-W)^{-1}-I=W+W^2+\cdots+W^{d-1},\qquad
 K_Z=DKD^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This algebra describes a change of units in an already stipulated structural
model. It does not prove that a fitted graph has a causal interpretation or
that a penalized optimizer is invariant to rescaling its data.
\citep{exec16_dagma_exact}

For example, suppose an authored three-variable model has
$W_{12}=2$, $W_{23}=3$ and $W_{13}=1$. Its total first-to-third coefficient
is $1+2\times3=7$. With $D=\operatorname{diag}(10,1,100)$, the transformed
edge weights are $20$, $0.03$ and $0.1$, and the transformed total coefficient
is $0.7$. Both describe the same stipulated mechanism in different units.
A raw threshold of $0.5$ retains all three original edges but only one
transformed edge. Thus a threshold needs an explicitly declared scale;
``the same threshold'' is not a scientifically matched comparison across
arbitrarily standardized borrower measures. Fitting, thresholding, refitting
and any subsequent uncertainty calculation must be treated as a whole
procedure.
\citep{exec16_dagma_exact}

The nonlinear alternative has a different problem. The inspected
\texttt{fc1\_to\_adj} routine takes a norm of first-layer weights and returns
nonnegative entries. It does not return signed intervention derivatives.
Consider two authored one-parent response functions,
\begin{equation}
 f_+(x)=4\sigma(2x)-2,\qquad
 f_-(x)=-4\sigma(2x)+2,\qquad
 \sigma(u)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}.
\end{equation}
Their first-layer norm is the same, namely two, and both have baseline zero.
Since $\sigma'(0)=1/4$, their derivatives at zero are $+2$ and $-2$.
In a stipulated servicing illustration with USD~100 per outcome unit and
USD~20 marginal cost per unit of action intensity, the local net-value
derivatives are USD~180 and USD~$-220$. The same nonnegative adjacency
weight is consistent with opposite local recommendations. These are exact
derivatives of declared functions; they are neither finite-action effects
nor results of executing the nonlinear PyTorch implementation.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_exact}

An intervention calculation therefore needs the fitted structural functions,
the intervention definition, units and assumptions that justify using those
functions causally. A graph-only export loses that information. For
equivalence-class, latent-variable or temporal questions, the reviewed
constraint-based and temporal routes remain substantive alternatives. A
convenient DAG representation cannot substitute for their different
information and uncertainty contracts.

\subsection{The reuse choice and the evidence that would change it}

The inspected repository carries Apache~2.0 terms. Redistribution requires
the license, relevant retained notices and marked modifications, together
with applicable NOTICE obligations. That root license does not settle the
terms of every dependency or of a different implementation. The package
metadata declares NumPy, SciPy, torch, tqdm and igraph without version bounds,
although the narrow linear source path imports only the first, second and
fourth. Its version configuration refers to \texttt{src/VERSION}, absent
from the inspected pinned tree. This is a concrete build-identity issue to
resolve against a selected release, not grounds to invent a release number
or declare that every published package is broken. Saved examples and the
inline demonstration function also do not establish a supported build or
passing continuous integration.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary}

Reference use is consequently justified now. Conditional integration of a
selected linear path is a plausible next prototype: establish a complete
permissible build, implement the stated conversion and failure contract,
and replay the boundary cases with independent graph and numerical oracles.
Direct adoption leaves those obligations with the caller. Porting adds
floating-point, solver, threshold and maintenance differences before any
measured need for a port has been demonstrated, so it is not selected here.
NOTEARS supplies a meaningful numerical comparator under matched model and
output assumptions, while PC, FCI or temporal alternatives answer different
questions where sufficiency, hidden causes or time order matter.

The initial evaluation should use generated mechanisms with known truth and
public inputs with declared limits. It should predeclare identifiable and
observationally equivalent cases, unit transformations, graph-error and
downstream decision criteria, and retain failed or incomplete requests in
the denominator. The prototype maintainer owns build reproducibility and
failure semantics; the scientific reviewer owns assumptions and any later
effect-identification argument. Full costs include copying, serialization,
optimization, diagnostic review and dependency maintenance. Their actual
prices and a banking return remain unknown. A validated wrapper and useful
matched public or generated results would justify reconsidering integration
and the expense of independent and bank-specific trials.
\citep{exec16_dagma_primary,exec16_dagma_runtime,exec16_dagma_exact}
\label{exec16:dagma-reuse-end}

\section{Causica: a generative reference with explicit admission boundaries}
\label{exec14:causica-contract}

An analyst wants to simulate a larger servicing effort for a borrower, compare
it with the observed treatment, and then aggregate the result into a contact
policy. A learned structural model makes these operations convenient: one
model can represent a graph, conditional mechanisms, noise and interventions.
Convenience does not settle whether the graph is identified, whether a
counterfactual refers to the same borrower, or whether the reported uncertainty
includes the relevant source of error. Causica is useful precisely because
its explicit abstractions let the proposal examine these questions together.

The selected source is Microsoft's Causica revision \texttt{c3b7dd2b09fe},
dated 24 August 2026, declaring package version 0.4.5. Its DECI path combines
an approximate distribution over graphs with learned functional relationships
and noise modules. This is a proposed reference for a structural-model
component. It is not an enabled estimator in the local six-operation service.
The distinction matters when comparing the proposal with an existing library:
describing a model in a monograph does not implement its training, inference,
diagnostics or failure handling. \citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_decision}

\subsection{What the original result does and does not justify}

Geffner et al. formulate DECI using a nonlinear additive-noise model with no
latent confounders. Its graph distribution and function/noise parameters are
learned by maximizing an evidence lower bound. Their Theorem 1 is an
infinite-data result for a maximizing solution. Appendix A assumes minimality,
structural identifiability, correct specification, causal sufficiency and
integrability of the log likelihood. Structural identifiability means that
another graph in the admitted model family cannot generate the same observed
distribution. It is an assumption with substantive content, not a property
created by calling the optimizer. \citep{exec14_causica_geffner}

These conditions expose four separate obligations for a banking use. The
variable set must represent the relevant common causes; the structural family
must distinguish the intervention query from observational alternatives;
the fitted mechanisms must approximate the admitted law; and the numerical
procedure must achieve an adequate solution. Universal function approximation
does not verify the first two obligations or make a finite training run a
global maximum. The paper explicitly says that the theorem does not cover
its mixed-type and missing-value extensions. A practical implementation may
support those inputs without inheriting that theorem for them.
\citep{exec14_causica_geffner}

After fitting, graph-averaged intervention simulation answers a model-based
question. In the selected source, graph draws share the learned functional
and noise modules. Those draws are therefore not samples from an independently
specified posterior over all function parameters. Their variation is also
not a frequentist confidence region for the true graph. The product should
report the fitted model, graph distribution and numerical simulation error
separately, keeping any unresolved structural ambiguity visible.
\citep{exec14_causica_primary}

\subsection{Conditioning, intervention and the same borrower's counterfactual}

The pinned \texttt{SEM} interface distinguishes \texttt{do},
\texttt{condition}, noise generation and noise reconstruction. Its selected
conditioning method supports a set only when that set has no parents outside
it; otherwise it raises an unsupported-operation error. This restriction is
meaningful. The paper's more general CATE discussion uses regression on
simulated interventional samples and assumes that treatment does not cause
the conditioning variables. That description should not be silently mapped
to an unrestricted call to the selected conditioning helper.
\citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_geffner}

For an exact illustration, let \(X=U_X\) be a standardized pre-action risk
factor, \(T=X+U_T\) a continuous servicing intensity, and
\[
       Y=20T+10X+U_Y
\]
the net recovery before the separately considered cost of changing intensity.
Suppose the three noises are independent centered Gaussians, with variances
\(1,1,100\), and measure \(Y\) in dollars. This is an authored structural
model, not a fitted bank law or an example satisfying general graph
identifiability. Since \(E[X\mid T=t]=t/2\),
\[
 E[Y\mid T=t]=25t,\qquad E[Y\mid\operatorname{do}(T=t)]=20t.
\]
Replacing an unsupported conditioning request by an intervention changes the
answer. Reversing that substitution changes it as well. The first request
selects borrowers through their observed intensity and risk; the second
changes intensity while retaining the population distribution of risk.
\citep{exec14_causica_exact}

Consider a borrower whose factual measurements are \((X,T,Y)=(1,2,55)\).
Reconstructing the noises gives \((U_X,U_T,U_Y)=(1,1,5)\). Holding these
noises fixed gives counterfactual
recoveries of USD~15 at intensity zero and USD~35 at intensity one. The
individual contrast is USD~20. Replacing factual noise by fresh population
noise gives marginal means zero and twenty instead. The contrast happens
to agree in this constant-effect construction, while the borrower's two
counterfactual levels do not. Agreement of an effect difference is therefore
an insufficient test of factual-unit preservation. The pinned \texttt{ite}
helper reconstructs noise once and reuses it for the two interventions;
the \texttt{counterfactual} helper follows the same abduction/action/prediction
logic. \citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_exact}

The ATE helper takes a different route: it samples each intervention arm and
subtracts component means. With independent samples of size \(n\) from each
arm in the stipulated model, the Monte Carlo variance of that contrast is
\(400/n\), because each arm has residual variance \(200\). At \(n=100\)
the variance is four square dollars. Coupling the two arms through the same
noise gives an exactly constant twenty-dollar contrast here. Such coupling
can reduce simulation variance, but its appropriateness must be checked for
the intended estimator. Neither calculation establishes sampling uncertainty
for learned functions, uncertainty about the graph, or causal truth.
\citep{exec14_causica_exact}

\subsection{A training penalty is not an output DAG check}

The selected DECI module applies a matrix-exponential acyclicity penalty during
training. Its ENCO adjacency distribution nevertheless samples independent
Bernoulli entries off the diagonal; the selected SEM-distribution path wraps
these matrices without a separate DAG filter. At the initialized zero logits,
each directed edge has probability \(\tfrac12\tfrac12=\tfrac14\).
For two nodes, both opposite edges are present with probability
\(1/16\). This is an exact statement about the initialized distribution,
not an observed failure frequency after training. A penalty may greatly
reduce cyclic mass; its existence alone does not certify every returned
graph. \citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_exact}

The numerical consequence is visible without running a neural model.
The selected structural simulator starts at zero and performs one simultaneous
update per node. For a DAG, induction over parent depth explains why a finite
number of such updates can propagate a fixed noise vector to every node.
That argument depends on acyclicity. Consider instead the authored equations
\[
        x_1=1+\tfrac12x_2,\qquad x_2=\tfrac12x_1.
\]
Two updates from zero produce \((1,0)\) and then \((1,1/2)\).
The latter has equation residual \((-1/4,0)\); the algebraic fixed point is
\((4/3,2/3)\). Thus a finite update result can look numerically ordinary while
failing even the supplied cyclic equations. The fixed point is a numerical
comparison, not an independently justified equilibrium causal interpretation.
The proposal should refuse an unqualified cyclic draw rather than promote
its finite update into a valid intervention result.
\citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_exact}

Hard knowledge constraints need the same admission check. The pinned source
uses zero for prohibited edges, one for required edges and NaN for unspecified
edges. Shape checks and diagonal masking do not by themselves prevent a
required directed cycle. A public contract should use explicit typed states,
validate their values and causal consistency, and translate them into this
internal representation. Adjacency entries also need a declared
parent-to-child convention; they must not be confused with signed structural
coefficients. If an adapter discards cyclic samples, it changes the effective
graph distribution by conditioning on acceptance. It must retain the rejected
mass and describe any reweighting, so a failure cannot disappear from the
denominator. \citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_decision}

\subsection{A vector score needs a vector reference}

The evaluation boundary has another concrete issue. The pinned ATE helper
averages over observations and retains the outcome's component dimension.
The selected \texttt{eval\_ate\_rmse} reference instead applies
\texttt{.mean()} without specifying an axis to each intervention dataset.
For a multidimensional node this collapses observations and components
together before subtracting the two arms. The resulting scalar is then
compared with a component vector. The source can therefore assign a nonzero
error to an exact vector estimate. \citep{exec14_causica_primary}

Take two equal-size reference arms whose component means are \((2,8)\) and
\((0,0)\), with both components measured in dollars. The correct vector
effect is \((2,8)\). An exact prediction has zero componentwise error, but
the all-axis reference is \(5\), producing
\[
       \sqrt{(2-5)^2+(8-5)^2}=\sqrt{18}.
\]
This is an independent exact trace of the inspected reduction, not the output
of an executed Causica test. The selected integration test checks result shape;
that assertion alone does not detect this counterexample. The pinned pytest
configuration also excludes the integration directory from default recursive
collection, so the existence of a CI workflow running the general test tree
is not evidence that this integration test ran.
\citep{exec14_causica_primary,exec14_causica_exact}

The repair route is specific. First preserve the observation axis and calculate
each component's treatment contrast. Then declare whether the requested score
is a vector, a weighted monetary sum or a norm. Different currencies, horizons,
one-hot categories or scales must not be pooled implicitly. An executable
prototype should supply a deterministic SEM or fixed prediction reference,
check zero error for an exact multicomponent effect, and test deliberate
perturbations component by component. Only after these checks should noisy
simulation be added. This separates a scorer error from simulation variation
and model error, reducing the chance of rejecting a correct estimator or
rewarding an incorrect one. \citep{exec14_causica_decision}

\subsection{Graph averaging must preserve the decision risk}

A useful numerical average may still conceal a structurally fragile decision.
Suppose two admitted models imply effects of USD~20 and minus USD~20 and have
stipulated weights \(3/4\) and \(1/4\). The weighted effect is USD~10 and the
between-model variance is \(300\) square dollars. Averaging \(R\) independent
model draws estimates that weighted mean with Monte Carlo variance \(300/R\).
As \(R\) grows, numerical error vanishes around the chosen weighted mean;
the two component effects remain different. The weights themselves are not
validated by increasing the simulation count. \citep{exec14_causica_exact}

With an assumed incremental cost of USD~8, the component net values are
USD~12 and minus USD~28, while their weighted average is USD~2. A manager who
requires robustness across the admitted models cannot infer it from that
positive weighted average. The output should show the component consequences
and the basis for the weights, alongside a model-averaged recommendation
when one is requested. If observationally compatible models remain relevant,
the next information-gathering decision concerns which restriction or
experiment could distinguish their action consequences. A narrow numerical
error bar around an average does not answer that question.
\citep{exec14_causica_exact,exec14_causica_decision}

\subsection{Reuse choice and a bounded prototype}

The pinned root license is MIT. The package declares Python~3.10 and
Torch~2.0.0; its saved lock contains 277 packages, including Lightning,
TensorDict, NumPy and MLflow. These records provide a reproducible dependency
starting point, not proof that an installation or every dependency right has
been qualified. The saved repository metadata does not mark it archived,
but neither an activity timestamp nor a CI badge is a support commitment.
The README directs users of older Rhino and DDECI paths to a previous release.
The current tree contains adjacent temporal classes, yet the selected
intervention helper explicitly refuses temporal functional relationships.
These are distinct paths requiring distinct reviews.
\citep{exec14_causica_primary}

Direct adoption is deferred because it would inherit unresolved graph,
conditioning, scoring and environment boundaries. A wholesale port is not
selected: it would transfer neural, noise-distribution, optimization and
dependency maintenance without demonstrated benefit. The selected near-term
use is a pinned reference, followed by a possible narrow adapter that preserves
upstream mechanics and validates the contract at its boundary. A supplied
graph simulator and a graph-class effect analysis remain substantive
comparators; they expose different tradeoffs between structural commitments,
flexibility and uncertainty. \citep{exec14_causica_decision}

The first prototype can use public or generated mechanisms. It should check
known DAG propagation, reversed node order, intervention versus conditioning,
factual-noise preservation, scalar and vector effects, required cycles, failed
optimization, repeated interventions, temporal refusals and batch dimensions.
It should retain unsuccessful requests and separate fitted-model error,
simulation error and structural ambiguity. Training and simulation time,
dependency work, expert review, conversion failures and update maintenance
belong in the cost comparison. Bank experiments become justified only after
this bounded work demonstrates that the proposed integration preserves a
useful causal question and improves a declared workflow. No trained-model
performance, bank return or enabled integration is inferred from the source
review or exact examples. \citep{exec14_causica_decision}

\label{exec14:causica-contract-end}

\section{Native discovery software and the meaning of a C++ boundary}
\label{exec13:cxx-discovery}

A bank may choose a native library because it must fit a larger numerical
system, reduce data movement or control deployment dependencies. Those are
reasonable engineering motives, but they do not select a causal model. A
fast graph search can still answer the wrong question, discard the direction
ambiguity that matters to a rate decision, or reinterpret a repeated customer
history as independent observations. The useful comparison therefore starts
with the statistical object and only then asks which implementation can
preserve it.

Four roles need separate treatment. A native discovery engine takes data and
method assumptions to a graph or graph class. An R or Python package with a
C++ backend supplies that engine through a host-language contract, including
coercion, missingness and graph conversion. A graphical-model inference
library evaluates a model already supplied to it. A research demonstrator
may expose a valuable search idea without distributing a complete reusable
implementation. Counting all four as interchangeable ``C++ causal
libraries'' would obscure the investment choice.

The bounded comparison uses two repository-search routes, for causal discovery
and Bayesian-network structure learning, together with known engines and
direct upstream references. All eleven returned candidates are represented
below, with four known leads and two direct upstream leads. This is a
seventeen-candidate comparison of inspected releases, not an exhaustive
catalogue of every native implementation. The searches used a current index
with a creation-date filter; selected commit snapshots separately enforce the
7 October 2026 cutoff. Repository language metadata identifies a lead. The
implementation files, release terms and mathematical contract determine its
role. For example, the selected SVI-DAG model imports JAX and Flax and is
implemented in Python, although its repository was returned by the C++ query
\citep{exec13_cxx_svidag}.

continuous bank covariates or imputing missing histories requires a separate
scientific argument, not merely a conversion function.

then applies the collected edge removals. The CI implementation dispatches
Chi-square, G-square or an oracle graph, calculates contingency-table
statistics within observed strata, and returns an independence decision. It
also applies a Yates adjustment in one-degree-of-freedom strata, including
before the G-square branch. A comparison against another implementation
must reproduce that convention or explicitly compare different tests
\citep{exec13_cxx_openbnsl}.

The build's CUDA option is not evidence that GPU-PC is implemented. The
selected feature list separately leaves that item unchecked. Similarly, its
published timing examples remain the authors' reported benchmarks, not
measurements of the proposed bank workflow. The proposal would measure the
complete path from categorical encoding through CI tests, graph conversion
and a meaningful result, with timeouts and unsupported requests retained.
Neither a worker count nor a short kernel time measures that path by itself.

\subsection{Scores, conditional independence and functional assumptions}

The native implementations also show why a common parameter name is not a
common statistical interface. In PC-style search, \(\alpha\) commonly controls
the rejection threshold of an individual conditional-independence test. In
the inspected XGES command line, \texttt{alpha} defaults to two and multiplies
the BIC complexity penalty. In \texttt{ccdr.run}, \texttt{alpha} limits the
number of edges relative to the number of variables. Mapping all three to a
generic ``confidence'' setting would change the method
\citep{exec13_cxx_agrum,exec13_cxx_xges,exec13_cxx_ccdr}.

For a target variable \(j\) and candidate parents \(P\), the inspected XGES
scorer computes centered empirical covariance, solves the parent regression
using an LLT factorization, and forms residual variance
\[
 \widehat v_{j\mid P}
 =\widehat\Sigma_{jj}
  -\widehat\Sigma_{jP}\widehat\Sigma_{PP}^{-1}\widehat\Sigma_{Pj}.
\]
Its local score, when these operations are well defined, is
\begin{equation}
 s(j,P)=-\frac n2\{1+\log\widehat v_{j\mid P}\}
        -\frac{\alpha\log n}{2}(|P|+1).
 \label{exec13:cxx-bic}
\end{equation}
This equation describes the selected implementation; it is not a new
consistency theorem. The centered covariance uses divisor \(n\), and the
residual variance, factorization status and finite logarithm need explicit
checks in a prospective numerical comparison. The inspected overload that
accepts intervention indices computes the same covariance without using
those indices. Its signature alone therefore does not establish an
interventional score \citep{exec13_cxx_xges}.

By contrast, ccdrAlgorithm follows a regularization path rather than returning
one universal best graph. Its documented \texttt{gamma} chooses MCP or L1
regularization; its \texttt{sigmas} argument can fix conditional variances;
and its input must be numeric without missing values. These decisions affect
the family of fitted models. The path should be retained with its tuning and
stopping information, while a later selection rule is evaluated separately.
BNSL likewise exposes a criterion choice and a parent-set bound. A fast
search over a restricted family should be described as such, rather than
as an unrestricted optimum or a calibrated causal answer
\citep{exec13_cxx_ccdr,exec13_cxx_bnsl}.

Constraint, score and functional methods should consequently remain separate
comparison families. Constraint methods require a suitable CI test and the
graphical assumptions that make its independence information meaningful.
Score methods require a score model, search space and declared regularization
or prior. Functional approaches add restrictions on structural functions and
noise that may orient edges left ambiguous by a Gaussian independence model.
The earlier Python comparison supplies selected functional and continuous
optimization alternatives. The C++ search does not establish their absence
from every native ecosystem. It does reveal that neither a nonlinear
experiment's title nor a repository language label supplies a reviewed
native contract.

The selected rCausalMGM route illustrates the host-language issue. Its
\texttt{pcStable} documentation distinguishes numeric and categorical
variables, drops missing rows, accepts an optional initial skeleton and
returns a CPDAG under causal sufficiency. The native path calls its stable
skeleton search, regression-based CI machinery and orientation routines.
A sparse initial skeleton can prevent consideration of an omitted adjacency;
missing-row deletion can change the population. Both need to appear in the
scientific contract. Its \texttt{adjMat2Graph} documentation defaults
\texttt{directed} to false and does not fully specify endpoint orientation.
The selected evidence supports an integration candidate, while the exact
converter still needs delegated-source inspection and a runtime fixture
\citep{exec13_cxx_rcausalmgm}.

\subsection{A funding calculation that detects a reversed matrix}

Consider a synthetic structural model for a bank offer. Let \(A\) denote an
administered-rate offer measured in ten-basis-point increments, \(M\) retained
deposits measured in USD~10 million units, and \(Y\) annual contribution
measured in USD~1 million units. Take independent centered structural errors
and stipulate
\begin{align}
 A&=\varepsilon_A,&
 M&=\tfrac12 A+\varepsilon_M,&
 Y&=-\tfrac1{10}A+\tfrac25 M+\varepsilon_Y.
 \label{exec13:cxx-funding}
\end{align}
The negative direct coefficient represents the modeled cost of the higher
offered rate. The mediated route represents the modeled contribution of
retained balances. These amounts are a transparent synthetic accounting
example, not bank estimates \citep{exec13_cxx_examples}.

Define \(W_{ij}\) as the coefficient from source \(i\) to target \(j\), in
node order \((A,M,Y)\). Then
\[
 W=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2&-1/10\\0&0&2/5\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad X=W^{\mathsf T}X+\varepsilon.
\]
For this acyclic system, \(W^3=0\), and the total effect of a unit offer on
contribution is
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{A\to Y}
 =[(I-W^{\mathsf T})^{-1}]_{Y,A}
 =-\tfrac1{10}+\tfrac12\tfrac25=\tfrac1{10}.
 \label{exec13:cxx-total-effect}
\end{equation}
It corresponds to USD~100,000 annually. Direct substitution after
\(\operatorname{do}(A=1)\) gives the same result. An adapter that instead
interprets the same bytes as \(X=WX+\varepsilon\) reverses the direction
convention and returns zero for this query. Dropping the negative direct
coefficient while preserving the positive route returns USD~200,000. Both
errors can survive a check that only counts nonzero entries or compares
absolute weights.

This example requires a coefficient matrix, not an unweighted adjacency
matrix. A graph alone cannot supply the monetary calculation. In addition,
units must transform with the coefficients. For \(X'=DX\), with diagonal
positive unit-conversion matrix \(D\), the row-source coefficient convention
requires \(W'=D^{-1}WD\). The effect in the new units is
\((D_{YY}/D_{AA})\theta_{A\to Y}\). Converting only the displayed output label
would leave the arithmetic wrong. Exact rational calculations verify this
transformation, all six permutations of the three named nodes and an
independent topological substitution \citep{exec13_cxx_examples}.

The selected OpenBNSL header provides a concrete reason to test storage and
meaning separately. Its directed-edge query accepts \((u,v)\) in source-to-target
order, while its packed storage looks up the bit for \(u\) inside the parent
array indexed by \(v\). Copying rows of that parent array into a public
source-row matrix would transpose the relationship. The proposed adapter
must decode the public graph semantics rather than infer them from a memory
layout \citep{exec13_cxx_openbnsl}. The exact bitmap exercise in the project
checks this distinction; it does not execute OpenBNSL.

Undirected edges need a different check. In the inspected header, two
potential arcs represent one undirected edge, while one arc represents a
directed edge. A generic directed graph with both arcs would instead contain
a two-cycle. A correct boundary therefore carries an explicit graph type,
endpoint marks and node identity. It cannot infer causal cycles from the
number of bits, or erase the marks to satisfy a DAG-only downstream API.

\subsection{A correct orientation can still leave an effect unidentified}

Byte-faithful conversion is necessary, but scientific ambiguity remains even
after it succeeds. Consider a different, standardized Gaussian example with
covariance
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&1/2&1/5\\1/2&1&2/5\\1/5&2/5&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The partial covariance of \(A\) and \(Y\) given \(M\) is zero. A forward chain
with coefficients \(1/2,2/5\) and error variances \(1,3/4,21/25\) generates
this law. So does a fork \(A\leftarrow M\to Y\), with coefficients
\(1/2,2/5\) and variances \((3/4,1,21/25)\) in \((A,M,Y)\) order.
A reverse chain also generates it, with variances
\((3/4,21/25,1)\). Each variance is positive. Exact covariance reconstruction
checks equality for all three systems \citep{exec13_cxx_examples}.

Their offer effects are \(1/5,0,0\), respectively. If one outcome unit is
USD~1 million, the structural alternatives allow USD~200,000 or zero for a
unit action. Choosing an arbitrary DAG extension from a PDAG would choose
one answer without evidence for that choice. Averaging extensions would add
a distribution over graphs that the observed covariance does not provide.
The useful output is a graph class and its justified effect alternatives,
with any sampling uncertainty handled separately. This is the same reason
the pcalg integration retains an effect multiset rather than presenting its
mean as an identified monetary answer \citep{exec13_pcalg_reuse_primary}.

Native score search may be the appropriate computational component for that
workflow. It does not, by itself, supply the identification layer, a confidence
region over graphs, a decision-useful effect set or banking validation. The
proposal should budget separately for each missing component instead of
treating an executable graph search as the complete causal product.

\subsection{Why categorical encoding and predictive rules need their own review}

Categorical support does not imply that an arbitrary discretization preserves
the required adjustment information. Let a binary risk state \(R\) have
probability one half, and suppose \(A\) and \(Y\) are independent given \(R\),
each having success probability \(1/5\) when \(R=0\) and \(4/5\) when
\(R=1\). There is no structural arrow from \(A\) to \(Y\); changing \(A\)
does not change \(Y\). Treatment probabilities are positive in both strata.
Nevertheless, exact enumeration gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y\mid A=1]=17/25,\quad
 \mathbb E[Y\mid A=0]=8/25,\quad
 \mathbb E[Y\mid A=1]-\mathbb E[Y\mid A=0]=9/25.
\]
Collapsing the two risk categories into one bin destroys the conditioning
information and retains that spurious association. The population contrast
within each original stratum is zero \citep{exec13_cxx_examples}. A finer
encoding also has a cost: conditioning on five binary variables creates up
to thirty-two strata before the two tested variables are cross-classified.
Cell sparsity, omitted combinations and test approximations consequently
belong in the proposed evaluation. More bins do not automatically improve
inference at a fixed sample size.

A second example addresses the conditional-entropy lead in the finite search.
Its README describes low conditional entropy as a causal rule. Let
\(U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). In one structural model take
\(A=U,\ Y=A\); in another take \(A=U,\ Y=U\). Both models have identical
observed atoms \((0,0),(1,1)\), each with probability one half, and both have
\(H(Y\mid A)=0\). Their intervention contrasts are one and zero. Thus even
perfect prediction does not establish an offer's causal effect. This exact
counterexample concerns the claimed inferential rule; it is not a benchmark
of the downloaded program \citep{exec13_cxx_examples,exec13_cxx_other_leads}.

The inspected COJEC implementation creates an additional, narrower concern:
its selected rule uses the expression
\(p(x,y)\log p(x,y)/\log p(x)\), a threshold of \(0.08\) and a count condition
above nine. That expression is different from the full conditional entropy
\(-\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log\{p(x,y)/p(x)\}\). The source observation and the
identification counterexample justify declining this rule as a causal-effect
contract. They do not establish that association screening has no useful
exploratory role \citep{exec13_cxx_other_leads}.

\section{Native discovery when important borrower variables are hidden}
\label{exec14:cxx-latent}

A credit officer may observe an offer, recorded arrears and eventual recovery
while missing the household shock that affected all three. A useful discovery
component must preserve that possibility. Returning a convenient directed
graph can change the subsequent intervention question even when every number
and variable name survives the software boundary. The relevant native choices
are therefore compared by their graph meanings, input assumptions and failure
behavior, as well as by language and deployment requirements.

Three inspected families provide concrete alternatives. aGrUM 3.2.0 has a
native FCI path returning a partial ancestral graph (PAG). rCausalMGM 1.0.1
exposes C++ FCI-Stable through an R interface for mixed data. MIIC 2.0.4 also
has R and C++ sources, but carries information-based orientation scores and
consensus fields that need their own interpretation. The legacy MIIC repository
explicitly points to the newer R package. aGrUM's implementations named Miic
and SimpleMiic are separate source paths; the shared method name does not make
these packages interchangeable
\citep{exec14_native_agrum,exec14_native_rcmgm,exec14_native_miic}.

These are selected primary-source findings at revisions dated no later than
7 October 2026, acquired later. The repository searches and known alternatives
are bounded. A language-filtered search returned no FCI hits despite the
inspected native implementation, demonstrating why a zero search count cannot
establish absence. Other returned MIIC-like names concerned games, coursework
and unrelated applications. The broader scholarly search still has
uninspected leads. Thus the comparison supports a prototype choice without
claiming a complete census or executed engine qualification.

\subsection{aGrUM: preserve the native PAG before asking an effect question}

The inspected learner requires the FCI algorithm for \texttt{learnPAG()} and
rejects missing database values in that route. Its FCI implementation performs
skeleton discovery, collider orientation, possible-D-Sep pruning, endpoint
reset and repeated orientation rules. It also exposes conditioning-set and
path restrictions. Those settings belong in the result: shortening a search
is a scientific change as well as a resource choice. Source comments and
upstream test fixtures do not establish completeness or finite-sample coverage
of this particular implementation \citep{exec14_native_agrum}.

The native PAG distinguishes a circle, a tail and an arrowhead at each
endpoint. Its accessor \texttt{markAt(src,dst)} refers to the mark at the
\emph{destination}. An adapter must bind a mark to its node identifier, not
assume that a method's first argument names the endpoint being returned. A
circle records unresolved orientation within the represented class; a tail
or arrowhead asserts more. The statistical assumptions supporting that class
still need review, including the distinction between latent confounding and
selection. The earlier FCI/RFCI discussion supplies the theoretical context
\citep{exec13_latentr_colombo2012}.

The generic conversion is consequential. \texttt{PAG::toMixedGraph()} checks
only whether each endpoint is an arrowhead. Consequently $X\mathbin{\circ\!\to}Y$
and $X\to Y$ both become the same directed arc; circle--circle and tail--tail
both become an undirected edge. Two arrowheads become two opposite arcs.
The subsequent FCI mixed-structure bridge calls a routine that removes
opposing arcs, sometimes removing both in its fallback. This route is useful
for a different graph representation, but it cannot serve as a lossless PAG
export \citep{exec14_native_agrum}.

An exact representation calculation enumerates the nine local pairs from
the three endpoint marks taken in pairs. The conversion maps them to
four generic outputs with class sizes $4,2,2,1$. The point is not that all nine
pairs form globally valid PAGs. It is that the representation itself cannot
recover the original marks after conversion. The proposed boundary therefore
serializes native endpoints directly and retains the raw native graph as
evidence. No conversion test can by itself verify a discovered graph's causal
meaning \citep{exec14_native_oracle}.

\subsection{rCausalMGM: the R contract remains part of the native method}

The documented \texttt{fciStable} entrypoint accepts numeric continuous
variables and factor or character categorical variables. It drops rows with
missing values, can start from a supplied undirected skeleton, and defaults
to majority collider orientation and possible-D-Sep search. It also offers
max-p, conservative and separating-set rules. These alternatives can return
different statements; they are not merely display settings. A skeleton
screen that removes a true adjacency cannot be justified by the speed of the
later FCI pass \citep{exec14_native_rcmgm}.

The inspected C++ code initializes circles, applies an RFCI-style R0 pruning
step, optionally searches possible-D-Sep and delegates final orientations.
Turning off possible-D-Sep changes the route. Neither the method name nor a
successful call would establish equivalence to another package's RFCI output.
The exported R graph list includes edge strings, graph type, algorithm and
ambiguous triples. The native edge type has tail, arrowhead and circle fields,
plus star and null states. An adapter should handle supported states explicitly
and refuse the others, preserving ambiguous triples and configuration.

The inspected string parser splits an edge into exactly three space-delimited
components and recognizes selected endpoint spellings. Stable internal IDs
are therefore preferable to embedding business labels with spaces in edge
strings. A tiny round-trip fixture must check both directions, partial and
bidirected edges and unsupported tokens before admission. The source review
does not claim that this fixture has been run. Likewise, silently dropping
accounts changes the observation law: either justify the complete-case target
and selection premises, or refuse that requested analysis.

\subsection{MIIC: retain scores, modes and unresolved orientation conventions}

The successor MIIC package declares version 2.0.4, Rcpp linkage and imports
including ppcor, scales and stats. Its README requests a C++14 compiler and
the inspected build fragment delegates OpenMP flags to R. This supports an
R-hosted native candidate, not a claim of a standalone C++ service or successful
local build \citep{exec14_native_miic}.

The default latent setting is \texttt{orientation}, distinct from
\texttt{yes} and \texttt{no}. The output contains adjacency codes, two
orientation-score columns, information quantities, an optional shuffle-based
confidence ratio, and, under consistency iteration, graph-cycle and consensus
fields. A directed edge with an uncertain tail is different from the package's
stronger causal label. That label is conditioned on allowing latent variables
and disabling propagation in the inspected parser. These fields must travel
together; an adjacency matrix alone cannot preserve their meaning.

There is a concrete conversion question to settle before an adapter is enabled.
The manual describes a probability-matrix entry as referring to its row
endpoint, while the summary calls the entry at $[x,y]$ \texttt{p\_x2y}.
The native code stores an $x$-to-$y$ score in its $(x,y)$ edge and flattens
the array. The final R reshaping step and an asymmetric two-node fixture are
needed to resolve the exported convention. This is a source-level ambiguity
to investigate, not a demonstrated runtime defect. The proposal retains the
raw matrices and named summary columns until that issue is settled.

An orientation score, permutation ratio or frequency among iterated graphs
is not automatically a calibrated probability that a bank intervention has
a stated effect. The linked methodological originals remain to be inspected
before adopting their theoretical guarantees. Temporal mode additionally
declares stationarity; its presence does not qualify arbitrary account panels,
policy feedback or changing bank conditions. A separate temporal contract is
required.

\subsection{The banking consequence and the proposed reuse decision}

Consider two deliberately stipulated worlds with independent accounts.
Let $U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ and
$V\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)$ be independent. In the hidden-risk world,
$A=U$ and recovery is $Y=100+20U+5V$ dollars. In the direct-effect world,
$A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ independently of $V$ and
$Y=100+20A+5V$. Both worlds generate exactly the same observed distribution:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
(A,Y)&(0,100)&(0,105)&(1,120)&(1,125)\\
\Pr(A,Y)&3/8&1/8&3/8&1/8.
\end{array}
\]
An intervention replaces the equation for $A$, leaving the other structural
equations fixed. Its mean effect is zero in the first world and USD~20 in the
second. At an assumed USD~8 offer cost, the incremental values are USD~$-8$
and USD~12. Both the observation law and the arithmetic reproduce exactly;
the worlds are illustrative and supply no bank effect estimate
\citep{exec14_native_oracle}.

This example explains why preserving graph ambiguity can change the decision.
More records from this same law cannot distinguish the two stipulated worlds
without additional information or restrictions. A faster native search, a
language-generated edge or a graph conversion cannot supply that information.
The next scientific question concerns an admissible experiment, a measured
common cause or another defended identifying restriction.

For the first native prototype, prefer aGrUM's explicit FCI-to-PAG boundary
with direct endpoint serialization. Retain rCausalMGM through its R interface
as a mixed-data comparator and MIIC through its own score-and-graph contract
after the unresolved source issues are settled. Porting an entire R/C++ method
would create responsibility for preprocessing, graph semantics and upgrades
without a demonstrated deployment benefit. The existing pcalg path provides
another comparator; it should not be counted twice because it has native
dependencies.

Rights and maintenance also differ. The selected aGrUM files declare
LGPL-3.0-or-later or MIT. rCausalMGM declares GPL-3. MIIC's DESCRIPTION declares
GPL at least version 2, its README lists GPL-2 or GPL-3, and its bundled full
license is GPL-3. These inspected notices need file and dependency review
before redistribution. Process separation alone is not a license conclusion.
The prototype budget must cover dependency locking, wrapper maintenance,
round-trip and failure fixtures, and comparison against independent graph
oracles. None of the three engines has been executed or admitted by this
review; the evidence supports a testable reuse recommendation.
\label{exec14:cxx-latent-end}

\subsection{A funding example distinguishes ambiguity from a matrix error}

Consider three jointly recorded quantities: a funding-pressure score $U$, a
quoted-rate change $Q$ in percentage points, and retained funding $F$ in
millions of US dollars. The score is a summary measured over the observation
window; its name alone does not establish that it precedes the quote. The
following centered linear Gaussian constructions stipulate three different
causal explanations. Disturbances are mutually independent within each
construction, with variances stated explicitly:
\begin{align}
\mathcal M_1:\quad &U=\epsilon_U,\quad
 Q=\tfrac12U+\epsilon_Q,\quad F=2Q+\epsilon_F,\\
\mathcal M_2:\quad &Q=\eta_Q,\quad
 U=\tfrac12Q+\eta_U,\quad F=2Q+\eta_F,\\
\mathcal M_3:\quad &F=\xi_F,\quad
 Q=\tfrac25F+\xi_Q,\quad U=\tfrac12Q+\xi_U.
\end{align}
In variable order $(U,Q,F)$, the disturbance variances are respectively
$(1,3/4,1)$, $(3/4,1,1)$ and $(3/4,1/5,5)$.
Each construction induces
the same covariance in variable order $(U,Q,F)$:
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&1/2&1\\1/2&1&2\\1&2&5
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For example, under $\mathcal M_1$, $\operatorname{var}(Q)=1/4+3/4=1$,
$\operatorname{cov}(U,F)=2(1/2)=1$, and
$\operatorname{var}(F)=4+1=5$. Under $\mathcal M_3$,
$\operatorname{var}(Q)=4(5)/25+1/5=1$ and
$\operatorname{cov}(Q,F)=2$. The other entries follow directly.
Because the laws are centered Gaussian, equal covariance here implies equal
observational distributions. Also
\[
 \operatorname{cov}(U,F\mid Q)
 =1-\frac{(1/2)(2)}{1}=0.
\]
The three graphs have the same undirected chain and no unshielded collider.
They differ in what a quote intervention does.

Replacing the equation for $Q$ with a value increased by $1/4$ changes
expected $F$ by $1/2$ in $\mathcal M_1$ and $\mathcal M_2$, and by zero in
$\mathcal M_3$. Thus the intervention response belongs to
$\{0,1/2\}$ million dollars in this declared three-model family. This is a
finite ambiguity set, not a confidence interval and not a claim about every
possible causal model for the observations. A justified observation clock
could exclude some of these explanations; the statistical column label
cannot do so by itself. Exact rational covariance and intervention
calculations reproduce the construction~\citep{exec13_other_exact}.

Now write a supplied structural model as $x=Bx+e$, so that $B_{ij}$ is the
coefficient from variable $j$ to variable $i$. For $\mathcal M_1$,
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1/2&0&0\\0&2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The total $Q\to F$ slope is two. If an adapter transposes this matrix while
keeping the variable order and column-vector convention, the displayed graph
is still acyclic but its $Q\to F$ slope is zero. This is a conversion error
in a supplied model. It differs from the legitimate ambiguity across the
three observationally equivalent models: one should repair the conversion
error and preserve the genuine ambiguity.

Unresolved edges introduce another representation problem. A Julia
\texttt{DiGraph} may store both $i\to j$ and $j\to i$ to represent one
undirected edge. It is then a container for a partially directed graph, not a
claim that the causal system has a two-way feedback loop. A generic acyclicity
check applied before decoding will incorrectly reject that representation.
A generic linear-system solver applied to its zero-one adjacency will invent
coefficients and feedback. Both failures can occur without changing a single
stored byte.

\subsection{Julia: composability requires checking the actual dispatch}

CausalInference.jl 0.19.4 provides a useful starting point for a Julia
backend. Its inspected PC entrypoint accepts a conditional-independence
callback and defaults to the stable skeleton path. It returns a CPDAG in the
reciprocal-arc representation. Gaussian GES exposes a different computation:
forward insertion and backward deletion continue while a score improvement
is available. The selected function returns a graph, score improvement
relative to the empty graph, and timing information. Its default penalty is
$0.5$, which should not be confused with the explicit values used in examples
or with another library's score normalization
\citep{exec13_other_causalinference}.

The covariance-based local score makes this distinction precise. With $n$
rows, $k$ parents, penalty multiplier $\lambda$, and residual variance $C_p$,
the inspected expression is
\[
 s=-\tfrac12\{n[1+\log(\max(0,C_p))]
                +\lambda(1+k)\log n\}.
\]
For a nonsingular parent covariance block, $C_p$ is computed by subtracting
the explained covariance contribution. A larger value is preferred by the
search. If $C_p=0$, however, the displayed logarithm is not a finite number;
the zero clamp is not a positive numerical floor. The adapter should diagnose
constant variables, deterministic relationships, singular parent systems and
nonfinite scores before using a chosen graph in a funding decision. A
perfect numerical fit can signal degeneracy, not overwhelming causal
evidence. Score comparison across engines also requires matching centering,
penalties, constants and the exact returned quantity.

Associations.jl offers a broader composition of association measures,
independence tests and graph algorithms. That flexibility is useful when the
banking variables are nonlinear, but it does not make arbitrary compositions
valid. The PC documentation requires nondirectional pairwise and conditional
measures and specifically warns against inserting transfer entropy into this
generic symmetric skeleton procedure. The selected \texttt{check\_input}
contains the direction check only as commented code. The proposed wrapper
must enforce the compatibility condition itself and record which measure and
test were admitted~\citep{exec13_other_associations}.

Defaults also need source-level attention. The PC constructor uses
\texttt{maxdepth}, while the docstring presents \texttt{max\_depth}.
The local-permutation constructor defaults to ten neighbors, although its
documented signature says five. It exposes the number of shuffles, random
generator and a temporal exclusion window. These are concrete parameters of
the proposed statistical experiment. A temporal window alone does not prove
that a resampling scheme gives valid uncertainty for repeated accounts,
common market shocks or a changing assignment process. The parameter,
sampling unit and relevant null law must be reviewed together.

The selected skeleton removes an edge when the returned $p$-value exceeds
$\alpha$. This is a procedural choice made by the algorithm. Failure to
reject independence is not proof that the variables are independent,
especially with small samples or high-dimensional conditioning sets.
Likewise a finite orientation-sweep limit can leave the graph less fully
oriented than an unrestricted run. A result should disclose the restriction
and retain the partially resolved object, rather than infer a unique graph
from the fact that an execution completed.

RecursiveCausalDiscovery.jl 0.2.0 gives a narrower alternative. Its README
lists RSL-D as implemented while marking MARVEL, L-MARVEL and ROL as future
work. Its boundary initialization tests every pair conditional on all other
variables. That initial conditioning problem can be statistically difficult
even when a later recursive search reduces the number of tests. The selected
implementation also falls back to a smallest-boundary variable when it finds
no removable variable. The output alone does not reveal that the intended
removability condition was successfully established
\citep{exec13_other_recursive}.

A specific dispatch issue must be resolved before relying on separate
boundary and search tests. The matrix overload accepts a distinct
\texttt{mkbd\_ci\_test} argument but forwards \texttt{ci\_test} in its place
when converting to the table path. Source inspection therefore does not
support the claim that both overloads honor the supplied boundary test.
The README's Julia requirement and the broader manifest declaration also
differ. These findings justify retaining the package as an experimental
comparison, with explicit overload and environment checks. They do not
establish that recursive methods in general are unsuitable.

The three Julia families have inspected MIT grants. That makes a proposed
integration easier to pursue subject to the notices and dependency terms,
but creates no statistical warranty. The preferred first step is a restricted
Julia process with a frozen package environment, explicit table orientation
and graph decoder. Reimplementing every graph method in the common library
would add mathematical and maintenance obligations before demonstrating a
consumer need for the port.

\subsection{Rust: a native implementation still needs a statistical contract}

FLOP/flopsearch 0.3.0 already connects a Rust numerical search to Python and R.
Its selected interface accepts observations by rows, a BIC penalty and a
termination choice. It documents a CPDAG matrix with directed entries one and
unresolved entries two. This offers a plausible alternative to building a
new Rust foreign-function interface merely because the core is native
\citep{exec13_other_flopsearch}.

The stopping contract needs closer treatment than the word ``timeout''
suggests. The selected configuration requires exactly one of a restart
count, a timeout or manual termination. The Python stub states that at least
one local search runs to a local optimum. In the inspected Rust loop, the
shown abort check is conditional on being beyond the first restart; it exits
an inner search loop. The surrounding restart loop and delegated numerical
calls require further qualification before the product can promise a strict
elapsed deadline. A proposal adapter can impose an outer worker deadline,
but must then say whether interruption returns a validated best-so-far
graph, a checkpoint or only a failed request. It cannot imply that the
library's timer already provides the desired service guarantee.

The same entrypoint uses a thread-local random generator, while the selected
public configuration exposes no seed. This limits the replay contract that
can be promised from that interface alone. A frozen binary and dataset are
insufficient to establish identical stochastic output. Before a matched
benchmark, decide whether the adapter can bind the random state or must
compare declared output distributions over registered repeated runs. The
inspected MPL-2.0 grant also requires a distribution-specific review of
covered source and notices; using an existing wrapper does not remove that
obligation.

The static DirectLiNGAM path in varlingam-rs 0.1.0 presents a different issue.
Its coefficient assignment is explicitly $B[\mathrm{target},\mathrm{predictor}]$,
consistent with the column-vector convention used above. It selects an
ordering, uses a sparse predictor-selection step, and refits retained
predictors on their original scale. The README advertises matching accuracy
and substantial speedups, but those statements are not independent evidence
for the proposed banking workload~\citep{exec13_other_varlingam}.

The inspected ordering optimization constructs entropy summaries once.
Subsequent iterations update residual data, yet the root-scoring helper
ignores its data argument and uses the cached summaries. There may be a
mathematical argument for a particular cached formulation, but it has not
been established by reading the performance claim or this helper alone.
The appropriate next comparison freezes the intended algorithm, inspects
the optimization argument and delegated numerical helpers, and tests
matched cases where the evolving residual distribution matters. It would be
premature either to assert algorithmic equivalence or to declare every
cached strategy incorrect. The recommendation is to defer default adoption
and retain this as a candidate for focused review. Its inspected MIT or
Apache-2.0 choice resolves one part of reuse, not the algorithmic question.

why-rs 0.2.1 supplies a particularly clear reason to inspect code beneath an
algorithm name. Its selected PC propagation method implements one rule and
explicitly leaves the second and third rules for future addition. Its
independence calculation has hardcoded normal critical values for
$\alpha=0.01,0.05,0.10$; every other supplied value receives $1.960$.
The recursive partial-correlation calculation returns zero when its
denominator is zero. These are source-level findings about the pinned path,
not claims derived from executing a Rust benchmark~\citep{exec13_other_why}.

For example, a two-sided normal reference test at $\alpha=0.02$ would use
$\Phi^{-1}(0.99)\simeq2.32635$. At a stipulated statistic of $2.2$, that
reference does not reject independence, whereas the inspected fallback
$1.960$ does. Under an exactly standard normal reference law, the latter
threshold has rejection probability approximately $0.05$, not $0.02$.
This scalar calculation demonstrates a parameter mismatch; it does not
estimate an empirical false-positive rate for a whole discovery procedure
\citep{exec13_other_exact}. Returning zero for a degenerate partial
correlation can similarly turn an undefined calculation into an
independence decision. The proposed common adapter should preserve numerical
failure as a distinct outcome.

Together these findings justify rejecting this particular PC implementation
as the reference discovery engine. They do not require rejecting its graph
modeling or structural simulation ideas, which would need their own
inspection. A language with strong memory-safety features does not supply
missing orientation rules or validate a statistical reference law.

\subsection{MATLAB: reference value and reuse rights are separate}

Causal Explorer 1.5 documents a broad family of discovery and variable-selection
methods. Its PC manual specifies variables by columns, domain counts for
discrete variables, and mutual-information, $G^2$ or Fisher-$z$ choices.
The saved release states P-code compatibility with MATLAB R2017b. These
details make it a useful documented comparator, but neither they nor a
public repository establish an unrestricted source-code dependency
\citep{exec13_other_explorer}.

The accompanying license restricts the grant to noncommercial educational
and research use by an academic recipient on a single-user system, without
permission to redistribute or create derivative works. The manual's
``public domain'' wording cannot by itself resolve this conflict. Under the
saved grant, the proposed commercial library should reject direct adoption
and porting of this distribution. Separately obtained rights could change a
later decision. The proposal can already compare the documented capability
against alternatives whose reuse terms support the intended route. No
MATLAB execution, current platform compatibility or agreement to those
terms is implied by this comparison.

The generalized-score GES reference provides readable MATLAB search and
scoring code. Its selected criteria are negative cross-validated likelihood
or negative marginal likelihood, with separate paths for multidimensional
variables. The search seeks decreases in this criterion. This differs in
sign and meaning from the maximized Gaussian score described for Julia;
the raw numerical values are not a common ranking of engines. Its output
uses one for a directed edge and minus one for an unresolved edge, adding
another reason to decode by method identity
\citep{exec13_other_generalized}.

The inspected cross-validation score forms dense $T\times T$ kernel and
centering arrays for $T$ observations, computes data-dependent bandwidths,
and slices contiguous validation folds. One dense double-precision array
alone takes approximately $8T^2$ bytes before workspace, copies and other
arrays. This storage account follows from the array dimensions; it is not
a measured peak-memory benchmark. Contiguous slices do not automatically
constitute a valid forward-in-time banking evaluation. Training can include
observations later than the held-out block, and the displayed preprocessing
is computed before that block is sliced. A proposed dependent-data study
must explicitly decide what is known when, how kernels are constructed and
what the score is intended to validate.

The top-level GES rights also remain unresolved. The saved repository tree
contains a redistribution notice for an embedded kernel-test component,
but that grant does not establish permission for all surrounding code.
The immediate choice is to retain this methodological comparison and defer
redistribution or porting. A clean new implementation would still require
an inspected mathematical specification, independent verification and a
reason to maintain it alongside existing engines. Merely translating MATLAB
syntax would not resolve those obligations.

Bayes Net Toolbox provides a useful older reference for how distinct search
contracts coexist inside one toolbox. Its K2 entrypoint places variables in
rows and observations in columns. It greedily adds parents from predecessors
in a user-supplied ordering, with Bayesian or BIC score options. Thus the
ordering supplies directional restrictions that the procedure does not
learn. If a funding analyst orders quoted rate before retained funding,
the algorithm's inability to return a reverse parent is partly a consequence
of that input, not evidence against the reverse causal explanation
\citep{exec13_other_bnt}.

The selected BNT PC function instead accepts a conditional-independence
callback and returns a partially directed adjacency with negative one for
a directed edge and symmetric positive ones for an unresolved edge.
Data orientation, algorithm output and coefficient interpretation are three
separate conversions. Tests that verify only the number of rows or edges
will miss a semantic reversal. Its BNT license file explicitly uses the GNU
Library General Public License version 2; its name ending in
\texttt{license.gpl.txt} is insufficient to characterize every component of
the larger collection. Preserve the actual grant, review bundled components,
and test a selected modern environment before recommending adoption.
An old documentation date establishes neither current maintenance nor
current failure, and historical price references should not enter the
investment account as contemporary quotations.

\subsection{JavaScript: support the analyst without inventing an effect}

DAGitty's JavaScript core is a plausible component for constructing and
analyzing a proposed banking graph. Its selected adjustment interface
accepts DAG, PDAG, MAG and PAG types, reads source and target nodes, and
handles adjustment and selection information. The browser interface and R
frontend expose the same underlying family of graph operations
\citep{exec13_other_dagitty}. This is valuable for reviewing a claim such as
``adjust for the customer's prior relationship score.'' It does not prove
that the supplied arrows correspond to the bank's assignment process.

The intended integration therefore keeps graph authoring, graph analysis
and data-based discovery separate. An analyst can propose alternative
diagrams, attach an observation clock and identify whether an adjustment
query has an answer under each diagram. The proposed numerical service can
then report what estimation would require. The JavaScript graph must retain
its class and endpoint marks through those steps. Rendering a PAG as a DAG
to simplify an interface would give the analyst a stronger assumption than
the source result supports. The inspected GPL-v2 terms and dependency
notices require an appropriate distribution design; process separation
alone does not establish exemption from those terms.

The selected expert-in-loop JavaScript demo illustrates a more subtle
boundary. Its \texttt{pillai\_test} fits random forests for two variables on
the conditioning variables, predicts on those same rows, computes residuals,
and obtains canonical correlations. Its \texttt{effectSize} field is the
sum of squared canonical correlations, followed by an $F$-reference
calculation. The display logic can combine that statistic with an arrow from
the supplied graph~\citep{exec13_other_webapp}.

A squared canonical correlation is an association statistic. It is
dimensionless and does not provide the sign or monetary units of the
funding response above. Attaching an arrow to it does not turn it into
$\partial\mathbb E[F\mid\operatorname{do}(Q)]/\partial Q$.
Nor does the inspected formula establish a calibrated null distribution
after fitting flexible residual models on the same rows. A serious
integration would need a stated statistical target, an argument or measured
calibration for the actual fitting procedure, and appropriate held-out or
cross-fitted alternatives. The product can use the interface idea for
discussing disagreements while declining to display the statistic as a
banking treatment effect.

The package metadata declares ISC, while the selected tree does not supply
a complete top-level grant sufficient for this review. Its test command is
a placeholder that exits unsuccessfully. Neither fact proves that the
interaction idea is unusable; both prevent treating a successful package
installation or a license field as a completed adoption assessment.
The recommended next use is a separately designed graph-review interaction,
with any numerical comparison delegated to an admitted scientific method.

\subsection{An integration sequence that preserves useful alternatives}

The proposed first implementation should admit a small set of mathematical
operations. One Julia PC/GES path can test the independence and score-search
boundary; FLOP can test an existing native engine behind an R/Python
interface; DAGitty can test graph review and adjustment. Each has a concrete
reason to be considered. The unqualified Rust paths, unresolved MATLAB
rights and browser residual statistic need not block those independent
experiments. They remain explicit alternatives or rejected current choices,
with the condition that would change the decision.

A common adverse-fixture suite should verify both input and result meaning.
The funding construction supplies a weighted DAG whose transpose changes
the effect, an unresolved chain with several compatible effects, and graph
mark conventions that cannot be read as coefficients. Other fixtures should
include constant variables, singular covariance, a caller-supplied
nondefault significance level, changed variable order, a limited conditioning
depth and an interrupted search. Each engine must preserve an undefined
calculation or unsupported request as such. Agreement on a convenient
regular dataset is inadequate if the adapter silently turns these cases
into a definite causal answer.

Matched comparison must then separate four families of assumptions.
Constraint methods depend on their selected independence tests and graph
premises. Score methods depend on likelihood or surrogate criterion,
penalties and search restrictions. Functional methods rely on the declared
structural and disturbance restrictions, including the non-Gaussian
conditions developed earlier. Continuous optimization methods impose a
numerical acyclicity condition in addition to their statistical objective.
The selected other-language sources do not complete the census of
functional or continuous methods; the earlier Python comparison supplies
concrete alternatives without implying that a Julia or Rust equivalent is
absent. No implementation family should inherit another's causal
qualification solely because both return a matrix.

Only after these semantic checks should a bounded prototype compare runtime,
memory, failed requests and the consequences for an actual decision rule.
Freeze the source and environment identities, data units, baselines,
stopping criteria and failure account before final evaluation. These
requirements define a proposed comparison; the source inspections and exact
examples here do not claim to have executed it. The investment choice is
therefore specific: integrate a few well-understood paths, preserve useful
alternatives, and spend on porting only when an identified consumer or
measured bottleneck makes that additional responsibility worthwhile.
\label{exec13:other-discovery-end}

\section{Latent discovery beyond the principal language

A lender may observe that an intensified contact policy accompanies
higher repayment. Unrecorded borrower distress can influence both
contact allocation and repayment, while the analysed records may include
only accounts that reached a particular servicing channel. A discovery
component must distinguish unmeasured common causes, conditioning on
selection and an unresolved causal direction. A common matrix interface
is useful only if it carries those distinctions into the intervention
query.

Julia, Rust, MATLAB and JavaScript offer different parts of this
workflow. The selected implementations below include an FCI path, a
functional-model method, a recursive skeleton learner, a
latent-measurement model, a tuning layer and graph analysis. They are
not interchangeable implementations of one estimand. The bounded
repository survey retained the earlier language leads and added five
targeted searches. The Rust and MATLAB latent searches returned only the
first twenty of 640 and 74 results; other queries contained mirrors and
unrelated matches. The search index was accessed after the 7 October
2026 cutoff, while inspected implementation claims use earlier pinned
source revisions. These limits preclude a complete historical census or
a claim that unrepresented languages lack suitable software.
\citep{exec14_olatent_search}

\hypertarget{julia-fci-preserve-the-graph-before-improving-the-interface}{%
\subsection{Julia FCI: preserve the graph before improving the
interface}\label{julia-fci-preserve-the-graph-before-improving-the-interface}}

CausalInference.jl exposes FCI through an independence-test callback and
through table interfaces for Gaussian and conditional-mutual-information
tests. Its result is a marked graph. For vertices \(i\) and \(j\), the
property on the stored arc from \(i\) to \(j\) describes the endpoint at
\(j\); the reverse arc stores the endpoint at \(i\). A tail--arrow pair,
two arrowheads and two circles therefore have different meanings even
though the container has two arcs in each case. A process adapter should
serialize the ordered endpoint pair and graph type explicitly.
Interpreting two stored arcs as two causal directions would invent
feedback. \citep{exec14_olatent_causalinference}

The implementation supplies useful components: an initial skeleton, a
possible-separation search, orientation rules and an option controlling
the additional orientation rules. The selected tests include a graph
with hidden variables and a selection example that closes over the
selected node in its oracle. Those tests show what the authors intended
to exercise. They are not a measured finite-sample coverage guarantee
for the proposed lender. \citep{exec14_olatent_causalinference}

Several operational details need to enter a prospective qualification.
The possible-separation and selected orientation searches request at
most one hundred shortest paths per endpoint pair. A claim that all
relevant witnesses have been considered needs an argument for that cap,
an explicit restricted regime, or a different search. Separating subsets
are materialized as power sets; a path cap does not make subset
enumeration cheap. The initial skeleton call also omits the keyword
arguments that the later independence calls receive. A selection
argument passed only through those keywords can therefore give the two
stages different questions. Binding the intended selection law inside
one tested callback is a concrete way to avoid that discrepancy, subject
to comparison with an independent oracle.
\citep{exec14_olatent_causalinference}

Graph presentation is another boundary. The inspected plotting
preparation creates one display edge and assigns styles for several
endpoint combinations, but has no explicit branch for two arrowheads.
The raw result and the upstream tests can contain that combination. A
new adapter should consume the raw endpoint properties and refuse an
unsupported display conversion. This is a static source finding; no
rendered Julia failure has been observed here. The root MIT notice makes
a narrowly reviewed integration plausible, but dependency notices, an
installed Julia environment, bounded resource behavior and matched graph
tests remain necessary. Rewriting the algorithm in another language
would add obligations without resolving these semantic questions.
\citep{exec14_olatent_causalinference}

\hypertarget{a-rust-rcd-label-does-not-establish-equivalence-to-the-published-method}{%
\subsection{A Rust RCD label does not establish equivalence to the
published
method}\label{a-rust-rcd-label-does-not-establish-equivalence-to-the-published-method}}

The original RCD paper specifies a linear acyclic model
\(X=BX+\Lambda F+E\), with mutually independent non-Gaussian external
influences. Its procedure repeatedly learns ancestor sets, distinguishes
parents from indirect ancestors and then identifies unresolved
confounded pairs. Its stated target does not identify the direct
relation between a pair that shares a latent confounder. This is a
stronger distributional regime and a different output contract from an
arbitrary latent-graph procedure. \citep{exec14_olatent_rcd_paper}

The inspected varlingam-rs path instead starts with a DirectLiNGAM
adjacency, examines residual dependence only for pairs without a direct
coefficient above \(10^{-10}\), and proposes to refine the adjacency by
reducing the coefficients of those flagged pairs. Its dependence score
combines absolute correlation with an entropy-based contrast. The
threshold is described as a p-value in a configuration comment, but the
returned quantity is not a calibrated p-value. The reported pair
confidence is the capped score. Connected groups of flagged pairs
receive latent effects computed from averages of those scores, rather
than estimated coefficients of an identified latent-variable model. The
inspected basic test prints a result without asserting that the
stipulated confounded pair was recovered.
\citep{exec14_olatent_varlingam}

The refinement rule has a useful exact implication. Let \(b_{ij}\)
denote an initial coefficient, let \(\epsilon=10^{-10}\), and let
\(c_{ij}\) be a flagged-pair score. A pair is considered for refinement
only if both directions have magnitude at most \(\epsilon\). Refinement
multiplies those entries by \(1-c_{ij}/2\). Therefore every entry whose
initial magnitude exceeds \(\epsilon\) is unchanged throughout the loop.
With exact zero entries, the proposed correction multiplies zero by a
factor and leaves it zero. Stopping when the number of flagged pairs is
unchanged does not establish convergence of a causal objective; the
declared coefficient-convergence tolerance is not used in this path.
This reasoning concerns the inspected formulas, without claiming that
the upstream engine was executed.
\citep{exec14_olatent_varlingam,exec14_olatent_oracle}

Consider a declared banking mechanism with independent centered
variables \(U,E_A,E_Y\), each of variance one:

\[
A=U+E_A,\qquad Y=\tfrac12 A+2U+E_Y.
\]

Here \(A\) is contact intensity and \(Y\) is a recovery deviation
measured in hundreds of dollars. The common cause \(U\) is hidden
borrower state. Since \(\operatorname{var}(A)=2\) and
\(\operatorname{cov}(A,Y)=3\), the population regression coefficient is
\(3/2\). The effect of increasing \(A\) by one unit is \(1/2\). At an
imposed intervention cost of USD 80, the corresponding net values are
USD 70 from the associational calculation and minus USD 30 from the
structural intervention. The projection permits both a directed effect
and a latent common-cause relation; retaining a directed coefficient
alone is insufficient. If an adapter receives \(3/2\) as an initial
substantive coefficient, the inspected refinement rule cannot change it.
This last statement is conditional on that input, not a claim that
DirectLiNGAM was run and returned it. \citep{exec14_olatent_oracle}

The proposal should reject this path as a default qualified RCD backend
and defer porting it. The published method remains a substantive
alternative, alongside a graph-class method when its assumptions are
appropriate. A revisit requires an explicit algorithmic correspondence
or a clearly different method claim, an inspected numerical dependency
closure, meaningful recovery tests and a registered comparison including
the failures above. The Rust package's MIT/Apache notices do not resolve
the scientific discrepancy or the remaining dependency review.
\citep{exec14_olatent_varlingam,exec14_olatent_rcd_paper}

\hypertarget{matlab-contributes-several-distinct-kinds-of-latent-analysis}{%
\subsection{MATLAB contributes several distinct kinds of latent
analysis}\label{matlab-contributes-several-distinct-kinds-of-latent-analysis}}

The selected L-MARVEL implementation returns a skeleton, separating sets
and conditional-independence counts. Its recursive removal logic uses
estimated Markov boundaries; the supplied initializer tests each pair
conditional on all other variables. The example evaluates skeleton
precision and recall. These outputs can support a discovery pipeline,
but the selected entrypoint does not return a fully oriented PAG or an
intervention effect. \citep{exec14_olatent_lmarvel}

The matrix is initialized with \(-1\) for unresolved entries. If no
removable variable is found, the routine exits its loop; unresolved
entries must not be silently converted to present or absent edges. One
removability subtest uses the fixed significance argument \(0.8\), even
when the main call uses \(0.01\). The independence helper inverts a
sample correlation matrix and combines a Fisher transform with a Student
distribution function, without an explicit singularity or
positive-degrees-of-freedom guard in the inspected function. These
concrete choices require an explained test contract and adverse cases,
including nearly collinear risk measurements and too few accounts for
the conditioning dimension. The root MIT notice is useful, while
embedded helper attributions and the MATLAB numerical/toolbox
environment still require review. The appropriate route is an optional
skeleton component after those checks, not a silent replacement for FCI.
\citep{exec14_olatent_lmarvel}

The GIN reference implementation addresses latent measurement structure.
It takes variables in rows, centers them, finds clusters of observed
variables, and orders latent variables. That layout differs from the
samples-in-rows interface of the selected L-MARVEL code. Its inspected
condition test derives a contrast from a singular-value decomposition of
a cross-covariance block and tests the contrast against other
measurements using a bundled kernel independence routine. The default
ordering path delegates to a mutual-information helper; the alternative
independence-test path is not the default. Neither cluster labels nor an
estimated order identify a monetary effect without a further model and
query. \citep{exec14_olatent_gin}

A small algebraic diagnostic clarifies one implementation risk. Suppose
\(Y_1=U+E_1\), \(Y_2=U+E_2\) and \(Z=U+E_Z\), with mutually independent
centered components of variance one. The cross-covariance vector is
\((1,1)^\top\). The nonzero vector \(\omega=(1,-1)^\top\) satisfies
\(\omega^\top\operatorname{cov}(Y,Z)=0\), and its contrast
\(Y_1-Y_2=E_1-E_2\) is independent of \(Z\) by construction.
Nevertheless, \(\sum_i\omega_i=0\). The selected source treats a zero
sum of the contrast coefficients as a trivial solution. A zero sum is
not a zero vector; the example has squared norm two and contrast
variance two. A real SVD returns a normalized vector subject to
rounding, so the runtime outcome requires an actual fixture rather than
an assumption about exact floating-point equality. The diagnostic still
identifies the invariant the proposed adapter must check.
\citep{exec14_olatent_gin,exec14_olatent_oracle}

The complete pinned GIN tree contains component notices for the bundled
kernel code but no inspected repository-wide grant covering the whole
method. The component notice cannot be promoted into permission for

\section{Reusing R bnlearn without losing graph, intervention or sampling meaning}
\label{sec:exec24-bnlearn-reuse}
A banking team may want one workflow that learns a dependency graph, fits local distributions, simulates an intervention and reports a customer-level counterfactual. These are different inferential tasks even when a package exposes them through neighboring functions. R bnlearn provides a useful concrete case for the proposed library: preserve the package's graph and fitted-model semantics, state the extra causal assumptions, and refuse a stronger interpretation than the supplied information supports. The present decision is to retain the selected release as a reference and a conditional original-worker candidate. It does not enable a native adapter.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\subsection{Identity, maintenance and a bounded reuse choice}
The selected CRAN mirror revision is 7d44622a52c29c2d666a8661d048e2b5709a7944, dated 17 July 2026, and declares package version 5.2.1. Its DESCRIPTION names Marco Scutari as maintainer, requires R at least 4.4.0 and compiled C17 support, declares GPL version 2 or later, and lists optional packages for graph conversion, inference, precision and regularization. The mirror is a distribution snapshot; its activity is not the author's complete development history or a promise of future support. Installed-library equivalence, transitive terms, build provenance and an operator remain to be qualified.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources}

The Python project with the same name is a distinct repository and distribution. Its pinned README describes Python workflows and links to another inference ecosystem. It is not an alias for the R package or evidence that the same functions, licenses, numerical conventions or objects are interchangeable. A source-identity record must therefore include language, repository, distribution and revision, rather than just the string bnlearn. The current review makes no technical or rights admission for that namesake.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_identity,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

Scutari's original article explains the historical separation between structure learning, local parameter estimation, conditional-independence tests and score-based search. Its discrete and Gaussian examples help motivate an interface that preserves data types and model assumptions. The locally retained author version is arXiv revision 2, dated 10 July 2010, with placeholder journal issue fields; it is not silently relabeled as the publisher's final typesetting. Its 2010 algorithm and computing environment cannot establish the capabilities or behavior of the selected 2026 source.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_original}

\subsection{An equivalent graph is not an equivalent intervention}
The inspected CPDAG documentation distinguishes an equivalence class from one consistent DAG extension. It also explains that parametric restrictions, such as the permitted direction between discrete and continuous variables in a conditional Gaussian model, can preserve directions independently of observational evidence. A returned orientation must therefore carry its provenance: compelled by the observational class, imposed by background knowledge, forced by the model family, or selected as an extension.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources}

An exact authored example shows why this distinction changes a banking decision. Let $A$ be a standardized action intensity and $Y$ a standardized response. Both variables have mean zero, variance one and covariance $1/2$. One Gaussian structural model is
\[
 A=U_A,\qquad Y=\tfrac12 A+U_Y,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(U_A)=1,\quad \operatorname{Var}(U_Y)=\tfrac34,
\]
with independent disturbances. Another is
\[
 Y=V_Y,\qquad A=\tfrac12 Y+V_A,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(V_Y)=1,\quad \operatorname{Var}(V_A)=\tfrac34,
\]
again with independent disturbances. Both generate exactly the same bivariate Gaussian observation law. Increasing $A$ by one unit changes expected $Y$ by $1/2$ in the first model and by zero in the second. At a stipulated 100 USD per unit response and a 20 USD action cost, the corresponding net values are 30 USD and minus 20 USD. More samples from that unchanged observation law cannot select the correct action. External direction information, a justified restriction or an intervention is needed.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_contract}

The package's causal frontend rejects a partially directed or cyclic graph for the inspected graph-to-SCM, intervention and counterfactual branches. This is a useful syntactic boundary. It does not establish that an accepted DAG is the correct causal graph. The proposed interface retains the CPDAG and the reason for each additional orientation; it never silently turns one consistent extension into an identified effect.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\subsection{A twin graph needs a specified cross-world model}
The inspected graph conversion creates one exogenous parent per factual node and checks generated labels for collisions. Twin construction shares exogenous nodes between factual and counterfactual copies. For a fitted Gaussian network, the implementation constructs fitted twin components. For other fitted-network classes, the inspected frontend falls back to a graph construction, which does not retain a full fitted counterfactual distribution. A caller must inspect the returned class and content before claiming a numerical counterfactual.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources}

Even an interventional response distribution need not identify an individual's cross-world outcome. Let $A$ and $U$ be independent fair binary variables. In world one set $Y=U$ for either action; in world two set $Y=U\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}A$. Both worlds give the same observed joint distribution, with probability $1/4$ for each $(A,Y)$ pair, and both give $P(Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(A=a))=1/2$ for either $a$. For a customer observed with $A=0,Y=0$, however, the counterfactual $Y(1)$ is zero in world one and one in world two. These are explicitly stipulated structural models; the second need not satisfy a faithfulness restriction. Their purpose is to show the information a cross-world claim requires. A convenient twin representation does not supply that information by itself.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_contract}

Intervention values also have a distributional meaning. The selected discrete fitted-node branch gives equal mass to the supplied set of levels. Supplying two levels is therefore not a declaration of an arbitrary 90/10 stochastic intervention. With losses zero and 100 USD, an equal mixture has expected loss 50 USD whereas a 90/10 mixture has expected loss ten. A request for a stochastic policy must carry its probability law and use a qualified path that preserves it.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

A separate static metadata question concerns Gaussian interventions. The inspected branch changes the intercept and sets the standard deviation to zero, but assigns the requested fixed values to the residual field before zeroing that field; it does not assign them to the stored fitted-values field in those lines. With stored fitted values $(1,2)$ and a requested fixed value five, the literal field assignments retain $(1,2)$ while residuals become zero. This is a targeted cache-consistency test for a future native worker, not an observed error in its returned intervention distribution.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\subsection{Bootstrap strength is a statistic with a sampling law}
The selected bootstrap frontend defaults to 200 replicates, a sample size equal to the number of input rows and conversion to a CPDAG for arc strength. The generic backend samples rows with replacement. A customer's repeated visits or a common calendar shock are therefore not automatically respected as independent sampling units. In an authored mean example with 20 independent customer clusters, 25 visits per customer, unit marginal variance and within-customer correlation 0.6, the mean variance is 0.0308. Treating all 500 visits as independent gives 0.002. That arithmetic is not a coverage theorem for graph search; it explains why a row-resampling option cannot certify the graph's sampling law.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

The inspected C counter gives half weight in each direction to an undirected arc. It computes adjacency strength by adding the two directional counts and direction by dividing one directional count by that adjacency strength. Suppose 100 equally weighted graph replicates contain 20 $A\to Y$ arcs, 40 undirected $A-Y$ arcs and 40 absences. The forward counter is 0.4, the reverse counter 0.2, adjacency strength 0.6 and reported forward direction $2/3$. Only 0.2 of the replicates actually contain the directed arc. None of these quantities is automatically a posterior probability that a causal orientation is true, or a confidence interval for its effect.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

Model averaging adds another transformation: the inspected backend retains strengths strictly above the selected threshold, always retains strength one, filters directions at least 0.5 and invokes a cycle-aware delegate where required. The returned graph and the complete strength table should both survive conversion. Thresholding must not erase unresolved orientations, missing edges or the distinction between stability and causal truth.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

The generic bootstrap's remapping of manipulated observations deserves an independent test. Its inspected branch applies a first-match operation to each original manipulated row index. For an original manipulated row two and a bootstrap index vector $(2,2,3,1)$, that operation returns only position one, whereas the replicate contains manipulated copies at positions one and two. This exact indexing witness motivates a targeted test of the selected score and experiment-label path. It does not claim that every bootstrap algorithm takes that branch or establish an observed end-to-end package failure.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\subsection{Conditional queries need denominators and retained weights}
The query documentation distinguishes logic sampling from likelihood weighting. Logic sampling retains observations matching the evidence; the number accepted can be much smaller than the number generated. The inspected probability routine returns zero when no simulated observations match. That zero conflates a numerical convention with an unresolved conditional query if an adapter reports it without the accepted-sample denominator. A future worker should return a distinct insufficient-evidence status, its generated and accepted counts and a justified Monte Carlo diagnostic.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

Parallel query aggregation also requires an explicit estimator. The inspected dispatcher takes the mean of worker probability estimates. If one worker has one accepted sample and one event success while another has nine accepted samples and no success, the mean of ratios is $1/2$ but the pooled ratio is $1/10$. These are different finite simulation statistics. This witness does not establish a general bias claim; it requires the product to name the aggregation law rather than suggesting a pooled denominator it did not use.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

Likelihood-weighted samples carry a weights attribute. Dropping that attribute while exporting a table changes the estimate: with outcomes $(0,1)$ and weights $(0.1,0.9)$, the weighted mean is 0.9 and the unweighted mean 0.5. The inspected C distribution sampler explicitly errors on all-zero or all-missing weights, while the R probability path ends with a weighted numerator divided by a weighted denominator. These are distinct failure interfaces to test. The adapter should bind weights, effective information, missingness, random streams and error status, rather than converting every numerical object into a successful causal answer.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\subsection{A concrete proposal decision and public prototype route}
Retain bnlearn as a reference for graph-class, fitted-model and query contracts. A future pinned R worker is a conditional candidate because it preserves native object semantics more directly than a whole-library port. Defer native admission until exact distribution and dependency rights, graph/class conversions, sampling units, numerical diagnostics and failure behavior are qualified. Reject an automatic DAG-extension-to-causal-effect path, silent weight removal, default zero for an unresolved query and a port justified only by language preference. Alternatives include an independent small exact enumerator, another separately qualified graph learner and a distinct symbolic identification stage.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_contract}

The proposed CLI/MCP request includes graph type and orientation provenance; variable states and units; population and information clock; model family and noise assumptions; fitted parameter identity; observational, interventional or counterfactual target; intervention law; sampling unit; seed, budget and computational limits. The response preserves the source object class, ambiguity, weights, denominators, convergence and refusal status. A typed query language should be translated through an allowlisted expression grammar; source examples that evaluate R expressions do not justify exposing unrestricted expression execution to a general caller.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract}

The next experiments can use generated finite networks and public data with documented permissions. Predeclare graph equivalence, counterfactual-coupling, intervention-mixture, manipulated-row, rare-evidence, parallel aggregation and weighted-export tests. Compare independent exact answers and complete workflows before using authentic bank data. The scientific owner supplies causal and sampling assumptions; the adapter owner supplies conversion and refusal behavior; rights and platform owners bind distributions and dependencies; the evaluation owner records all attempts and complete costs. At a stipulated 1,200 USD total, 100 requests, 80 conformant outputs and 50 useful outputs imply 12, 15 and 24 USD per respective unit. These costs explain denominators; they are not measured prices or adoption evidence.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_contract}

\section{LiNGAM reuse: model assumptions, matrix meaning and bootstrap summaries}
\label{sec:exec24-lingam-reuse}

A bank may ask whether a funding-price change affects retained balances
through service quality as well as directly. A learned directed matrix
can organize that question, but only a declared structural model gives
its entries intervention meaning. The selected LiNGAM source is a useful
reference for a bounded linear discovery prototype. Its many static,
latent and temporal interfaces must be qualified separately.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source,exec24_lingam_choice}

The inspected revision is dated 8 September 2026 and its initializer
reports version 1.13.0. The selected technical files and newly acquired
build, requirements and initializer text authenticate to the preserved
repository tree. A repository version string does not establish equality
with an installed wheel. The top-level MIT text is retained; transitive
distribution rights, binary dependencies and supported deployment remain
unverified. No downloaded package is imported or executed here.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

\subsection{A discovery model must match the banking information problem}
The inherited original LiNGAM model assumes linear acyclic equations,
mutually independent non-Gaussian disturbances and causal sufficiency,
with independent observations of the variable vector. Those restrictions
are substantial for a bank with common calendar shocks, endogenous
customer selection or missing operational causes. The original ICA-based
argument is not a proof for every current DirectLiNGAM option or for the
entire proposed banking workflow.~\citep{exec13_pydisc_shimizu2006}

An LLM may help elicit candidate variables, units, clocks and prohibited
directions, but those suggestions require scientific review. They cannot
establish independent shocks or the absence of hidden confounding. The
proposed interface must retain the human-approved assumption set and its
source, and return an unsupported or conditional result when that model
does not fit the question. A latent-variable or longitudinal class elsewhere
in the package is a separate candidate, not automatic repair of this model.
\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}

The selected DirectLiNGAM path validates the data shape and prior matrix,
iteratively selects a causal order, residualizes other variables and then
estimates adjacency. It exposes hard or soft prior knowledge, several
independence measures and optional adaptive-Lasso pruning. The inspected
dispatch has explicit kernel and GPU branches and an alternative branch
for the pairwise routine. A wrapper should whitelist intended options
rather than rely on an unrecognized string reaching that alternative.
The GPU branch requires an additional dependency and separate qualification.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

\subsection{Matrix orientation, path priors and total effects are distinct}
The coefficient convention is $B_{ij}$ for the effect of predictor $j$
in the equation for target $i$, so
\[
 X=BX+\varepsilon.
\]
For a strictly acyclic three-variable system, $B^3=0$ and
$(I-B)^{-1}=I+B+B^2$. An authored example uses
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\2&0&0\\-1&3&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The direct first-to-third coefficient is $-1$, while the mediated path
contributes $2\cdot3=6$, giving total derivative $5$. If the third variable
is measured in thousand USD and the stipulated intervention changes the
first by $1/5$ unit, the assumed gross response is 1,000 USD. Subtracting
an assumed 800 USD cost gives 200 USD. These calculations condition on
the invariant equations; they are not learned graph evidence or observed
bank returns.~\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}

The inspected prior-knowledge utility writes a declared path from $i$ to
$j$ into matrix position $(j,i)$. Path restrictions are not simply a list
of direct edges. The model conversion must retain the node order and the
distinction between required paths, forbidden paths and unknown relations.
Round-trip fixtures should use an asymmetric chain and an indirect path,
so a transpose or a path-to-edge substitution cannot pass unnoticed.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source,exec24_lingam_choice}

The base estimation method fits a destination on the source and its
estimated parents. It warns when the fitted causal order places the
destination before the source, but still returns a regression coefficient.
A separate utility sums products over directed paths in a supplied matrix,
with a declared continuity restriction. These two numerical constructions
must remain named separately; agreement in an idealized model is not a
guarantee of equality after finite-sample pruning or model error.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

Adaptive-Lasso selection standardizes the data, derives weights from
ordinary regression and applies a BIC criterion. It then refits retained
predictors on original units. The returned coefficient is therefore not
merely the raw penalized coefficient on the standardized design. A faithful
port would need to preserve the selection rule, centering, scaling,
retained columns and final refit. An external worker can preserve that
behavior with less reimplementation, but still needs native fixtures and
versioned dependencies.~\citep{exec24_lingam_source,exec24_lingam_choice}

\subsection{Bootstrap frequencies and selected-effect summaries have different denominators}
The generic bootstrap resamples rows and refits the model, retaining
adjacency matrices, path-based total effects and resampled indices.
Its local resampling call does not receive the object's random-state
attribute explicitly. Reproducibility therefore needs an inspected RNG
contract, not an assumption based only on a constructor parameter.
The loop mutates the fitted object through successive fits; a worker must
declare whether the returned object represents the initial fit or the
last replicate.~\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

The bootstrap result's probability method counts coefficients above an
absolute threshold. Its total-effect summary calculates the corresponding
threshold frequency, then takes a median among nonzero effects using a
different filter. For authored replicate effects $(0,1/10,9/10)$ and
threshold $1/2$, the threshold frequency is $1/3$. The nonzero-effect
median is $1/2$, whereas the median restricted to values above threshold
is $9/10$. The two summary fields describe different selections.
Neither the frequency nor the conditional median is, by itself, a
posterior probability that an edge is true or a confidence interval for
the intervention effect.~\citep{exec24_lingam_source,exec24_lingam_choice}

The probability method also converts missing matrix entries before
thresholding. A proposed result contract should retain nonfinite entries
and their origins as explicit states rather than silently interpreting
them as evidence of edge absence. Failed fits, empty selections, zero
effects and unestimated directions need separate statuses. Every request
and replicate belongs in the availability denominator.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source,exec24_lingam_choice}

Independent row resampling does not automatically respect common shocks
or repeated servicing histories. Under a stipulated law of four independent
unit-variance shocks, their mean has variance $1/4$; under a single shared
unit-variance shock observed four times, its mean has variance $1$.
The sample count alone cannot select the correct resampling law.
These are authored probability calculations, not package coverage results.
\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}

The selected VAR bootstrap uses a separate residual-resampling and
sequence-reconstruction path. It disables the lag-selection criterion
before fitting and restores it after normal completion. That path does
not automatically include lag-selection uncertainty, and its local
restoration is not protected by a surrounding finally block. A bounded
worker needs exception cleanup, original-versus-resolved settings,
initialization history and a scientific argument for the innovation law.
Those source observations do not establish a native failed run.
\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

\subsection{A reference choice and a conditional worker route}
The selected build metadata requires Python at least 3.10, scikit-learn
at least 1.6 and SciPy at most 1.13.1, alongside declared numerical,
graphical, statistical and modeling dependencies. The requirements text
explains selected compatibility constraints and lists the GPU package as
optional. The initializer imports numerous model families. A narrow use
case still requires a reproducible environment and inspection of the
actual dependency and import path; a successful source audit does not
qualify an installation.~\citep{exec24_lingam_source}

The present choice is source reference. Conditional integration through
an isolated Python worker is preferable for a justified prototype once
scientific premises, environment, rights and fixtures are qualified.
Direct adoption of a general bank discovery service is deferred. A full
port is deferred until measured constraints justify maintaining equivalent
graph, pruning, solver, RNG and bootstrap behavior. Constraint-based or
score-based discovery can address different model assumptions; inherited
Tetrad and other-language comparisons remain separate candidates with
their own limits. An authored matrix oracle is useful for conversion
checks, not a replacement discovery algorithm.
\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}

The scientific owner must approve variables, clocks, assumptions and
intervention meaning. The adapter owner must preserve orientation, path
priors, matrix versus regression effects, thresholds and refusal states.
The platform and rights owner must qualify the full distribution and
resource envelope. The evaluation owner must preregister independent and
dependent known-law fixtures, adverse priors, non-Gaussianity departures,
hidden confounding, constant columns, failed fits and RNG replay.
An illustrative 4,200 USD workflow over 210 requested, 175 finite and
140 accepted results costs 20, 24 and 30 USD respectively; measured
all-request compute, engineering, validation and maintenance remain unknown.
\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}

Public fixtures can test the proposed worker before costly bank experiments.
They cannot certify an unknown business graph, create unobserved causes
or supply a universal dependence correction. Selected static, temporal,
latent and nonlinear families require their own qualification. Execution and independent validation remain outstanding.
\citep{exec24_lingam_choice}
