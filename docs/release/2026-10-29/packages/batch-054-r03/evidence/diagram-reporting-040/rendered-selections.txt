70.3.2     Unavailable observations and correlated answers

Now suppose dH returns no observation with probability 1/4, independently of the world, and
every attempt still costs USD 5. On failure the posterior remains the prior and the bank
abstains. Gross expected value becomes (3/4)28 = 21, and all-attempt net value is USD 16.
Restricting the comparison to successful acquisitions would instead report USD 23. Both
calculations can be arithmetically correct, but they answer different questions. A purchase
or investment decision needs the attempted-work denominator. If failure depends on H, the
failure event itself has a likelihood and must enter the posterior; treating it as independent
missingness would be another model error.

70.3.3     A finite stopping rule and the danger of a narrow model pool

Suppose at most two useful signals are available and their errors are conditionally independent
given H. Always purchasing both gives expected terminal value 148/5 = 29.6 and costs
USD 10, leaving net value 98/5 = 19.6. This is below the one-query value of USD 23. A
conditional calculation verifies the stopping decision. After a positive first signal, the best
possible gross increment from a second useful query is 16/5 = 3.2; after a negative signal it is
zero. Both are below its USD 5 cost. A query about Z adds no deployment value at either
history. Thus, for this menu and horizon of at most two, the optimal policy purchases one
useful query and stops. This result does not establish that a myopic stopping rule is optimal
for arbitrary longer horizons or complementary experiments.

assigns probability one to good outcomes and predicts USD 100 from deployment. If the
omitted bad world is true, deployment instead yields USD −120. No entropy calculation
confined to the single-model pool detects the omission. The bank therefore needs sensitivity
to model support as well as uncertainty within the pool. Challenging a favorable mechanism

74.6     Monitoring requires a statistical design and an owner
A service can monitor whether an artifact hash changed, whether a response arrived, whether
a measured distribution shifted, or whether a causal claim remains applicable. These are
different events. The first two admit direct engineering checks. Distributional change can
sometimes be tested under a specified sampling law. Continued causal applicability depends
also on assignment, measurement, support and target population. The catalog example’s
observationally equivalent worlds demonstrate why an unchanged observed distribution need
not settle an intervention contrast. Repeated queries to the same observations cannot remove
that ambiguity. (Causal banking proposal, 2026bf)
