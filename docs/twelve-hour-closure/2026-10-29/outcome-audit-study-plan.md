# Random follow-up after outcome-dependent dropout

Question: Can a known random follow-up design repair the preceding three-decision servicing example without assuming routine dropout is independent of the unseen future shock? How much extra information does a specified audit buy, and what happens if follow-up is itself selective?

Use the exact existing generated state, reward and historical action law. Preserve every old experiment. Evaluate fixed threshold0.5 against never acting; do not relabel a fixed-policy result as selected-policy performance. Full latent histories are available only to truth generation and deliberately sampled follow-up records.

Derive an estimator using every routine observed reward plus inverse-probability sampled missing reward histories. Let K be the observed part of a full action-weighted policy score, M its missing part, and B a Bernoulli audit with known q among customers with incomplete records. Then Z=K+B M/q has conditional mean K+M, regardless of how routine dropout depends on future outcomes. Derive Var(Z)=Var(K+M)+(1/q-1)E(M²) under constant independent audit probability, and the paired-policy counterpart. A learned residual predictor must be fitted independently and cannot require unobserved inputs; consider it only after the basic comparison is verified.

Run two mechanisms: conditionally independent routine dropout and future-shock-dependent dropout. Compare complete full-history diagnostic weighting, original g-weighted estimator, and random-audit repair at q=.05,.10,.25,.50,1. Include a violated audit design that depends on an unseen outcome but is analysed as constant q; this must retain failure rather than grant repair by declaration. Use same datasets and coupled audit draws for fair comparisons. Collect audit counts, recovered rewards, bias, RMSE, interval coverage, width, effective information and positive-lower-bound frequency for the fixed policy improvement. Report Monte Carlo and numerical-reference uncertainty separately.

Start with500 replications,n2000,per routine-dropout mechanism and500 for selective-audit failure; exact known propensities and reward rule. Use bounded independent batches and retained result directories. No bank prices invented. Express added data cost symbolically per audited customer and show the marginal variance tradeoff. Derive a design rule under a fixed expected budget only when all required quantities are estimable from a separate pilot.

Review: random follow-up is a known two-phase sampling idea,not a new universal identification theorem. Recover authentic primary sources for fair attribution. Complete histories must be recoverable; informative nonresponse to the audit,incorrect inclusion probabilities,unrecorded confounding,interference or follow-up changing the outcome invalidate the repair. Random audit cannot repair treatment confounding. Usual Gaussian intervals are evaluated,not promised exactly calibrated. Preserve low-audit heavy-tail failures and confidence intervals that remain too wide for the illustrative policy gain.

Promotion criterion: a reproducible scientific explanation and an explicit design choice, including a negative precision result, integrated into the monograph. Prototype and bank qualification remain later.
