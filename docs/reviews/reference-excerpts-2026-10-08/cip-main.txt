\noindent\begin{mdframed}[style=reportbox]
\textbf{Notation reminder.}\quad
Throughout Parts~II--VI, the \emph{model} sign convention is used for
the cross-currency basis: $x_t(\tau) > 0$ when dollar funding is
scarce (intermediary constraints bind).  This is the \emph{negative}
of the Du~et~al.\ market convention ($x_{t,t+\tau}^{i/\$} < 0$ when
dollars are scarce).  The reason for the difference: the market
convention arises naturally from defining the basis as the residual in
the CIP equation (negative when synthetic dollar borrowing is
expensive), while the model convention arises naturally from the
no-arbitrage pricing recursions, where the SDF loadings produce a
positive basis when intermediary constraints tighten.  A full
explanation and formal reconciliation are in
Chapter~\ref{ch:cip} (box after Definition~\ref{def:ch02_basis}) and
Section~\ref{sec:basis_reconciliation}.
\end{mdframed}
