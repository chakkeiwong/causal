A warning in a case note is not the
effect of a policy

A bank reading thousands of case notes faces two different questions. Which borrowers appear
to be in difficulty? Would a change in the terms offered to them reduce that difficulty? A
language model can help answer the first and organise evidence for the second. It can also
make the distinction harder to see. If borrowers receiving relief are already in worse condition,
a model may correctly associate the words describing relief with subsequent default. The
association can be large even when relief helps.
   Three studies in pharmacovigilance make this distinction concrete. Their subject is medical
reporting, not banking, but the proposal faces the same question about what a text-derived
comparison means. InferBERT combines an ALBERT classifier with comparisons between
reports containing and not containing a feature. DeepCausality extends the approach to
named entities in free text. A later assessment compares general, biomedical and task-specific
BERT models. These are useful sources for text processing and candidate discovery. Their
intervention interpretation requires a separate examination of the actual calculation. (Wang
et al., 2021, 2022, 2023)

  The distinction survives a very good classifier. If m(W ) = E(Y | W ) exactly and both E

                      GE = E(Y | E = 1, S = 1) − E(Y | E = 0, S = 1).                        (1.3)

This is a difference between selected populations. It need not equal the effect of changing
treatment in a common population. A small standard error in (1.2) can establish that the
populations differ while leaving that identification question unanswered. General calibration
by score alone is weaker than the conditional prediction equality used here; the argument
deliberately grants the classifier the stronger property.
   There are also limits to what can be reproduced from the inspected revisions. The Infer-
BERT scoring routine filters one-sided normal-reference probabilities at 0.05. In the public
DeepCausality notebook that filter is commented out, although the paper discusses adjusted
probabilities. The notebook still calculates and exports probabilities and ranks terms. That
difference does not prove that the published numerical results are wrong: further analysis
may have occurred elsewhere. It does mean that this notebook alone does not document the
paper’s complete multiplicity procedure. The two revisions are fixed at their May 2021 and
November 2022 commits, respectively. (Wang, 2021, 2022)


1.2     What the reported results establish
InferBERT constructs two FAERS-based tasks, using 36,661 analgesics reports and 27,245
tramadol reports after processing. Its upstream source is the commercially curated
PharmaPendium database. The study uses a stratified 64:16:20 training, development and
test split. Thus public availability of FAERS in general is not evidence that an independently
prepared public extract reproduces this particular curated cohort. (Wang et al., 2021, pp. 3–4,
Table 1)
   DeepCausality constructs 14,361 sentences from LiverTox, carrying the drug-level likelihood
classification into sentence labels. It reports a stratified 90:10 sentence split and selects an
optimised model using the reported test-set classification results. That evaluation should not
be described as an untouched held-out assessment on new drugs or new patients. Nor does the
split alone establish that a particular amount of leakage occurred. The paper reports that
18 of 20 enriched terms agree with clinical knowledge; it also acknowledges that the method
cannot distinguish confounders from colliders. These statements support a role in finding
relevant concepts, while delimiting a role in selecting adjustment variables. (Wang et al., 2022,
pp. 5–8, Table 1; pp. 9–11, Table 2 and Discussion)
   The 2023 assessment offers a useful warning against choosing a model solely by classification
accuracy. In its LiverTox task, several models report an accuracy of 0.91, while the expert-
reviewed relevance of enriched terms differs. The reported positive predictive value for
DILI-BERT is 0.95, compared with 0.20 for RoBERTa and 0.25 for SciBERT. The paper’s
quantity labelled predictive positive rate is T P/(T P + F P ); Figure 5 concerns the relevance
of enriched terms. None of these numbers is an estimate of the effect of prescribing a drug,
granting a loan concession, or changing a collection policy. They are reported source results,
not independently reproduced performance in this proposal. (Wang et al., 2023, pp. 1911–1916,
Table 4 and Figure 5)
  The proposal should therefore retain both lessons. Domain-specific training can change
which concepts a model ranks as relevant, even when its headline classification score changes

   The example identifies a practical danger. Rejecting relief because its mention predicts
default would reject a beneficial policy in the second economy. Better text prediction does
not resolve the comparison, because the prediction itself is already correct. The missing
information concerns why the policy was assigned.


1.4     A constructive route: text as measured baseline informa-
        tion
The appropriate conclusion is conditional adoption, rather than abandoning text. In the
constructed economy, observing truthful baseline U would justify comparing relief within its
two levels and then averaging over the common baseline population. The resulting effect
is exactly −τ . Randomising relief would provide a different route by breaking its relation
with baseline risk. Neither route is produced merely by relabelling a classifier contrast as an
intervention.
  There is a constructive literature on preserving adjustment information while reducing
text to a manageable representation. Veitch et al. (2020) begin by assuming that the text
contains enough information for adjustment. Their Theorem 3.1 gives conditions under which
an embedding preserves the relevant conditional outcome or propensity information; Theorem
1.5. What to adopt, integrate and decline                                                       5


3.2 adds estimation conditions. The initial sufficiency assumption is essential. An embedding
cannot acquire an omitted baseline cause simply by predicting the observed outcome accurately.
For the hardship example, a baseline narrative would need a defensible relationship to U ; the
noisy signal R alone does not become sufficient by being written in fluent language.
   The target population must also remain explicit. Veitch and colleagues develop an effect on
the treated, with equation (2.1) averaging over treated units. The present constructed compar-
ison is an average effect in the full population. A bank must select its target before choosing
the weights in an estimator. The source’s theorem display omits the treated conditioning in
its final expectation while the earlier definition and estimators retain it; the proposal uses the
explicit target definition rather than silently changing the population. (Veitch et al., 2020,
Section 2, equations (2.1)–(2.3); Section 3, Theorems 3.1–3.2)
   For a practical evaluation, the bank would first fix the decision time and separate baseline
information from later descriptions of treatment and outcome. It would then assess extraction
accuracy, agreement with audited baseline facts, the assumptions supporting adjustment,
overlap between treatment groups, and effect uncertainty. Borrower- or document-grouped
splits and a final unused evaluation set address predictive evaluation; they do not by themselves
supply missing confounding information. Public documents and generated assignment laws
can test these distinctions before an expensive institutional experiment is justified.


1.5     What to adopt, integrate and decline
The reusable idea is a traceable route from documents to candidate variables and ranked
associations. Adopt that conceptual separation. Integrate the extracted term, supporting
passage, information date, eligibility rule and score as evidence for a reviewer. Preserve whether
a term denotes a treatment, a baseline cause, a symptom, a consequence or an uncertain
relation. A relevant term should not automatically become an adjustment variable or a directed
causal arrow.
   Direct reuse of the inspected implementations requires further work. Both repositories
target TensorFlow 1.15; DeepCausality specifies Python 3.6 and external BioBERT weights
and BERN results. The repositories refer to external data downloads, and InferBERT’s cohort
derives from commercial curation. The inspected InferBERT tree has no repository-wide
licence file. In DeepCausality, an Apache licence accompanies the embedded BioBERT subtree;
the project-specific scoring notebook has no explicit licence notice. These observations leave
the permission for copying the project-specific code unresolved; a dependency’s notice does
not answer that question. No legal clearance or native reproduction is claimed here. (Wang,
2021, 2022)
   For the proposal, favour an independently specified, tested implementation of the disclosed
mathematical comparison if that diagnostic is useful and its permitted scope is settled. Keep
it labelled as an association or hypothesis-ranking result. Decline its direct promotion to a
general intervention estimator. A separate effect calculation should require a defined policy,
time ordering, identification assumptions and appropriate uncertainty. This preserves the value
of language assistance while preventing a familiar business error: mistaking the customers
selected for a policy for the customers’ response to it.
