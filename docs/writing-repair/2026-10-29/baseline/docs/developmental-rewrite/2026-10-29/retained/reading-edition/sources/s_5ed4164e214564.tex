\section{Julia and gretl temporal workflows: a common clock is part of the model}
\label{sec:exec25-other-dynamic}

A funding analyst comparing a panel estimator, a local projection and a state-space forecast needs more than the same variable names. The bank must decide whether one lag means one calendar period or one observed row, whether a shock has unit variance or unit impact, whether a covariance conditions on estimated parameters, and whether missing components remove an entire observation. These choices determine the scientific target before the implementation language matters. The present comparison examines four Julia projects and gretl's C implementation and command environment. It complements the R, Python, JVM and C++ reviews without claiming that a finite set of packages exhausts the remaining ecosystems.

The selected authentic revisions precede 7 October 2026. DynamicPanelModels.jl reports version 0.4.0 at a 1 October 2026 revision; LocalProjections.jl reports 0.2.1 at 26 April 2024; MacroEconometricTools.jl reports 0.1.1 at 22 September 2026; StateSpaceModels.jl reports 0.8.0 at 24 August 2026. The gretl source version is 2026c-git at 7 October 2026. A repository date and version string identify the reviewed bytes; they do not establish a tested installed artifact. In particular, source-level findings below are conditional descriptions and proposed native tests, not measured failures of published binaries.~\citep{exec25_odyn_dpm,exec25_odyn_lp,exec25_odyn_macro,exec25_odyn_ssm,exec25_odyn_gretl}

\subsection{DynamicPanelModels: sorting, differencing and instrument clocks}

The Julia panel constructor explicitly rejects duplicate entity/time pairs and groups a sorted table by entity. Those are valuable safeguards for a bank panel. They do not by themselves establish one calendar convention throughout the estimation path. In the inspected transformation, lagged regressor values are shifted by row position. A separate first-difference check admits a row only if its current and previous time labels differ by one. Instrument construction then indexes earlier positions in the globally observed time-label vector and looks up the selected value for the entity. These three operations must be compared as a composed contract.~\citep{exec25_odyn_dpm}

An authored example uses dates $(1,3,4)$ and levels $(10,30,40)$. At date four, the current and previous dates are adjacent, so the current first-difference gate passes. A row-shifted lagged regressor contains values 30 and 10 at the two relevant rows, yielding a difference of 20. A one-calendar-period lagged difference would require the level at date two, which is absent. The same globally observed label sequence can make a nominal second-lag instrument select date one rather than date two. This does not show that all requests are wrong: regular complete calendars avoid this particular discrepancy. It shows why the adapter must declare the integer calendar grid and test a gap followed by adjacent dates, not merely a gap at the current pair.~\citep{exec25_odyn_dpm,exec25_odyn_choice}

The forward-orthogonal-deviation path subtracts future observed-row means and applies the corresponding count-based scaling. Its suffix sums propagate missing values. A calendar-grid transformation, an observed-row transformation and a transformation using only finite future values are different conventions. The admissible sample and instrument matrix must be recorded with the result. The moment conditions also need the usual substantive restrictions on errors, initial values and the roles of regressors; a label such as system GMM does not verify those restrictions for bank data.

The numerical path distinguishes regressors from instruments. Collinear regressors are rejected; optional instrument pruning uses pivoted QR, a relative diagonal tolerance and the selected pivot columns returned in original order. The actual pivot selection is more precise than an informal promise to retain the earliest member of each collinear set. The subsequent count comparison is useful but is not a proof of identification or valid uncertainty. For example, with QR diagonal magnitudes $(1,10^{-10})$, a relative tolerance $10^{-9}$ retains one direction, including after scaling both magnitudes by $10^4$. A different absolute cutoff can change the retained count under that rescaling. This authored witness defines an interoperability test, not a Julia linear-algebra benchmark.~\citep{exec25_odyn_dpm,exec25_odyn_choice}

The proposal retains DynamicPanelModels as a bounded Julia adapter candidate after calendar, instrument-identity and covariance tests. Direct adoption requires the exact transformed row keys, retained instrument columns, covariance choice and refusal reason. A port must reproduce those decisions from a reviewed specification, including any deliberate repair, rather than copy only a coefficient solve.

\subsection{Local projections: horizon samples and dependence are explicit choices}

A local projection estimates a separate relationship for each horizon, schematically
\[
 y_{t+h}=a_h+\beta_h s_t+\Gamma_h'w_t+u_{t,h},
\]
where $s_t$ is the proposed shock and $w_t$ contains the declared controls. Identification depends on the shock and information set, not the availability of a regression routine. Even before identification, horizon $h$ changes which observations are available. The inspected LocalProjections.jl path constructs row-position slices for regressors and outcomes, filters finite numeric values, and stores a separate sample count for each horizon. Options also determine whether IV first stages and weak-instrument diagnostics are recomputed by horizon.~\citep{exec25_odyn_lp}

With 12 dates and two presample lags, horizon zero can use origins three through twelve. If the outcome at date five is missing and the illustrative regressors remain complete, nine origins survive. At horizon two, eligible origins three through ten lose origin three because its outcome is date five, leaving seven. The missing observation removes different origins at different horizons. A curve of estimated responses can therefore combine different populations and information sets. A common-sample comparison may be appropriate, but it is an additional design decision with its own loss of observations; it must not be silently imposed or silently assumed.~\citep{exec25_odyn_lp,exec25_odyn_choice}

The covariance alternatives are substantive. The inspected heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-robust path supplies a cosine-transform long-run covariance, a bandwidth rule and corresponding critical-value and p-value functions. The cluster path instead delegates to Vcov.jl. These names do not imply equivalent uncertainty for shared calendar shocks, few periods or few banks. A qualification request must freeze the estimator, score ordering, bandwidth or clustering key, effective sample, degrees of freedom and interval construction. Complete Vcov and fixed-effect delegates, numerical tolerances and installed dependencies remain separate work. References mentioned in package comments do not import their scholarly theorems into this comparison.

LocalProjections is retained as a conditional integration candidate for explicit horizon and dependence contracts. A generic replacement of all temporal estimators by one projection API is rejected. Where native comparison later succeeds, the library, CLI and MCP should expose the horizon-specific row digest and uncertainty method so that a downstream agent cannot mistake different sample composition for a changing causal mechanism.~\citep{exec25_odyn_lp,exec25_odyn_choice}

\subsection{Proxy VARs: unit impact and unit shock variance are different}

MacroEconometricTools provides a recursive covariance-factor route and a selected proxy-VAR route. The recursive path factors a covariance under the chosen ordering and restores original indexing. The proxy path fits a first stage with an intercept, derives impact ratios and sets the target-variable coefficient to one. Its instrument helper accepts an array with the same residual-row count, or keeps the last required number of rows from a longer array. That is positional alignment, not a join on dates or an audit of the proxy's information clock. The displayed first-stage threshold is a relevance diagnostic convention; it cannot establish exclusion, timing or stability.~\citep{exec25_odyn_macro}

Let $b$ be an impact vector normalized so that its target coordinate is one. A unit-variance shock impact is generally $q=\sqrt{\omega}\,b$, with a scale $\omega$ that must be justified. The inspected completion routine places $b$ in the target column, subtracts $bb'$ from the reduced-form covariance $\Sigma$, clips negative eigenvalues of the remaining principal block, and fills other columns and cross terms. The equality $PP'=\Sigma$ must therefore be checked rather than inferred from the returned matrix shape.~\citep{exec25_odyn_macro}

An exact witness takes
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 b=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The selected completion algebra yields a first-column contribution of one to the first variance, whereas the reduced-form variance is $1/4$. Filling the other coordinate cannot repair that $3/4$ discrepancy. This is an authored scale counterexample to conflating unit effect with unit shock variance; it is not a theorem about all proxy estimators or an execution of this package. The appropriate next test includes the exact normalization, reconstructed covariance, identified column and arbitrary completion columns separately. Only the instrument-identified response should receive the corresponding causal interpretation.~\citep{exec25_odyn_macro,exec25_odyn_choice}

The proposed decision is reference use pending version-specific normalization and alignment qualification. Direct admission of a full returned impact matrix as independently identified is rejected. A repair may require an explicit shock-variance scale, a bounded partial-identification output or refusal; additional scientific restrictions, not eigenvalue clipping alone, determine which route is justified.

\subsection{StateSpaceModels: partial missingness and covariance time indices}

The state-space alternative addresses a different target: a latent state and future observation law conditional on model parameters. In the selected time-invariant multivariate filter, a predicate that finds any NaN in the observation vector sends the entire vector through the missing-observation branch. The state transition and process covariance still advance, but observed coordinates do not receive a measurement update on that branch. The time-varying multivariate path has a separately structured likelihood update, including an update after its missingness branch, so behavior must be qualified per dispatch path.~\citep{exec25_odyn_ssm}

A simple authored witness starts with a scalar state having mean zero and variance one. Of two potential measurements, suppose one is absent and the other equals two with unit measurement variance. Using the observed measurement gives gain $1/2$, posterior mean one and posterior variance $1/2$. Skipping the entire observation vector preserves mean zero and variance one before the transition. Both behaviors are expressible in an API, but they do not deliver the same inference service. A bank feed with asynchronous measurements needs an explicit partial-observation contract.~\citep{exec25_odyn_ssm,exec25_odyn_choice}

Forecast indexing also deserves a known-law test. The output storage records innovation covariance at index $t$ and the next predictive state at $t+1$. The no-exogenous forecast selects future covariance using an index ending in $+i$, while the inspected exogenous forecast uses the corresponding expression ending in $+i-1$. For a stored sequence containing the last observed variance seven followed by future variances eleven and seventeen, the desired two future entries are $(11,17)$; selecting one entry earlier produces $(7,11)$. Constant covariance can hide this discrepancy, so the fixture must vary observation or state uncertainty over time. This is a source-index finding for the selected generic path; specialized methods require their own review.~\citep{exec25_odyn_ssm,exec25_odyn_choice}

The forecasting routines copy fitted hyperparameters into the new model and propagate the state-space law. That is not automatically integration over parameter-estimation uncertainty. The fitting path also maps selected numerical failures to a fixed optimizer penalty and can retain NaN standard errors after Hessian failure. An adapter must preserve optimizer diagnostics, covariance status and the distinction between conditional prediction and uncertainty over fitted parameters. A finite forecast is not evidence that the parameter uncertainty was successfully quantified.

StateSpaceModels is retained for bounded state-family comparison and possible integration after partial-missingness, covariance-index, optimizer and parameter-uncertainty tests. A universal causal state-space interface remains unsupported without a causal model and its identifying information.~\citep{exec25_odyn_ssm,exec25_odyn_choice}

\subsection{gretl: public command contracts and numerical repair must agree}

The gretl dynamic-panel implementation offers distinct option paths for two-step estimation, covariance correction and collapsed instruments. Its first weighting-matrix inversion can fail, compute a rank mask, reduce the instrument system and retry, with error codes retained. The inspected rank-mask delegate uses QR diagonal magnitudes and an absolute threshold of $10^{-8}$. This is a different contract from the relative pivoted-QR rule described above. Under the authored diagonal witness $(1,10^{-10})$, the absolute rule retains one direction; after multiplication by $10^4$, it retains two. This comparison motivates scale-aware parity tests and records the effective instrument set; it does not imply that either method universally dominates.~\citep{exec25_odyn_gretl,exec25_odyn_choice}

The native panel routine uses the declared dataset layout to find entity-specific usable positions and dependent-variable lags. It records those positions for residual placement. Upstream dataset construction, period frequency and padding therefore belong in the command-level qualification. A row layout that merely has the right dimensions is insufficient. Full serial-correlation, covariance-correction and command-to-native paths still need matched runtime fixtures and exact dependency review.

The VAR response API distinguishes a point response from a bootstrap result. Without a data matrix, or when the interval parameter is zero, the inspected route returns the point estimate; otherwise it calls a separate bootstrap delegate. An adapter must expose that branch and cannot label a point-only response as an interval service. Likewise, the selected Kalman missing-step helper advances the state and adds process covariance while recording zero measurement contribution. A scalar unit-variance witness evolves through variances two and three after two missing steps, preserving the elapsed model clock. Other Kalman variants and partial-observation paths remain separately scoped.~\citep{exec25_odyn_gretl,exec25_odyn_choice}

The proposal treats gretl as a potential command-process and C-library integration candidate. The command-process choice can make complete input and output records easier to retain, but does not establish binary compatibility, permissions or legal clearance. Its selected sources declare GPL version 3 or later; the four Julia project roots carry named MIT declarations. Complete dependency rights, compiled artifacts, interface stability and operating costs remain unqualified. No downloaded code was executed for this review.~\citep{exec25_odyn_gretl,exec25_odyn_dpm,exec25_odyn_lp,exec25_odyn_macro,exec25_odyn_ssm}

\subsection{An ordered public test program across independent candidates}

The first test group concerns representation: unique entity/date keys, gaps followed by adjacent dates, absent periods, lagged instruments and horizon-specific rows. The second concerns scientific semantics: recursive versus proxy restrictions, unit impact versus unit variance, identified versus completed response columns, and conditioning versus intervention. The third concerns uncertainty: rank thresholds under rescaling, cluster and long-run covariance, partial missingness, forecast time indices, parameter uncertainty and numerical failure. The fourth concerns reproducibility and operation: artifact hashes, dependencies, request/response equivalence across library, CLI and MCP, complete failure denominators and measured costs. Independent candidates can advance through their own tests without waiting for an unrelated source-access problem.~\citep{exec25_odyn_choice}

Every comparison requires a project-authored reference and a frozen target law before native experiments. For example, a hypothetical USD27,000 study costs USD30 per 900 attempts, USD45 per 600 completions and USD60 per 450 accepted outputs. Those arithmetic facts do not establish measured efficiency or bank value. Public data and known-law generators can justify further expenditure, while bank assignment truth, exclusions and operating acceptance remain later evidence. The bounded searches retain irrelevant solar-panel projects, repository-language limitations, uninspected leads and an incomplete gretl search tail. Octave-specific coverage and additional temporal ecosystems remain open. Do not interpret the selected originals as ecosystem saturation or complete temporal qualification.~\citep{exec25_odyn_choice}
