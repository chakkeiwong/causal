         On the other hand, the LLM priors achieve better performance at distinguishing the
     true causal edges due to their domain knowledge — we suppose the LLM has seen more
     examples of the causal relationships between the traits (e.g. ‘height’, ‘flowering time’),
     which hide the genes confounders, than all the genes and traits jointly.

          Interestingly, we also observe that DiBS with either of the LLM priors performs sim-
     ilarly to DiBS using the original edge prior. This suggests that, at least for DiBS, prior
     information is insufficient to correct for the data-generating assumptions being violated.

         As mentioned before, MAGIC-ORIGINAL has 44 nodes, thus, it is a hard graph to
     find. In DiBS’ original paper, the authors mention that the hyperparameters of the model
     are optimized and tested for graphs up to 20 nodes. However, this dataset has more than
     two times this number of variables. Indeed the results show that DiBS does not yield good
     results, with neither priors. Considering GPT’s prior alone, we observe a better result for
     E-SHD, once more, possibly because GPT’s prediction does not have many edges. Nev-
     ertheless, the average AUROC of the prior distribution is only 0.5, which is the worst
     possible measure. We conclude that, even though DiBS is not optimized for a graph this
     large, it still outperforms the LLM alone.
