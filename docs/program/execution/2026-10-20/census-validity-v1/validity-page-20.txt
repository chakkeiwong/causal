Figure 2. Handling non-independence in LLM response data. Panel A illustrates the nested structure of
repeated LLM outputs: responses are clustered within models and often within model × condition
combinations, so the clustering unit for inference is typically the model, model × condition, or item rather
than the individual response. Panel B summarizes three analytic options: aggregation to cluster-level
summaries, multilevel modeling or generalized estimating equations (GEE) to model nested responses
directly, and cluster bootstrap to resample whole clusters while preserving dependence. Panel C guides
method selection by dependence structure and inferential target. When responses are nested within
models or model × condition combinations, the choice between aggregation and multilevel modeling or
GEE hinges on the inferential target: if the goal is a cluster-level summary, aggregation is a principled first
choice that is mathematically equivalent to multilevel modeling under balanced cluster sizes (McNabb &
Murayama, 2021); if response-level variability or lower-level covariates are substantively informative,
multilevel models or GEE with finite-sample correction are preferable. When the same items are reused
across models or conditions, analyze paired item-level differences; if those items are additionally grouped
within passages or scenarios, apply clustered standard errors to the paired differences (Miller, 2024).
