\section{Dynamic-panel reuse: instruments, calendars, and the cost of searching for a model}
\label{sec:exec24-pydynpd}

A dynamic-panel coefficient becomes useful to a bank only after its time scale and identifying moments are justified.
Consider monthly account balances following a servicing intervention, or deposit flows
following a change in offered rates. Persistence makes a static regression an incomplete
description. It also makes the distinction between an immediate association, a structural
response and a long-run consequence economically important. The pydynpd package offers
a concrete Python candidate for a bounded linear dynamic-panel service. Its original paper
describes difference and system GMM, several estimation steps, diagnostic tests and automatic
lag-model search. The paper's historical comparisons motivate inspection; they do not establish
current relative speed, correctness or commercial parity.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_paper}

The reviewed repository commit predates the 7 October 2026 cutoff. The root license is MIT.
The selected setup file declares Python at least 3.6, version 0.2.1 and dependencies on NumPy,
SciPy, pandas and prettytable, whereas the separate version module declares 5.2.0. The setup
file's nonempty version takes its own branch. We therefore identify the reviewed code by its
commit and hashes, without pretending that these declarations identify one verified wheel or
supported environment. No package code, benchmark or test was executed. The following
source findings and exact project examples support a reference disposition and a conditional
original-worker design.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source}

\subsection{The equation and the information supplied by instruments}
For account $i$ and period $t$, start with the deliberately restricted model
\begin{equation}
 y_{it}=\alpha y_{i,t-1}+\beta a_{it}+\eta_i+\varepsilon_{it}.
\end{equation}
Here $a_{it}$ is a declared action or exposure, $\eta_i$ an account effect and
$\varepsilon_{it}$ the remaining disturbance. Coefficient $\beta$ is a structural
causal response only under a model in which this action and disturbance have the
required intervention meaning. An economic label on a regression column does not
provide that meaning. Differencing removes the fixed account effect but leaves
\begin{equation}
 \Delta y_{it}=\alpha\Delta y_{i,t-1}+\beta\Delta a_{it}+\Delta\varepsilon_{it}.
\end{equation}
The lagged dependent regressor generally shares a disturbance with the differenced
error. The classical short-panel argument uses suitable lagged levels as instruments
under the stated moment and serial-correlation restrictions; the original discussion
also distinguishes predetermined from strictly exogenous regressors.
The relevant asymptotic regime and cross-unit sampling conditions are part of that
argument. They cannot be replaced by a successful software call.
\citep{arellano1991}

Write $g_i(\theta)=Z_i'u_i(\theta)$ for account-level moments, where $Z_i$ contains
the chosen instruments and $u_i$ the transformed residual vector. A GMM calculation
minimizes $\bar g_N(\theta)'W_N\bar g_N(\theta)$ for a declared weight matrix.
At least three questions must remain separate: whether the moments are valid, whether
they identify the requested coefficient, and whether the estimated weight and covariance
give useful finite-sample inference. System GMM adds level-equation moments and hence
additional restrictions. For the simple autoregression, the inspected original account
of the method makes the initial-condition restriction
$\mathbb E[\Delta y_{i2}\eta_i]=0$ explicit. High persistence can make otherwise valid
lag instruments weak; adding level moments is justified only when their extra conditions
hold.\citep{blundellbond2023}

A small exact example shows why a moment solver cannot supply these conditions.
Let $U$ be equally likely to be minus one or one, set $x=z=U$, and let the structural
equation be $y=x+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon=U$. The true structural coefficient
is one, but
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[z(y-\theta x)]=2-\theta.
\end{equation}
The moment criterion is minimized at two because the proposed instrument is invalid.
It is precisely solved and scientifically wrong for the stated target. In another
example, $x_1=x_2=z$ identifies only $\beta_1+\beta_2$. Coefficients $(1,0)$,
$(0,1)$ and $(1/2,1/2)$ generate the same fitted values. A generalized inverse can
choose a numerical representative without identifying the two effects.
These are authored algebraic witnesses, not native pydynpd benchmark results.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}

\subsection{The calendar and the instrument ledger}
The inspected parser requires a lag-one dependent variable and rejects declared
dependent-variable GMM instruments starting before lag two. It derives a corresponding
level-lag entry and makes regressors not assigned to a GMM or IV list into IV entries.
Default options select two steps, a level equation, first differences and uncollapsed
instruments. These defaults are computational conventions, not evidence that a bank's
rate or servicing action is exogenous or that level moments are valid. The proposed
request must carry an explicit instrument ledger: variable, treatment of endogeneity,
equation block, permitted lags, collapse choice, availability clock and scientific
justification. It should reject an implicit scientific classification.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source}

The same ledger must specify what one time step means. The inspected panel constructor
encodes observed time values categorically and creates a balanced array over those codes.
Globally absent periods are not automatically supplied as calendar gaps. If observed
months are one, three and four, adjacent categorical codes represent calendar intervals
of two months and one month. Treating both as the same lag changes the equation.
The source also calls sorting without assigning its returned table before a subsequent
positional assignment into occupied balanced-array locations. This creates an ordering
contract that must be checked before reuse.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source}

An independent positional example makes the concern concrete. Suppose encoded key/value
pairs arrive as $(2,30),(0,10),(1,20)$. Writing their value sequence into sorted occupied
keys produces $(0,30),(1,10),(2,20)$, whereas a keyed assignment gives
$(0,10),(1,20),(2,30)$. The abstract result explains the source concern without claiming
to have reproduced a native failure. The proposed adapter must copy the input, verify
unique unit-period keys, impose a declared regular calendar and bind a sorted key-to-row
map. A future qualification test must permute the original rows and demand invariance
of that mapping and the estimator. Sorting alone does not repair duplicate keys or an
incorrect time grid.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}

Instrument proliferation and instrument strength are also different. In a complete
six-period illustrative series, difference equations for periods three through six
can have one, two, three and four lag-level instrument slots, giving ten uncollapsed
slots for that variable. Grouping the same stipulated moments by lag gives four
collapsed slots. Other regressors, level equations, missingness and lag restrictions
change these counts. Neither ten nor four is a rank certificate or a proof of valid
moments. The inspected builder has explicit lag-window and collapse branches, so the
worker should return the realized instrument identities, dimensions and numerical
rank diagnostics as well as the requested options.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source,exec24_pydynpd_reference}

\subsection{Weights, covariance and numerical termination}
The inspected estimator computes weighted normal equations using a pseudoinverse;
the step-result class also pseudoinverts its weight matrix. It accumulates moment
outer products by account and invokes a covariance helper named for the finite-sample
two-step correction. Its Hansen calculation uses the nominal difference between
instrument and regressor counts as degrees of freedom. These paths must be reported
as inspected implementation choices. They do not establish effective-rank adequacy,
an asymptotic approximation under arbitrary dependence, or numerical reliability near
singularity. A finite-sample correction cannot repair invalid instruments.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source,blundellbond2023}

Common bank-wide shocks give an elementary warning about the covariance unit. If
$g_i=U$ for all one hundred accounts, with a common mean-zero unit-variance shock,
then $\operatorname{Var}(N^{-1}\sum_i g_i)=1$. Keeping only the diagonal contributions
gives an expected value of $1/100$. Adding more copies of the common shock does not
create independent information. This witness does not assert that every bank panel
has this dependence or that time dummies are ineffective. It shows why the chosen
sampling law and any remaining calendar or group dependence require a separate
argument.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}

Estimation step and diagnostic step also need explicit identities. In the inspected
one-step path, the code computes a second step and uses its weight/residual aggregate
for the Hansen calculation, while the reported coefficient table selects the requested
step. This may be an intentional diagnostic convention; an adapter must preserve it
rather than label every output as coming from the reported estimator. Missing transformed
regressor or outcome rows are zeroed together with the corresponding instrument columns.
In system mode, the inspected observation count follows the differenced block. The
proposed service should return eligible accounts, transformed equation rows, retained
observations, instrument columns and each diagnostic denominator separately.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source}

The iterative routine stops when relative coefficient change falls below a threshold.
The inspected loop has no explicit iteration cap or finite-value guard; its relative
criterion divides by the squared norm of the previous coefficient. A zero denominator,
nonfinite iterate or nonconvergence therefore requires an explicit worker policy.
The parser's large step marker is not an inspected cap on this loop. A future worker
needs an iteration budget, timeout, convergence record and refusal outcome. If that
requires a source repair, retain it as a separately versioned implementation and
qualify the repaired algorithm. A timeout cannot be reported as an estimated effect.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_source}

\subsection{A search survivor is a selected result}
The original paper describes an automatic mode that searches lag combinations and
returns models satisfying specification tests. In the inspected source, candidates
are generated from adjustable lag ranges; ordinary exceptions during candidate
processing are caught and skipped. The acceptance predicate requires the last
autocorrelation-test probability to exceed 0.05 and the Hansen probability to lie
strictly between 0.05 and 0.99999. Ordinary process errors, diagnostic failures,
rejected candidates and returned models are consequently different denominators.
Some parser errors use process exit rather than the ordinary exception path.
An authorized isolated worker must translate every terminal outcome into a typed
result and preserve all attempted candidates.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_paper,exec24_pydynpd_source}

Searching changes the interpretation of a surviving test. For a stipulated collection
of twenty invalid candidates, suppose each independently passes a screening rule with
probability 0.1. The probability that at least one survives is
\begin{equation}
 1-(1-0.1)^{20}\simeq0.8784.
\end{equation}
This is not an estimate of the power of Hansen or autocorrelation tests, and real
candidate events need not be independent. It is a finite-law explanation of why a
search for nonrejection is not a certification procedure. Predeclare the economic
model and admissible moments where possible; otherwise retain the full search and
address selection in the inferential design. A held-out or simulation-based check
must still be justified for the panel's dependence and available information.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}

\subsection{From coefficients to a proposal for reuse}
For a stable scalar linear response with invariant coefficients, the effect of a
permanent unit increase in exposure is $\beta/(1-\alpha)$. With a stipulated immediate
response of USD 10, persistence 0.8 gives USD 50 and persistence 0.95 gives USD 200.
These are model calculations, not measured bank value. An uncertainty region reaching
the pole at one cannot be converted into a bounded long-run estimate by plugging in
the center. Regime changes, intervention feedback and a general servicing policy lie
beyond this calculation.\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}

The present choice is reference-only reuse, with an original Python worker as a
conditional prototype candidate. Before admission, its typed library/CLI/MCP contract
must bind the equation, estimand, calendar, immutable data, sorted key map, instruments,
requested and diagnostic steps, covariance unit, rank tolerances, search ledger and
termination policy. The response must preserve full covariance, diagnostic identities,
excluded rows, errors and scientific refusal; a printed regression table is insufficient.
An agent may propose a lag or exposure classification, but its proposal cannot silently
authorize system moments, model selection or intervention meaning.

A targeted repair around input alignment, bounded execution and complete candidate
accounting is more plausible than silently porting the entire estimator into another
language. A wholesale port would require independent checks of transforms, weighting,
covariance, missingness and diagnostics. Existing panel implementations remain comparators
under a frozen common specification; historical author timing claims are not a substitute
for matched fixtures. Qualification should begin with public or generated panels whose
moments, calendar, dependence and failure outcomes are known, then use fresh evaluation
under a justified protocol. Native qualification and expensive bank experiments follow
only when the prototype's merit is demonstrated.

The method owner must justify the moments and economic response; the data owner must
certify keys, clocks and panel selection; the numerical owner must resolve rank,
covariance and convergence; the adapter and rights owners must freeze the implementation
and dependency profile. Measure failed attempts and correct refusals alongside accepted
artifacts. In a stipulated twenty-attempt workflow costing USD 100, eight diagnostic
survivors cost USD 12.50 each, but two accepted artifacts cost USD 50 each. Four correct
refusals remain a separate outcome. This is not an observed price or performance claim.
The decision preserves the source concerns and a concrete qualification route without
granting native execution, general panel validity or bank acceptance.
\citep{exec24_pydynpd_reference}
