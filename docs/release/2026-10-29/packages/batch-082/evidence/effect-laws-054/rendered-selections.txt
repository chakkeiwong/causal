Chapter 43

Effect estimation, heterogeneous
actions and threshold comparisons

A servicing team may want to know the value of offering assistance to every eligible account,
the value among accounts already receiving assistance, or the value in the population where
receipt remains uncertain. Those questions need not have the same answer. Nor does learning
a large conditional benefit identify the best use of a scarce specialist team. A threshold
experiment can support a local comparison while leaving the portfolio decision unresolved.
The proposal therefore needs a vocabulary for comparing estimators through their population,
uncertainty and decision consequences.
   This chapter develops three connections: assignment-dependent populations and orthogonal
estimation; joint heterogeneous comparisons and resource allocation; and threshold identifica-
tion versus finite approximation. The numerical laws are stipulated, reproducible examples in
USD per eligible account over a fixed thirty-day horizon. They establish algebra and decision
consequences under those laws. They are neither bank observations nor new executions of the
external software discussed below. (Causal banking proposal, 2026ce)
   The later comparisons follow these distinctions into individual software paths: the population
actually averaged, the provenance of nuisance predictions, and the construction of the final
uncertainty. Repeated examples use the same stipulated customers where that makes a
difference between implementations visible. The alternatives remain separate when their priors,
sampling units, missingness rules or numerical conventions answer different questions.


43.1     The population can depend on the assignment mechanism
Let O = (X, A, Y ) denote one independent account observation, where X is information
available before a binary action A, and Y is the subsequent monetary outcome. Assume
consistency, no interference, conditional exchangeability of the two potential outcomes given
X, and positive probabilities for both actions on the population being studied. These are
scientific restrictions; fitting a flexible regression does not verify them. Define

 e(x) = P (A = 1 | X = x),       ma (x) = E(Y | A = a, X = x),        τ (x) = m1 (x) − m0 (x).

remainder bound needs them.

choice unnecessary. The appropriate comparison holds the target fixed first and then studies
estimation error, rather than treating every numerically stable answer as an improvement.

43.23       Julia effect estimators and the meaning of a reusable
            result
A servicing manager asks whether an additional contact is worth its cost for each risk group.
A numerical answer requires more than a treatment-effect method name. The population
average, the average among previously contacted customers, a conditional contrast and a
coefficient from residual regression can differ even when every conditional model is known.
Missing outcomes can change the averaging population again. A reusable interface must carry
this meaning from the business question through the numerical calculation.
   The selected Julia sources offer several distinct building blocks. CausalELM 0.8.0 sup-
plies g-computation and effect learners; CausalForest 0.1.5 implements tree-based contrasts;
TMLE 0.20.5 supplies targeted and one-step estimation; and CausalTables 1.3.4 represents
causal data and interventions in supplied structural models. Their selected source revisions
precede the 7 October 2026 cutoff. Their MIT notices permit substantial reuse subject to
retaining the specified notices, but do not establish dependency clearance or statistical validity.
The following comparison concerns inspected source paths, not native execution or a tested
combination of these packages. (CausalELM.jl contributors, 2026; CausalForest.jl contributors,
2026; TMLE.jl contributors, 2026; CausalTables.jl contributors, 2026)

43.23.2     Source-level uncertainty checks before adoption

The inspected CausalELM generic null routine samples treatment independently and uniformly
from its distinct observed values, or uniformly over its observed range for a continuous
treatment. This does not preserve the observed treatment count. It also does not reproduce a
nonuniform propensity law or a cluster-randomized design without additional construction.
For four customers assigned exactly one contact, independent fair binary draws retain that
design with probability 4/16 = 1/4 and create an empty treatment or control arm with
probability 2/16 = 1/8. This exact calculation identifies an assignment-law mismatch, rather
than estimating a package error rate. (CausalELM.jl contributors, 2026; Causal banking
proposal project, 2026ba)

43.26.3     Bootstrap counts, missing outputs and uncertainty admission

Both inspected branches iterate from one through B. Iterations below B resample identifiers,
while the last uses the original sample so returned fits have the original identities. Quantiles
are calculated over the full matrix. Consequently the inspected loop contains B − 1 resampled
rows and one original row, which must be distinguished from a request for B independent
resamples. For an authored vector (0, 1, 100), where the last value represents the original
sample, linear-interpolated 2.5 and 97.5 percent quantiles are 0.05 and 95.05. Using only the
two stipulated resamples gives 0.025 and 0.975. These numbers isolate a counting convention;
they do not measure empirical coverage. (Gruber,van der Laan and tmle contributors, 2025;

do. With the illustrative vector (1, NA, 3) the variance is missing but the finite-only quantile

future worker must return requested, attempted, original-sample, finite and failed counts, and
identify the actual nuisance-fitting and targeting steps repeated. The intermediate-variable

includes all rows for each sampled identifier. Identifiers spanning both groups require explicit
