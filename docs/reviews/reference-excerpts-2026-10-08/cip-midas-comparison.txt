\section{Literature and method choice}

\subsection{The forecasting benchmark}

The forecasting MIDAS literature holds the low-frequency dependent variable
fixed and estimates a parsimonious distributed lag of observed high-frequency
regressors, usually by nonlinear least squares with exposure separated from
normalized lag shape \citep{ghyselsSCV2004,ghyselsSCV2006,ghyselsSV2007}.
When the frequency ratio is modest, U-MIDAS instead estimates unrestricted lag
coefficients by ordinary least squares; with daily regressors, regularization or
a low-dimensional lag function is needed to control parameter proliferation
\citep{foroniMarcellinoSchumacher2015}.

\citet{andreouGhyselsKourtellos2013} is the closest economic benchmark. The
authors predict quarterly US real GDP growth from daily financial data using
individual assets, daily principal-component factors, factor-ADL MIDAS models,
within-quarter leads, recursive pseudo-out-of-sample evaluation, and forecast
combinations. Their two reductions address different dimensions: PCA compresses
the financial cross-section, while a normalized two-parameter
exponential-Almon function compresses approximately one quarter of daily lags.
The paper uses about 991 series in five asset classes---commodities, corporate
risk, equities, foreign exchange, and government securities---and also smaller
93- and 65-series panels. It estimates predictor-, factor-, horizon-, sample-,
and forecast-origin-specific profiles, not one published universal pair
$(\theta_1,\theta_2)$. The project therefore replicates the procedure and tests
the new common-profile restriction; it does not copy a historical numerical
pair.

Factor-MIDAS more generally extracts high-frequency factors and then enters
their distributed summaries in a distinct low-frequency forecasting equation
\citep{marcellinoSchumacher2010}. \citet{baighyselwright2013} compare a
single-equation MIDAS forecast with a fully specified state-space forecast and
derive ideal population cases in which MIDAS reproduces steady-state Kalman
weights. This is a forecasting equivalence under stated conditions, not an
identification theorem for an unrestricted latent MIDAS measurement block.

\subsection{Modern flexible and high-dimensional amendments}

The later literature changes how shape uncertainty, predictor dimension, and
target instability are handled; it does not supply a universal MIDAS shape.
\citet{moglianiSimoni2021} use grouped linear-Almon coefficients with Bayesian
group-Lasso and spike-and-slab hierarchies. Their contraction results concern
prediction and grouped coefficient recovery under fixed-design sparsity and
regularity conditions. The method is useful when predictor selection and
predictive distributions justify a large Bayesian computation, but its prior
does not identify a common temporal profile and its polynomial can remain
misspecified.

\citet{babiiGhyselsStriaukas2022} approximate each predictor's lag curve with a
Legendre dictionary and estimate it by sparse-group LASSO. Their theory covers
dependent, heavy-tailed time series under mixing, moment, restricted-eigenvalue,
and approximate-sparsity conditions; their simulations directly study weight
recovery. This is the project's main flexible frequentist challenger because it
permits non-Almon and signed shapes. Its native coefficient vector can be
transferred through \eqref{eq:midas_scale_shape_transfer} only after nonzero
exposure, sign, stability, and full-procedure uncertainty checks.

\citet{breitungRoling2015} instead penalize second differences of unrestricted
lag coefficients. For $\lambda>0$, assume
$\ker(X'X)\cap\ker(D'D)=\{0\}$, so the inverse below exists:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat b_\lambda=(X'X+\lambda D'D)^{-1}X'y.
 \label{eq:breitung_roling_smoother}
\end{equation}
This transparent smooth-U-MIDAS estimator is the second mandatory shape
diagnostic. \citet{omerEtAl2024} multiply this estimate by an additional scalar
$q$. For $q\ne0$ and $\ind'\widehat b_\lambda\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{q\widehat b_\lambda}{\ind'(q\widehat b_\lambda)}
 =\frac{\widehat b_\lambda}{\ind'\widehat b_\lambda}.
 \label{eq:omer_shape_invariance}
\end{equation}
Thus $q$ may alter fitted magnitude and coefficient MSE, but it adds no
normalized-shape information once exposure and shape are separated. It is not
a separate freeze arm.

\citet{beyhumStriaukas2026} combine dense PCA factors with sparse direct MIDAS
predictors. This addresses a cross-sectional question---whether common factors
miss isolated predictive signals---rather than producing one common temporal
profile. Their reported factor gains are regime dependent, so factor
augmentation is an empirical arm, not an automatic improvement.
\citet{kohnsPotjagailo2025} add a time-varying target trend, stochastic
volatility, Student-$t$ errors, and grouped Bayesian shrinkage. Their MIDAS
coefficient curve is constant: the time variation is in the target-side states.
The model is therefore a later regime/outlier diagnostic if a profile appears
unstable, not the initial estimator and not a time-varying-weight model.

\subsection{State-space alternative and the boundary of the claim}

Mixed-frequency state-space models fix aggregation weights using stock/flow
accounting and release timing, or specify a latent high-frequency transition
and measurement system directly
\citep{marianoMurasawa2003,schorfheideSong2015,
foroniMarcellino2014,schorfheideSongYaron2018}. These systems are appropriate
when feedback, full predictive distributions, and release-calendar dynamics
are the estimand. They are not computationally necessary for externally
calibrating a daily predictive summary.

None of these strands claims that $\vartheta$, $K_{AA}$, $K_{AM}$,
$\Sigma_A$, and proxy noise are jointly identified from one
parameter-dependent monthly aggregate. The earlier MacroFinance failure lies
outside the standard forecasting claim, not against it.

\subsection{Chosen hierarchy}

The primary estimator is therefore the fixed-outcome AGK-style normalized
exponential-Almon regression in
\eqref{eq:macro_predictive_midas}--\eqref{eq:macro_predictive_profile_objective}.
Babii--Ghysels--Striaukas and Breitung--Roling are mandatory shape diagnostics.
Mogliani--Simoni is a Bayesian selection/uncertainty arm;
Beyhum--Striaukas is a later dense-plus-sparse cross-sectional arm; and
Kohns--Potjagailo is a later target-regime arm. These roles answer different
questions and must not be collapsed into a descriptive leaderboard.
