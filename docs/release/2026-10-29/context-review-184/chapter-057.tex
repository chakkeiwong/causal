\chapter{Hidden states and Bayesian comparisons}

An uncertain customer state can be useful for a banking decision only if its
meaning, observation law and action mechanism are specified. This chapter first
examines state filtering and probabilistic model surgery through Dynamax, PyMC
and NumPyro. It then compares the resulting requirements across languages and
commercial engines. The comparison keeps causal identification, parameter and
state uncertainty, predictive variation and numerical approximation distinct.
Similar finite examples provide common tests of those distinctions; their
repetition is not additional empirical evidence about a package or a bank.

\section{Dynamax reuse requires a declared conditional law and information clock}
\label{exec19:dynamax-scope}

A servicing team may infer a borrower's unobserved repayment propensity from
payments, contacts and changing account conditions. A state-space engine is a
natural computational component, but its likelihood must describe the experiment
that generated those observations. Treating an adaptive contact as an ordinary
input can omit information carried by the contact decision itself. Replacing an
online filter by a full-history smoother can introduce information unavailable
when the bank acted. These are distinct scientific errors even when both calls
return finite arrays.

The selected Dynamax revision is commit
\texttt{5098927f2c377c0dd548cda750e6f1436220aac6}, dated 29 September 2026.
Twelve original files at that revision supply the present comparison: the
project description, root notice, dependency metadata, HMM abstraction,
filtering and smoothing implementation, standard transition component,
state-space fitting wrapper, version helper, selected tests and parameter/shape
helpers. This is a historical source inspection. The inherited PyPI version
1.0.2 is a separate release observation; the selected dynamic version helper
has not been executed to establish release equivalence.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

\subsection{An input-conditioned model is not a logger-aware causal model}
\label{exec19:dynamax-logger}

The inspected HMM wrapper supplies initial-state probabilities, transition
matrices and emission log likelihoods to its filter. Its marginal-probability
method returns the filter's marginal log likelihood. No separate observed-action
likelihood is automatically inserted. For externally fixed inputs this is a
reasonable computational representation of a declared conditional experiment.
For an action selected using a private state, the joint law generally also needs
the action mechanism. Merely passing the realized action as an input does not
establish that the omitted factor is ignorable.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

Consider an authored one-period construction. Let latent repayment propensity
$Z$ be Bernoulli with probability $1/2$. Conditional on $Z$, let action $A$
match $Z$ with probability $9/10$, and outcome $Y$ match $Z$ with probability
$4/5$. The action and outcome noises are conditionally independent. The outcome
has no structural dependence on the action. The observed law therefore factors as
\begin{equation}
 p(z,a,y)=p(z)\,p(a\mid z)\,p(y\mid z).
 \label{exec19:dynamax-law}
\end{equation}
Exact enumeration gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 P(Y=1\mid A=1)=\frac{37}{50},\qquad
 P(Y=1\mid A=0)=\frac{13}{50},\\
 E[Y\mid\operatorname{do}(A=1)]-E[Y\mid\operatorname{do}(A=0)]=0.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{exec19:dynamax-contrast}
\end{equation}
The observational contrast is $12/25$, although acting has no effect in the
stipulated structural model. Given $A=1,Y=1$, the posterior probability of
$Z=1$ is $36/37$. Omitting the logger and conditioning only through the outcome
factor gives $4/5$ instead. Both calculations are coherent for their respective
information sets; only the former uses the observed informative action under
this law. They are exact project calculations, not Dynamax execution results.
\citep{exec19_dynamax_reference}

Suppose a unit improvement in repayment probability is assigned USD 200 of
value and an action costs USD 20. These are assumptions. Substituting the
observational contrast gives USD 76, whereas the stipulated causal effect gives
minus USD 20. No model-fitting improvement can make those two targets equal
without additional assumptions or information. A proposed adapter must either
justify the conditional-input experiment, provide the logger-aware law, or
return a clearly described unsupported causal request.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_reference}

\subsection{Preserve the observation and transition clocks}
\label{exec19:dynamax-clocks}

The source distinguishes predicted, filtered and smoothed probabilities.
Filtering at time $t$ conditions on the observation prefix; smoothing can use
all observations through the end of the sequence. The distinction belongs in
the product's result type and availability timestamp. An online decision cannot
silently consume the smoother because it is more concentrated or easier to
compute. Retrospective state reconstruction is a legitimate but different task.
\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

For a separate exact example, let $Z$ persist across two dates, with prior
probability $1/2$, and let two conditionally independent signals each match $Z$
with probability $4/5$. Observing $Y_1=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 P(Z=1\mid Y_1=0)=\frac15,\qquad
 P(Z=1\mid Y_1=0,Y_2=1)=\frac12.
 \label{exec19:dynamax-filter}
\end{equation}
Suppose a decision between the two signals earns USD 100 when $Z=1$ and costs
USD 30. Under that explicitly assumed response rule, the legitimate first-date
expected net is minus USD 10. Using the later signal would report USD 20 for
the same first-date decision. This is an information-clock violation, not an
observed advantage of smoothing or evidence about real servicing returns.
\citep{exec19_dynamax_reference}

Input indexing is equally consequential. The generic HMM transition helper
constructs its time-varying transition matrices from \texttt{inputs[1:]},
consistent with the inspected model abstraction's destination-time input.
The low-level filter description instead writes its transition using the
source-time input. A wrapper must declare the mapping rather than infer it from
an argument name. The standard transition class accepts an input argument but
returns its fixed transition matrix without using that argument. Passing a
contact sequence therefore does not ensure that the selected transition model
actually responds to contacts. A deterministic indexed-sequence reference
should compare sampling, model wrappers and low-level calls before admission.
\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

\subsection{Numerical completion and parameter learning need separate checks}
\label{exec19:dynamax-numerics}

The inspected normalizer preserves exact zeros, floors very small positive
entries and substitutes one for a zero denominator. An all-zero input consequently
remains all zero rather than becoming a valid probability distribution. The
emission update subtracts the maximum log likelihood without a dedicated
all-minus-infinity branch. The smoother masks near-zero predicted probabilities
using a closeness test before another normalization. These are local source
observations motivating native conformance cases; they are not measured defect
rates or a claim about every reachable backend execution. Invalid support,
nonfinite arithmetic, unsupported laws and valid negative scientific findings
must not collapse into the same output status.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

The fitting wrapper exposes a specified iteration count and an objective trace.
For nonpositive iteration counts it returns the supplied parameters with an
empty trace. The reviewed path does not make a general convergence or
identification guarantee. Selected parameter helpers also distinguish fully
frozen groups from partially frozen groups that certain joint EM updates
reject; the inspected tests compare that boundary with SGD support. A caller
must retain initialization, trainable flags, shapes, optimization settings and
failures. A state posterior conditional on fitted parameters is not automatically
a confidence statement about parameter estimates or a selected policy.
\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

Selected upstream tests compare filtering and smoothing with explicit finite
latent-path sums, exercise nonstationary transitions and inspect fitting
behavior. They provide useful candidate conformance specifications, but none
was executed here. A future native suite should include impossible emissions,
zero support, one-step and empty sequences, input shifts, frozen parameters,
multiple starts and device synchronization. Resource and timing claims must
include compilation, conversion and failed requests.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

\subsection{Prefer a narrow original-engine comparison; defer executable admission}
\label{exec19:dynamax-choice}

The selected root notice is MIT. The project metadata requires Python at least
3.10 and eight direct runtime dependencies: JAX, JAXlib, TFP-nightly,
fastprogress, Optax, jaxtyping, typing-extensions and NumPy. Those declarations
are unversioned; build and optional groups are separate. A complete distribution,
transitive notices and resolved backend/precision environment remain necessary
before executable admission. Root license text alone does not qualify the whole
installed stack.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source}

The proposal should retain the original engine as a conditional-state-space
comparator behind a narrow adapter. A small exact enumerator remains the
transparent baseline for finite cases; it does not offer a scaling solution.
A complete port would duplicate numerical, differentiation and maintenance
obligations without a demonstrated platform need. A causal policy optimizer
built solely from a conditional likelihood is outside the initial profile.
Scientific ownership must cover the target, logger and clocks; engineering
ownership must cover the environment, conversions and failure contract; release
ownership must cover the actual distribution and notices.

This is a bounded reuse recommendation with executable admission deferred.
Public sources and generated examples suffice to specify the first comparison.
Native conformance, parameter uncertainty and measured full-workflow cost remain
prototype work; authentic bank assignment and value remain later institutional
questions. Neither the package name nor an API returning a posterior closes
those distinct obligations.

\section{Bayesian model surgery needs a separate prediction contract}
\label{exec23:pymc-scope}

PyMC is a plausible worker for bank-specific probabilistic models, including
hierarchical likelihoods and explicitly modeled latent quantities. The proposed
product, however, needs to distinguish three questions: which model is being
conditioned on data, which mechanism is replaced by an intervention, and which
variables are subsequently regenerated. The inspected repository revision is
dated 7 October 2026 and is identified by its immutable commit and twelve
authenticated source, documentation and test files. Its version is dynamically
resolved; this review does not assert that the selected development revision is
a named stable release. Its metadata requires Python at least 3.12 and below
3.15, and a specified PyTensor compatibility range. Those requirements, the
root Apache license and the actual dependency artifacts must be part of a
future worker contract rather than inferred from an older installed environment.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

\subsection{Conditioning does not replace a treatment mechanism}
\label{exec23:pymc-intervention}

The inspected \texttt{observe} transformation converts selected random or
deterministic variables into observed variables, with compatible shape and
type. The \texttt{do} transformation instead replaces named model variables
by intervention expressions in a cloned graph. An expression depending on
random variables is represented as a deterministic; a constant intervention
is shared data by default. These operations provide useful model construction
machinery. They do not establish that the supplied graph correctly represents
assignment, latent common causes, information timing or a bank's feasible
interventions.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

For an exact example, let hidden borrower type $U$ be Bernoulli with probability
$1/2$, let observed treatment $A$ agree with $U$ with probability $3/4$, and let
the outcome be $Y=2A+4U$. Then
\[
 E[Y\mid A=1]=5,\qquad E[Y\mid A=0]=1,
 \qquad E[Y\mid\mathrm{do}(A=1)]-E[Y\mid\mathrm{do}(A=0)]=2.
\]
The association is four, while the intervention effect is two because changing
$A$ leaves the population law of $U$ unchanged. At a stipulated value of
USD 100 per outcome unit and action cost USD 250, the association-based decision
reports net USD 150 but the causal calculation gives USD $-50$. The numerical
example is an authored finite-law calculation; it is neither a package
benchmark nor estimated bank economics.
\citep{exec23_pymc_reference}

Even a perfect posterior computation cannot resolve every causal ambiguity.
The structural models $A=U,Y=A$ and $A=U,Y=U$, with a fair binary $U$, induce
the same observed law for $(A,Y)$ but treatment effects one and zero.
Under equal observational likelihoods, posterior odds between these models
remain their prior odds. A narrower posterior obtained by choosing one model
is conditional on that choice, not evidence that observations distinguished
the models. A useful separate computational oracle is the conjugate update
from a uniform beta prior after six successes and four failures: the posterior
is beta$(7,5)$ with mean $7/12$ and variance $35/1872$. Agreement with this
oracle checks a declared probability calculation; it does not validate a
causal interpretation of those successes.
\citep{exec23_pymc_reference}

The transformation API also allows interventions that reference the original
variable. The inspected code warns and gives the replacement a new name with
a \texttt{do\_} prefix. Such an expression needs an explicit scientific
interpretation, for example a declared shift policy; it must not be silently
described as a fixed assignment. Optional pruning removes variables detached
from observed variables, and its helper explicitly refuses models containing
Potentials because their prior-versus-likelihood role is ambiguous. Therefore
pruning cannot substitute for reviewing the target and retained information.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

Graph cloning has concrete limits. The inspected conversion refuses
nondefault initial values and nested submodels, and warns that reusing old
Gaussian-process objects can reintroduce variables from the original model.
It copies shared random generators, data and symbolic dimension lengths.
A worker must therefore bind the original and transformed model identities,
coordinates and initialization choices, and inspect rejected conversions.
These source operations are a basis for a future isolation test; source
inspection alone does not certify a concurrent service or a complete snapshot.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

\subsection{Stored draws, regenerated variables and changed expressions}
\label{exec23:pymc-prediction}

The selected forward-sampling implementation separates output selection from
regeneration. \texttt{var\_names} controls what is returned; it does not request
a fresh draw. \texttt{sample\_vars} identifies variables to regenerate, while
\texttt{freeze\_vars} preserves trace values. Random variables present in the
trace and not explicitly selected for regeneration are automatically frozen.
Missing random variables, including ordinary observed outcomes absent from
the trace, are regenerated. The code detects some changed-ancestor cases and
issues an implicit-freeze warning, but warning availability does not establish
that the chosen predictive experiment is appropriate.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

Suppose a stored draw contains $X=1,Y=1$ from a model with
$Y\mid X\sim N(X,1)$. After changing $X$ to three, retaining the stored $Y$
returns one, whereas regenerating $Y$ from the changed model has conditional
mean three. Both operations can be intentional, but they answer different
questions. A policy simulation must identify which parameter draws remain
conditioned on the original evidence and which post-intervention variables
are generated anew. Otherwise an apparently successful transformation can
leave precisely the outcome of interest tied to the old experiment.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_pymc_reference}

Changed deterministic expressions require their own attention. The inspected
documentation explicitly describes replacing a deterministic expression without
changing any random variable or data value: the predictive machinery can then
reuse its stored value unless regeneration is requested. For two illustrative
trace points $X=1,2$, replacing $X^2$ by $X^3$ changes the desired values from
$(1,4)$ to $(1,8)$ and their mean from $5/2$ to $9/2$. A model digest must
therefore identify expressions, not just variable names and current data.
Name-based trace matching with compatible shapes and coordinates is a software
matching rule, not proof of causal or semantic equivalence between models.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_pymc_reference}

Predictive handling of Potentials adds another boundary. The inspected prior
and posterior predictive routines warn that their direct effect on other
parameters is ignored during forward sampling. A fair binary generator with
a hard weight excluding zero has weighted probability one of a positive value,
whereas the unconstrained forward generator still has probability $1/2$.
This witness explains why a density factor cannot automatically be treated
as a generative rule. It does not imply that all effects of a Potential vanish:
posterior ancestors supplied by the trace may already reflect that factor.
The worker must identify the factor's role, whether it enters the inferential
posterior, and how the requested predictive law is constructed.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_pymc_reference}

The selected upstream tests include intervention, self-reference, dimensional
and predictive cases, as well as a constant-intervention predictive case marked
as an expected failure. These are useful conformance leads, not executed results
in this project. The proposed fixture set must exercise both shared and constant
interventions and inspect the actual result and warning, without converting
an upstream test marker into a claim that failure was reproduced here.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

\subsection{Computation and diagnostics must remain visible to the caller}
\label{exec23:pymc-decision}

Sampler identity cannot safely be left implicit. At the selected revision,
the default dispatch can choose nutpie when the installed environment, model,
compilation mode and arguments permit it, otherwise the PyMC sampler. The
external paths impose restrictions on variable types and supplied steps,
and reject custom trace backends and per-draw callbacks. Consequently,
partial-trace persistence described for a PyMC trace backend cannot be
advertised for every sampler choice. Persisting draws also does not by itself
establish resumable chain state, independent restarts or distributed recovery.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

The inspected convergence routine reports missing posterior data, too few
draws, a single chain and fewer than four chains separately. It checks
reported divergences and tree-depth statistics; missing statistics return no
corresponding warning. Its inspected thresholds include an $\widehat R$
warning above 1.01 and an effective-sample-size warning below 100 per chain.
These are the package's conditional diagnostics, not a theorem of convergence
or a guarantee that an intervention model is correct. NUTS source documentation
also describes energy, trajectory-depth and timing statistics and distinguishes
CPU from wall time. The product must preserve missing, unavailable, adverse
and satisfactory diagnostics as different states, rather than flatten them
into a single successful API status.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources}

A posterior credible interval is conditional on the declared prior,
likelihood and model class. Its numerical stability does not establish
frequentist coverage across a bank's data-generating processes, nor account
automatically for selecting a model or policy after examining the same
outcomes. Those uncertainty questions need their own assumptions and evaluation
law, including sensitivity to the indistinguishable models above.
\citep{exec23_pymc_reference}

The current disposition is a source-qualified reference with a conditional
native Python worker route. A blanket port to other languages would reproduce
graph rewriting and predictive rules at considerable maintenance cost without
a demonstrated advantage. A concrete prototype should instead pin the release
or commit, Python, PyTensor, compiler, sampler and dependencies; register exact
conjugate and finite latent-model oracles; exercise intervention/freeze/sample
semantics, Potentials, dimensions and warnings; and measure compilation,
effective samples, memory, predictive cost and recovery together. Scientific
owners must justify model and intervention meaning, while maintainers own
conversion conformance and operators own budgets and persistence. Library,
CLI and MCP should expose the same target and refusal conditions. LLM-generated
models can be proposed for review, but cannot certify their own causal graph,
identification assumptions or scientific validity. Native qualification,
general causal identification and bank adoption remain open.
\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_pymc_reference}

\section{NumPyro: preserving the experiment through handlers and compiled inference}
\label{sec:exec23-numpyro-worker}

NumPyro is a plausible Python worker when a bank can state a probabilistic model and wants to explore a JAX-based computational route. Its role begins with that supplied model. It does not determine whether a servicing action is manipulable, whether an unrecorded customer state confounds treatment, or whether a financial reporting field was available when the decision was made. These scientific choices must enter the model contract before a sampler can answer the intended question. The pinned source examined here declares version 0.22.0 at revision \texttt{033f7e2e57bd0396fba8493ae246debe52f2c973}, committed on 5 October 2026. This is a source declaration at a revision within the literature cutoff, rather than an assertion that an independently acquired release artifact has identical contents. The root license is Apache-2.0, the Python requirement is at least 3.11, and the direct numerical requirements include JAX and jaxlib at least 0.7.0. Device-specific extras and the additional Funsor inference route require their own artifact and rights review. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

The comparison with PyMC is therefore a comparison of proposed worker contracts. PyMC's inspected route transforms a graph and controls predictive freezing and regeneration; NumPyro's inspected route applies handlers to executions of a stochastic function and passes arrays through compiled inference. Neither representation supplies missing assignment information. Both need the same banking estimand, information clock, outcome units and refusal semantics. Their different defaults make a transparent adapter more useful than a wrapper that merely gives identically named commands. \cite{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_numpyro_sources}

\subsection{Conditioning, intervention and the meaning of an execution trace}

In the inspected handler implementation, \texttt{condition} sets a matched sample value and marks the site observed. \texttt{substitute} can replace several kinds of primitive, including deterministic values, without making the same observation declaration. \texttt{replay} takes matched sampled values from an execution trace, checks the sampled-versus-observed role and leaves already observed target sites alone. Thus matching a site name is a mechanism for moving values; it is not evidence that two models describe the same financial experiment. An adapter must bind the model, data and site roles as well as their array shapes. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

The \texttt{do} handler makes an especially consequential distinction. It creates a fresh sample site with the original name whose value does not propagate to descendants, then sends the supplied intervention value down the original computation. A returned treatment value and a recorded sample under the original treatment name can consequently differ by design. The upstream counterfactual tests explicitly distinguish the model's returned values from the trace values, including when observation and intervention are composed. Those tests describe intended behavior; they have not been executed in this proposal. A converter that extracts the original-name trace entry and calls it the administered action could misreport the experiment even when the handler follows its own contract. Multiple intervention handlers at the same site also require attention to the inspected warning and handler order. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

The causal distinction can be checked without a numerical backend. Let an unobserved binary type $U$ have probability $1/2$, let a binary action $A$ agree with $U$ with probability $3/4$, and set the response to $Y=2A+4U$. Enumeration gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{E}(Y\mid A=0)=1,\qquad
 \mathrm{E}(Y\mid A=1)=5,\qquad
 \mathrm{E}\{Y\mid\operatorname{do}(A=a)\}=2a+2.
\end{equation}
The association is four response units and the intervention contrast is two. This is the same deliberately confounded law used in the PyMC comparison, recalculated by an independently authored finite enumeration here. It supplies a common semantic oracle: conditioning an action and cutting its assignment mechanism have different answers. Neither backend acquires identification simply by returning one of these numbers. \cite{exec23_pymc_reference,exec23_numpyro_reference}

Composing handlers also does not make every customer-specific counterfactual identified. To see the extra requirement, let $Y^0$ and $Y^1$ denote binary responses under two actions. One structural model has $(Y^0,Y^1)=(U,U)$ for $U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$; another has $(Y^0,Y^1)=(U,1-U)$. Both have
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(Y^0=1)=\Pr(Y^1=1)=\tfrac12,
\end{equation}
but $\Pr(Y^1=1\mid Y^0=1)$ is one in the first model and zero in the second. Equal single-world intervention marginals leave this cross-world quantity undetermined. A shared-noise construction or another justified restriction can define a model-specific answer, and sensitivity across these constructions can expose the ambiguity. A model proposed by an LLM must state that construction explicitly; handler composition cannot verify it. This example is an authored identification witness, not an upstream software failure. \cite{exec23_numpyro_reference}

\subsection{Prediction must preserve which latent variables are regenerated}

The inspected \texttt{Predictive} implementation substitutes supplied posterior values, then executes the model. By default it ignores stored deterministic values during substitution and recomputes them from their parents. Setting \texttt{condition\_deterministic=True} instead permits those stored values to be substituted. Output selection through \texttt{return\_sites} has a separate role. As a simple arithmetic check, equal-weight parent values $(1,2)$ give a stored-square mean of $5/2$ and a recomputed-cube mean of $9/2$. Keeping the old deterministic values after changing the expression answers a different question from recomputation. NumPyro's default and PyMC's inspected predictive controls should therefore be represented by explicit regeneration choices in a common interface. One cannot infer equivalence from matching output names. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources,exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_numpyro_reference}

Posterior substitution also fixes supplied stochastic sites. Including such a site among requested outputs does not ask the model to redraw it. Conversely, an absent latent site is not automatically inferred from an observed response during ordinary forward execution. The inspected \texttt{infer\_discrete} path invokes additional enumeration and posterior-sampling machinery; ordinary prediction uses a different masking and execution path. The documentation further requires clearing an observed response to generate a new realization of that response. Leaving an old observation attached can preserve that value rather than create a new future outcome. A bank worker must distinguish parameter draws learned from historical data, historical customer states, states to infer from new evidence and genuinely new outcomes. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

A servicing example shows why this is an economic distinction. Suppose a hidden type $U=1$ has prior probability $1/3$, and an alert $S=1$ has probabilities $3/4$ under type one and $1/4$ under type zero. The alert probability and posterior type probability are
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(S=1)=\tfrac{5}{12},\qquad
 \Pr(U=1\mid S=1)=
 \frac{(1/3)(3/4)}{(1/3)(3/4)+(2/3)(1/4)}=\tfrac35.
\end{equation}
Let an additional action have stipulated net payoffs USD 100 for type one and USD $-200$ for type zero; taking no additional action pays zero. Averaging over the posterior gives
\begin{equation}
 V(1\mid S=1)=\tfrac35(100)+\tfrac25(-200)=-20.
\end{equation}
Replacing the type distribution by its most probable state instead reports USD 100 and selects the action, with an actual expected loss of USD 20 under the specified posterior law. Ignoring the alert and drawing the type from its prior gives yet another action value, USD $-100$. Posterior averaging, MAP substitution and unconditioned regeneration are three different procedures. These exact, stipulated amounts motivate the adapter's uncertainty contract; they are not measured returns from a bank or observed package behavior. \cite{exec23_numpyro_reference}

\subsection{Discrete inference and continuous sampling have separate contracts}

For enumerable latent sites, the inspected model utilities prepare parallel enumeration, with plate dimensions controlling the tensor arrangement. The Funsor contribution is an additional dependency and is labelled experimental by its import path. The inspected discrete handler accepts temperature one for posterior sampling and temperature zero for MAP-style decoding; other temperatures are rejected. Its source also warns that inferred-trace log probabilities are not meaningful. A downstream likelihood audit must not reuse those trace log probabilities as an ordinary fitted likelihood. Likewise, a MAP path is not a collection of posterior paths and cannot propagate hidden-state uncertainty in the servicing example. The scan boundary needs more careful qualification: the handler documentation says the scan primitive is unsupported, whereas the selected tests include scan hidden-Markov examples of lengths one and two and mark length ten as expected to fail because of a Markov sum-product adjoint limitation. This documentation/test tension justifies a registered length- and construction-specific trial, not a blanket assertion of support or a reproduced failure. None of those upstream tests has been run here. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

NumPyro also exposes composable Gibbs and HMC-within-Gibbs routes. The inspected discrete selector distinguishes enumerable sites being updated from sites already marked for marginalization. The proposal must record that choice, support sizes, block structure and proposal settings. The inspected composition rejects some configurations, including subsample plates and value-dependent supports across blocks; the HMC-within-Gibbs entry rejects a bare potential-function model. These restrictions do not establish that a different kernel can repair the case. Native qualification should compare a small hidden-state model against complete enumeration and retain separate acceptance for marginal likelihood, posterior state probabilities and policy value. A state decoder agreeing with one selected path cannot satisfy all three. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

A continuous reference can be equally transparent. With a $\mathrm{Beta}(2,3)$ prior and four successes plus one failure, the likelihood gives a $\mathrm{Beta}(6,4)$ posterior. Ratios of its normalizing integrals yield mean $3/5$ and variance $6/275$. This provides a check of both location and spread without treating an optimizer's answer as a posterior. A later registered trial can assess chains against those moments, finite-sample Monte Carlo tolerances and diagnostic behavior. Passing that trial would qualify this numerical construction under its declared law; it would not identify a confounded action or validate an unknown causal model. \cite{exec23_numpyro_reference}

\subsection{Devices, random keys, diagnostics and restart identity}

The inspected MCMC interface requests parallel chains by default but falls back to sequential chains with a warning if too few devices are available. Vectorized chains are another declared route. Device count, execution method and actual array precision must therefore enter a run identity; a requested GPU or parallel configuration is insufficient evidence that it was used. The source utilities expose platform and 64-bit configuration, and the host-device-count helper carries its own configuration caveat. These are controls to test, not measured throughput or a general speed advantage over PyMC. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources}

Randomness needs equally explicit treatment. The seed handler carries a mutable random key and documents a closure pattern to avoid leaking this state through JAX transformations. MCMC splits a single key for multiple chains; it also accepts a supplied batch of keys. An adapter should preserve how chains and prediction tasks receive keys and check the intended independence or deliberate coupling. Four exact copies of a Bernoulli$(1/2)$ draw have average variance $1/4$; four independent draws have average variance $1/16$. Labelling copies as independent would understate variance by a factor of four. This is an authored dependence counterexample, not a finding that NumPyro's scalar-key splitting duplicates chains. Deliberately coupled random numbers for a paired comparison can be legitimate, but their covariance must be represented. \cite{exec23_numpyro_sources,exec23_numpyro_reference}

\section[Bayesian SCM and hidden-state workers]{Bayesian structural models and hidden states: a common contract across five language families}
\label{sec:exec25-bsw}

A bank selecting a Bayesian or hidden-state worker must distinguish the model it is willing to defend from the computation it wants to perform. A posterior can be numerically stable for a model that does not identify the effect of a servicing intervention. A state smoother can be accurate for retrospective explanation while using information unavailable at the original credit decision. A graph may represent a joint probability law without specifying what changes under an intervention. The proposed product therefore needs a common scientific contract across R, Python, Java, C++ and other relevant languages, with backend-specific computation and refusal rules. The comparison extends the already reviewed PyMC, NumPyro, bnlearn, Dynamax, graphical-model and state-space records with pinned Stan, RStan, NIMBLE, Keanu and Turing originals.~\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_numpyro_sources,exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec19_dynamax_source,exec24_libdai_source,exec24_opengm_source,exec25_bsw_stan,exec25_bsw_rstan,exec25_bsw_nimble,exec25_bsw_keanu,exec25_bsw_turing,exec25_bsw_choice}

The source comparison retains 57 original files across five repositories, with immutable Git identities and local integrity records. Four bounded discovery queries do not establish ecosystem saturation. The selected Keanu revision is from September 2021 and is treated as a historical reference, not evidence of current supported service. The other selected revisions precede the 7 October 2026 cutoff. Declared development versions and dependency compatibility ranges are not proofs that particular distributed binaries were acquired or installed together. Reused audits retain their original identity, date and scope; they are not counted as newly collected evidence. No downloaded implementation was executed, and no new scholarly paper is admitted through a package's bibliography or comments.~\citep{exec25_bsw_choice}

\subsection{The posterior is conditional on an identification decision}

Let $D$ be the retained observations, $M$ a candidate structural model, $\theta$ its parameters, and $Z_{1:T}$ a latent-state path. A numerical worker may target
\[
 p(\theta,Z_{1:T}\mid D,M)
 \ \propto\ p(D\mid\theta,Z_{1:T},M)
 p(Z_{1:T}\mid\theta,M)p(\theta\mid M).
\]
This expression identifies the conditioning information and prior but does not specify whether the intervention contrast is identified from the observed law. For an exact witness, take a fair binary $U$ and compare $M_1:A=U,Y=A$ with $M_0:A=U,Y=U$. The observed pairs coincide under the two models. The intervention effects differ: one under $M_1$ and zero under $M_0$. Equal observational likelihoods leave posterior model odds equal to prior model odds. Prior odds of $3:2$ give a posterior mean effect of $3/5$, entirely dependent on that unresolved model choice. More draws from either model cannot create the missing assignment information. This is a mathematical limitation under the stated models, not a defect in a sampler.~\citep{exec23_pymc_reference,exec25_bsw_choice}

The interface should consequently return two separate statements: a computational assessment conditional on the declared model, and a scientific assessment of the intervention assumptions. Sensitivity across observationally indistinguishable models is more informative than reporting one narrow interval without its model restriction. Additional measurements, a justified assignment design, or an explicitly narrower target can change this situation. Universal identification of an unrestricted hidden state from arbitrary observed banking fields is not a promised repair. A model-writing LLM may retrieve candidate restrictions and explain their consequences, but cannot establish them by producing valid model syntax.~\citep{exec23_numpyro_reference,exec24_bnlearn_contract,exec25_bsw_choice}

Posterior parameter uncertainty also differs from state and observation uncertainty. Under a $\operatorname{Beta}(2,3)$ prior and six successes plus four failures, the posterior is $\operatorname{Beta}(8,7)$. Its mean is $8/15$ and variance $7/450$. For two future Bernoulli trials sharing this unknown probability, the posterior predictive count variance is $119/225$. Plugging in the mean probability gives only $112/225$, omitting $7/225$. The law of total variance explains the difference: uncertainty about the common probability creates dependence between future outcomes. This exact reference checks more than a posterior point estimate and gives a small public oracle for whether a worker propagates the uncertainty promised by its result type. It does not validate the causal interpretation of the success indicator.~\citep{exec25_bsw_choice}

\subsection{R: explicit sampler configurations and component-specific diagnostics}

RStan and NIMBLE offer different proposed worker routes. The inspected RStan metadata declares a development version, an R interface to Stan, GPL version 3 or later for RStan, and dependencies including StanHeaders. The separately inspected StanHeaders metadata has a BSD three-clause declaration. The standalone Stan revision reviewed here declares version 2.40.0, whereas the selected RStan and StanHeaders metadata declare 2.39.0.9000. These are independent source pins; compatibility of a particular combined installation must be qualified rather than inferred from the shared project name. A direct R worker can retain RStan's model and diagnostic objects; a port must separately reproduce model compilation, unconstrained transformations, generated quantities and numerical diagnostics.~\citep{exec25_bsw_rstan,exec25_bsw_stan}

The inspected automatic-warning routine and explicit diagnostic helper illustrate why a common label is insufficient. Missing divergence or tree-depth columns contribute zero to the automatic warning counts, and the fixed-parameter algorithm bypasses that warning routine. This does not mean an unavailable diagnostic was measured and passed. The automatic energy warning uses parameter count divided by sample energy variance, while the explicit BFMI helper uses average squared successive energy increments divided by sample variance. For the illustrative energy sequence $(0,10,0,10)$ and one parameter, sample variance is $100/3$. The former ratio is $3/100$, and the latter is $9/4$. At the selected threshold $1/5$ they give different flags. The proposal should record the function, formula, inputs and missing-diagnostic status; it should not silently relabel every quantity as the same universal convergence certificate. These are exact consequences of the inspected formulas, not native results obtained from a fitted bank model.~\citep{exec25_bsw_rstan,exec25_bsw_choice}

The same routine applies its selected R-hat and effective-sample-size thresholds. Such diagnostics are useful evidence about sampled chains, but an apparently satisfactory chain can remain conditional on an inadequate model or an unvisited mode. A two-mode target with equally weighted means $-10$ and $10$ has mean zero; a collection restricted to the positive mode has mean ten. Replicating that restricted behavior across chains does not correct the omitted mode. The public experiment should include deliberately separated modes and known moments, alongside ordinary conjugate examples, and retain all attempts rather than only chains that returned attractive diagnostics.~\citep{exec25_bsw_rstan,exec25_bsw_choice}

NIMBLE's selected configuration distinguishes sampled nodes, monitored nodes, conjugate updates and scalar or block updates. Its posterior-predictive grouping checks the downstream sampling set; the corresponding sampler simulates downstream stochastic and deterministic nodes, recalculates stochastic log probabilities and updates saved state. The build path can reorder predictive samplers after other updates, but refuses certain custom orders and option-dependent mixtures of predictive and nonpredictive nodes. In the inspected default multivariate assignment branch, partially observed multivariate-normal nodes receive a specific partial-normal sampler; other partially observed multivariate distributions are refused there. This bounded statement concerns that branch, not every possible user-written NIMBLE algorithm. A bank adapter must preserve which values are posterior states and which are newly generated outcomes.~\citep{exec25_bsw_nimble}

Chain construction and continuation have separate meanings. Without supplied initial values, the selected \texttt{runMCMC} path uses the current model values for the first chain and the preceding chain's endpoint for later chains. It has explicit per-chain seed handling. This is not a claim that every such run is invalid, but it is a reason to avoid describing the chains as independently initialized without evidence. The wrapper does not expose the low-level continuation option. At the lower level, first execution forces a reset; later continuation can retain adaptation and append draws, while a separate flag can clear retained draws. Nonzero burn-in is refused during continuation, and a changed thinning interval can warn if a prior save cycle is incomplete. Persistence of these live objects is not itself a demonstrated durable crash-recovery protocol.~\citep{exec25_bsw_nimble}

For a causal R workflow, bnlearn remains a distinct alternative when its supported fitted graphical-model operations match the target; its reviewed graph surgery, Gaussian fitted paths and other fallback classes must not be conflated. For state-space work, bssm's R interface and C++ kernels offer another route with separately reviewed innovation thresholds, posterior subsampling and importance weights. RStan, NIMBLE, bnlearn and bssm should therefore be selected by the model, intervention and uncertainty contract, not ranked as interchangeable implementations of a generic Bayesian command. Conditional direct reuse with typed adapters is the default proposal; a cross-language reimplementation requires a demonstrated operational benefit and a much larger conformance obligation.~\citep{exec24_bnlearn_sources,exec24_bnlearn_contract,exec25_cdyn_bssm,exec25_cdyn_choice,exec25_bsw_choice}

\subsection{Python: model surgery, posterior prediction and state information clocks}

Sections~\ref{exec23:pymc-scope} and~\ref{sec:exec23-numpyro-worker} develop the PyMC and NumPyro evidence. Their comparison here focuses on the common scientific contract. PyMC distinguishes observed-variable transformation from intervention and controls whether stored random or deterministic quantities are frozen or regenerated. NumPyro uses execution handlers, distinguishes conditioning, substitution and intervention, and has its own predictive substitution and discrete-inference routes. A converter must state which model variables remain conditioned on historical evidence, which mechanisms change, which outcomes are regenerated, and whether the requested result is a posterior average, a decoded state, or a cross-world counterfactual. Name and shape matching alone cannot establish equivalence.~\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_numpyro_sources}

The existing exact references give common tests of those differences: association versus intervention under hidden confounding, recomputed versus retained deterministic expressions, posterior averaging versus a most-probable-state decision, and distinct cross-world couplings with identical intervention marginals. The implementation should carry those references into one request schema and require each worker to state which requests it supports. General cross-world identification should be refused when the model has not supplied a defensible coupling, even if a backend can mechanically reuse random values. PyMC's Potential and predictive-regeneration limits and NumPyro's discrete scan documentation/test tension remain part of this comparison; a common interface must not hide either restriction.~\citep{exec23_pymc_reference,exec23_numpyro_reference,exec25_bsw_choice}

Dynamax and PyDLM occupy a different role. The reviewed Dynamax HMM interface uses initial, transition and emission factors; it does not automatically introduce an observed-action logger into that factorization. Its filtering and smoothing information sets differ. Its EM helper returns a parameter/objective sequence under a specified iteration budget, which is not a global convergence or causal-identification certificate. Its destination-time input convention, fixed standard-transition branch and impossible-support behavior also require the explicit restrictions developed in Section~\ref{exec19:dynamax-scope}. The reviewed PyDLM path has its own missing-step and innovation conventions, while dynamic-feature availability remains a caller obligation. These records justify model-specific state workers and public finite-path comparisons, not a universal causal interpretation of every hidden credit factor.~\citep{exec19_dynamax_source,exec19_dynamax_reference,exec25_pydyn_pydlm,exec25_pydyn_choice}

An exact information-clock example makes this operational. Let a binary customer state persist for two periods, with prior probability $1/2$. Each observed signal agrees with that state with probability $3/4$, conditionally independently. After a positive first signal, filtering gives probability $3/4$ that the state is one. After two positive signals, the retrospective probability for the first-period state is $9/10$. If an action pays USD 100 in state one and loses USD 400 in state zero, its first-period expected value is USD $-25$, while the future-informed calculation gives USD 50. Acting on the latter as though it were available at the first decision can change the action. Applying that mistaken rule to 10,000 otherwise comparable first-signal cases gives an illustrative expected loss of USD 250,000 under the first-period law. The numbers are invented exact examples, not measured bank performance; the point is that a correct smoother can answer the wrong decision-time question.~\citep{exec25_bsw_choice}

Python qualification should therefore compare supported finite models against enumeration, preserve exact observation and action timestamps, and separate state filtering, smoothing, prediction and posterior parameter integration. GPU or compilation claims need actual device and full-cost measurements. Existing PyMC and NumPyro diagnostic and continuation evidence should remain backend-specific; matching a field called ESS does not prove the same definition, chain layout or diagnostic availability. The current source evidence supports conditional worker routes, while full ecosystem coverage, installed dependency rights and native qualification remain incomplete.~\citep{exec23_pymc_sources,exec23_numpyro_sources,exec25_bsw_choice}

\subsection{Java: observation status, adaptation and retained samples}

The selected Keanu original provides a historical Java probabilistic-programming comparison. Its network separates latent vertices from observed vertices and includes both in the joint probability calculation. The inspected \texttt{observe} method sets a value and observation status; it does not itself remove incoming factors. The probability calculator still evaluates probabilistic vertex contributions. Accordingly, observing an action is not established as structural intervention by that method. A normal value setter also leaves an already observed vertex unchanged. A model-conversion test must verify the graph and observation state rather than assume that setting a Java value has replaced a mechanism. Numeric deterministic vertices face specific observation restrictions in the selected method.~\citep{exec25_bsw_keanu}

The default Metropolis--Hastings configuration uses a prior proposal and single-variable selection. The selected NUTS setup obtains continuous latent variables, expects a supported gradient model and checks the initial joint probability. These choices differ in support and proposal geometry. One should not advertise a universal mixed-state NUTS route from the existence of both APIs. Moreover, the selected NUTS default adapts for 1,000 steps, while its generic sample generator defaults to dropping zero initial steps. The adapter must deliberately align adaptation and retained inference draws. The presence of an adaptation setting does not imply that the returned samples have automatically excluded it.~\citep{exec25_bsw_keanu}

Sample accounting is explicit in the generator: the requested count includes transitions that will be dropped or skipped, and retained positions start at zero after the initial drop. With eleven transitions, two dropped and thinning interval four, the remaining positions $0,4,8$ give three retained draws, not the floor of $9/4$. This is a useful boundary fixture for translating budgets between libraries. It also shows why an API parameter called sample count should not be mapped without its definition. General NUTS statistics are disabled by default in the selected builder, but post-adaptation divergence storage and warnings have a separate branch. Tetrad and JDemetra+ supply substantive Java alternatives in different roles. The reviewed Tetrad temporal operations construct lag identities and enforce selected restrictions; those graph operations are not themselves Bayesian parameter integration. JDemetra+'s reviewed state-space paths propagate process covariance through missing measurements and distinguish diffuse initialization and regression uncertainty. \subsection{C++: service success, probability targets and approximation error}

Stan's selected adaptive diagonal-metric NUTS service illustrates why process success, service return codes, retained draws and scientific acceptance must all be inspected. Configuration failures and propagated sampling exceptions return different codes. Inside the inspected adaptive-run helper, however, an exception during initial step-size search is caught, logged and followed by a void return before sample headers and draws. The outer service can then reach its normal return. This is a source-level conditional path, not a reproduced native incident. When sampling proceeds, warmup, adaptation shutdown, adapted-state writing and retained transitions have distinct stages. The selected diagnose service compares automatic derivatives with finite differences at the evaluated point. That local derivative check does not establish global exploration, a proper posterior, correct missing-data assumptions or causal identification. \subsection{Julia and other languages: component composition does not prove a valid joint kernel}

The selected Turing source declares version 0.49.0 and a Julia 1.10.8 compatibility requirement, alongside separately versioned DynamicPPL, AbstractMCMC, AdvancedHMC and other dependencies. The reviewed HMC wrapper resolves adaptation and initial-draw removal for a fresh chain. When an initial state is supplied, the selected branch requests zero additional adaptation and zero initial dropping. That is an in-memory continuation contract, not demonstrated persistence across changed model code, dependency versions or interrupted serialization. A public restart fixture must bind the model, data, transformations, sampler state, RNG lineage and actual runtime artifacts.~\citep{exec25_bsw_turing}

Gibbs composition needs an additional scientific and numerical review. The inspected implementation conditions each block on current non-target values and checks appearances, disappearances and missing reported values. Its documentation separately warns that support changes across blocks can destroy irreducibility even when each conditional update has the right invariant law. A changed block design or a supported joint update may repair a particular model, but a general repair cannot be inferred from interface compatibility alone.~\citep{exec25_bsw_turing}

The analytical \texttt{GibbsConditional} component draws from user-supplied conditional distributions and refuses use outside the intended Gibbs context. The callback's correctness is a substantive obligation. For a small finite joint law, the qualification oracle can enumerate the full conditionals and compare the callback to them before any long run. The particle path makes the time contract equally concrete. Particle weight ESS is also not MCMC ESS or an ancestor-diversity certificate. Weights $(0.9,0.1)$ give $1/(0.9^2+0.1^2)=50/41$. Two equal weights give two, even if the two particles descend from one identical ancestor. The output should retain weight concentration and genealogy as separate diagnostics. Nor does a log-normalizer estimate acquire every property of a normalizer estimate: random estimates $1/2$ and $3/2$ with equal probability have mean one, but mean log $\tfrac12\log(3/4)$ differs from log mean zero. This elementary example motivates repeated public calibration of the reported quantity without asserting any uninspected theorem about the selected particle implementation.~\citep{exec25_bsw_turing,exec25_bsw_choice}

StateSpaceModels.jl and gretl remain distinct, already reviewed alternatives for classical state-space computation. The selected Julia fitting paths retain fitted hyperparameters, numerical penalties and particular missing-coordinate handling, while gretl has its own missing-step propagation and dataset-layout assumptions. A fitted state-space likelihood with plug-in parameters should not be described as full Bayesian model averaging without an additional parameter-integration mechanism. Other language families and candidates remain outside this bounded comparison; their omission is recorded rather than interpreted as absence of relevant software.~\citep{exec25_odyn_ssm,exec25_odyn_gretl,exec25_odyn_choice,exec25_bsw_choice}

\subsection{A public prototype portfolio and a defensible integration decision}

The third checks conjugate posterior and predictive moments, including the dependence created by shared parameter uncertainty. These are proposed responsibilities to be assigned, not evidence that a production team or service already exists. Direct adoption should preserve a library's supported native semantics. \section{Commercial Bayesian and hidden-state engines: what a posterior can justify}
\label{exec22:state-scope}

A servicing decision may depend on a credit state that the bank cannot directly
observe. A missed payment, account balance and contact history provide evidence
about that state, but the proposed action changes a mechanism rather than merely
revealing another measurement. A useful commercial comparison must therefore
separate four questions: what state is plausible given information available now;
what parameters are plausible within a specified model; which model restrictions
the observations identify; and what happens when the bank changes an action.
An accurate answer to the first two questions need not answer the last two.

The relevant commercial landscape includes graphical-model engines, statistical
estimators and general numerical environments. Three complete Stata command
manuals add detail to the previously inspected GeNIe/SMILE, Netica, BayesiaLab and
Mplus profiles. These five supported families allow a substantive comparison,
while SAS, EViews, HUGIN, Bayes Server and commercial numerical environments
remain candidates requiring further matched technical evidence. The comparison
retains the 7 October 2026 literature cutoff. The Stata copies have July 2026 PDF
metadata and release-19 example routes, but later retrieval does not authenticate
their exact cutoff-era bytes or an installed licensed build. The older sources
retain their stated manual families and mutable-page limitations. None of these
qualifications licenses a claim that an unevaluated product lacks a feature.

\subsection{Supplied-model computation and the missing causal restriction}
\label{exec22:state-identification}

Let $A\in\{0,1\}$ denote a servicing action and $D\in\{0,1\}$ a subsequent
default. Consider two deliberately small, fully specified mechanisms. In the
selection mechanism, $U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ is an unobserved
favourable credit state, $N\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/5)$ is independent noise,
and
\begin{equation}
 A=U\mathbin{\oplus}N,\qquad D=1-U.
 \label{exec22:state-selection}
\end{equation}
The exclusive-or operation flips its first binary argument when $N=1$. In the
causal mechanism, an independent action $A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$
replaces the first equation, and $D=(1-A)\mathbin{\oplus}N$. Both mechanisms give
exactly the same observable table:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &D=0&D=1\\ \hline
A=0&1/10&2/5\\
A=1&2/5&1/10
\end{array}
\label{exec22:state-table}
\end{equation}
Consequently, both yield $P(D=1\mid A=0)=4/5$ and
$P(D=1\mid A=1)=1/5$. Under an intervention that replaces only the action
equation, however, the selection mechanism retains default risk $1/2$ for
either action. The causal mechanism has risks $4/5$ and $1/5$. The identified
contrast within each stipulated mechanism is therefore different even though
every observable cell agrees. This is a project derivation by complete finite
enumeration, not a finding about actual customers.~\citep{exec22_state_reference}

With an assumed loss of USD~1,000 per default and action cost USD~100, the net
value of universal action relative to no action is USD~$-100$ under selection
and USD~500 under the causal mechanism. More observations from this same law
cannot distinguish those values. If $w$ is the prior probability of the causal
mechanism in a two-model analysis with these fixed parameters, equal likelihoods
leave its posterior probability equal to $w$ for every observed sample. The
posterior expected margin is
\begin{equation}
 V(w)=600w-100\quad\text{USD},\qquad V(w)>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ w>1/6.
 \label{exec22:state-prior}
\end{equation}
Priors $w=1/10$ and $w=9/10$ imply USD~$-40$ and USD~440. These numbers expose
the decision's sensitivity to a causal assumption that the likelihood cannot
learn in this example. They do not prohibit Bayesian decisions under declared
assumptions; they require the source and consequences of those assumptions to
remain visible.

BayesiaLab's inspected documentation distinguishes observation from intervention
and names graph surgery and likelihood matching. Its identification discussion
requires causal information expressed in a DAG before estimation. This is a
useful conceptual strength for an adapter. It does not mean that drawing the
action arrow or invoking graph surgery establishes that the bank's graph is
true.~\citep{exec13_ct_bayesia_inference,exec13_ct_bayesia_identification}
An agent could help enumerate the two mechanisms, identify which records might
distinguish them and draft a public experiment. It must retain both until justified
restrictions or new information resolve their difference. A natural-language
statement of confidence cannot replace that missing evidence.

\subsection{State inference must preserve the decision clock}
\label{exec22:state-clock}

The supplied state model may nevertheless be valuable for prediction and decision
support. GeNIe's inspected DBN overview represents a stationary discrete-time
stochastic process. Its discrete-DBN learning page fits parameters by EM in an
already specified structure; the selected page explicitly excludes learning the
dynamic structure. Time-indexed columns must match node identifiers, and existing
network evidence is ignored during that parameter-learning operation. These are
concrete semantic requirements, not interchangeable user-interface details.
\citep{exec13_ct_genie_dbn,exec13_ct_genie_learning}

SMILE's C++ tutorial distinguishes initial and transition conditional-probability
tables, temporal lag order, the number of unfolded slices and integer outcome
indices. A flattened belief array is ordered by slice and outcome. A proposed
Python, Java or R wrapper must preserve those mappings before its output can be
compared with a native model. A matching array shape is insufficient if the
meaning of outcome zero or the initial slice changes.
\citep{exec13_ct_smile_tutorial}

Stata's documented \texttt{sspace} procedure estimates linear state-space
parameters by maximum likelihood using Kalman or diffuse-filter likelihoods.
Its general form separates a latent state $z_t$, measured variables $y_t$,
exogenous inputs $x_t,w_t$ and disturbances:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 z_t&=Fz_{t-1}+Bx_t+C\varepsilon_t,\\
 y_t&=Hz_t+Gw_t+J\nu_t.
\end{aligned}
\label{exec22:state-model}
\end{equation}
The coefficient matrices are time invariant in this documented profile.
The stated likelihood uses Gaussian, serially uncorrelated disturbances with
state and observation errors uncorrelated with each other. The documented robust
variance option and stationary/nonstationary initialization methods address
different statistical and numerical choices; they do not certify that a bank
action placed among the regressors is exogenous. A proposed adapter must retain
the declared covariance structure, model constraints and initial-state method.
\citep{exec22_state_stata_sspace}

The postestimation interface makes an especially important distinction:
one-step predictions use past information, filtered states use past and current
information, and smoothed states use all sample information. Its prediction
sample can change the recursive state estimates. The bank's information set
must therefore include availability times, not merely a date column.
\citep{exec22_state_stata_sspace_post}

For an exact example, let a hidden distress state $H$ remain constant for two
dates, with $P(H=1)=1/2$. Conditional on $H$, independent indicators $X_0,X_1$
equal $H$ with probability $3/4$. On observing $X_0=1$, the filtered probability
is $3/4$. If the later observation is $X_1=0$, retrospective smoothing gives
\begin{equation}
 P(H=1\mid X_0=1,X_1=0)
 =\frac{(3/4)(1/4)}{(3/4)(1/4)+(1/4)(3/4)}=1/2.
 \label{exec22:state-smoothing}
\end{equation}
Suppose a separate, stipulated causal model says an action prevents default with
probability $1/10$ when $H=1$, has no effect otherwise, costs USD~60 and avoids
USD~1,000 when successful. Its filtered expected margin is USD~15; using the
later smoothed probability gives USD~$-10$. The latter is a legitimate
retrospective calculation, but it was unavailable when the action was chosen.
The example assumes that the causal benefit law is known; filtering does not
identify that law.~\citep{exec22_state_reference}

Observability is another distinct obligation. In a noise-free two-state model
with $F=(9/10)I$ and $H=(1,1)$, the observability matrix formed by $H$ and $HF$
has rank one. The initial states $(1,0)$ and $(2,-1)$ generate the same output
$(9/10)^t$ at every date, but their first-minus-second component contrasts are
one and three. The null direction $(1,-1)$ remains invisible. Adding a prior
can regularize or select a state representation; it does not make the contrast
observable from these measurements. New measurements, constraints justified by
the application, or a decision defined only on observable combinations are
substantive alternatives. State observability, parameter identification and
causal identification each require their own argument.

\subsection{A posterior, a prediction error and a simulation error answer different questions}
\label{exec22:state-uncertainty}

Stata's \texttt{bayesmh} provides adaptive Metropolis--Hastings estimation with
explicit likelihoods and priors. Its manual cautions that allowed combinations
need not produce a proper posterior, and that no single diagnostic establishes
convergence. Multiple starting points, all model parameters and requested
functions need attention; apparent mixing can conceal multimodal
pseudoconvergence. Chain count, burn-in, adaptation, thinning and random seeds
must therefore be retained in a reproducible specification. The documented
defaults are starting configurations, not universal adequacy guarantees.
\citep{exec22_state_stata_bayesmh}

Netica's inspected learning text instead describes counting, EM and
conjugate-gradient parameter fitting, with prior information supplied through
preexisting tables and experience. Its objective includes both data likelihood
and prior terms, and local optima remain possible. A fitted table should not be
relabeled a sample from a full parameter posterior.~\citep{exec13_latent_netica_algorithms}
The Mplus 8.1 addendum supplies a complementary Bayesian dynamic-SEM profile,
including latent centering and outcome versus residual lag distinctions. Its
missing-covariate rule can remove observations unless the covariate is modeled
as an outcome. A servicing comparison must preserve that modeling decision and
its observation set. The addendum alone does not supply a complete selected
release's prior, sampler and licensing specification.~\citep{exec13_ct_mplus81}

Uncertainty must be named before being compared. For a parameter $\theta$,
training information $\mathcal D$ and a future outcome $Y$, total variance is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid\mathcal D)
 &=E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid\theta,\mathcal D)\mid\mathcal D]\\
 &\quad+\operatorname{Var}(E[Y\mid\theta,\mathcal D]\mid\mathcal D).
\end{aligned}
\label{exec22:state-variance}
\end{equation}
The first term describes conditional outcome variation; the second propagates
parameter uncertainty. For $\theta\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$ and
$Y\mid\theta\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ with no data, these terms
are $1/6$ and $1/12$, totaling $1/4$. For two future outcomes conditionally
independent given their shared $\theta$, the variance of their count is
$2(1/6)+4(1/12)=2/3$. Treating the probability as fixed at its posterior mean
$1/2$ gives $1/2$ instead. The shared unknown parameter induces dependence.
\citep{exec22_state_reference}

The selected \texttt{sspace} postestimation manual explicitly states that its
reported prediction RMSE includes disturbance variation but does not account
for parameter-estimation error. It must therefore be labeled accordingly.
An adapter could add a justified parameter-propagation layer, but its result
would be an additional construction requiring its own assumptions and tests.
Neither layer automatically represents graph uncertainty or uncertainty about
the action mechanism.~\citep{exec22_state_stata_sspace_post}

Monte Carlo error is separate again: it measures finite simulation error in a
posterior summary. The \texttt{bayesmh} manual distinguishes effective-sample-size
and batch-means MCSE calculations. Its saved dataset also compresses repeated
states into rows with chain, iteration and frequency fields. Frequencies are
required when summarizing these rows.~\citep{exec22_state_stata_bayesmh}
For values $0.1$ and $0.9$ with frequencies nine and one, the posterior-sample
mean is $0.18$, whereas the unweighted row mean is $0.5$. A CSV conversion that
retains parameter values but drops frequencies silently changes the result.
This exact arithmetic is an export-contract example, not a native execution
test.~\citep{exec22_state_reference}

\subsection{From numerical tolerances to a conditional decision bound}
\label{exec22:state-approximation}

An approximate belief need not support the same action as its reference. Suppose
the exact and approximate probabilities of a beneficial state are $0.61$ and
$0.59$, an action produces USD~100 in that state and costs USD~60. The expected
margins are USD~1 and USD~$-1$. A small probability discrepancy can matter near a
decision threshold even when average predictive scores look similar.

A conditional bound clarifies what an approximation contract would need. Let
$b_t$ and $\widetilde b_t$ be exact and approximate marginal state laws under
the \emph{same fixed policy}. Define total variation as
$\|b-\widetilde b\|_{\rm TV}=\frac12\sum_s|b(s)-\widetilde b(s)|$.
If the reward at each date has range at most $R$, and the marginal discrepancy
is at most $\epsilon_t$, then
\begin{equation}
 |V-\widetilde V|\leq
 R\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t\epsilon_t,\qquad 0\leq\gamma<1.
 \label{exec22:state-bound}
\end{equation}
To see this, subtract the reward minimum, bound each expected-reward difference
by its range times total variation, and apply the triangle inequality to the
discounted sum. With $R=100$ dollars, $\epsilon_t=0.02$, $\gamma=0.9$ and
$T=12$, the bound is
$2(1-0.9^{12})/(1-0.9)$, approximately USD~14.3514.
\citep{exec22_state_reference}

This argument assumes the marginal error bounds. A one-step transition
approximation, a sampler stopping rule or agreement on one benchmark does not
establish them. Nor does a bound for one fixed policy automatically cover the
regret of a different policy selected using approximate beliefs. Deriving
propagation under a specified transition law, verifying it on known-law public
examples, and separating policy-selection error are the next scientific steps.
The proposed product should report these limits instead of attaching an
unqualified confidence label to the action value.

\subsection{Commercial comparison and an executable proposal route}
\label{exec22:state-comparison}

The vendors have different documented strengths. Stata supplies explicit
state/observation equations, initialization options, information-set choices,
Bayesian diagnostics and persistent simulation exports. GeNIe/SMILE supplies
graphical DBNs and concrete native temporal representations. Netica supplies
parameter-learning alternatives with latent or incomplete data. BayesiaLab
distinguishes observation, intervention and identification assumptions. Mplus
supplies a Bayesian dynamic-SEM modeling route. The proposal currently has
exact authored references and semantic requirements; it has no qualified native
adapter for these profiles. Its proposed contribution is to preserve causal
meaning and refusals consistently across the library, CLI and MCP, including
the agent's translation of a business question into a model.

The appropriate reuse route is a conditional, isolated licensed worker with a
versioned exchange contract. A request must identify its task, decision time,
outcome horizon, units, observed and latent variables, action mechanism and
admissible information. A returned result must distinguish filtered from
smoothed states, process from parameter uncertainty, and posterior from Monte
Carlo variation. It must preserve native diagnostics, chain frequencies,
parameter names, initialization and failed calculations. Replacing a vendor's
engine or porting its mathematics is a separate rights and validation decision;
public documentation is not a redistribution entitlement. Prices, commercial
deployment rights and measured operating costs remain unavailable in this
comparison, so no saving or superiority is asserted.

SAS's selected MCMC PDF transfer was incomplete, and the selected EViews
endpoints failed. Initial HUGIN requests also failed; the later official
comparison in Section~\ref{gap29:software-followup} supplies conditional-Gaussian
network and finite influence-diagram documentation. It does not establish a
qualified hidden-state servicing path. The MATLAB panel documentation in that
section answers a different estimation question. \citep{gap29hugin,gap29matlab}
Complete selected-release MCMC/SSM manuals, official state-model help, HUGIN
DBN and intervention documentation, Bayes Server's versioned SDK contracts,
and MATLAB/Wolfram state-model originals remain concrete recovery targets.
Broader complementary searches must also test whether the shortlist misses
a product family. These source obligations can proceed independently of
public prototype design; failed routes do not prove a capability absent.

The first prototype comparison can use the finite laws above, supplied-state
models with known parameters, and deliberately misspecified alternatives.
Before execution, freeze the query, information clock, causal assumptions,
uncertainty components, monetary inputs and admissible errors. Compare exact
enumeration with a licensed native inference path; test a timestamp violation,
an unobservable contrast, a frequency-loss export, posterior impropriety and a
policy near its decision threshold. Record both correct answers and justified
refusals. Only after such comparisons show merit should the proposal request
costly bank-specific experiments. This route develops useful public evidence
while retaining the unresolved assumptions and commercial terms.


\subsection{From a conditional calculation to a defensible banking action}

The chapter supports conditional use of these engines, with a separate argument
for the action being evaluated. A justified likelihood and a well-behaved
sampler answer computational questions within a model. An intervention requires
an account of which mechanism changes; a prospective decision requires the
information actually available at that time. Posterior averaging, state decoding,
conditional prediction and cross-world simulation remain different requests.
Their numerical and scientific failure states belong in the recommendation.

The next chapter adds another source of dependence: one customer's action can
affect another customer's outcome. A precise individual-state calculation does
not by itself define that allocation problem. The relevant joint policy,
exposure mapping and population must be supplied before comparing its value.
