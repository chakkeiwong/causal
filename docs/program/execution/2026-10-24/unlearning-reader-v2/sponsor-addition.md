# Agent forgetting needs an explicit observation contract

Removing an obsolete customer record from retrieval does not show what remains
in prompts, tool replies, summaries and model weights. K-Bench supplies a public
design for observing these surfaces together with retained functionality and
protocol degeneration. Its declared observer tests the queried entity in
individual channels; it does not establish knowledge erasure or universal
information-flow safety. Joint reconstruction and other-entity disclosures lie
outside its principal endpoint.\citep{exec24_unlearning_original}

The proposal adds synthetic tests for split values, any forget-entity disclosure,
correct refusal, service quality and artifact identity. In an exact authored
binary example, either isolated channel leaves expected decision loss USD 100
while their combination permits zero under a stipulated USD 200 error cost.
This illustrates information combination, not measured bank value or a privacy
price. Repeating 200 entity outcomes three times gives variance 1/800 rather
than 1/2400; query alignment alone does not establish independence.
\citep{exec24_unlearning_reference}

The pinned implementation offers useful transcript and health contracts, but
the paper's twofold reduction condition disagrees with a visible classifier
branch, and its real-data wording conflicts with its synthetic-only statement.
The local model fingerprint omits weight contents. Direct MIT code terms do not
cover every model, corpus or dependency. A conditional original Python worker
with typed CLI/MCP records is a prospective option after scorer, provenance,
environment and rights review; no native integration is claimed. The authors'
approximate 500 GPU-hour study total is not a costed bank experiment. Public
generated fixtures can establish prototype merit before institutional validation.
\citep{exec24_unlearning_software}
