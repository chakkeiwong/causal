12.1      What a hidden-state fit must establish
The likelihood and policy-replacement recursions in Chapter 10 leave an identification question
open. Which controlled transition, reward and observation kernels can the recorded law
determine? The answer depends on who sees the state, how actions are assigned, what is
measured, and which actions the target policy will use. A repayment trajectory can identify a
useful predictive state without identifying the effect of an untried restructuring.
   For an uncontrolled stationary K-state HMM, Alexandrovich et al. (2016, Assumptions
A1–A3, Theorem 1) identifies a full-rank ergodic transition matrix and distinct emission
distributions from a block of 2K + 1 observations, up to state-label permutation. Distinct
emissions need not be linearly independent in that theorem. It is a result about the specified
uncontrolled observation law. Treating a privately selected action as an exogenous input
changes its factorization, so the theorem cannot simply be transferred to that fit.
   Identification of the value of one supported policy can require less information than iden-
tification of every state parameter. Conversely, identifying all parameters of one chosen
observational model says nothing about excluded causal models with the same observed law.
The following finite construction separates these issues explicitly.

12.2.1      An observed reward can carry additional state information

Mean rewards suffice for evaluating a policy that ignores reward as information and uses the
recovered signal process. They need not suffice for the full-information belief recursion in
Equation (10.16). If the bank observes the reward before its next decision, its conditional
distribution can change the posterior over states even when its mean is unchanged.
   To identify that richer recursion, strengthen the emission restriction: conditional on
(St , St+1 , At , Rt ) the two signals have independent laws E·St and E·St+1 . For every measurable
reward set D, the observed marked joint matrix then satisfies

            Ja,D (o, o′ ) = P (Ot = o, Rt ∈ D, Ot+1 = o′ | At = a) = E diag(µt )Ka,D E ′ ,

where Ka,D (s, s′ ) = Ta (s, s′ )P (Rt ∈ D | s, a, s′ ). The same multiplication yields

                                    Ka,D = diag(µt )−1 LJa,D L′ .

Thus the marked observed law identifies the transition–reward measure, and division by
Ta (s, s′ ) > 0 identifies its reward conditional law on supported state transitions. For finite
reward categories this is one matrix inversion per category. For general rewards it is an identi-
fication statement about all measurable D, not a finite-sample density-estimation guarantee.
Together with known invariant emissions, these joint kernels supply the information update as
well as expected reward. Merely recovering ra does not establish this stronger result.
   For a summary-preservation counterexample, let a fair binary state persist, with a binary
signal correct with probability 4/5. In one model the first reward is independently +1 or −1
in either state. In the other it is independently +1 or −1 in state zero and +2 or −2 in state
one. Both have the same zero reward mean, transition and signal kernel. At the next decision,
choosing the state correctly pays one. The optimal success probability is 4/5 in the first model
and one in the second, because absolute reward reveals the state. Their full reward observation
laws differ; the example proves insufficiency of the compressed mean-reward interface, not
nonidentification from the full marked law.

12.3      Proxy value identification without a complete latent fit
Past and current observations can sometimes serve as two views of the hidden state. The
matrix identification result of Tennenholtz et al. (2020, Section 3, Theorem 1) uses specific
invertibility conditions and the declared behavior/evaluation information sets. An independent
hidden state at every date can destroy the needed past-to-current rank: a long history is then
not automatically an informative proxy for today’s private confounder.
   The bridge approach makes the connection to Chapter 9 more explicit. Bennett and Kallus
(2023, Section 4, Assumptions 1–2 and Theorem 2) uses a hybrid law Pt∗ that applies the target
policy before t and the logging policy from t onward. For a deterministic target, let Dt be the
action that its observed information would select. Its negative-control restriction has the form

                             (Zt , At ) ⊥ (Wt , Dt , Yt (a)) | Ut   under Pt∗ ,

for the allowed future outcome variables Yt . Here Zt is the action-side proxy, Wt the outcome-
side proxy and Ut the sufficient hidden confounder, possibly augmented by observed context.
This is stronger than saying both proxies predict repayment. In particular, giving the target
policy extra history can invalidate independence of Zt and Dt , even if the physical environment
is unchanged.
   The source’s Assumption 2 requires existence of latent action and outcome bridges qt† , h†t
satisfying

                   E∗t [qt† (Zt , At ) | Ut , At = a] = Pt∗ (At = a | Ut )−1 ,
                  E∗t [h†t (Wt , At ) | Ut , At = a] = E∗t [1(Dt = At )Yt | Ut , At = a].

This includes latent action positivity. It is stronger than merely finding solutions of observed
conditional equations, and is separate from the negative-control independence. Under these
two assumptions its Theorem 2 allows solutions qt , ht of the corresponding observable equations

                   E∗t [qt (Zt , At ) | Wt , At = a] = Pt∗ (At = a | Wt )−1 ,
                  E∗t [ht (Wt , At ) | Zt , At = a] = E∗t [1(Dt = At )Yt | Zt , At = a].

The outcome on the right is recursively constructed from later bridges. These observed
solutions need not be asserted to be the latent bridges themselves. The theorem uses latent
existence and the independence restriction to justify the causal target of the observed solution.
The static augmented-bridge cancellation can then be applied at each hybrid-law step. The
resulting observed-trajectory functional identifies policy value without assigning a uniquely
interpretable latent state to each account. Bridge existence, support and estimation stability
remain necessary; the identification result is not a claim that every matrix inverse or neural
minimax fit will be reliable.
   In the source’s tabular example with Ut = St , Zt = Ot−1 , Wt = Ot and a policy using only
Ot , the outcome bridge requires solving a linear system through Et′ = P (Ot | St )′ . The action
bridge additionally involves the backward state kernel Pt∗ (St−1 | St ) (Bennett and Kallus,

2023, Proposition 1). Full ranks provide a sufficient solvability route, but the backward kernel
depends on the induced policy law. It must be checked for that law; invertibility of each
action-specific transition should not be substituted for checking a policy-dependent mixture

their equal mixture has rank one. This is the same distinction between structural identification
and inverse stability encountered in NPIV, now at each decision date.

12.7.2    Numerical tolerance in the same units as policy value

Within a basin where the negative average log-likelihood Hessian has smallest eigenvalue at least
κ > 0, an average score residual ϵs bounds parameter optimization error by ϵs /κ. A gradient
bound ∥∇V ∥ ≤ GV then gives value error at most GV ϵs /κ. For asymptotic equivalence in
Equation (12.7), require ϵs = o(N −1/2 ) as well as selection of the correct basin. The observed
eigenvalue is a local diagnostic, not proof of a uniform population curvature bound. Solver
success and small likelihood increments alone do not establish an acceptable monetary error.
   Spectral moment inversion is a meaningful alternative: it has a constructive population
solution and avoids iterative latent-path fitting, but inverses and eigengaps can amplify noisy
empirical moments into negative probabilities. Constrained likelihood uses all recorded patterns
and enforces valid probabilities at a cost in nonconvex optimization. Spectral initialization,
likelihood refinement and the exact small-state reference therefore serve different purposes.
Gaussian filtering, particle likelihood and variational inference retain the approximation
distinctions in the preceding section; none inherits this finite-alphabet likelihood theorem
without a new argument.

12.9.3    Numerical and inferential challengers

The new finite experiment fits three states, four signals, private logging, eight decisions, 600
customer trajectories and either 90 or 600 validation labels. It estimates 32 parameters using
two data-based starts. Reward noise standard deviation is known at 12. The true fixed
observable-policy contrast is 59.5645. With 90 labels, bias is 2.0798 and coverage .850; with
600, bias is .1926 and coverage .875. These denominators include all 80 requested replications
per case, including four and five boundary/numerical failures. Those failures yield unavailable
inference. Model, sandwich and fixed-emission approximations happen to give the same
coverage here; the reward is informative about state and the measured calibration contribution
is small. This experiment does not demonstrate a large calibration variance penalty. Omitting
the private logger on the first 20 datasets yields biases of −36.51 and −41.12, respectively.
   The first 600-label dataset was selected before a 199-refit parametric bootstrap challenger. It
resimulates complete trajectories and independent emission measurements conditional on the
audited label counts, then refits the complete model. Seventeen refits hit a boundary or failed,
exceeding the prespecified 5% availability limit, so no percentile/basic interval is released. The
standard deviation among 182 usable refits is 10.20, only a descriptive conditional diagnostic.
Extra starts change the fitted contrast by .0012 and the average negative log likelihood by less
than 10−10 . Information differencing steps 3 × 10−5 , 10−4 , 3 × 10−4 change the model standard
error by less than 4 × 10−8 . The local information condition number is about 1,929. These
checks make numerical roundoff an implausible sole explanation of the observed undercoverage;
they do not prove the global optimum or finite-sample normality.
   The method decision is therefore explicit: this validated finite route is an identified research
fit with a useful value functional, but its current normal/bootstrapped interval is not a
routine calibrated product default. The larger-sample and audited-trajectory experiments
in Section 12.13 provide selected alternatives; they do not retroactively qualify this 600-
trajectory interval. Weak-state/profile inference and bank measurement validation remain
distinct requirements. The observed failure is part of the proposal’s method hierarchy, not a
hidden exception.


12.9.4    Proxies identify a different object with different information

Sequential proxy bridges can identify value without recovering a uniquely labelled latent
transition model (Bennett and Kallus, 2023; Zhang and Tchetgen Tchetgen, 2024). Their
conditional independence and completeness restrictions are different from labelled measurement

validation. The two-stage dynamic-regime source identifies joint counterfactual quantities
along compatible treatment histories under its stated bridge restrictions; it does not identify
every cross-world joint law. Bridge existence must be asserted or proved separately from
uniqueness. For an operator K, injectivity means Kh = 0 implies h = 0; it does not mean
that every observed right-hand side lies in range(K).
   This matters for missing rewards. The 2026 missingness-aware MDP preprint uses augmented
state, a shadow next-state variable, exclusion and completeness to motivate a bridge (Wei et al.,
2026, Assumptions 3.1–3.4, Theorem 3.6). Its general existence discussion cannot be adopted
from injectivity alone. The operator contract therefore needs an integrable bridge solution
and the relevant range condition before uniqueness can justify identification. An inverse-
problem regularization analysis is then needed to control estimation error. In a finite square
full-rank system existence follows for every right-hand side; an infinite-dimensional conditional
expectation operator needs more. Overlap, residual and rank diagnostics are conditional checks.
They cannot certify the unobserved exclusion and completeness assumptions that make an
MNAR query causal.

12.12       Estimation alternatives and the bank–customer interface
A full-solution likelihood estimates utility/transition parameters and solves Equation (12.17)
at each parameter value. A CCP route estimates choice probabilities and transitions first, then
uses an inversion such as Equation (12.18) with identifying restrictions on payoffs. It trades
repeated dynamic programming for first-stage approximation and support requirements: very
small CCPs make log odds unstable, and unobserved customer types cannot be ignored by
pooling their nonlinear choice probabilities.
   Bayesian estimation can integrate preference uncertainty, but the likelihood still depends on
a value solution. Imai et al. (2009, Sections 2.2–2.3) compares conventional nested posterior
simulation with an algorithm that coordinates parameter simulation and approximate value
updates. Its convergence result has explicit regularity, compactness, memory and bandwidth
conditions (Assumptions 1–8 and Theorems 1–2). Performing one approximate Bellman update
inside arbitrary MCMC does not inherit that result.
   For the normalized logit model, a value error at most ϵV changes each vd by at most βϵV .
Since log-sum-exp is one-Lipschitz in sup norm, each log choice probability changes by at
most 2βϵV . Combined with the Bellman-residual bound, this gives a transparent likelihood
error budget. It is a deterministic approximation bound, not a sampling theorem for the fitted
preferences.
   The bank’s action a should remain distinct from the customer’s response d. An offer can
change a contractual cost in ud (x; a), the transition law Pd (· | x; a), or the information available
to the customer. A structural intervention must state which mechanism changes and what
remains invariant. Competitor repricing, funding conditions and capacity can then alter the
84                                                              Causal analysis for a universal bank


environment again. A single-customer policy response conditional on those quantities is not
automatically a banking-group equilibrium effect. The product must return the corresponding
target and require an explicit equilibrium model for the latter question.


12.13      A resolved information and inference design for servicing
The privately logged three-state model is useful precisely because it can fail despite an identified
population value. Resolving that failure requires separating the statistical experiment from
the optimizer and from the proposed decision. Here the servicing decision is whether to offer
temporary assistance; the objective is discounted collected cash net of assistance and operating
cost. Signals arrive before the decision, while the cash rewards used in this four-decision policy
are settled after its decision horizon. The policy may use signals and its own previous actions.
It may not use the later audited state or settled cash as contemporaneous information.


12.13.1     What the retained failure actually shows

For the original 600-trajectory experiment, all nine unavailable fits were on a constrained
transition-logit boundary. Reconstructing those exact seeds reproduces the stored values,
rather than diagnosing a different simulation. With 90 emission labels, 76 of 80 fits were
available: conditional coverage was .8947, empirical value standard deviation 10.668 and mean
model standard error 9.657. With 600 labels, 75 were available: conditional coverage was .9333,
empirical standard deviation 9.141 and mean standard error 9.687. Counting unavailable fits
gives the previously reported .850 and .875. The latter case therefore does not mainly show
underestimated standard errors among the usable fits. Availability, tail error and bias are
different questions. Eighty repetitions leave substantial Monte Carlo uncertainty.
   The binding coordinates are transition logits, including near-zero destinations under privately
selected actions. Larger emission calibration alone does not supply those scarce action/state
transitions. The model’s positive population transition probabilities do not prevent an observed
likelihood maximum near the boundary. The observed fit reconstructions, larger-trajectory
follow-up and complete-state frequency experiment separate this scarcity explanation from
the earlier differencing/start checks. They do not certify a global maximum or establish one
universal cause of undercoverage. At 3,000 observational trajectories and 600 independent
emission labels, all 120 follow-up fits are available; coverage is .9417, bias −.213, empirical
standard deviation 4.097 and mean reported standard error 4.301. This supports a bounded
larger-sample regime on this mechanism, not a universal 3,000-customer rule. The failed
199-refit bootstrap remains adverse evidence for the original design.

12.13.4     Three information routes on the same servicing problem

An observed-history g-formula substitutes the factual conditional law given public signals and
actions. Under private logging its state posterior after action a is proportional to bo (s)qs (a),
while the intervention posterior is bo (s). Thus even an exactly estimated public-history
conditional law uses the wrong state mixture for the intervention. Adding a flexible learner
cannot remove this information gap. Recording the action-relevant private information,
or randomizing assistance with adequate support, changes that conclusion. The common-
mechanism comparison evaluates both recursions on the same frozen policy. Its exact public-
history g-formula contrast is −35.647, against the intervention contrast 25.029. The sign
reversal occurs even without nuisance estimation error.
   Validated emissions can identify the kernels using the earlier marked moment/hidden-
likelihood route, with fewer labels but difficult finite curvature and boundary behavior. Fully
audited trajectories identify the same kernels and policy target more directly, with the explicit
acquisition cost above. Their precision and availability are properties of different sampling
designs, not proof that hidden-state modeling is unnecessary.
   Proximal evaluation instead seeks policy value without recovering named states. For this
servicing mechanism an action-side proxy might be a separately recorded officer assessment, and
an outcome-side proxy an independent affordability measurement. Merely calling them proxies
proves nothing: after conditioning on the latent confounder and allowed context, the action-side
proxy and logged action must be independent of the outcome-side proxy, proposed action and
counterfactual continuation under the source’s hybrid laws. The proposed policy cannot reuse
forbidden private proxy information. Bennett and Kallus (2023, Assumptions 1–2, Theorem 2
and Table 1) give these policy-dependent restrictions and the recursive action/outcome bridges.
In particular their outcome bridge targets E[1{Et = At }Yt | Ut , At = a], where Et denotes the
proposed action; dropping that indicator changes the theorem.
   The source’s recursive bridge score is a substantive challenger when the bank can defend
those exclusions and bridge existence/rank conditions but cannot obtain accurate state labels.
The production stream in this constructed servicing experiment has one public signal stream
and no independently validated second proxy. It cannot invoke that theorem by relabelling
the same signal twice. If an additional proxy is collected, its conditional operator, range,
regularization and policy information set become new scientific inputs. This explains why the
audited trajectory route is selected for the bounded decision capability and proximal evaluation
remains a qualified alternative, rather than claiming that one existing dataset satisfies all three
information regimes.

12.13.5     The initial product boundary

The offering includes finite-state estimation and observable-history policy evaluation under

restrictions affect which bank requests can be accepted; they are not postponed as a generic

independently misclassifying 10% of predecision state labels at 1,200 audit trajectories produces

misspecification example, not a calibrated bound for every 10% audit-error process.

12.14.1     Identification comes from the observation design

Production observes (O, A, R); an independent representative audit observes complete labelled
(S, O, A, R) trajectories. The loading one in O = S + e and the labelled scale of S fix state
meaning. Under positive definite population Gram matrices, regressing St+1 on (St , tanh St , At )
identifies the three transition coefficients and its residual identifies q. The signal residual
identifies ro . Regressing reward on (1, St , At , St At ) identifies its coefficients and variance;
labelled initial states identify (µ0 , v0 ). The clipped logistic probability is strictly increasing in
its index. Equality of these probabilities on a full-rank support for (1, S, O) therefore implies
equality of the logger coefficients. The positive action floor supports both actions; full rank
and representative labels remain substantive requirements. This establishes identification
before filtering. Filtering does not create the audited states. Noisy or selected labels change
this argument.

12.14.4     Boundary failures and a fresh information comparison

The first registered baseline uses 160 audits and 80 production trajectories, with |ρ| ≤ .55,
|γ| ≤ .4. Only 55 of 100 fits are interior; 45 hit a boundary. Of the 55 available intervals, 54
cover. Reporting only 54/55 would hide availability: coverage over requested calculations is
54/100. Misspecified transitions yield 25 unavailable fits out of 25. Weak signals, scarce actions,
biased audits and a six-date horizon are separate retained arms; successful optimization does
not validate their assumptions.
   A fresh registered comparison keeps the original policy and tests |ρ| + |γ| ≤ .95, each
coefficient in [−.9, .9], against the original rectangle. Other bounds stay fixed. With 160
audits the stable domain gives 99 available intervals and 94 covering draws out of 100. With
640 audits all 100 are available and 95 cover, with Monte Carlo interval [.8872, .9836]. Mean
standard error falls from about 168.06 to 95.84 currency units. The rectangular challenger is
available in 59 and 79 draws on these same designs. No result switches methods after observing
its interval.
