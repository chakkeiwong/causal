A complete search can still give an
incomplete lending comparison

A bank can remove an unsound computational shortcut and still obtain an unreliable effect
estimate. The preceding exact-oracle comparison establishes what the search does when every
independence answer is correct. Actual records offer estimates of those answers. A relationship
that is weak after adjustment may disappear from an estimated graph even when its economic
contribution is substantial. If that disappearance then removes a confounder from a lending
comparison, a narrow confidence interval can surround the wrong quantity.
   This distinction motivates a second, deliberately limited study. It asks how sampling error
in the repaired search can affect the controls used to compare rate concessions and balance
responses. It also asks whether the bank’s information clock helps: should a score produced
after the balance response enter the discovery exercise in the first place? The examples are
constructed Gaussian economies with known equations. Their role is to expose a failure
mechanism and evaluate one response, not to estimate its prevalence in banks.


1.1    An independence test is not a confidence statement about
       the graph
For centred Gaussian variables, conditional independence between Xi and Xj given XS is
equivalent to a zero partial correlation ρij|S . The sample test used here follows the Fisher
transformation in Kalisch and Bühlmann (2007, Section 2.2.2, equation (3)):

                   Zij|S =     log            ,   Tij|S =   n − |S| − 3 |Zij|S |.         (1.1)

The search treats the pair as independent when Tij|S ≤ Φ−1 (1 − α/2), where n is the number
of observations, |S| is the number of conditioning variables and Φ is the standard normal
distribution function. Failure to reject a zero correlation is operationally used to delete the
candidate link. It is not proof that the population correlation is zero.
   The same source’s consistency result requires more than a large sample. Its assumptions
include a faithful Gaussian law, graph sparsity and bounds on nonzero partial correlations; the
asymptotic significance sequence depends on an unknown lower signal bound. These conditions
do not supply a finite-sample guarantee at a fixed threshold. (Kalisch and Bühlmann, 2007,
Assumptions A1–A4 and Theorem 1, pp. 619–620)

1.3     What is compared on the same observations
For each mechanism and each sample size n ∈ {100, 500, 2000}, the study generates 200
independent samples. It applies thresholds α = 0.01 and 0.05 to three searches: full stable
subsets, the all-neighbour dependence screen and a depth-two cap. This gives 1,800 datasets
and 10,800 search results. The methods receive identical observations within each comparison.
The depth cap keeps its unfinished-search flag whenever relevant larger sets remain.
  To reveal the consequence of graph errors, an explicitly naive rule chooses baseline variables
that are adjacent to both X and Y in the estimated skeleton. It then regresses Y on X
and those selected variables using the same observations, reporting an ordinary 95 per cent
Student-t interval for the coefficient on X. The later score C is always excluded from this
regression by its information date. This rule is a diagnostic comparator; common estimated
neighbours are not asserted to be a valid general adjustment criterion. The reference regression
uses every stipulated baseline cause.
   Coverage means the proportion of the 200 intervals that contain the known intervention
effect. It is distinct from graph recovery. A graph can be wrong in an economically irrelevant
edge while the selected adjustment is correct, or it can lose exactly the variable needed to
remove confounding. The study does not isolate the pure penalty from reusing observations
after selection: its interval failures combine selection, omitted confounders and inappropriate
confidence in the resulting regression. Splitting the sample would not by itself make an invalid
adjustment set valid.
   The smaller-coefficient case illustrates how more observations can expose rather than remove
a comparison error. The selected estimator’s bias remains positive while its intervals narrow.
Coverage falls from 93 to 81.5 per cent. For the latter proportion, a descriptive 95 per cent
Wilson Monte Carlo interval is 75.5–86.3 per cent. It quantifies variation over the declared
independent simulation draws; it is not an interval for performance across unknown banking
environments.
   In the larger-coefficient case, the selected rule almost always omits important causes. At
n = 2000 the full search selects no baseline variable in 145 of 200 samples and selects only Z3
in another 54. No interval contains the zero effect. The ordinary all-baseline regression covers
zero in 191 of those same 200 samples. The failure is therefore consequential for the lending
contrast, not simply a disagreement over an incidental arrow.
  The screen and depth cap do not solve this sampling problem. At n = 2000 in the larger-
coefficient case, full search retains the spurious X–Y link in 193 samples; the screened and
capped searches retain it in all 200. The depth cap labels every one of these searches unfinished.

1.4     The information clock helps, but does not guarantee recov-
        ery
After inspecting those results, a separately specified follow-up changes one thing: it excludes C
from the discovery exercise as well as from the outcome regression. Coefficients, sample sizes,
thresholds, seeds and observations are unchanged. Removing this particular node does not
conceal a common cause: it is only a later descendant of X and Y . The reduced five-variable
covariances are verified against the corresponding graph. The cancellation case contains no
such node and is not rerun.
   For the smaller-coefficient case at n = 2000 and α = 0.05, full-search coverage rises from
81.5 to 97.0 per cent. The paired difference is 15.5 percentage points, with a Monte Carlo
standard error of 2.66 points. The reference set of all three causes is selected in 188 samples.
Exact skeleton recovery rises from nine out of 200 for the six-variable task to 173 out of 200
for the five-variable task. The graph tasks differ because the latter omits C; retained paired
records also compare the common five-variable edges.
   The larger-coefficient case improves much less. At n = 2000, interval coverage is still
only 3.5 per cent and the reduced skeleton is wrong in 194 samples. Even after removing C,
conditioning on other variables leaves some direct-edge correlations close to zero. Correct
timing is useful economic information; it does not guarantee a favorable statistical signal or
establish faithfulness.

1.5     What the proposal should require before valuing a graph
The study supports a conditional recommendation. Keep full subset search as a logical reference
and report unfinished searches honestly. Also retain the economically defended information
clock, assess the signals required by the discovery procedure, and justify the adjustment used
for the particular lending contrast. A graph can help formulate questions without supplying a
qualified effect estimate. A significance setting such as 0.05 should never be presented as a 95
per cent probability that the entire graph is correct.
   The numerical checks target the study itself. Exact rational covariance and independently
implemented graph/path tests agree on every declared population query in the faithful cases.
Precision-matrix and residual-regression partial correlations agree within 5 × 10−15 in 486
sample checks. Independent QR residualization reproduces 270 retained interval calculations
within 7 × 10−16 ; reversing variable order preserves the checked full-search skeletons. These
checks reduce implementation error in the examples. They cannot establish a universal
calibration guarantee.
   All 54 first-study method/threshold cells and all 36 timing-follow-up cells are retained,
including adverse results. The follow-up was specified after the first study and is described
as such. Neither experiment evaluates native R or Java software, arrow orientation, latent
confounding, a live language model or an actual bank. Those questions retain their own tests.
For the proposal, the scientific advance is narrower and useful: repairing the search does not
complete the causal comparison, and timing information can change its reliability without
making it universally reliable.
