15.9.4    What this comparison permits an implementation to claim

The follow-up’s Theorem 1 concerns fixed-dimensional, full-rank linear GMM with the stated
sampling and moment conditions. Its correction is not a general weak-instrument or many-
instrument remedy. The paper also distinguishes iterated GMM from continuous updating
and explains why the same finite-sample argument does not automatically extend to nonlinear
moment models (Hwang et al., 2020, Sections 1, 4–6). The score construction of Kleibergen
and Zhan (2021), discussed below, answers a different question: its robustness to identification
and moment misspecification should not be conflated with this variance correction.
    A detail in the selected preprint’s proof requires care. After equation (94), it infers that a
fifth derivative of an inverse-weight functional vanishes because the underlying second-moment
matrix is quadratic. Matrix inversion does not preserve polynomial degree. In the finite
construction above, put

asymptotic covariance conclusion. The local Taylor argument instead uses smooth inversion on

  For the panel comparison that follows, the historical point estimates, first-order sandwiches
and all adverse coverage results remain exactly as reported. This primary comparison supports
a distinct implementation candidate: preserve entity-level moment vectors, expose the weight
convention and target, include the required derivative and cross-covariance terms, and then
qualify the complete estimator on a declared design. No corrected native panel result has
been produced here. Replacing the reported standard errors without rerunning that design
would misstate the evidence; treating misspecification robustness as instrument validity would
misstate the scientific question.


15.9.5    A paired comparison and a banking decision rule

The synthetic short-panel experiment uses N = 800, eight transitions after an initial observation,
and yit = αi + ρyi,t−1 + .6xit + vit , with xit = .5xi,t−1 + .4vit + ηit . A 400-period burn-in
generates the regular initial law. The initial-condition challenger then adds a multiple of αi to
the initial balance; the serial-error challenger changes the innovation law. The four methods
use identical datasets. Coverage below is joint for both coefficients, using two-coordinate
