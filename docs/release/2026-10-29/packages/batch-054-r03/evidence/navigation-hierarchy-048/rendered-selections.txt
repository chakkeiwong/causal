36.11.1     What an effect multiset would mean for a bank

Let the observed vector contain an offered rate change X, an intermediate customer response
M , and a monetary outcome Y . The initial model is a linear Gaussian structural system with
independently sampled units, no hidden common causes and a distribution faithful to its causal
DAG. The sampling unit could be a customer in a stipulated independent-unit experiment;
repeated customer months cannot be relabeled as independent rows. The graph search
estimates conditional independence structure. Its significance level is a level for individual
tests, not a confidence level for the entire graph or for the effect of a future bank action (pcalg
contributors, 2026).
  For a compatible DAG G, the singleton total effect is the derivative
                                          ∂
                                   θG =     EG [Y | do(X = x)].
                                         ∂x
In the stated linear model it is constant in x. When Y is not a parent of X, it can be calculated
by regressing Y on X and the parents of X in G. If Y is a parent, its total effect from
X is zero. The global IDA construction considers compatible DAGs; the local construction
uses admissible local parent configurations. They need not attach the same multiplicity to
a numerical value. Neither construction turns a frequency in this multiset into a posterior
probability of a graph (pcalg contributors, 2026; pcalg contributors; CRAN source mirror,
2024b).
  The distinction is visible without fitting a package. Consider the exact covariance matrix,
ordered as (X, M, Y ),

The partial covariance of X and Y given M is 0.48 − 0.6(1.088)/1.36 = 0. The three DAGs
X → M → Y , X ← M → Y , and X ← M ← Y can each generate this same Gaussian law
with positive independent error variances. Exact rational reconstruction of all three systems
gives total effects 0.48, 0, 0. Global enumeration therefore has multiset [0, 0, 0.48]; the two local
parent configurations of X give [0, 0.48]. Averaging the first would produce 0.16, and averaging
the second would produce 0.24. Neither average is identified as the bank’s intervention effect.

pcalg (Causal proposal project, 2026b).

36.11.3     Failures must be caught before they are hidden

The selected source makes three failure boundaries especially consequential. First, with
solve.confl=false, relaxed PC can overwrite an earlier edge direction when another collider
or orientation rule conflicts with it. With solve.confl=true, it can preserve conflicting
directions using reciprocal entries of two. The proposed profile therefore uses conservative
PC, stable skeleton search, relaxed orientation and conflict preservation. Such a result may
still fail to be a valid CPDAG. The discovery result can retain this diagnostic, while the
effect calculation must refuse it. The conflict is not evidence of a latent common cause (pcalg
contributors; CRAN source mirror, 2024b).
   Second, a finite conditional-independence p value need not show that every preceding
numerical operation was valid. The inspected pcorOrder uses a pseudoinverse for conditioning
sets of size at least two, replaces a missing partial correlation by zero, and clips its result.
zStat likewise replaces a missing statistic by zero; gaussCItest then evaluates a two-sided
Gaussian tail probability. Disabling NAdelete acts later in the skeleton routine and cannot
recover a failure already replaced inside these functions (pcalg contributors; CRAN source
mirror, 2024b).

36.12      R discovery when borrower risk and selection are hidden
The preceding PC/IDA boundary assumes a causally sufficient system. That assumption is
consequential in a servicing application: private borrower distress can affect both the bank’s
contact decision and subsequent recovery, while inclusion in an observed account panel can
depend on both. A larger regression or a more elaborate graph display does not by itself
remove either problem. The R comparison must therefore ask what information survives
latent common causes and selection, which object an algorithm returns, and which additional
argument would turn that object into a decision.
   The selected implementation is again pcalg 2.7-12 at the preserved September 2024 CRAN
mirror revision. FCI, RFCI, FCI+, conservative and majority variants, and the Joint Causal
Inference options are related operations in this package. They are not six independent software
implementations. The bounded R search also returns methodological papers, a Python software
paper and other-language leads; their presence in a search result does not establish an R
implementation. The following claims concern inspected pinned manuals, implementation
paths and authentic method papers. They do not constitute a complete census of every
R extension or an executed package comparison (pcalg contributors; CRAN source mirror,
2024a).


What a latent-variable graph says. Let X denote the recorded variables, L unrecorded
variables, and S the variables on which inclusion is conditioned. For an underlying acyclic
causal graph, a maximal ancestral graph can represent the conditional-independence structure
left after marginalizing L and conditioning on S. A partial ancestral graph, or PAG, records
features shared by compatible ancestral graphs. The marks carry information: an arrowhead
at a vertex rules out its ancestry of the opposite vertex and the selection variables under the
stated model; a tail carries an ancestral restriction; a circle leaves an endpoint unresolved. In
particular, selection can make the interpretation of a tail refer to ancestry of selection, rather
than a uniquely established direct causal effect on the other recorded variable. An estimated
PAG is neither a single fully specified structural model nor a numerical intervention estimate
(pcalg contributors; CRAN source mirror, 2024a; Colombo et al., 2012).
   The distinction is stronger for RFCI. In the definition of an FCI-PAG, an adjacency asserts
dependence for all eligible conditioning subsets of the recorded variables, conditional on
selection. In an RFCI-PAG the corresponding requirement concerns subsets of the adjacent
sets. This weaker statement can leave additional edges and can represent more than one Markov
equivalence class. The causal endpoint information is sound in the oracle theorem under
faithfulness, but it is incorrect to relabel the RFCI output as the complete FCI equivalence
class. A common serialization class named fciAlgo does not erase this difference. The
proposed artifact must retain the algorithm, version and object semantics as well as the matrix
(Colombo et al., 2012; pcalg contributors; CRAN source mirror, 2024a).

and mechanisms, not bank measurements. The three values demonstrate unresolved decision
consequences; they are not a sharp identified set over all possible causal models. A useful
product keeps the conflicting explanations and asks which restriction, assignment evidence or
intervention could distinguish them. Selecting the graph whose implied profit is most attractive
cannot supply that missing information (Causal banking proposal project, 2026l).


How the algorithms spend information and computation. FCI begins with an
adjacency search and then considers separating sets that can lie outside a node’s immediate
neighborhood. Its Possible-D-SEP stage can remove edges the initial search retained, after
which orientation rules refine endpoint marks. RFCI omits that global stage and instead
performs additional local tests for unshielded triples and discriminating paths. This is a trade
between computational work and informativeness, not a claim that RFCI finds exactly the
same graph more cheaply on every input. The oracle soundness result requires a faithful
underlying DAG and correct conditional-independence information. Neither a software return
code nor a large nominal sample size establishes these premises (Colombo et al., 2012; pcalg
contributors; CRAN source mirror, 2024a).
   The high-dimensional consistency result is also specific. It assumes IID copies of the selected
observed law, multivariate Gaussianity, faithfulness, restrictions on graph-neighborhood growth,
and nonzero partial correlations bounded away from a shrinking detection boundary while
remaining bounded away from one. The FCI result uses a stronger restriction on the growth
of Possible-D-SEP sets. Its conclusion uses a sequence of tuning levels; it is not a finite-sample
graph-confidence guarantee at the user’s chosen alpha. Repeated monthly observations on the
same borrowers do not become IID merely because they are supplied to the same function
(Colombo et al., 2012).
   The per-test level should consequently travel as a test parameter, not a confidence attached
to the entire graph. For an explicitly hypothetical family of 100 independent true-null tests at
level .05, the probability of at least one rejection is 1 − .95100 . That elementary calculation
does not describe FCI’s adaptive testing error, but it exposes the unsupported step from a
per-test parameter to a simultaneous claim. Graph uncertainty, orientation ambiguity, model
restrictions and sampling uncertainty need separate treatment before an effect set can receive
a coverage label (Causal banking proposal project, 2026l; Colombo et al., 2012).
