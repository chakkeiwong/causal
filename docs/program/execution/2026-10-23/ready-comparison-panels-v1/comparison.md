# Panel and difference-in-differences software must preserve the rollout target

A branch policy adopted at different dates produces several reasonable business questions: the effect for one adoption cohort at one date, the average across treated branch-months, and the effect for a typical branch. Those questions need different weights and sometimes different comparison populations. The inspected Stata DID commands, R fixest implementation and HonestDiD sensitivity paths provide complementary evidence. Their useful division is target construction, estimation and sensitivity, with an explicit information and dependence contract between them. This comparison synthesizes the corresponding developments already in the integrated monograph. It supplies no new native qualification or historical market-completeness claim. \citep{exec18_gp_stata_did,exec18_gp_stata_hdid,exec18_gp_stata_xthdid,exec20_fixest_source,exec22_honestdid_engine}

Let G_i be branch i's first adoption date and Y_it(g) its outcome if adoption starts at g. Define theta(g,t)=E[Y_it(g)-Y_it(infinity)|G_i=g] and a requested aggregate Theta=sum omega_gt theta(g,t), with nonnegative weights summing to one. Identification needs consistency, an explicit anticipation window, admissible untreated comparisons, overlap and the appropriate conditional or unconditional untreated parallel-trend restriction. A not-yet-treated branch ceases to be an untreated comparison after adoption; excluding anticipated periods can further shrink support. An absorbing-treatment estimand cannot silently admit a reversal (0,1,0). Repeated cross-sectional sampling also differs from repeated observations of the same branches. The inspected Stata interfaces distinguish didregress/hdidregress from the panel xtdidregress/xthdidregress pair and expose different comparison and base-period choices. \citep{exec18_gp_stata_did,exec18_gp_stata_hdid,exec18_gp_stata_xthdid}

The exact five-branch example makes aggregation economically consequential. At dates 0,1,2, branches E1,E2 adopt at 1, L1,L2 at 2 and N never adopts. Number them i=0,...,4 and stipulate untreated outcomes 10i+2t in thousands of USD. Treatment effects are 1 and 100 in the early cohort's two treated dates and 1 for the late cohort. All six treated branch-periods have positive effects and their average is 34. Never-treated comparisons recover these effects under the stipulated law. The scalar two-way fixed-effect projection instead assigns cohort-time weights 5/8,-1/8,1/2 and gives -91/8=-11.375. Rational within residualization, full normal equations and inverse-Gram weights agree. With an assumed USD 10,000 cost per treated period, the correct retrospective average net effect is USD 24,000; substituting that projection gives the invalid value -USD 21,375. This is a target mismatch in a fully specified finite panel, not a vendor runtime result or a universal objection to fixed effects. \citep{exec18_gp_panel_reference}

The heterogeneous Stata postestimation manual permits cohort, calendar, exposure-time and joint aggregation, with timecohort and cohort weighting rules. These depend on eligible cells and retained observations. Selected xthdidregress robust inference clusters at the treatment group. Its postestimation multiplier simultaneous intervals are unavailable after TWFE, and aggregation is unavailable after the selected bootstrap/jackknife estimation routes. A requested uncertainty object must therefore state whether it covers one cell, one fixed aggregate or a simultaneous family, and whether the selected estimator and options support it. Returning a coefficient and a generic robust label loses this information. The five-branch example establishes arithmetic only; it cannot validate cluster asymptotics. \citep{exec18_gp_stata_post,exec18_gp_stata_xthdid}

Fixest offers a different boundary: a pinned linear fixed-effect engine whose sample, formula, covariance and aggregation behavior must travel together. The reviewed 0.14.2 revision recursively removes relevant singleton/perfect-fit observations, excludes zero weights and records collinearity. Startup covariance defaults are IID and session options can alter defaults; fixed-effect syntax is not a clustering instruction. Explicit small-sample corrections and reference degrees of freedom matter. In the independent three-branch, two-period example, the coefficient is 4, cluster CR0 variance is 8/9, and two specified parameter-count corrections yield variances 4/3 and 10/3 with reference df=2. These different conventions are reproducible; three clusters supply no general calibration claim. Absorbed treatment must return unavailability, rather than an invented zero effect. \citep{exec20_fixest_source,exec20_fixest_reference}

Fixest's inspected sunab construction can classify a cohort absent from the observed calendar as never treated, making reconstruction of the full treatment history necessary. Aggregation must preserve the full covariance: coefficients (2,8), weights (3/4,1/4) and covariance [[4,1],[1,9]] give effect 7/2 and variance 51/16. Dropping cross-covariance gives 45/16, while averaging standard errors is a different, unjustified calculation. Equal effect weights give 5; at cost 4 the indicated net value changes from -1/2 to 1. Covariance repair also remains visible: the inspected eigenvalue-fixing option defaults on and a warning threshold can hide a small repair. The proposal favors a qualified R worker for an explicitly declared linear profile, while pyfixest and other model families remain distinct assessments. \citep{exec20_fixest_source,exec20_fixest_reference}

Parallel trends is an identifying assumption, not an output of a pretrend test. Exact preperiod equality permits both a postperiod effect of 2 plus a differential untreated shock of 3 and an effect of 5 without that shock. HonestDiD supplies sensitivity machinery for stated restrictions, not evidence that those restrictions are true. In the integrated one-preperiod illustration, x is the preperiod differential trend, y the observed post contrast and the reference-period trend is normalized to zero. Relative magnitude |delta1|<=m|x| gives [y-m|x|,y+m|x|]; smoothness |delta1+x|<=M gives [y+x-M,y+x+M]. For x=-2,y=4, m=0 yields 4 and M=0 yields 2. With m=M=1 the intervals are [2,6] and [1,3]. The parameters have different meanings and units and are not interchangeable tuning knobs. \citep{exec18_gp_panel_reference,exec22_honestdid_reference}

Sampling uncertainty must surround the sensitivity calculation. For estimated (x,y)=(0,4) with a stipulated known Gaussian covariance [[1,1.5],[1.5,4]], projecting a joint 95% ellipse over the relative-magnitude model m=1 yields approximately [-2.9233,10.9233]; dropping the off-diagonal terms changes it to [-1.4733,9.4733]. Treating the estimated zero pretrend as a known zero produces a much narrower ordinary post interval. The integrated exact reference derives this conservative ellipse projection independently; it does not claim to implement HonestDiD's optimized methods. At cost 1 its net interval spans [-3.9233,9.9233], so the example does not support a confident positive investment decision. \citep{exec22_honestdid_reference}

The inspected HonestDiD 0.2.8 paths make numerical and interface choices material. Smoothness defaults to FLCI with the first postperiod contrast, relative magnitude to C-LF with ten sensitivity values from zero to two and 1,000 inversion nodes. The selected did bridge uses 100 grid points and forwards that option differently across the two restrictions. A finite grid can miss a narrow acceptance set, its endpoint hull can fill rejected gaps, and an accepted boundary leaves extent unresolved. Selected failed linear-program paths return point endpoints, so solver status and numerical validity must be checked separately from a finite reported interval. Neither an empty grid nor a solver failure establishes an empty scientific identified set. The proposal currently retains this engine as a reference pending qualification. \citep{exec22_honestdid_engine,exec22_honestdid_bridges,exec22_honestdid_reference}

The resulting interface should preserve unit and time keys, complete adoption histories, observation masks, outcome and cost units, comparison cohorts, anticipation and base periods, target weights, supported cells, covariance and degrees of freedom. It should return raw and repaired numerical objects, explicit excluded populations, solver/convergence status and typed uncertainty. A library, CLI or MCP call must retain the same meanings; an agent translating a rollout question cannot infer a missing treatment history or justify parallel trends from text alone. The saved dependent-panel dispatcher probe returns unsupported for its selected request, which is a concrete interface gap and not evidence that all panel research is absent. \citep{exec18_gp_dispatch}

The appropriate prototype comparison first matches finite targets, masks, rank and covariance to the exact references, then evaluates generated multi-cluster panels under prespecified dependence, anticipation, heterogeneous effects, attrition and trend violations. Supported cases need coverage, useful width, availability and full failure denominators; out-of-domain cases need explicit refusal or sensitivity instead of an unqualified result. Ordinary cohort-time DID, a scalar projection used for its actual target, event-time estimation and bounded trend sensitivity are substantive alternatives. Their relative suitability depends on the question and restrictions, rather than one universal estimator ranking.
