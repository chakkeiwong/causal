\section{Python discovery across clocks, contexts and interventions}
\label{exec14:python-temporal}

A bank comparing discovery libraries must first say what changed in its data.
A funding-rate movement can be a decision, a response to a common calendar
shock, a change in the mechanism setting that rate, or simply the next
observation of a persistent process. These possibilities require different
inputs and support different conclusions. A library accepting several data
matrices does not, by that fact, identify an intervention; a time-series
library does not recover the physical clock from a row number. This comparison
therefore separates temporal search, context and regime search, and discovery
from labelled or partially known interventions. The proposed service should
return the relevant graph object and assumptions before attempting an effect
query. The reuse decisions below are proposed adapter and research choices,
with no enabled integration or upstream execution established by this review.
\citep{exec14_pt_contract}

\subsection{The structural object and its decision horizon}

Write a linear structural time-series model using column vectors as
\[
 x_t=B_0x_t+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}B_\ell x_{t-\ell}+\varepsilon_t.
\]
Here $B_\ell[j,i]$ is the coefficient from variable $i$ at lag $\ell$ to
variable $j$ now. The within-period matrix $B_0$ describes an acyclic ordering,
while the lag matrices allow feedback through time. Premultiplying by
$(I-B_0)^{-1}$ gives reduced-form coefficients
$\Phi_\ell=(I-B_0)^{-1}B_\ell$ and reduced-form innovations
$(I-B_0)^{-1}\varepsilon_t$. A forecasting coefficient, a structural
coefficient and an impulse response are thus different quantities even in
this small model. Identifying the contemporaneous structure needs additional
restrictions. The DYNOTEARS paper studies independent realisations of a
stationary series with independent innovations, and discusses non-Gaussian
innovations or standard Gaussian innovations as identifiable cases. Its
within-slice acyclicity condition does not itself establish stability of the
dynamic process. \citep{exec14_pt_paper_dynotears}

For an exact banking illustration let the two coordinates be standardized
funding pressure and liquidity contribution, and stipulate
\[
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Phi_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The direct lagged funding coefficient in the second equation is $1/4$;
the reduced-form counterpart is $1/2$. A unit innovation to the funding
equation, with zero initial deviations, produces immediate deviations
$(1,1/2)^\mathsf{T}$. The next two liquidity responses are $3/4$ and $5/8$.
Since both eigenvalues of $\Phi_1$ are $1/2$, the infinite-horizon sum is
\[
 (I-\Phi_1)^{-1}(1,1/2)^\mathsf{T}=(2,3)^\mathsf{T}.
\]
If each liquidity unit is valued at a stipulated USD 100 and the one-time
action costs USD 60, the immediate net is minus USD 10 whereas the undiscounted
cumulative net is USD 240. These are conditional arithmetic examples. They
do not estimate a bank return, and changing the action to a sustained rate
policy, adding discounting, or allowing feedback into the decision rule
changes the target. A reusable interface must therefore distinguish an
innovation, an equation replacement and a sustained policy, and retain the
horizon and units. \citep{exec14_pt_exact}

Sampling frequency is equally substantive. Consider two stationary Gaussian
processes $X_t=\rho X_{t-1}+\epsilon_t$, with independent innovations of
variance $3/4$ and $\rho$ equal to either $1/2$ or $-1/2$. Both have marginal
variance one. If the bank retains only every second day, both satisfy an
observed autoregression with coefficient $1/4$ and innovation variance
$15/16$. Their complete stationary observed laws agree, but their one-day
impulse responses have opposite signs. Recovering the original day-level
response is therefore impossible from this coarsened law alone within this
two-world comparison. A daily timestamp declaration cannot repair information
that was discarded; the response must retain the observation frequency and
the unresolved finer-clock target. \citep{exec14_pt_exact}

\subsection{Tigramite: three distinct questions behind one ecosystem}

Tigramite offers a useful primary family of proposed adapters because its
documented graph classes distinguish PCMCI, PCMCI+, LPCMCI, RPCMCI and
J-PCMCI+. PCMCI+ combines lagged and contemporaneous search under causal
stationarity and sufficiency, while LPCMCI retains the partial ancestral
graph needed when latent confounding is admitted. Neither endpoint
ambiguity nor the restrictions of the chosen conditional-independence test
should disappear in a common wrapper. Earlier selected installed operations
are separate evidence from this static extension review.
\citep{runge2020,gerhardus2020,runtime_tigramite_scope,exec14_pt_tigramite}

J-PCMCI+ addresses multiple datasets with system variables and temporal or
spatial contexts. Its primary paper requires a joint acyclic structural
model, context exogeneity, restrictions excluding context--system
confounding, complete observation of system nodes, and a particular
context-determinism construction. A temporal context is shared across
datasets; a spatial context is constant within a dataset. This offers a
route for common funding calendars and bank-specific characteristics, but
does not license arbitrary unobserved common causes. Coding a bank identifier
or calendar as a context is a scientific assertion about the model.
\citep{exec14_pt_paper_jpcmci}

The inspected implementation makes those assertions operational: it accepts
five node classes, orients context-to-system links as exogenous, and removes
specified reverse and dummy links. A generic graph adapter must preserve the
effective constraints after these transformations, the pooled datasets, time
offsets, masks and conditioning choices. Before enabling this candidate,
generated comparisons should separately vary shared-calendar dependence,
bank-specific fixed contexts and hidden system-to-system confounding. The
last case deliberately violates this J-PCMCI+ model and should not be used
to award it a latent-variable guarantee. The raw conditional-test results
and final endpoint graph should remain distinguishable.
\citep{exec14_pt_tigramite,exec14_pt_contract}

RPCMCI answers a different question: it alternates regime learning and
causal search under a finite persistent regime structure, with stationarity
within regimes. Its paper initially develops lagged structural relations
with independent stationary innovations. A recovered regime can describe
different behaviour without representing a randomized or externally assigned
intervention. In the inspected constructor, only one multivariate series with scalar rather than vector-valued nodes
is accepted and a supplied mask is replaced by an all-false mask;
missing values have a separate flag. The adapter must either reject an
unsupported user mask or require an explicit, reviewed transformation before
calling this path. It must retain the regime count, transition limit,
annealing settings and convergence information. Regime membership should
not be exported as an observed experiment label.
\citep{exec14_pt_paper_rpcmci,exec14_pt_tigramite}

Tigramite's preserved root terms specify GPLv3 and its README declares
version 3 or later. That is useful source evidence for a reuse decision,
but a process boundary or command-line interface is not, by itself, a
completed distribution-rights analysis. The proposal should compare a
versioned adapter with the cost of independently maintaining a port and
qualifying its numerical equivalence. The immediate recommendation is
separate, explicitly scoped adapters for the different temporal methods.
\citep{exec14_pt_tigramite,exec14_pt_contract}

\subsection{CausalAI, VARLiNGAM and DYNOTEARS as contrasting baselines}

Salesforce CausalAI's inspected PC and Granger paths enumerate candidate
parents at strictly negative lags. Its VARLiNGAM wrapper separately exposes
lag-zero coefficients, preserving the dependency's target-row convention.
The time-series data helper forms lagged observations inside each supplied
array and then concatenates those slices, which avoids simply linking the
last row of one array to the first of another. However, the selected
interface has no physical timestamp argument: a missing business day and
a regular one-step transition can look identical after the caller has
formed the arrays. Its optional complete-case deletion also does not
establish an ignorable missingness law. \citep{exec14_pt_causalai}

The uncertainty interface deserves particular caution. The inspected
Granger routine fits LassoCV coefficients and then evaluates a t-tail
expression using a residual sum of squares and inverse design cross-product.
The source does not establish the additional justification needed after
selection and under dependent observations. When the cross-product is
rank-deficient, that routine substitutes a vector of p-values equal to one;
the VARLiNGAM wrapper has a similar branch. An operational refusal is then
numerically indistinguishable from a valid large p-value if the wrapper
discards the diagnostic. The proposal should preserve a separate
\emph{uncertainty unavailable} status, effective sample/design rank and
the selected model. A successful coefficient fit is insufficient evidence
for a calibrated interval or a causal graph. These are static-source
findings, with no claimed runtime failure frequency.
\citep{exec14_pt_causalai}

The inherited direct LiNGAM inspection provides a clearer starting point
for a declared linear non-Gaussian structural model: fit the lag process,
discover contemporaneous structure in its residuals, and preserve
$B[\text{target},\text{predictor}]$. Its residual bootstrap reconstructs
a sequence, unlike the generic row bootstrap, but the inspected bootstrap
loop fixes the lag-selection criterion to None. Consequently its existence
does not establish uncertainty including lag-order selection. Before an
adapter is enabled, the proposed generated study must include Gaussian
innovations, hidden common causes and persistent residuals as explicit
scope violations, alongside the eligible non-Gaussian innovation cases.
\citep{v131_sw_lingam,exec13_pydisc_lingam,exec14_pt_contract}

DYNOTEARS supplies a simultaneous optimization comparator. The inspected
CausalNex path minimizes a penalized residual loss based on
$X(I-W)-X_{\rm lags}A$ and constrains the within-slice matrix $W$ to be
acyclic. Its dataframe transformer checks numeric columns and sorted integer
indices, splits gaps larger than one, and constructs lags separately for
each resulting realisation. This is more specific than assuming that every
row is adjacent. It still admits a nondecreasing sequence with duplicate
indices: the authored sequence $(1,2,2,3,6,7)$ creates two segments and four
retained one-lag pairs, only three of which have a strictly positive unit
step. The proposed adapter must reject duplicates, define the calendar and
record every exclusion. It should also preserve raw weights, threshold
choices and optimization diagnostics.
\citep{exec14_pt_causalnex,exec14_pt_exact,exec14_pt_paper_dynotears}

The inherited maintenance review records CausalNex's discontinuation notice.
That supports retaining DYNOTEARS as a methodological and conversion
reference, while deferring default dependency adoption. A port would require
an explicit maintenance owner and equivalence tests; it is not a free
substitute for a maintained upstream package. CausalAI's inspected BSD terms
and LiNGAM's inspected MIT terms likewise do not certify their entire
resolved dependency environments. \citep{exec14_causalnex_primary,
exec14_pt_causalai,exec13_pydisc_lingam,exec14_pt_contract}

\subsection{Intervention data need a mechanism contract}

An intervention label must include the target set, mechanism type and
assignment context. To see why, let $H,U,W$ be independent standard
normal innovations, define $A=H+U$, $Y=2A+H+W$, and observe $(H,A,Y)$.
A perfect stochastic
intervention replaces the action equation with $A=\delta+Z$, where
$Z\sim N(0,2)$ is independent of the other innovations. A soft intervention
instead sets $A=H+U+\delta$. In both cases $E(A)=\delta$,
$\operatorname{var}(A)=2$, and $E(Y)=2\delta$. Nevertheless,
$\operatorname{cov}(H,A)$ is zero under replacement and one under the soft
change; $\operatorname{cov}(A,Y)$ is respectively four and five. The action
margin and its label alone cannot tell the library which graph truncation
is justified. If $H$ is hidden, the same example additionally violates a
causally sufficient observed-variable model. \citep{exec14_pt_exact}

Python GIES is a useful known-perfect-intervention baseline. The original
paper defines stochastic interventions by replacing targeted mechanisms
and requires a conservative target family: every variable remains
unintervened in at least one environment. Observational data satisfy this
particular requirement, although they do not supply all other assumptions.
The paper's interventional equivalence theory concerns graph classes under
the stated intervention family. Independent observations from several
environments are not automatically the same sampling model as repeatedly
observed banks on a shared calendar. \citep{exec14_pt_paper_gies}

The inspected Python Gaussian score centres data within each environment,
forms covariances from observations in which the target variable was not
intervened on, and checks conservative target coverage. Its output is an
estimated I-essential graph: reciprocal nonzero matrix entries denote an
undirected edge, not a two-cycle. A proposed adapter should validate unique
target indices, dataset/target alignment, environment sizes and the
effective score before preserving these endpoints. It should refuse to
present the score as an effect or confidence interval. This candidate is
attractive for a transparent generated perfect-intervention comparison;
soft targets and dependent panels need their own justified treatment.
\citep{exec14_pt_gies,exec14_pt_contract}

Unknown-target IGSP offers another route when intervention targets are
partially known. Its primary paper requires observational data and an
I-faithfulness condition relating invariance to separation; its theorem
identifies an interventional equivalence class and targets under those
conditions. The algorithm needs both a conditional-independence tester and
a conditional-invariance tester. An unknown target is therefore not
recovered merely by dropping a target column from a known-target method.
\citep{exec14_pt_paper_utigsp}

The pinned CausalDAG facade reexports external graph and learning packages,
while its older tutorial points to an implementation path absent from the
selected tree. The recovered graphical-model-learning implementation
defaults to depth four, five starts, and tuple scoring. Its default score
orders edge count and variant count lexicographically, whereas the
inspected paper defines their sum. The distinction can matter: candidate
counts $(3,5)$ and $(4,0)$ are ordered in opposite ways by these rules.
This arithmetic example does not claim those two candidates are reachable
in a particular search. It identifies a precise theory-to-code question
that must be resolved before transferring the theorem to a wrapper's
default. A public prototype should pin all three facade dependencies,
retain score mode and search restrictions, and compare oracle-level and
finite-test behaviour separately. \citep{exec14_pt_causaldag,exec14_pt_gml,
exec14_pt_paper_utigsp,exec14_pt_exact}

\subsection{Differentiable discovery: optimization and evidence remain separate}

DCDI's primary score theorem concerns a global population optimum with a
sufficient density class, positivity, finite entropy and I-faithfulness.
Its finite nonconvex optimization is not guaranteed to attain that optimum.
The inspected constructor implements perfect interventions with known or
unknown targets and imperfect interventions with known targets; the
combination of imperfect interventions and unknown targets explicitly raises an unimplemented error.
Known perfect targets are removed from the mechanism likelihood using a
retention mask, while the other implemented branches use regime-specific
parameters. These distinctions should be visible service variants, with
the original masks, regime identities and optimizer diagnostics returned
in the evidence bundle. They should not be compressed into one boolean
intervention option. \citep{exec14_pt_paper_dcdi,exec14_pt_dcdi}

ENCO provides a contrasting parameterization of edge existence and
orientation. Its documented array format indexes intervention samples by
the single targeted variable. The inspected gradient path suppresses
incoming-edge updates at that variable and treats orientation updates
separately. The primary theorem and supporting argument require informative
interventions, sufficiently accurate conditional models, suitable
regularization and the stated data conditions. The paper itself distinguishes
the extension to fewer intervention targets, for which the same convergence
guarantee is unavailable. A comparison should report actual target coverage
and cycle handling instead of treating the method's title or learned edge
probabilities as a finite-sample confidence guarantee.
\citep{exec14_pt_enco,exec14_pt_paper_enco}

SDCD is worth considering for numerical stability and its two-stage
structure. The primary paper's stability result concerns the acyclicity
constraint; its first-stage screening argument uses exact population
optimization and explicit model, faithfulness and positivity conditions.
Neither result establishes that every finite training run retains every
true parent. The inspected output helper sorts weighted edges and omits
those that would form cycles. A caller receiving only that DAG loses the
distinction between a learned low-weight edge and an edge removed to impose
acyclicity. The proposal should return raw adjacency, the retained graph,
the threshold and a record of the removed edges.
\citep{exec14_pt_paper_sdcd,exec14_pt_sdcd}

There is also a concrete input risk. SDCD's inspected dataset helper maps
unmatched perturbation labels to a sentinel, drops those entries, and
turns an empty target list into an all-ones retention mask. In the authored
two-variable example, the label \texttt{rate} gives mask $(0,1)$ but the
misspelling \texttt{raet} gives $(1,1)$. This traces the selected transformation;
it is not an observed frequency in user data. The proposed wrapper must
distinguish an explicitly observational record from an unresolved target,
validate all labels before conversion, and refuse unknown or duplicate
targets with the original input preserved. Successful optimization after
an unintended input reinterpretation would not resolve the mistake.
\citep{exec14_pt_sdcd,exec14_pt_exact}

\subsection{A bounded reuse portfolio and the next evidence}

The immediate portfolio has distinct roles. Tigramite supports proposed
endpoint-preserving temporal/context adapters; LiNGAM and DYNOTEARS supply
declared linear structural comparators; CausalAI provides an additional
interface and benchmark reference with explicit uncertainty restrictions.
Python GIES supplies a transparent known-perfect intervention baseline.
UT-IGSP adds an invariance-based unknown-target comparison once its scoring
and dependencies are reconciled. DCDI, ENCO and SDCD provide complementary
neural research comparators, each restricted to its reviewed data and
intervention contract. Direct adoption is deferred; no port is selected
merely because source terms permit modification. A port must justify its
maintenance and numerical-equivalence cost against a versioned adapter.
\citep{exec14_pt_contract}

The first prototype can use generated structural laws with known graphs,
observed intervention assignments and saved observation clocks. Separate
strata should vary lag order, contemporaneous links, hidden common causes,
shared calendars, regime persistence, regular versus coarsened sampling,
target knowledge and perfect versus soft intervention. Eligible and
deliberately ineligible cases must be reported separately. Software
acceptance cases should include duplicate clocks, unknown target spelling,
rank failure, an unsupported RPCMCI mask, an unsupported DCDI combination,
raw-to-DAG conversion and the UT-IGSP score switch. These cases test whether
the adapter preserves or refuses meaning; they cannot by themselves establish
statistical recovery. \citep{exec14_pt_contract,exec14_pt_exact}

A matched scientific comparison must then keep each method's assumptions,
search budgets and target objects explicit. A selected DAG cannot be ranked
against a PAG as though every unresolved endpoint were an error. Report
coverage of eligible requests, refusals, graph and intervention-target errors,
downstream identified effects or ambiguity, runtime and full preparation
costs. For dependent series, use a design justified for that process rather
than an independent-row bootstrap by default. Public datasets can test
ingestion and observable diagnostics; unless they supply independently
credible intervention truth, they cannot award causal correctness by
agreement between algorithms. \citep{exec14_pt_contract}

This is a developed comparison, not a complete census. The bounded search
retains unresolved families including amortized discovery, subsampled
proxy methods, periodic/nonstationary discovery, Bayesian unknown-intervention
methods and event-process methods. CUTS still needs primary identity and
embedded implementation resolution. The CD-NOD lead has a more developed
disposition in Section~\ref{exec17:other-temporal}: its primary method and
selected MATLAB paths are inspected there, with the lagged implementation
boundary and reuse rights still explicit. That resolves this source lead
without completing the wider census. For the remaining families, acquisition
must establish the original method and implementation path, classify their
clock and intervention assumptions, and compare their contribution against
this portfolio. An unresolved lead is an evidence gap, not evidence that its
capability is absent. Each comparison can be developed independently.
\citep{exec14_pt_contract}
