## Language-model measurement and causal evidence

Two genuine author-version originals strengthen the LLM proposal. The computational-social-science study evaluates 13 historical models on 25 English tasks with explicit sampling, prompts, metrics and human judgments. It reports useful assistance alongside task-dependent and correlated errors, unresolved leakage, and a boundary between predictive text characterization and causal explanation. The knowledge-mechanism survey separates observation of model components from computational interventions and discusses retrieval/memory conflicts and agent conformity. Its hypotheses and domain/scale limits remain explicit. Neither establishes current banking capability. \citep{exec24_css_original,exec24_knowledge_original}

Authored examples connect annotation quality to the decision. Equal 90 percent label accuracy can create an eight-point prevalence gap when the true gap is zero, giving an 80 USD distortion under stipulated loss. Five correlated prompt errors give variance 0.84 rather than 0.20. Identical observations support causal effects one and zero; an internal answer-restoration score of 6/7 does not distinguish them. These are exact design witnesses, not measured model defects. \citep{exec24_css_knowledge_reference}

A public prototype should preserve constructs, clocks, source conflicts, graph alternatives, identification, refusal and complete costs. With 100 requests, 90 answers and 81 correct, answered accuracy is 90 percent but correct per request is 81 percent. Stipulated 240 USD cost gives 2.40 per request, 8/3 per answer and 80/27 per correct artifact. Prototype merit should justify later costly bank validation; absent assignment or institutional truth remains an information requirement. \citep{exec24_css_knowledge_reference}
