           addition, statistical signiﬁcance tests can be performed using 100(1 − α) % nonparametric bootstrapping
           conﬁdence intervals (Davison & Hinkley, 1997).
               A key point to recognize is that Δ1 relies solely on the fourth-order joint cumulants and presupposes
           nonzero marginal fourth-order cumulants for both u and ex . In contrast, both Δ2 and Δ3 incorporate
           third- and fourth-order joint cumulants, requiring the existence of third-order cumulants (i.e., skew-
           ness) for u and ex . In this sense, Δ1 is regarded as a more general test that relies on fewer assumptions.
           However, by incorporating third-order information when available, using Δ2 and Δ3 oﬀers incremental
           power to detect the presence of η. Furthermore, even when the third-order cumulants are zero, both Δ2
           and Δ3 are expected to be zero (as shown in Equations B2 and C2), which does not necessarily lead to
           incorrect conclusions, particularly within the framework of the multiple testing algorithm introduced
           in the following section.


           2.4. Operationalizing a multiple testing algorithm
           We proposed three test statistics (Δ1, Δ2, and Δ3 ) for evaluating the presence of a nonzero direct causal
           eﬀect η. In ﬁnite samples, the statistical power to detect a true eﬀect (η ≠ 0) is expected to be inﬂuenced by
           two primary factors: the sample size (n) and the absolute magnitudes of the test statistics (Δ1, Δ2, and Δ3 ).
           As shown in the previous section, the magnitudes of these test statistics can be expected to be inﬂuenced
           by the complex interplay of several parameters, including Cum4 (u), Cum4 (ex ) , Cum3 (u), Cum3 (ex ),
           β1 , and β2 . Therefore, each test statistic is expected to exhibit greater sensitivity to detect η for a given
           combination of these parameters. In practice, the values of the above parameters are unknown, given
           that u is unobserved. To achieve higher statistical power, we recommend using all three test statistics in
           conjunction within a multiple testing algorithm, as described below.

                A multiple testing algorithm
                1. Set the signiﬁcance level α and apply the Bonferroni correction (Bonferroni, 1936) for multiple
                   testing using the adjusted α′ = α/3.
                2. Standardize the observed variables x and y in subsequent analyses.
                3. Compute Δ1 , and obtain the 100(1 − α′ ) % percentile bootstrapping conﬁdence interval, [ΔL1 , ΔU1 ].
                4. Compute Δ2 , and obtain the 100(1 − α′ ) % percentile bootstrapping conﬁdence interval, [ΔL2 , ΔU2 ].
                5. Compute Δ3 , and obtain the 100(1 − α′ ) % percentile bootstrapping conﬁdence interval, [ΔL3 , ΔU3 ].
                6. If 0 ∉ [ΔL1 , ΔU1 ]∨0 ∉ [ΔL2 , ΔU2 ]∨0 ∉ [ΔL3 , ΔU3 ], then select x → y. Otherwise, fail to detect the presence
                   of a direct causal path.
