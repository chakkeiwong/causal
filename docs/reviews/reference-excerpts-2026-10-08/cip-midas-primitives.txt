\chapter{External Estimation of MIDAS Weights}
\label{ch:midas_external}

\section{Purpose and scope}

MIDAS weights are often introduced as a small two-parameter device, but their
identification depends on what is observed and on which target the weights are
intended to approximate. This chapter separates three objects that are
frequently conflated: a forecasting weight, chosen to predict a low-frequency
variable; a filtering weight, chosen to approximate the conditional estimate
of an end-of-period latent state from high-frequency observations; and a
structural measurement weight, which is part of a joint latent state-space
likelihood.

The first two objects can be estimated externally because the daily panel and
an identifying target are available before the monthly latent model is fitted.
The third object is mathematically legitimate, but it is not a safe first
estimation target for the Daily Asset MIDAS model considered here. The reason
is not that a joint likelihood cannot be evaluated. The reason is that the same
parameters change the transition law, the observation loading, and the induced
measurement covariance, so the monthly aggregate alone does not provide a
clean source of information for separating them.

The promoted specification in this chapter is therefore conditional on an
externally estimated, shared weight profile. The exact correlated MIDAS
measurement remains a reference model and a later robustness target.

For the current project the promoted external estimator is the canonical
shared-Almon L1 procedure documented in Section~\ref{sec:midas_l1_default}.
``Promoted'' here means the default preprocessing policy for constructing the
monthly MIDAS observable. It does not mean that the first-stage coefficients
are known without error, that they are structurally identified by the monthly
likelihood, or that an empirical HMC run is automatically authorized.

\section{Basic MIDAS construction}

Let $t$ index low-frequency periods and $d=1,\ldots,D_t$ index admissible
business days. Let $f_{t,d}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ be the daily financial vector,
with its units and any factor sign normalization fixed before the MIDAS fit.
Assume $D_t\geq2$; a singleton period is assigned weight one separately.
Because calendar periods contain different numbers of business days, define
$u_{t,d}=2(d-1)/(D_t-1)-1$. The project's normalized exponential-Almon
profile is
\begin{equation}
 w_{t,d}(\vartheta)=\frac{\exp(\theta_1u_{t,d}+\theta_2u_{t,d}^2)}
 {\sum_{j=1}^{D_t}\exp(\theta_1u_{t,j}+\theta_2u_{t,j}^2)},
 \qquad \vartheta=(\theta_1,\theta_2)'\in\Theta,
 \label{eq:external_almon}
\end{equation}
and the monthly aggregate is
\begin{equation}
 z_t(\vartheta)=\sum_{d=1}^{D_t}w_{t,d}(\vartheta)f_{t,d}.
 \label{eq:external_aggregate}
\end{equation}
This is the usual two-parameter exponential-Almon shape adapted to a common
within-period coordinate. Following AGK, the family itself does not impose a
sign restriction on $\theta_2$; any compact numerical search domain is an
estimation choice to be validated, not part of the economic definition. There
is no latent or observed state-dependent
coefficient in this first specification. The profile is shared across factors
only as a restriction to be tested against factor-, target-, and
horizon-specific profiles; it is not assumed to hold because there are only
two parameters.

In a conventional MIDAS regression, the aggregate is a regressor rather than
the dependent variable:
\begin{equation}
 y_t=\alpha+\beta'z_t(\vartheta)+\epsilon_t.
 \label{eq:external_conventional_midas}
\end{equation}
The intercept and slope $\beta$ are estimated separately from the normalized
weight shape. This distinction is essential. If $f_{t,D_t}$ is the target and
the aggregate on the right-hand side includes $f_{t,D_t}$ itself, the endpoint
point mass fits mechanically; that criterion does not test whether a MIDAS
distributed lag is useful.

The end-of-month state-space representation used by the reference model is
\begin{equation}
 z_t(\vartheta)=G(\vartheta)A_t+[I-G(\vartheta)]\mu_A
 +C_M(\vartheta)(M_{t-1}-\mu_M)+\zeta_t,
 \label{eq:external_reference_measurement}
\end{equation}
where $A_t$ is the end-of-month asset state. In the exact shared-shock
construction, $G$, $C_M$, $R_\zeta=\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_t)$, and
$S_A=\operatorname{Cov}(u_{A,t},\zeta_t')$ are all functions of the daily
transition, innovation covariance, proxy-error covariance, and $\vartheta$.

\section{Why unrestricted joint estimation is unsafe here}

The issue can be seen without appealing to a numerical sampler. Suppose that
$a_s\in\mathbb R^J$, $m_{t-1}\in\mathbb R^P$, $K_{AA}\in\mathbb R^{J\times J}$
is nonsingular, and $K_{AM}\in\mathbb R^{J\times P}$. The daily asset dynamics
are
\begin{equation}
 da_s=-K_{AA}a_s\,ds-K_{AM}m_{t-1}\,ds+\Sigma_A\,dW_s.
 \label{eq:external_sde}
\end{equation}
For a within-month time $\tau_d$, write
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_d=\exp(-K_{AA}\tau_d),\qquad
 B_d=-K_{AA}^{-1}(I-\Phi_d)K_{AM}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_w=\sum_dw_{t,d}\Phi_d,\quad B_w=\sum_dw_{t,d}B_d,\quad
 G=\Phi_w\Phi_m^{-1},\quad C_M=B_w-G B_m.
 \label{eq:external_projection}
\end{equation}
Thus $K_{AA}$ and $\vartheta$ both determine the loading $G$. The spillover
block $K_{AM}$ enters both the transition and $C_M$. The same daily shocks
determine $Q_A$, $R_\zeta$, and $S_A$. Proxy-error scales enter
$\operatorname{Var}(\eta_t^w)=(\sum_dw_{t,d}^2)R_e$.

This creates a near-equivalence in persistent systems. If
$\|K_{AA}\|\Delta_m$ is small, then
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_d=I-K_{AA}\tau_d+O(\|K_{AA}\|^2\tau_d^2),
\end{equation}
so, with $\bar\tau_w=\sum_dw_{t,d}\tau_d$,
\begin{equation}
 G=I+K_{AA}(\Delta_m-\bar\tau_w)+O(\|K_{AA}\|^2\Delta_m^2).
 \label{eq:external_persistence_ridge}
\end{equation}
Many profiles therefore produce almost the same loading when the factor is
persistent. The remaining profile dependence is routed through covariance
terms that themselves depend on $Q_A$ and $R_e$. Consequently, a change in
$\vartheta$ can be offset by changes in persistence or noise. This is a weak
identification result: it need not be an exact global invariance at every
parameter value, but the likelihood can be nearly flat in the corresponding
directions.

The same point applies to a fully free $K_{AM}$. A macro spillover changes the
monthly transition and the MIDAS loading through $C_M$, while sparse macro
releases provide little independent information about the latent macro path.
Estimating $K_{AM}$, $\vartheta$, $\Sigma_A$, and $R_e$ together asks one noisy
monthly proxy to separate several mechanisms that all change its conditional
mean and covariance.

A positive-definite posterior Hessian can be created by the prior even when
the likelihood Hessian is indefinite or nearly singular. Such a Hessian can be
useful as a numerical metric, but it is not evidence that the structural
parameters are identified.

There is also a likelihood-design issue. The raw observations are the fixed
daily panel $\mathcal D_H=\{f_{t,d}\}$. If the model instead recomputes
$z_t(\vartheta)$ for every proposed $\vartheta$ and treats that transformed
quantity as the observed left-hand side of a monthly likelihood, the data
object itself changes with the parameter. An exact likelihood must either model
the full daily panel, or use an invertible transformation of it and include the
corresponding density terms. A likelihood for the aggregate alone after
discarding the orthogonal daily information is a parameter-dependent reduced
criterion. It can be useful when explicitly defined that way, but it does not
inherit the identification theory of the conventional fixed-outcome MIDAS
regression in~\eqref{eq:external_conventional_midas}.

\section{Three external estimators}

\subsection{End-of-month nowcast projection}

The first estimator targets the observed end-of-month factor without placing
that target on both sides of the regression. Let $f_{t,D_t}$ denote the final
daily factor and form $z_t^{(-D)}(\vartheta)$ from days
$1,\ldots,D_t-1$. Estimate
\begin{equation}
 (\widehat\vartheta_{\mathrm{EOM}},\widehat\alpha,\widehat B)
 =\arg\min_{\vartheta,\alpha,B}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{train}}}
 \left\|D_f^{-1}\left[f_{t,D_t}-\alpha-Bz_t^{(-D)}(\vartheta)\right]\right\|^2.
 \label{eq:eom_estimator}
\end{equation}
The scale matrix prevents a high-variance factor from dominating the common
profile; $D_f$ is diagonal with strictly positive training-sample scales.
The matrix $B$ separates regression magnitude from normalized lag shape.
After estimating $\vartheta$ without target-day leakage, the same shape can be
deployed over all days when constructing the monthly measurement.

\subsection{Daily state-space/Kalman projection}

The second estimator first fits a separate daily linear-Gaussian model,
\begin{align}
 x_{t,d}&=F_d x_{t,d-1}+u_{t,d}, &u_{t,d}&\sim N(0,Q_d),\\
 f_{t,d}&=H_d x_{t,d}+e_{t,d}, &e_{t,d}&\sim N(0,R_d).
 \label{eq:daily_auxiliary_ssm}
\end{align}
The daily model is estimated using the training daily panel only. Its Kalman
filter or smoother yields an end-of-month conditional mean of the form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x_{t,D_t\mid 1:D_t}=c_t+\sum_{d=1}^{D_t}K_{t,d}f_{t,d}.
 \label{eq:kalman_weights}
\end{equation}
The exact coefficients are generally matrix-valued and factor-specific. In a
scalar steady-state filter with transition $\phi$ and Kalman gain $K$, the
coefficient on an observation $j$ steps before the endpoint is proportional to
\begin{equation}
 K\{\phi(1-K)\}^{j}.
 \label{eq:kalman_geometric_weights}
\end{equation}
Thus small measurement error implies $K\to1$ and strong concentration on the
latest observation. Nearly uniform finite-window weights require both high
persistence and a small gain. The process and measurement variances therefore
must be estimated rather than assigned an arbitrary split.

After fitting the daily state-space parameters, normalize the derived
observation coefficients to profiles $\bar k_{q,d}$ for factors $q=1,\ldots,K$.
The shared Almon profile is obtained by projection,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\vartheta_{\mathrm{KALMAN}}
 =\arg\min_\vartheta\sum_{q=1}^K\sum_{d=1}^{D_t}
 \left[\bar k_{q,d}-w_{t,d}(\vartheta)\right]^2.
 \label{eq:kalman_projection}
\end{equation}
This estimator targets an approximation to the daily minimum-mean-square-error
end-state filter, not the full monthly structural likelihood.

In the multivariate case, the Almon profile is a parsimonious projection of
matrix-valued optimal weights, not an exact identity.

\subsection{Fixed-outcome macro-predictive estimator}

The promoted external estimator follows the forecasting design of
\citet{andreouGhyselsKourtellos2013}. Let $y^{(k)}_{t+h}$ be a declared future
macroeconomic outcome $k$ at horizon $h$, let $C^{(k,h)}_t$ contain only
information available at the forecast origin, and let $z_{r,t}(\vartheta)$ be
the daily summary for financial predictor or factor $r$. For each candidate
$\vartheta$, estimate
\begin{equation}
 y^{(k)}_{t+h}=\alpha_{k,h}+\Gamma_{k,h}'C^{(k,h)}_t
 +\sum_r\beta_{r,k,h}z_{r,t}(\vartheta)+\epsilon^{(k,h)}_{t+h}.
 \label{eq:macro_predictive_midas}
\end{equation}
