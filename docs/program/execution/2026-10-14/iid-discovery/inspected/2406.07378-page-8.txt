Performance of CIT Table 1 summarizes the evaluations of the CI queries over the different prob-
lems. We compute the standard classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores).
The results show varying performance over the different causal graphs and models. As some of these
problems constitute pedagogical examples or hypothesized graphs, they are not accompanied by
observations. Hence, we only compare against data-driven baselines for sachs and asia. Overall,
we can see that the statistical tests, which imply certain assumptions about the graph’s sparsity
and require stronger evidence (i.e., more votes) for rejecting either dependence or independence,
give some control over the metrics, usually implying a trade-off between recall and precision. For
cancer, GPT-3.5 yields the best results in terms of F1 and precision, while GPT-4 has the higher
accuracy. Inspection of the implied graph under the chatPC (see fig. 4) suggests that GPT-4 as-
sumes more dependencies and finds a denser graph. The context of burglary contains mostly all
information on how the variables are connected. The language models still need to perform the task
of extracting valid CI statements from there. GPT-4 clearly outperforms GPT-3.5 in this regard.
This suggests that given access to the relevant information, GPT-4 mostly makes correct statements
about CI. On the asia graph, the LLM models beat the data-driven baseline based on mutual in-
formation in all metrics. Only in precision, GPT-3.5 achieves a slightly higher value than GPT-4.
On the sachs data, in terms of F1, the data-driven method (Sachs et al., 2005) outperforms the two
language models, whilst GPT-4 shows the highest accuracy. It is noteworthy that GPT-4 avoids
eliminating the connection of the proteins Plc-γ and PIP2, with PKC, two known mistakes of the
data-driven approach. The LLM-driven CITs do not yield a perfect oracle in their current state.
Some improvement can likely be made with the next generations of language models, fine-tuning
of the prompt (Cheng et al., 2023), or disambiguation of the context. A final source of error is
that the required knowledge may be controversial or may simply not exist. Here, systems based on
retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2021) could help to ground answers on secured
knowledge or weigh sources against each other (old vs. new; unsystematic vs. scientific). As data-
driven methods also reveal major failure modes, using knowledge-driven evidence to complement
data can be a promising future research avenue.
