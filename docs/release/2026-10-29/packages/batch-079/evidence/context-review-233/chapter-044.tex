\chapter{Instruments, eligibility thresholds and the population affected}
An invitation or eligibility rule can create a useful comparison even
when customers choose whether to participate. The effect for customers whose
behaviour changes at that margin need not equal the effect for every customer.
Software choices must respect the instrument, cutoff and population that
supply the identifying information.

\section{Python IV reuse: rank and diagnostic names are insufficient contracts}
\label{exec22:pyiv-scope}

An instrument-based banking proposal must say which variation changes the
action, which exclusion restriction gives that variation a causal meaning,
and which sampling law justifies uncertainty. The pinned Python implementations
provide useful computational components, but their interfaces expose different
rank and diagnostic behavior. The selected \texttt{linearmodels} and
\texttt{pyfixest} revisions predate the literature cutoff; their equality to
particular distributed release binaries has not been established. The following
comparison concerns inspected source paths, with no native execution or claimed
coverage across the full Python IV and regression-discontinuity landscape
\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_linearmodels,exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest}.

\subsection{The projected design and the causal exclusion restriction}
\label{exec22:pyiv-rank}

In the inspected \texttt{linearmodels} path, positive weights are normalized by
their mean, missing rows are removed, and square-root weights transform the
design, instrument matrix and outcome. The checks include instrument counts
and separate full column rank of $X$ and $Z$. For two-stage least squares the
solve uses the projected design, with matrix $X'P_ZX$, where
$P_Z=ZZ^+$ and $Z^+$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse. Separate ranks do not prove
that $X'P_ZX$ is invertible or well conditioned. They also say nothing about the
exclusion restriction~\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_linearmodels}.

An exact four-row witness makes both distinctions visible. Work with centred
vectors and no additional regressors, and define
\begin{equation}
 z=(-1,-1,1,1)',\qquad u=(-1,1,-1,1)',\qquad
 d=\varepsilon z+u,\qquad y=2d+z/100.
 \label{exec22:pyiv-witness}
\end{equation}
Here $z'u=0$ and $z'z=u'u=4$. Each single-column design $d$ and $z$ has full
column rank, including at $\varepsilon=0$. Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
 d'P_zd=4\varepsilon^2,\qquad
 \widehat\beta_{\rm IV}=\frac{z'y}{z'd}
      =2+\frac{1}{100\varepsilon},\quad\varepsilon\ne0.
 \label{exec22:pyiv-ratio}
\end{equation}
At zero the projected design is singular. At $\varepsilon=1/100$ and $1/1000$
the fitted ratios are $3$ and $12$, while the stipulated action coefficient is
$2$. The term $z/100$ represents a direct instrument effect and violates
exclusion. For a branch encouragement instrument it could represent a small
direct change in service attention outside the action being evaluated.
Small direct effects become consequential when the first stage is weak.
This is an exact sensitivity witness, not a sampling experiment, a package
failure or an empirical assertion about a bank~\citep{exec22_pyiv_reference}.

The inspected \texttt{pyfixest} path removes collinear within-transformed
columns, retaining their names and a mask, before delegating weighted 2SLS.
Its selected fit kernel constructs the first-stage projection with $Z'WZ$,
fits the projected design, retains residuals in response units and includes
weights in the score contributions. The collinearity wrapper passes the
unweighted within Gram matrix to a separate core routine.
Section~\ref{sec:exec25-python-iv-rd} follows that routine and the solver
backend, finding an order- and tolerance-dependent column test and solver
branches that do not retain every returned diagnostic. These findings make
it necessary to retain the actual surviving variables, weight roles and
tolerances at the interface; native numerical qualification remains open. It is not evidence that a column-removal warning is a successful
causal repair~\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest}.

\subsection{Preserve the actual covariance model}
\label{exec22:pyiv-covariance}

The selected \texttt{linearmodels} fit defaults to heteroskedastic-robust
covariance with the finite-sample adjustment disabled. Its robust score meat
uses row contributions and does not by itself account for serial dependence.
The kernel path has a separate bandwidth choice, including an automatically
selected option. The clustering class documents one or two grouping columns
and contains a two-way inclusion--exclusion branch, even though a nearby class
name and fit description say one-way. Its two-way score covariance takes the
form $S_1+S_2-S_{12}$ with component adjustments when requested. Actual grouping
columns and the intersection construction must therefore accompany the result;
a label alone does not establish the implemented dependence model
\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_linearmodels}.

In the absence of an explicit grouping array, the inspected class assigns
each row its own cluster. That is a computational default, not a choice of
bank-level or calendar-level dependence. An adapter should require the intended
sampling units and ordering, report the realized HAC bandwidth or cluster counts,
and retain covariance warnings or singularity. Additional validation is needed
for small numbers of groups, joint calendar dependence, group-code conversion
and the covariance's statistical assumptions. No uniform robustness to arbitrary
banking dependence is implied.

\subsection{A statistic and its p-value can use different displayed scalings}
\label{exec22:pyiv-diagnostics}

For a restriction $R\beta=q$, distinguish the Wald quadratic $W$ from its
F-scaled display $W/k$, where $k$ is the number of restrictions. In the selected
\texttt{pyfixest} parent method, an F reference is used only for the joint zero
restriction on all coefficients. A subset restriction changes the reference
to chi-square and emits a warning. The first-stage helper selects excluded
instruments, suppresses warnings around this call, then retains the F-scaled
value and the returned p-value. When excluded instruments are a proper subset
of the first-stage coefficients, these fields can therefore combine $W/k$
with a chi-square p-value. This is a source-level description of the selected
branch, not evidence of an executed diagnostic error
\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest}.

For example, $W=8$ and $k=2$ give displayed $W/k=4$. A chi-square-two reference
gives $p=e^{-4}\simeq0.0183$, whereas an $F_{2,20}$ reference gives
$p=(1+4/10)^{-10}\simeq0.0346$. The same displayed number cannot identify
which p-value is intended. The selected low-level Wald utility retains both
scalings and the distribution, but the summarized first-stage object drops
some of that information. A wrapper should recover the parent result and
retain the null, restriction matrix, covariance, actual distribution, degrees
of freedom, both scalings and the p-value. A covariance pseudoinverse in the
quadratic calculation does not itself justify inference from singular or
invalid uncertainty~\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest,exec22_pyiv_reference}.

The earlier \texttt{linearmodels} diagnostic inspection likewise distinguishes
its first-stage F or chi-square reference and its \texttt{anderson\_rubin}
overidentification property~\citep{exec22_pyiv_diagnostics_linearmodels}.
The latter's method name is not a contract for
testing every proposed coefficient value. A weak-IV confidence set requires
the appropriate coefficient-null test and its inversion, including empty,
disconnected or unbounded results when justified. The proposed CLI and MCP
surfaces should expose named statistical operations with those targets, rather
than map similarly named fields to a common confidence-interval slot.

For prototype selection, preserve these source distinctions and compare
matched models before choosing either engine. Qualify row and column conversion,
weight semantics, projection conditioning, covariance and diagnostic metadata
before comparing speed. Generated designs should separately vary instrument
strength, exclusion violations, dependence and retained samples, with failures
counted in the denominator. Section~\ref{sec:exec25-python-iv-rd} supplies the
separate Python RD and CausalPy IV source comparison, including model and prior
contracts, cutoff conventions and confidence-set geometry. Those findings
extend this kernel analysis without granting general landscape completion or
native admission. The next investment should buy a
specific testable capability, with its unresolved assumptions visible to the
decision maker.

\section{Choosing an instrumental-variable method for the stated model}
\label{exec23:iv-choice}

A reusable IV component must preserve the model being estimated as well as its
numerical answer. The preceding Python comparison develops the projected-rank
and exclusion witness; it also shows why a diagnostic's number, reference
distribution and null must travel together. The resulting proposal choice for
\texttt{linearmodels} is a reference for a bounded IV comparison. Its pinned
revision is \texttt{13390eee49ea06fc42f831255ff2163f2ad13e8b}, dated
7 October 2026. The repository's package version is dynamic; equality to a
distributed 7.0 wheel or an installed environment has not been authenticated.
The inspected NCSA license requires retention of notices and conditions and
does not establish rights for every transitive dependency. Build requirements,
including Meson and Cython, differ from runtime dependency floors. An executable
bridge therefore needs a resolved environment and distribution review in
addition to the estimator contract.~\citep{exec22_linearmodels_reuse}

This reference choice has practical merit: the inspected paths expose weights,
row removal, covariance options and first-stage results that can support a
matched comparison. Its limits are equally specific. A low-level static
$k$-class estimate explicitly performs no error checking. A successful direct
inverse is not an exclusion test. The \texttt{anderson\_rubin} property uses
the LIML quantity for overidentification and has no coefficient-null argument;
it cannot be relabeled as inversion of a weak-instrument test for every
proposed effect. A proposed adapter should retain the actual sample, weight
normalization, projection conditioning, covariance design, warning state and
diagnostic null. Qualification of those fields is a future conversion task;
reference selection itself supplies no native capability. Panel, system and
GMM capabilities require their own primary technical inspections.
\citep{exec22_linearmodels_reuse}

\subsection{A Java two-stage method can address a different scientific object}
\label{exec23:iv-java}

The selected Tetrad source illustrates why searching for ``two-stage least
squares'' cannot establish interchangeable software coverage. At revision
\texttt{b89afaf952a4fd647df0}\allowbreak\texttt{d85b08102250106c6764}, dated 22 September 2026
and declaring \texttt{7.6.11-SNAPSHOT}, its package-private \texttt{Tsls}
class targets a supplied pure latent measurement model. Other measurements of
latent parents supply instrument columns. The method creates and returns a
structural-equation-model object; this is a different entry contract from a
generic observed-outcome, treatment and excluded-instrument regression, and
it does not establish a regression-discontinuity interface.
\citep{exec22_java_latent_iv_preflight}

Suppose a bank represents an unobserved service-quality construct by several
survey indicators. Fixing one loading defines the construct's scale. In the
inspected default branch, lexicographic indicator names select a fixed loading;
an explicit fixed-loading list is another branch. Renaming columns could
therefore alter the normalization under the default. A comparison must either
preserve that normalization or explicitly transform coefficients and their
interpretation. It cannot equate a latent loading with the dollar effect of a
contact merely because both are coefficients.

The source also supplies concrete conversion questions. A latent iteration
with no instrument collection is skipped, and a measurement iteration with
no auxiliary measures is skipped. The loading path forms two direct inverses.
One matrix-construction read uses \texttt{getDouble(colIndex,i)}, whereas the
inspected data interface declares case then variable, and nearby reads follow
that declared order. This is an observable source mismatch; its reachable
runtime consequences still require caller and data-layout reproduction.
The inspected matrix path delegates inversion to EJML. Its numerical behavior,
including failure signaling for singular inputs, cannot be inferred from the
wrapper alone. Section~\ref{sec:exec25-java-iv-rd} follows the concrete data
boxes and EJML delegates: storage orientation does not repair the swapped
indices, and the inverse wrapper does not preserve the backend's boolean
return. That source analysis narrows the unanswered questions to complete
caller coverage and native behavior; it does not supply an executed trial. A helper computes a residual-scaled inverse into a local array
without returning or storing it in that method. This limits what the inspected
method establishes about returned uncertainty; it is not a finding that the
whole library lacks covariance estimation.~\citep{exec22_java_latent_iv_preflight}

The next prototype should compare observed-IV and latent measurement models
separately. Its fixtures need explicit marker choices, rectangular arrays of
cases and variables, empty-instrument cases and near-singular projections. It must inspect
callers and backend behavior before attributing an executed defect. The
authenticated source header permits GPL version 3 or later, while the build's
Java 21 setting and README's JDK 25 guidance leave environment compatibility
to resolve. Generic Java/JVM and Maven IV/RD discovery remains necessary.
Neither a favorable Python reference decision nor this bounded Java inspection
closes the broader IV/RD software landscape.

\section{Julia and Rust alternatives for instruments and discontinuities}
\label{exec23:otheriv-scope}

A credit eligibility threshold produces several different empirical
questions. The discontinuity in loan approval can differ from the
discontinuity in loan receipt, and both differ from the effect of receiving
credit on a later monetary outcome. A bank comparing software must preserve
the assignment rule, the observation clock, the local population and the
uncertainty of every estimated discontinuity. Language coverage is useful
only when it exposes those distinctions. This section examines five concrete
Julia and Rust implementations at revisions preceding 7 October 2026. The
selected sources provide prospective alternatives and reasons for refusal;
they do not establish native numerical qualification or exhaustive coverage
of either language.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0,exec23_otheriv_source_1,exec23_otheriv_source_2,exec23_otheriv_source_3,exec23_otheriv_source_4}

\subsection{Fixed effects and instrumental variables in Julia}
\label{exec23:otheriv-fe}
FixedEffectModels.jl, version 2.1.0 at the selected 30 September 2026
revision, provides a substantive fixed-effect and two-stage least-squares
route. Its formula distinguishes exogenous regressors, endogenous
regressors, instruments and absorbed effects. Let $X$ contain the structural
regressors and $Z$ the included instruments after the declared weighted
fixed-effect transformation. In the full-rank case, the familiar projection
representation is
\begin{equation}
 P_Z=Z(Z'Z)^{-1}Z',\qquad
 \widehat\beta=(X'P_ZX)^{-1}X'P_ZY.
 \label{exec23:otheriv-2sls}
\end{equation}
This numerical formula presumes sufficient rank. A causal interpretation
additionally needs a defended instrument exogeneity and exclusion argument,
and the intended homogeneous effect or heterogeneous local-effect
interpretation. An instrument-count check cannot establish any of those
scientific claims.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0}

The inspected implementation retains finite-input checks, positive-weight
sample selection, singleton and column removals, a relative sweep tolerance
and first-stage residuals. It can recategorize a numerically collinear
endogenous regressor as exogenous, with an informational message. The
adapter must report that change and its reason. It cannot silently transfer
the user's original instrument interpretation to a different model.
Coefficients omitted for collinearity are reinserted as zero with missing
covariance. That zero is a representation of omission, not an estimated
zero effect. \citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0}

Two further distinctions matter operationally. First, the fixed-effect
solver's convergence flag is retained, but nonconvergence is logged and
fitting continues. Returning a fitted object therefore does not establish
that the residualization reached its requested accuracy. Second, the
first-stage rank-test call is inside an exception handler; an unsuccessful
test leaves missing statistics. The proposed worker should distinguish a
weak statistic, an unavailable statistic and a failed calculation. The
reported rank diagnostic is not a weak-instrument-valid confidence set.
Actual FixedEffects and Vcov dependency artifacts require their own
numerical and inferential review.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0}

The source rejects particular HC2/HC3 combinations with absorbed effects
and exposes cluster and fixed-effect degree adjustments. It also floors
the residual inference degrees of freedom at one. A wrapper must retain
the original cluster/sample counts and establish adequate support rather
than treating that numerical floor as evidence of information. The fitted
object stores a sample mask and formula schema but not the original data,
so exact reconstruction needs external custody of input rows and their
ordering. The package's MIT notice and Julia 1.10 compatibility are useful
planning information, not clearance of every dependency or proof of a
particular installed CPU/GPU profile.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0}

The older InstrumentalVariables.jl is a different candidate, with a
selected August 2016 revision and a REQUIRE file declaring Julia 0.3. Its
source implements Cholesky-based IV projection and covariance methods,
but several local contracts make it unsuitable for direct adoption in
the proposed engine. Three dimension guards construct a
\texttt{DimensionMismatch} object without explicitly throwing it; the
instrument-count expression compares a regressor count with the number
of instrument rows. Later operations may still throw, but these guards
are not reliable evidence that invalid dimensions are rejected at the
intended boundary.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_2}

Its coefficient-table expression applies a normal upper tail to the
squared statistic, despite a two-sided label. This should be treated as
a source-expression concern requiring an appropriate legacy runtime to
determine whether evaluation succeeds. Its no-argument weighted covariance
method also retrieves the stored residual vector and multiplies it in
place by square-root weights. If invoked repeatedly on that mutable
object, a residual of one with weight four becomes two and then four; its
squared contribution changes from four to sixteen. This exact mutation
witness is independent of the rest of the covariance calculation. It
shows why repeated accessor calls must be tested for purity, not that an
upstream Julia execution has been performed here. The README's default
HC0 wording also needs reconciliation with the inspected default scalar
residual-variance covariance formula.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_2,exec23_otheriv_reference}

\subsection{Sharp RD, curvature bounds and probability conventions}
\label{exec23:otheriv-sharp}
RegressionDiscontinuity.jl declares version 0.4.0 at its selected May 2024
revision and explicitly warns that the package is experimental. Its
running-variable object records whether treatment is assigned above or
below the threshold, including equality. This is a valuable feature: a
credit policy applying at score $s\geq c$ is not the same data rule as
$s>c$ when many accounts lie exactly at $c$. The local-linear interface
fits a weighted outcome regression with treatment, centered score and
their interaction, and identifies the treatment coefficient as the
sharp-RD contrast. It labels its inference as naive and its bias as
unaccounted. That limitation should remain visible in the product.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_1}

The inspected local-linear table computes $1-\Phi(|z|)$ for its p-value
but uses the usual two-sided $\alpha/2$ critical value for its interval.
The density-test implementation uses $2[1-\Phi(|z|)]$. At $z=2$, the
one-tail quantity is approximately $0.02275013$, the conventional
two-sided quantity is $0.04550026$, and the legacy IV expression
$1-\Phi(z^2)$ is approximately $0.00003167$. These are plainly different
probability conventions. A common output label does not reconcile them;
the adapter needs a declared hypothesis and a validated calculation.
The values are independently computed normal tails, not observed native
outputs. \citep{exec23_otheriv_source_1,exec23_otheriv_source_2,exec23_otheriv_reference}

The package also exposes an optimized sharp-RD route with an explicit
bound $B$ on the second derivative, a chosen discretization and an
external optimization solver. That is a distinct scientific contract
from bias-unadjusted local linear fitting. Its source constructs a
cone problem, calls optimization and then extracts solution values
without a local termination-status guard. A worker must check solver
status, primal/dual validity and numerical accuracy before treating the
reported maximum bias as a certificate. The curvature bound needs a
scientific justification and sensitivity analysis. The named paper and
the complete solver/dependency argument require their own original-source
review before any theorem-level guarantee is adopted.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_1}

Noise-induced randomization is another exposed research route, with
outcome bounds, a latent convex class, target, noise model, solver,
discretization and a switch that skips bias calculation. The presence
of that interface does not solve arbitrary noisy credit scores. A
proposal should preserve these assumptions and identify the extra
measurement information needed to justify them. Only selected entry
and optimization spans were inspected here; the full method, proofs
and empirical performance remain separate obligations.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_1}

\subsection{A separate Julia port of the rdrobust workflow}
\label{exec23:otheriv-port}
The repository xiangao/RDRobust.jl declares version 0.1.1 at its
3 October 2026 revision. Its repository identity and license notice
must be retained separately from any metadata author string or claimed
similarity to the R package. No official affiliation or endorsement is
inferred here. The README claims a numerical tolerance on selected test
cases, but those tests have not been executed in this review. The
selected source does expose substantive local-polynomial machinery: main
and bias bandwidths, covariates, clustering, nearest-neighbor residual
estimation, mass-point adjustments and sharp/fuzzy targets.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_3}

Its output distinguishes conventional, bias-corrected and robust rows.
The bias-corrected row uses the corrected point estimate with the
conventional standard error, while the robust row uses the corrected
estimate and its robust standard error. A common reporting layer must
not combine one row's coefficient with another row's interval without
an explicit reason. The fuzzy path carries a two-component ratio
gradient into its covariance calculation. That provides a useful
source-level contrast with the Rust implementation considered below,
while leaving the actual statistical and numerical qualification open.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_3}

Several implementation details need deliberate controls. The helper
named \texttt{qrXXinv} directly inverts a cross-product matrix. Its
complete-case helper excludes NaN but does not itself exclude infinity.
Cluster labels are converted to floating-point values, so an adapter
needs a lossless category map instead of blindly casting identifiers.
The cluster correction contains $G/(G-1)$ and $(n-1)/(n-k)$, requiring
valid cluster and residual degrees. When covariates become collinear
with local polynomials, the code warns and still uses a generalized
inverse. A computed number must not become an accepted identified
contrast merely because that inverse exists.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_3}

Mass-point counting and bandwidth enlargement help describe the local
sample; they do not create variation that is absent. Five observations
at score $-1$ on one side and five at score $1$ on the other still give
only one distinct score on each side. The two-column intercept/score
design has determinant zero within each side. Adding more duplicate
accounts does not identify a local slope. The response should report
support, rank and the assumed smoothness or discrete-score interpretation
explicitly. \citep{exec23_otheriv_source_3,exec23_otheriv_reference}

\subsection{Fuzzy ratios and a Rust example that should not be adopted}
\label{exec23:otheriv-ratio}
The selected Rust repository suenot/127-regression-discontinuity
contains a trading-oriented library named rdd\_trading, version 0.1.0
at its March 2026 revision. It implements separate local fits and a
fuzzy ratio, but the inspected uncertainty and diagnostic behavior
do not support adoption as the proposed scientific engine. No trading,
exchange, network or downloaded-code execution is involved in this
review. A financial example in a repository is not evidence that
threshold crossing is a valid causal assignment mechanism.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_4}

Let $a$ denote an estimated outcome discontinuity and $b$ an estimated
treatment-receipt discontinuity. The fuzzy target is $\theta=a/b$.
Under a justified regular joint limit, with $b$ bounded away from zero,
the first-order variance uses
\begin{equation}
 g=\left(\frac1b,-\frac{a}{b^2}\right)',
 \qquad V_\theta\simeq g'V_{a,b}g.
 \label{exec23:otheriv-delta}
\end{equation}
This expression is not weak-instrument-robust inference. It explains
why dividing an outcome standard error by $|b|$ is generally incomplete:
that shortcut freezes the estimated denominator and omits its covariance
with the numerator. The inspected Rust fuzzy method uses that shortcut
and rejects first-stage magnitudes below a fixed $0.01$. A fixed numerical
cutoff is not a calibrated test of weak identification.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_4}

For a reproducible stipulated example, take
\begin{equation}
 a=2,\qquad b=\frac15,\qquad
 V_{a,b}=\begin{pmatrix}1/25&1/250\\1/250&1/100\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{exec23:otheriv-example}
\end{equation}
The ratio is ten dollars and $g=(5,-50)'$. The full delta variance is
$1+25-2=24$ dollars squared; freezing the denominator gives only one.
With an assumed six-dollar cost per additional treated account, the
ratio-based net is four dollars. This is an illustrative regular-case
calculation; the numbers do not establish that a small first stage
satisfies the regular approximation, that the instrument is valid, or
that a real bank earns this return. If the denominator is weak, the
appropriate route is a justified joint confidence-set or test-inversion
procedure, retaining unbounded or disconnected sets where warranted,
rather than adjusting a symmetric interval to look precise.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_reference}

The Rust sharp and fuzzy methods also set the test statistic to zero
when the computed standard error is nonpositive. Its weighted local
solver adds a fixed determinant ridge and floors variance denominators.
These choices need numerical and statistical justification; an interval
or p-value produced after such a fallback is not automatically usable.
The method named for an optimal bandwidth uses a simplified curvature
calculation, regularization and range bounds. Its label alone does not
establish correspondence to a named published selector.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_4}

Its density diagnostic is particularly important for banking use. The
source calculates near-cutoff counts and their density ratio. If either
local density is nonpositive, it substitutes a zero log contrast and a
standard error of one, yielding a p-value of one. The correct product
interpretation is unavailable local evidence, not reassurance that
scores were not manipulated. The binned counts calculated earlier in
the function are not used in that returned statistic. A declared
density-test name cannot substitute for inspection of the actual
calculation, and a failure to reject manipulation never certifies all
RD identification assumptions.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_4,exec23_otheriv_reference}

\subsection{Choosing a route and preserving omissions}
\label{exec23:otheriv-choice}
The useful proposal choice is method-specific. FixedEffectModels.jl is
a conditional Julia worker candidate after numerical, covariance and
failure-contract qualification. RegressionDiscontinuity.jl remains a
research reference whose naive and optimized/noise-based paths need
separate treatment. The old InstrumentalVariables.jl remains historical
reference material; its runtime and local hazards argue against current
native adoption. The distinct RDRobust.jl port is a comparator requiring
provenance, rank and matched-output trials. The inspected Rust trading
implementation is rejected as the current scientific engine because
its ratio uncertainty and unavailable-density fallbacks would require
substantive repair. All five retained license texts declare MIT terms,
but the actual numerical, optimization and runtime dependencies still
need distribution-specific review.
\citep{exec23_otheriv_source_0,exec23_otheriv_source_1,exec23_otheriv_source_2,exec23_otheriv_source_3,exec23_otheriv_source_4}

An integration trial should compare the complete intervention request
under declared assumptions, not just regression coefficients. Retain
sample exclusions, score support, timing, first-stage strength, complete
covariance, numerical convergence, diagnostics and failures. Use the
independent ratio, probability, rank and mutation witnesses as initial
oracles, followed by predeclared public or synthetic calibration and
cost trials in an authorized installed environment. The scientific
owner approves the estimand and inference; the maintainer owns exact
versions and regression cases; the operator owns custody and complete
cost accounting. A new port needs a demonstrated benefit before
duplicating these obligations. \citep{exec23_otheriv_reference}

Eight additional Julia repositories returned by the bounded discovery
queries remain uninspected, including alternative IV, shift-share,
system-projection and model-averaging routes. Other languages and
registries are also unresolved. A zero-result Rust IV query is a
coverage limit, not proof that no implementation exists. These omissions
prevent a claim of comprehensive coverage while allowing the concrete
source findings and proposed comparisons above to guide the next
prototype decision.

\section{R instruments and credit thresholds: preserving the design and its limits}
\label{exec22:riv-scope}

A credit eligibility threshold can change access to a product without forcing
every eligible customer to take it. A bank comparing losses above and below the
threshold must decide whether its question concerns eligibility, actual take-up,
the customers whose take-up changes, or universal provision of the product. These
are different interventions and populations. An R interface that returns one
coefficient under the label ``causal effect'' can conceal the most consequential
choice in the analysis. The useful comparison is therefore between complete
designs: the assumptions, target, statistical procedure and information that must
survive a library, CLI or MCP request.

Six concrete package profiles supply complementary pieces. AER~1.2-17 and
ivreg~0.6-8 provide separately implemented instrumental-variable regression
interfaces. ivmodel~1.9.1 adds weak-instrument tests and sensitivity procedures
for a specified linear model. rdrobust~4.0.0 supplies local-polynomial estimation
and bias-corrected inference at a cutoff; rddensity~3.0 supplies density diagnostics;
rdlocrand~3.0 supplies methods under local randomization. AER remains relevant
both as a comparison and as a declared dependency of rdlocrand. The shared
\texttt{ivreg} function and class names do not make the first two packages the
same engine.~\citep{exec22_riv_aer,exec22_riv_ivreg,exec22_riv_ivmodel,
exec22_riv_rdrobust,exec22_riv_rddensity,exec22_riv_rdlocrand}

The release date also matters. rdrobust~4.1.1 carries the package date
7~October~2026, but its CRAN publication field is 23:20:02~UTC on that date,
after the client cutoff of 15:59:59~UTC. The cutoff comparison therefore retains
the earlier authenticated 4.0.0 distribution. This resolves the CRAN distribution
question without asserting that every possible upstream channel had the same
publication history. Current manuals cannot silently supply capabilities to an
earlier version.~\citep{exec22_riv_rdrobust}

\subsection{What the instrument identifies, and which policy it values}
\label{exec22:riv-target}

Let $Z\in\{0,1\}$ denote eligibility or a randomized encouragement, $D(z)$
actual take-up under eligibility $z$, and $Y(d)$ a fixed-horizon monetary loss
under treatment $d$. Loss is positive when it costs the bank money. In an
independent-encouragement design, assume consistency, no interference,
independence of $Z$ from the potential variables, exclusion of a direct $Z$
effect on loss, monotonicity $D(1)\geq D(0)$, and a positive first stage.
Write $C=\{D(1)>D(0)\}$ for the complier group. Independence and exclusion give
\begin{align}
 \Delta_D&=E[D\mid Z=1]-E[D\mid Z=0]=P(C),\nonumber\\
 \Delta_Y&=E[Y\mid Z=1]-E[Y\mid Z=0]
          =P(C)E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid C],\nonumber\\
 \theta_C&=\Delta_Y/\Delta_D.
 \label{exec22:riv-late}
\end{align}
The second equality follows because always-takers and never-takers do not change
treatment, while every complier changes from zero to one. It does not identify
their unobserved treatment contrasts, and therefore does not generally identify
the population average effect. With a deterministic score cutoff, global
independence of eligibility is unavailable: an appropriate continuity argument
at the cutoff, or an explicitly justified local-randomization window, must supply
the corresponding design. Calling eligibility an instrument does not supply that
argument.

Consider the following complete, project-authored population. The encouragement
is independent of type and has probability $1/2$. All monetary values are
hypothetical dollars over the same horizon.~\citep{exec22_riv_reference}
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c|cc|rr}
 \text{type}&\text{mass}&D(0)&D(1)&Y(0)&Y(1)\\\hline
 \text{complier}&2/5&0&1&200&50\\
 \text{always-taker}&1/5&1&1&200&150\\
 \text{never-taker}&2/5&0&0&200&200
\end{array}
\label{exec22:riv-population}
\end{equation}
Mean take-up is $1/5$ without encouragement and $3/5$ with it. Mean loss changes
from USD~190 to USD~130. Thus $\Delta_D=2/5$, $\Delta_Y=-60$ and
$\theta_C=-150$, while the full population effect is $-70$. If treatment costs
USD~100, the stipulated complier treatment margin is USD~50, but treating everyone
instead of no one has margin USD~$-30$. Complier membership is a latent response
type, so the first margin does not imply an implementable policy that identifies
and treats only compliers.

The encouragement itself is an implementable alternative under this law. If it
costs USD~10 per customer and changes treatment costs only through the extra
take-up, its incremental margin relative to no encouragement is
\begin{equation}
 V_Z=-\Delta_Y-100\Delta_D-10=10\quad\text{USD}.
 \label{exec22:riv-value}
\end{equation}
This calculation explains why the proposed service should return the reduced
form and first stage together with their ratio. A business request about changing
eligibility may be answered by the eligibility effect without extrapolating a
complier effect to universal treatment. In actual estimates, uncertainty in both
components and their covariance must enter the policy calculation.

Two violations show what software cannot repair by fitting more accurately.
If encouragement directly lowers every customer's loss by USD~20, holding the
treatment law fixed, the observed ratio becomes $(-60-20)/(2/5)=-200$ although
the treatment contrast among compliers remains $-150$. Alternatively, let
compliers have mass $2/5$ and effect $-150$, defiers have mass $1/5$ and effect
$-50$, and the remaining customers never take treatment. Independence and
exclusion then imply a first stage $1/5$, reduced form $-50$ and ratio $-250$.
The ratio is outside the two changing groups' effects because the defier weight
is negative. Neither an overidentification test nor a first-stage statistic
establishes the exclusion and monotonicity assumptions used in
equation~\eqref{exec22:riv-late}. These finite examples isolate the failure;
they are not empirical evidence of either violation at a bank.

\subsection{Regression interfaces and weak-instrument sets}
\label{exec22:riv-inference}

AER documents a two-part regressor/instrument formula, with exogenous regressors
also included as instruments. Its summary method defaults to diagnostics being
off; when requested it reports first-stage, endogeneity and overidentification
diagnostics. The dedicated ivreg package offers additional formula conventions,
including separate exogenous, endogenous and excluded-instrument parts, and
ordinary two-stage least squares or M/MM robust regression. Its summary defaults
to diagnostic reporting, and an alternative covariance must be supplied as a
function when auxiliary diagnostic regressions also require it. A saved matrix
for the final regression does not specify covariance for those auxiliary fits.
\citep{exec22_riv_aer,exec22_riv_ivreg}

The integration should therefore name the package and version, construct an
allowed formula from typed variable roles, and record the realized design
matrices, row mask, contrasts, weights, intercept, numerical ranks and covariance
method. It should retain aliased or unavailable coefficients with their reasons.
Automatically dropping an absorbed or collinear treatment and reporting zero
would change an unavailable target into a false scientific result. The M/MM
options address outlier-resistant regression; heteroskedastic or cluster covariance
addresses other uncertainty issues. Neither label by itself supplies inference
valid under a weak first stage.

ivmodel describes a continuous-outcome model with one endogenous variable,
$Y=X\alpha+D\beta+\epsilon$, and $E[\epsilon\mid X,Z]=0$. Some procedures,
including its conditional likelihood ratio path, add a linear first-stage model.
The Anderson--Rubin and conditional likelihood ratio interfaces return a matrix
whose rows are interval components, together with a human-readable description.
The pinned source explicitly constructs whole-line and disconnected outputs.
Those are substantive answers, not serialization defects.
\citep{exec22_riv_ivmodel}

An exact Gaussian reference makes the required representation transparent. Let
$(\widehat\Delta_Y,\widehat\Delta_D)$ be jointly normal with mean
$(\beta\pi,\pi)$ and known positive-definite covariance
\begin{equation}
 V=\begin{pmatrix}v_Y&c\\c&v_D\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{exec22:riv-gaussian}
\end{equation}
For a candidate $b$, the standardized contrast
$(\widehat\Delta_Y-b\widehat\Delta_D)/\sqrt{v_Y-2bc+b^2v_D}$ is standard
normal under $\beta=b$, including when $\pi=0$. Inverting a squared critical
value $q$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_q=\left\{b:
 (\widehat\Delta_D^2-qv_D)b^2+
 (-2\widehat\Delta_Y\widehat\Delta_D+2qc)b+
 (\widehat\Delta_Y^2-qv_Y)\leq0\right\}.
 \label{exec22:riv-set}
\end{equation}
This is a project derivation for a known-covariance Gaussian law. It is neither
a reproduction of the package's finite-sample implementation nor a guarantee
under arbitrary heteroskedasticity or clustering. Choose $q=4$ for exact simple
roots: the model coverage is $2\Phi(2)-1\simeq0.9545$, not exactly 95\%.
With $c=0$, the following observed inputs produce three different shapes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{cc|cc|l}
 \widehat\Delta_Y&\widehat\Delta_D&v_Y&v_D&\mathcal C_4\\\hline
 2&0&3/4&1/4&(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)\\
 2&2&5/12&1/4&[1/3,7/3]\\
 0&0&3/4&1/4&\mathbb R
\end{array}
\label{exec22:riv-topology}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution and quadratic roots agree in each case.
\citep{exec22_riv_reference}

For the first row, searching only $[-1/2,1/2]$ finds no accepted value even
though the true inverted set contains two infinite rays. An interface must
distinguish ``empty on the searched grid'' from a globally empty set. It must
also distinguish an infinite endpoint from missing information and from an
unfinished numerical search. Replacing the two components by their convex hull
would insert all the rejected values between $-1$ and $1$; replacing them by a
finite interval would remove supported tail values. A monetary decision can
change under either alteration.

Static inspection adds a specific implementation concern. In the pinned
ivmodel CLR routine, a numerical error during conditional p-value evaluation is
caught and replaced with zero; a root-search error similarly substitutes zero
for an intermediate quantity. The AR routine uses a projection F statistic,
while CLR constructs a residual cross-product covariance. The parent object's
generic heteroskedastic or cluster standard-error options do not establish that
these paths implement the corresponding robust tests.~\citep{exec22_riv_ivmodel}
A proposed worker must expose or independently detect the numerical failure
before accepting the result. An explicit zero p-value is otherwise
indistinguishable from the substituted failure value at that field alone. The
source establishes the branch; its frequency and native triggering cases remain
to be measured in a controlled prototype. This is a reason to retain the
package as a reference while withholding runtime admission for that path.

\subsection{A local polynomial cannot fill a gap in the score support}
\label{exec22:riv-continuity}

rdrobust's continuity-based profile distinguishes sharp, fuzzy and kink designs,
the degree used for estimation and bias correction, left/right estimation
bandwidths $h$, bias bandwidths $b$, kernel, variance procedure and clustering.
Its outputs distinguish conventional estimates, bias-corrected estimates and
the associated uncertainty rows. The adapter must preserve these associations;
a corrected estimate with the wrong standard error is a different procedure.
Effective sample sizes, unique score counts and warnings are part of the result.
\citep{exec22_riv_rdrobust}

Rounded credit scores make the support issue concrete. Suppose the only score
values are $R=-1$ and $R=1$, eligibility is $D=1\{R\geq0\}$, and observed mean
losses are USD~100 and USD~80 respectively. Two continuous potential-mean models
agree at every observed score:
\begin{align}
 \text{A: }&m_0(r)=100,\qquad m_1(r)=80,\nonumber\\
 \text{B: }&m_0(r)=m_1(r)=90-10r.
 \label{exec22:riv-support}
\end{align}
Model A has treatment effect USD~$-20$ at zero, while model B has effect zero.
There are no observations approaching the cutoff from either side, so continuity
alone cannot select between the models. More customers at the same two scores
reduce uncertainty about the observed means but do not identify the unobserved
limits. A mass-point option can alter estimation behavior; it cannot create
missing score support.~\citep{exec22_riv_reference,exec22_riv_rdrobust}

If additional substantive information bounds each potential-mean slope in
absolute value by $L$, and the nearest observations are distance $\delta$ from
the cutoff, each extrapolation contributes at most $L\delta$. In this
two-point law the cutoff effect belongs to
\begin{equation}
 \theta(0)\in[-20-2L\delta,-20+2L\delta].
 \label{exec22:riv-bound}
\end{equation}
The endpoints are attainable by choosing the two potential-mean slopes within
the declared bound. With $L=10$ and $\delta=1$, the effect set is
$[-40,0]$ dollars. At an assumed treatment cost USD~10, the net-margin set is
$[-10,30]$ dollars, and it does not justify a uniformly positive-margin claim.
These are identification bounds with known observed means; sampling uncertainty
would need to be added rather than silently absorbed into the slope restriction.

rddensity supplies a different diagnostic: local-polynomial density estimates
and tests for a discontinuity in the running-variable distribution, with explicit
bandwidth, variance and mass-point options. Its documentation distinguishes a
conventional statistic from robust bias correction and warns about conventional
inference with an MSE-selected bandwidth.~\citep{exec22_riv_rddensity}
Even exact equality of the score density on both sides would not prove continuity
of potential losses. For example, a continuously uniform score can coexist with
another policy that changes at zero and affects loss, even when the treatment
being studied has no effect. A density test examines the score law, not that
competing intervention. Conversely, rounded scores need their own support and
measurement discussion before a continuous-density interpretation is applied.

\subsection{Local randomization is an alternative design with its own obligations}
\label{exec22:riv-randomization}

rdlocrand assumes local randomization within a supplied or selected window. The
window-selection routine considers nested windows and covariate balance tests.
That procedure is useful evidence for design assessment, but a favorable balance
p-value cannot prove assignment independence. Outcome-driven window search also
changes the inference problem. The proposed request must retain whether the window
was fixed before outcome inspection, selected with pre-treatment covariates, or
chosen through a procedure requiring additional selection analysis.
\citep{exec22_riv_rdlocrand}

The distinction is visible in a finite reference. Fix six customers' losses under
the sharp null at $(0,0,0,100,100,100)$ dollars. Assume exactly three are assigned
treatment, uniformly over the $\binom63=20$ allocations, in a window fixed in
advance. If the first three receive treatment, the difference in mean loss is
$-100$. Exactly two allocations have an absolute difference at least this large,
so the two-sided randomization p-value is $2/20=0.1$. This is complete enumeration
under a Fisher sharp null of no effect for any customer. It does not supply a
general test of zero population average effect under arbitrary heterogeneous
effects.~\citep{exec22_riv_reference}

The pinned rdlocrand documentation also distinguishes requested from effective
polynomial degree. For the documented mean contrasts and fuzzy procedures,
positive degree invokes large-sample normal inference; a randomization p-value
is not computed. Some statistic/degree combinations revert to degree zero with
a warning. Fuzzy test inversion can require an explicit effect grid. Consequently,
the adapter must export the effective method, degree, variance choice, assignment
law, seed, repetitions, window and grid, not just the originally requested
options. HC3 standard errors do not confer finite-sample randomization validity,
and a TSLS/Wald label does not remove weak-first-stage concerns.
\citep{exec22_riv_rdlocrand}

\subsection{Reuse choices, rights and a prototype that can change the decision}
\label{exec22:riv-reuse}

The immediate recommendation is reference reuse with conditional plans for
original R workers. The dedicated ivreg interface is the natural initial
ordinary-2SLS candidate; AER remains a distinct baseline and dependency.
ivmodel is valuable for documenting sets and sensitivity, with CLR admission
conditional on a qualified failure path. rdrobust serves an explicit continuity
profile, rddensity a supplementary diagnostic, and rdlocrand a separate
local-randomization profile. A direct port adds numerical and maintenance duties
and should follow an identified operational need. Existing grf effect-estimation
and fixest panel-worker proposals do not automatically qualify an IV target or
this threshold design merely because they share an R execution path.

The pinned metadata declares GPL version alternatives for AER, GPL version~2 or
later for ivreg, and GPL~3 for the three RD packages. ivmodel declares GPL~2 or
its local license file, whose inspected text supplies an MIT alternative with
copyright/permission-notice conditions and a warranty disclaimer. These facts
improve the package-level comparison but do not clear the full dependency tree.
rdlocrand imports AER and sandwich; other profiles also depend on statistical,
formula, matrix or plotting packages. Exact installed versions and their notices
must be included in the selected distribution.~\citep{exec22_riv_aer,
exec22_riv_ivreg,exec22_riv_ivmodel,exec22_riv_rdrobust,exec22_riv_rddensity,
exec22_riv_rdlocrand}

The GPL texts distinguish running a program from copying, modifying and conveying
it, and impose applicable notice and corresponding-source conditions. Moving an
engine behind a process boundary does not itself decide all obligations of a
particular distributed product. Original-worker integration and porting therefore
need a review of the actual distribution, including third-party material. No
current package recommendation grants that implementation clearance.
\citep{exec22_riv_gpl2,exec22_riv_gpl3}

A useful public prototype would first compare typed requests with independent
projection and set-inversion references, then test the complete native workflow.
The ordinary-IV cases should vary rank, weights, missingness and covariance
conventions while preserving the target. Weak-IV cases should stress a vanishing
first stage, disconnected sets, numerical integration and root-search failures.
Threshold cases should distinguish smooth support approaching zero, a small
take-up discontinuity, rounded scores and competing policies. The local-randomization
path should reproduce the twenty-allocation example before larger simulations,
and analyze selected-window behavior separately.

Before final simulation, freeze the data laws, target, package/dependency versions,
seeds, replication budget, tolerances and stopping rule. Keep failed fits in the
denominator and report Monte Carlo uncertainty. Native execution, serialization
fidelity, statistical calibration and decision value answer different questions;
passing one must not silently mark the others complete. An LLM can help translate
the business question, propose alternative eligibility graphs and assemble these
typed requests. It must preserve unresolved exclusion, compliance and score-support
questions, and must not convert a failed search or a diagnostic p-value into a
causal certificate. This design makes a bounded prototype informative before
an expensive experiment with bank data.

\section{CausalPy at a lending threshold: what a reusable experiment must preserve}
\label{exec14:causalpy-contract}

A credit policy often turns a continuous score into a discrete action. A borrower
just above a threshold might receive a different offer, servicing contact or
review requirement. The useful software question is therefore not simply whether
a package can fit two curves. It is whether the resulting contrast answers the
bank's intervention question, whether its uncertainty refers to that contrast,
and whether the interface preserves the design when observations, units or models
change. CausalPy offers an instructive candidate because its experiment interface
can expose a Bayesian regression or a point-estimation backend. The source
revision considered here declares version 1.0.0rc1 and is dated 6 October 2026;
the earlier distribution-metadata observation of 0.9.0 is a different artifact.
The following analysis concerns the selected sharp regression-discontinuity
path, not automatic approval of its other designs.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary}

\subsection{The local question precedes the fitted contrast}

Let $X$ be the score available at the decision time, $c$ the cutoff and
$T=\mathbf{1}\{X\geq c\}$ the stipulated sharp assignment rule. Define $Y(t)$ as
the fixed-horizon outcome under action $t$. If observed outcomes obey consistency,
the conditional potential-outcome means are continuous at $c$, and score support
approaches the cutoff from both sides, the limiting observed jump identifies
the local mean effect. To see the dependence on these assumptions explicitly,
write $\mu_t(x)=E[Y(t)\mid X=x]$. Sharp assignment and consistency imply
$E[Y\mid X=x]=\mu_0(x)$ below the cutoff and $\mu_1(x)$ above it. Continuity
then gives
\[
 \tau_c=\mu_1(c)-\mu_0(c)
 =\lim_{x\downarrow c}E[Y\mid X=x]-\lim_{x\uparrow c}E[Y\mid X=x].
\]
This derivation is conditional on the stipulated design. It does not establish
that a real score cannot be manipulated, that a simultaneous pricing change is
absent, or that the effect transports to all borrowers. A servicing outcome also
requires a stable measurement horizon and a declared treatment of censoring.
Those are parts of the question, not properties acquired by importing a regression
class.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_exact,exec14_causalpy_decision}

The inspected CausalPy experiment builds design matrices and later obtains
predictions at two rows: $(c-\epsilon,T=0)$ and $(c+\epsilon,T=1)$. Its result
subtracts the first prediction from the second. The default separation is
$\epsilon=0.001$ in the running variable's units. Consequently the returned
quantity is a finite-separation model contrast,
\[
 \Delta_\epsilon=m(c+\epsilon,1)-m(c-\epsilon,0),
\]
which approaches a model's threshold jump only under the relevant limiting
behavior. The implementation's name does not make a finite separation identical
to that limit. The inspected treatment rule assumes treatment above the cutoff;
the selected validation method requires a dummy-coded treated column but does
not itself demonstrate that every observed treatment matches this rule.
An eligibility cutoff with imperfect take-up is a different design. Relabeling
actual receipt as deterministic eligibility would hide the first-stage question.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary}

Consider the exact, stipulated conditional mean with the score centered at zero,
\[
 m(x,t)=100+20x+5t+10xt.
\]
Its threshold jump is 5. At $\epsilon=1/1000$, however,
$\Delta_\epsilon=5+(2\cdot20+10)/1000=5.05$.
More generally, for $m(x,t)=\alpha+\beta x+\tau t+\gamma xt$ centered at the cutoff,
the difference is $(2\beta+\gamma)\epsilon$. This is an exact contrast identity,
not an observed software bias estimate or an assertion about a fitted sample.
Shrinking the separation reduces this particular deterministic difference but
does not repair a misspecified regression, weak support or an invalid design.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_exact}

Units make the distinction operational. Expressing the same score as $Z=100X$
changes the coefficients to $0.2$ and $0.1$ for the score and its interaction.
Keeping the numeric separation at 0.001 now produces 5.0005; preserving the
physical separation requires $\epsilon_Z=0.1$, which restores 5.05. The cutoff,
bandwidth and donut exclusion must also transform. A unit conversion that leaves
all numeric tuning inputs untouched therefore changes the question asked of an
otherwise equivalent model. A proposed adapter should return the two physical
prediction locations and their units alongside the contrast.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_exact,exec14_causalpy_decision}

\subsection{What fit quality cannot resolve}

The need for a design restriction can be demonstrated without simulated noise.
In world A let $Y(t)=100+20X+5t$; in world B let
$Y(t)=100+20X+5\mathbf{1}\{X\geq0\}$ for both treatment values. Retain
$T=\mathbf{1}\{X\geq0\}$ in each world. Both worlds have exactly the same
observed outcome function, including the jump of 5, but their treatment effects
are respectively 5 and 0. World B violates the continuity restriction. No
estimator that sees only this observed law can use fit quality to decide which
counterfactual extension is true. For an illustrative outcome measured in dollars
and an action cost of USD 3 per borrower near the cutoff, the stipulated net
effect is USD 2 in world A and minus USD 3 in world B. These are alternative
local mechanisms, not a bank-wide return estimate.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_exact}

This ambiguity suggests a useful public-data prototype. Generate known mechanisms
with and without baseline discontinuities, retain the same observed law where
possible, and require the workflow to state which assumption separates them.
Public lending or policy datasets can exercise data preparation and diagnostics,
but their public availability alone does not reveal the missing counterfactual
truth. Density or covariate checks can contribute design evidence; neither should
be represented as a proof of every identifying restriction. The evaluation should
score appropriate qualification and refusal as well as numerical recovery.
The proposed prototype can therefore demonstrate whether the interface preserves
the question before an expensive institution-specific study is justified.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_decision}

\subsection{Uncertainty is a semantic type, not an array dimension}

The inspected Bayesian linear model places Normal priors with scale 50 on
coefficients and uses a Normal likelihood whose noise scale has a HalfNormal
prior with scale 1. Its deterministic mean is the design matrix times the
coefficient vector. The experiment adapter extracts these expected-outcome means;
the response-predictive variable has a different interpretation. The selected RD
plotting path uses equal-tail posterior quantiles. Such quantiles condition on the
model and prior: they are not automatically highest-density intervals, robust
bias-corrected frequentist intervals, or uncertainty about the truth of the causal
design. The sampling path merges stored settings and call-time overrides before
invoking the sampler. A reproducible request consequently needs the actual runtime
and sampler configuration, not just the experiment class name.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary}

A point-estimation backend introduces a subtler conversion hazard. Its adapter
places point predictions into arrays with singleton chain and draw dimensions.
Those dimensions provide a common representation; they do not manufacture
posterior variation. An interval routine applied mechanically to a singleton
would return equal endpoints, but that degeneracy is not evidence of statistical
certainty. The proposed result contract must distinguish a point estimate from
posterior expected outcomes, response-predictive draws and prior draws. Requests
for unsupported uncertainty should return an explicit unavailable result, never
a zero-width confidence claim.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary,exec14_causalpy_decision}

Prior scales must follow units as well. In the coordinate change $Z=100X$, a
coefficient distributed as Normal with scale 50 in the old score units has scale
0.5 in the new units if the prior is to describe the same model. Resetting both
to the same default numeric scale changes prior beliefs. Outcome transformations
likewise affect coefficient and noise priors. These observations do not condemn
the default model: they explain why a reusable banking operation must record and
review its units, likelihood and prior rather than silently rebuilding them.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary,exec14_causalpy_exact}

\subsection{Support, lifecycle and the choice of an alternative}

The selected implementation filters a symmetric bandwidth and optionally removes
a donut around the threshold. At ten or fewer retained observations it warns;
the warning is not a scientifically sufficient qualification rule. A positive
donut leaves no observations arbitrarily close to the cutoff in that selected
sample, so threshold predictions rely on the model's extrapolation across the
excluded region. The default infinite bandwidth also does not constitute a
data-driven local bandwidth choice. A bank wrapper needs left and right support,
row-selection records and an explicit extrapolation flag alongside fitted output.
It should separately check invalid tuning inputs and reversed treatment coding.
These are proposed boundary obligations; the selected source has not been run
against those cases here.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary,exec14_causalpy_decision}

Lifecycle is equally consequential for an agent or a long-running service. The
source warns that its frozen design fingerprint does not detect later edits to
the experiment's data and recommends constructing a new experiment for changed
data. Assigning a fresh model resets result state. Therefore a request should bind
an immutable data identity and a formula/design identity, create a fresh experiment
when either changes, and keep configured, built and fitted states distinguishable.
The selected test sources cover RD smoke paths, treatment normalization and donut
masks. Reading those tests is evidence of intended cases, not evidence that this
proposal executed them or obtained calibrated RD intervals.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary}

For a different uncertainty objective, the inspected R documentation for
\texttt{rdrobust} describes local-polynomial estimators and robust bias-corrected
confidence intervals, with separate fitting and bias bandwidths, polynomial
orders, kernels and sharp/fuzzy/kink options. This supplies a concrete comparison
to a Bayesian model contrast. The choice depends on the design, target and
uncertainty statement; the packages should not be interchanged by a generic
causal-effect endpoint. The R source snapshot used for this comparison
precedes the stated literature cutoff. Documentation of an algorithm is not a
qualification result for the bank's sampling law.~\citep{v12_sw_rdrobust}

\subsection{A scoped reuse decision and the work it creates}

The proposal retains CausalPy as a pinned reference and a candidate for a narrow
future integration. Direct production adoption is deferred until the conversion
and failure contract has been implemented and exercised. Porting the model or
sampler is not presently justified: it transfers numerical and maintenance
ownership without an established benefit. Rejecting the entire package is also
unsupported by this review. Its root source license is Apache 2.0, its packaging
requires Python 3.12 or later, and its declared direct dependencies include broad
PyMC and PyTensor major-version ranges. These declarations establish neither a
resolved environment nor complete dependency rights. The pinned pre-release
version and dated activity are observable facts; they are not promises of future
support.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_primary,exec14_causalpy_decision}

The next prototype should exercise cutoff equality, direction reversals, physical
unit transformations, bandwidth and donut masks, changed-data reconstruction,
point/posterior/predictive distinctions and failure retention. A scientific owner
must specify the local estimand, continuity restrictions and the uncertainty
claim. An integration owner must preserve data, formula and prediction identities.
A runtime owner must resolve dependencies, retain notices, fix sampler settings
and define diagnostic failures. An evaluation owner must predeclare generated
mechanisms, public-data uses and appropriate recovery and refusal criteria.
DiD, synthetic control, IV and longitudinal uses remain separate design decisions.
\citep{exec14_causalpy_decision}

Costs should include environment resolution, model and prior review, sampling
time, failed fits, diagnostic inspection, conversion maintenance and comparison
against a simpler alternative. These costs can be measured on public or generated
cases before committing to bank experiments. The evidence supports a provisional product choice and specifies the tests needed before integration. Integration
remains disabled until executed upstream behavior supports the proposed contract;
the exact examples in this section establish mathematical distinctions, not a
working external adapter.~\citep{exec14_causalpy_decision}
\label{exec14:causalpy-contract-end}

\section{Python instruments and discontinuities: align the null, cutoff and returned set}
\label{sec:exec25-python-iv-rd}

A lending rule based on a credit-score cutoff and an encouragement that
changes take-up pose different causal questions. The first asks about a
local discontinuity under a continuity design; the second asks which
structural or instrument-induced contrast is identified. Their software
can share regression machinery without sharing a target or an uncertainty
claim. This comparison extends the retained linearmodels and pyfixest
kernel reviews with CausalPy IV/RD, rdrobust, rdd and ivmodels sources.
Every recommendation is conditional on the specified design and artifact.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice,exec22_pyiv_kernels_linearmodels,
exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest}

\subsection{Preserve the IV target before improving computation}
The earlier linearmodels review distinguishes design and instrument rank
checks from rank of the projected design, instrument strength and exclusion.
It also distinguishes heteroskedastic covariance from a declared cluster or
calendar-dependence model. The retained pyfixest review follows weighted
two-stage regression, response-scale residuals and diagnostic distributions.
A first-stage relevance test or an overidentification test does not become
a coefficient-null confidence-set inversion because all are called IV
diagnostics. Those distinctions remain prerequisites for a bank adapter.
\citep{exec22_pyiv_kernels_linearmodels,exec22_pyiv_kernels_pyfixest,
exec22_pyiv_reference}

The newly inspected pyfixest delegate solves normal equations using a
selected NumPy or SciPy route. Least-squares and LSQR branches extract
the solution component, while the SciPy dense route declares a positive
matrix assumption. Thus a returned coefficient vector does not preserve
every available rank or iterative-termination diagnostic. The Rust
collinearity delegate uses an ordered Cholesky-style residual-diagonal
test on a Gram matrix. Column ordering and the absolute tolerance affect
which columns survive.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_pyfixest}

For an exact scaling example, a one-column Gram entry $10^{-8}$ lies below
a tolerance $10^{-6}$. Multiplying that column by 100 changes the entry to
$10^{-4}$, which exceeds the same tolerance although the algebraic rank
has not changed. Qualification must preserve units, ordering, removed
columns and tolerance. The inspected Rust error text mentions a positive
finite tolerance, but its local guard tests only whether the tolerance is
nonpositive. Nonfinite inputs therefore need an explicit boundary test and
complete caller review; this observation does not establish an observed
package failure.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_pyfixest,exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The same source header says a reimplementation of fixest code has written
permission for MIT redistribution and names a permissions document. That
document is absent from the retained complete tree at this pin. The source
assertion remains recorded, but authentic permission bytes are not supplied
by repeating the assertion. File-level provenance is unresolved, without
a legal conclusion. The proposal keeps the numerical component as a
reference and defers new distribution or porting until the artifact-specific
record and native numerical contract are established.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_pyfixest,exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\subsection{CausalPy's Bayesian IV route adds a model and prior contract}
The inherited CausalPy 1.0.0rc1 revision constructs separate outcome and
instrument formula matrices, computes deterministic OLS and two-stage
reference fits, and can use those fits to center default priors. Its IV
constructor does not sample; explicit build and fit steps construct the
model and sample the posterior. The inspected continuous-treatment backend
uses a joint multivariate-normal likelihood with an LKJ covariance
construction. Its binary-treatment branch uses a logistic latent variable,
a Bernoulli treatment likelihood and a conditional normal outcome model
with a clipped coupling parameter. These are additional modeling choices,
not a model-free repair for weak or invalid instruments.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_causalpy}

A bank request must identify the coefficient associated with the
instrumented treatment, the structural interpretation, instrument and
outcome populations, row identities, priors and sampling diagnostics.
Formula construction and treatment-name checks cannot prove exclusion,
monotonicity, truthful encouragement or a stable target population.
Data-centered priors should be distinguished from externally fixed priors
when interpreting sensitivity and uncertainty. Variable selection is
another model operation requiring its own contract, rather than evidence
that automatically discovered instruments are valid.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_causalpy,exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The proposed use is a version-pinned model-based reference and conditional
Python worker. Public tests should check formula alignment, transformed
treatments, repeated fitting, finite inputs, weak first-stage scenarios,
prior sensitivity and posterior diagnostics before native admission.
None of those tests can manufacture the banking facts that make an
instrument credible.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\subsection{A finite cutoff prediction is not automatically the limiting jump}
The selected CausalPy RD implementation fits a sharp-design regression
after optional bandwidth and donut-hole filtering. It predicts at the
threshold minus and plus a user-supplied epsilon, with treatment flags zero
and one, and subtracts the two conditional expectations. Its input checks
require a dummy-coded treatment column and a formula treatment term;
the inspected checks do not establish that the observed assignments match
the declared cutoff direction. The adapter must validate that relationship
and preserve the actual prediction rows.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_causalpy}

Let a local mean below the cutoff have slope 2 and the mean above it have
slope 6, with a limiting jump of 10. Comparing symmetric points at distance
$\epsilon$ gives $10+8\epsilon$. The contrast is 14 at $\epsilon=1/2$ and
10.008 at $\epsilon=0.001$. These authored values show a target difference,
not numerical error in a package. The units and transformation of a credit
score affect the meaning of epsilon. A donut design adds extrapolation
across an excluded region; its target and assumptions must remain explicit.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The small rdd 0.0.3 package provides a different contract. Its helper can
truncate data around a cutoff and another function creates a treatment
indicator and a statsmodels weighted-least-squares model. The default
formula includes the treatment indicator and the running variable with a
common slope; the function returns the model before fitting. It does not
automatically perform the separate truncation step or turn the default
formula into a slope-interacted local polynomial. Users can supply a
different equation and weights, but those choices and the fitted covariance
must be recorded.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_rdd}

For the proposal, rdd is a compact instructional/reference route. Direct
adoption for a bank RD workflow is deferred until a wrapper fixes window,
kernel, formula, fitted state, covariance and diagnostics. Its MIT notice
does not qualify the complete statsmodels environment, and a coefficient
labeled as an effect still needs the design's causal assumptions.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_rdd,exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\subsection{rdrobust keeps bias correction and its uncertainty in separate rows}
The pinned rdrobust Python source declares version 2.1.1 and GPL-3 terms.
Its interface distinguishes sharp and fuzzy designs, polynomial and bias
orders, derivative targets, kernels, bandwidths, covariates, weights and
variance options. The inspected guards require the bias order to exceed
the estimation order, reject negative or all-zero weights, validate a
finite interior cutoff and require enough distinct running-variable values
for the polynomial. The treatment-side partition places values at the
cutoff on the right. Repeated-score handling and numerical refusal are
useful operational checks, not proofs of the continuity design.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_rdrobust}

The returned rows pair conventional and bias-corrected estimates with
different standard errors. Specifically, the selected construction uses
the conventional estimate with the conventional standard error, the
bias-corrected estimate with that conventional standard error, and the
bias-corrected estimate with its robust standard error. A consumer must
preserve the row identity; extracting the corrected coefficient from one
row and a standard error from another changes the reported test.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_rdrobust}

Suppose an authored example has conventional estimate 10, corrected
estimate 8, conventional standard error 2 and robust standard error 3.
The three reported ratios are 5, 4 and $8/3$. With a stipulated normal
multiplier 1.96, the corresponding interval half-widths are 3.92, 3.92
and 5.88. No coverage claim follows from this arithmetic. It provides a
deterministic serialization oracle that prevents a bridge from silently
mixing rows.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

For fuzzy designs the inspected formulas divide an outcome discontinuity
by a treatment discontinuity and propagate the ratio through bias and
variance calculations. A guard rejects a fully degenerate treatment
variable with no variation and no jump. That guard does not establish a
strong first stage. An outcome jump of two divided by treatment jumps
0.2 and 0.02 yields 10 and 100 respectively. A narrow conventional interval
cannot be assumed reliable merely because division returned a finite
number. Weak-first-stage behavior and the intended local population need
separate scientific evaluation.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_rdrobust,
exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The proposal favors a supported rdrobust Python worker as a reference
candidate for declared local-polynomial tasks, conditional on exact artifact
identity, complete bandwidth/covariance delegates, license and dependency
review, and matched native public fixtures. It does not port the method
solely to avoid process isolation, nor claim general serial-dependence or
weak-instrument coverage from an option label.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\subsection{ivmodels makes confidence-set shape part of the API}
The selected ivmodels 0.10.0 source offers coefficient-null Anderson--Rubin
tests and inversions. Its inspected path residualizes controls, projects
null residuals onto instruments, constructs a statistic and selects an
explicit chi-square, F or supported subvector critical-value route.
The inversion forms a quadratic inequality and projects it onto requested
coefficient coordinates. This is distinct from the earlier relevance and
overidentification diagnostics. No general covariance-robust guarantee is
inferred from the method name or source comments.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_ivmodels}

The confidence-set class represents unions of intervals, including empty,
bounded and unbounded sets. That representation is substantively important.
The authored inequality $1-\beta^2\leq0$ accepts
$(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)$ and excludes zero. Replacing it by the
interval spanning its extremes would add excluded parameter values.
A banking result schema must represent disconnected, empty and unbounded
sets, together with the null, nuisance treatment, critical-value option,
degrees of freedom and numerical tolerance.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_ivmodels,
exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The numerical boundaries still require work. The inspected projection
delegate uses SciPy least squares while the quadratic constructor obtains
its center with a direct linear solve. Singular or nearly singular
quadratic boundaries, numerical rank, root search and complete caller
validation need explicit fixtures. An algebraic acceptance region can
exist when a particular center parameterization is singular. Native
qualification must distinguish a scientific empty set from a numerical
failure. The proposed role is a weak-IV inference reference and conditional
worker integration preserving set geometry, not automatic admission of
all instrument laws or dependence regimes.~\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_ivmodels,
exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\subsection{Public qualification before costly banking experiments}
The public package should test exact cutoff inclusion, assignment direction,
epsilon and score scaling, donut filtering, formula slopes, duplicate rows,
finite weights, rank tolerance, solver status, weak first stages, robust-row
serialization and disconnected confidence sets. It should distinguish
simulation under a declared valid law from deliberate violations of
exclusion, continuity or dependence assumptions. Costs include refusals,
failed requests and human review, not only returned estimates.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

A hypothetical 18,000 USD total over 600 attempts, 450 conformant results
and 300 useful results gives 30, 40 and 60 USD respectively. These are
planning denominators, not observed prices or banking value. Repository
search tails, nonlinear IV alternatives, complete dependence and
bandwidth delegates, artifact rights and native evidence remain open.
The comparison of Python instruments and discontinuities remains incomplete.
The packages have not been validated by execution in this comparison.
\citep{exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

\section[Java and JVM IV/RD contracts]{Java and the JVM: separating a latent coefficient, a numerical primitive and an instrumental-effect service}
\label{sec:exec25-java-iv-rd}

A bank that already operates Java services might prefer to estimate a lending-policy effect in the same runtime. That preference does not determine the scientific target. An encouragement to contact a customer, a credit-score eligibility threshold and a latent repayment-propensity measurement model ask different questions. The first requires an instrument and a stated population of affected customers; the second requires a cutoff, assignment direction and local comparison; the third gives coefficients meanings only after its latent scale and measurement restrictions are fixed. The JVM comparison therefore begins with the actual callable calculation and its information contract, rather than inferring a common capability from the names regression, causal forest or two-stage least squares. The earlier four-original Tetrad inspection remains part of this argument. The present followup resolves selected concrete data and numerical delegates and screens complementary candidates; it does not establish ecosystem saturation.~\citep{exec22_java_latent_iv_preflight,exec25_javaiv_choice}

\subsection{Tetrad: follow the observation axis and the parameter lifecycle}

At the retained Tetrad revision, \texttt{Tsls.estimate()} constructs a new \texttt{SemIm}, calls its coefficient procedure and returns that object. The default fixed measurement is chosen by lexicographic name order unless an explicit loading list is supplied. A loading normalized to one defines a coordinate for the latent variable. To see the consequence without making a package-performance claim, write an authored structural relation as $Y=2L+\varepsilon$. Under the coordinate $L^*=4L$, the same relation is $Y=\tfrac12 L^*+\varepsilon$. The numeric coefficient changes while the represented relation does not. A banking adapter must carry the fixed indicator, units and scaling with the coefficient; a renamed or rescaled indicator must not silently turn a measurement normalization into a new economic claim.~\citep{exec22_java_latent_iv_preflight,exec25_javaiv_tetrad,exec25_javaiv_choice}

The earlier source finding concerns a specific measurement-stage loop, not every Tetrad estimator. Its intended vector is the fixed indicator's column, but it calls \texttt{getDouble(colIndex,i)} while iterating over cases. The concrete \texttt{BoxDataSet} forwards its first argument as row and second as column to its data box. \texttt{DoubleDataBox} indexes a row-major array accordingly; \texttt{VerticalDoubleDataBox} uses column-major storage while retaining the same public row/column contract. Thus storage orientation does not repair the call. An exact authored $5\times3$ table $D_{ij}=100i+j$, with zero-based indices and fixed column one, has the intended vector $(1,101,201,301,401)$. Swapping indices first reads $(100,101,102)$ and then requests columns three and four, outside this table. This is a source-level reachability condition and an independently reproduced indexing witness. No installed Tetrad invocation was performed. A useful native qualification must reach this branch on nonsquare data, preserve case identities, and distinguish a repaired call from a wrapper that simply avoids the branch.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_tetrad,exec25_javaiv_choice}

Parameter lifecycle is a separate issue. \texttt{SemIm} initializes fixed and free mappings before the coefficient procedure runs. Its initialization selects either configured starting values or random values, depending on the parameter flag. The inspected \texttt{Tsls} procedure skips a measurement when no auxiliary measurement remains. It also restricts work when a node name is supplied. Consequently, a returned object needs a per-parameter fitted mask: untouched initial values must not be represented as estimated coefficients. The separate \texttt{SemEstimator} path attaches a covariance matrix, invokes its optimizer and sets an estimated flag; those actions cannot be assumed for the distinct \texttt{Tsls} path. The latter's local asymptotic covariance helper still has no outward assignment in the inspected method. The broader \texttt{SemIm} class does have standard-error methods, including a missing-sample-covariance guard. This distinction prevents the overly broad conclusion that the entire library has no uncertainty interface. Caller search in the selected estimator, optimizer and latent-search files did not establish an internal invocation of \texttt{Tsls}. Its constructors and estimate method are declared public inside a package-private class. Those member declarations do not establish an externally accessible, supported API. A supported caller path and whole-repository caller coverage remain open.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_tetrad,exec22_java_latent_iv_preflight}

\subsection{The matrix inverse is a boundary, not a statistical guarantee}

The pinned Tetrad build declares EJML 0.44.0. Its matrix wrapper checks that an input is square and treats the empty matrix specially, then calls \texttt{CommonOps\_DDRM.invert} without preserving the returned boolean. The corresponding authentic EJML implementation documents a stronger caution than an ordinary success flag: completion can coexist with bad results for singular or nearly singular inputs. For dimensions up to five it uses an unrolled inverse (with a scalar reciprocal for dimension one); larger matrices use an LU path. The selected LU decomposition returns completion after its loop, while a separate singularity predicate exists. The inspected path does not turn that predicate into a general conditioning guarantee.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_tetrad,exec25_javaiv_ejml}

For example, the authored matrix
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\hbox{has}\quad \det(A)=0.
\]
Finite input elements cannot make its inverse exist. The inspected unrolled formula divides cofactors by a determinant; this observation motivates an explicit nonfinite-output and residual test, without claiming a native run or importing a new numerical theorem. The proposed adapter should report the selected solver route, dimensions, finite-output checks, rank/conditioning diagnostics under a declared scale, and an inversion or projection residual. It should refuse a scientifically unsupported coefficient even when a numerical routine returns a value. Conversely, changing a direct inverse to a pseudoinverse changes the numerical selection and may change the estimated target in a rank-deficient model; that change requires a declared estimand and comparison, not merely a passing arithmetic test.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_ejml,exec25_javaiv_choice}

\subsection{A real repository can contain a proposed estimator}

Kausaali provides an additional source comparison. This Scala 3 repository contains causal-forest and generalized-forest code. At the inspected September 2026 revision, its file named \texttt{InstrumentalForest.scala} explicitly contains an unimplemented placeholder; its three instrumental-forest tests are pending. The public generalized-forest factory inspected here exposes causal and regression constructors. The placeholder describes proposed observation, moment, guard, leaf and weighted-estimator extensions. These comments are evidence of intended development, not evidence that the instrumental estimator works or that the cited scholarly guarantees have been inherited. The source build declares Scala 3.3.5 and Apache-2.0 metadata. A README badge and Maven coordinates do not authenticate a released binary or prove that a binary contains the proposed estimator.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_kausaali}

The intended weighted-Wald extension raises a substantive contract issue even before implementation. In an authored two-component example, let numerator terms be two and four, and denominator terms one and four, with equal component weights. Averaging the two local ratios gives $\tfrac12(2+1)=1.5$, whereas dividing the summed numerator by the summed denominator gives $6/5=1.2$. Neither operation can silently replace the other. A proposed forest adapter must specify the moment being solved and retain the kernel weights and weak-denominator state. Conditional exclusion, relevance and the population interpretation remain additional scientific requirements. A placeholder provides a development lead; it supplies no IV or RD admission.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_kausaali,exec25_javaiv_choice}

ScalaTion and the retained SuanShu mirror provide different comparison points. The inspected ScalaTion regression trains an ordinary least-squares model using a selected factorization, defaulting to a QR/LQ route and providing other branches. The source build declares Scala 3.8.3; its root license and the README-linked HTML license retain distinct copyright notices, so both belong in distribution provenance. The SuanShu mirror's inspected OLS constructor passes its weighted design and response into an OLS solver and then constructs residual and diagnostic objects. Its Maven metadata identifies the historical 20120606 artifact family, while the retained license text and source notice have their own dates and wording. These bounded originals establish comparison inputs, not current commercial support, a general external-instrument estimator, or a bandwidth-aware RD procedure. Their complete solver delegates, artifact equivalence and dependency rights remain unqualified.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_scalation,exec25_javaiv_suan}

\subsection{An executable proposal decision for a Java banking service}

The proposal should reject substitution of these inspected paths for a generic IV/RD service on their names alone. Tetrad remains a conditional latent-measurement reference, pending the axis, initialization, scale and numerical qualifications. Kausaali's instrumental placeholder is a development candidate to revisit after an implemented, tested revision. The selected ScalaTion and SuanShu OLS paths are numerical or predictive comparison candidates; composing them into a causal estimator would be new project work requiring its own identification and uncertainty argument. For a Java application needing a presently specified IV/RD target, the previously reviewed R or Python implementations can instead be considered as versioned worker candidates, subject to their own unresolved scientific, artifact and operational gates. A process boundary itself supplies neither scientific equivalence nor license clearance.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_choice,exec25_pyrdiv_choice}

The public qualification plan begins with known-law generated tables and fixed task definitions. It includes nonsquare shape and missing-value cases, explicit latent normalization, skipped-parameter masks, singular and near-singular designs, zero projected norms, weak first stages and tests of the requested aggregation operator. A proposed CLI or MCP request must carry case and variable identities, instrument or cutoff role, observation clock, target population, estimator version and uncertainty contract. Its response should separate completed, unsupported, weakly identified, numerically failed and unavailable requests. A shape test can qualify a data boundary; it cannot verify exclusion or a bank's assignment process. Real operator and bank acceptance remain later evidence gates.

Costs should retain all requests. A hypothetical 12,000 USD public trial with 400 submitted requests costs 30 USD per request; 300 completed calculations give 40 USD per completion, and 200 scientifically accepted outputs give 60 USD per accepted output. These are exact authored denominators, not measured vendor prices or return on investment. Repository search covers only the saved queries and returned pages; Maven discovery, remaining candidates, whole-library callers and native qualification remain open. Other methodological comparisons can proceed independently. The packages have not been validated by execution in this comparison.~\citep{exec25_javaiv_choice}


The comparisons support a common decision rule without making the engines
interchangeable. Choose the intervention and affected population first, then
preserve the identifying design, numerical exclusions and actual uncertainty
object. A latent loading, a local receipt effect and the value of changing
eligibility can each be useful, but one cannot stand in for another. The next
chapter draws together the reporting and diagnostic implications before the
argument turns to channels, doses and sensitivity to assumptions.
