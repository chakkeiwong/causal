1.3     Timing determines what a decision can use
Information available after a customer accepts an offer cannot ordinarily justify a decision
made before acceptance. The distinction is easy to miss when every field appears in one table.
An observed call duration, for example, belongs to a completed call. It cannot be used as
though it were known when deciding whether to make that call. Chapter 86 examines the
roles and limitations of public financial data in more detail.

   Repeated servicing decisions make the problem more demanding. A contact today may
change both tomorrow’s repayment and tomorrow’s information. Comparing contacted and
uncontacted borrowers without accounting for that history can confuse a response to financial
distress with a cause of it. A policy must therefore specify what the bank observes when it
acts. The later longitudinal analysis compares such rules, including cases in which relevant
borrower information is hidden.


1.4     Uncertainty has an economic consequence
A confidence interval describes sampling uncertainty under stated assumptions. It does not
establish those assumptions. A set of effects compatible with several causal models answers a
different question: how much ambiguity remains even if the observed relationships were known.
Both can affect the decision. Their difference is developed formally in the identification and
inference chapters; it should also remain visible in a management report.
  If the plausible values all favour the same action, greater precision may be worth little.
When the preferred action changes across plausible values, better information may be valuable.

Commercial Bayesian-network systems already address substantial parts of the workflow that
a new causal product would have to build. The relevant comparison includes interactive model
construction, structure and parameter learning, background restrictions, model exchange and
programmatic inference. The proposal should specify which of these services it would reuse
and which scientific contract it would add. A common command-line or MCP interface alone
would not establish an advantage over an existing modeling environment.
  The following comparison uses inspected vendor technical documentation. The GeNIe
manual declares version 5.0.R2, built on 23 November 2024; the SMILE programmer’s manual
declares version 2.2.4.R1, built on 27 April 2024. The public URLs were retrieved after the 7
October 2026 literature cutoff, so those headers identify the declared manuals without proving
their exact historical bytes at the cutoff. The inspected BayesiaLab pages are mutable and
do not consistently pin the technical discussion to a numbered release. Their findings are
consequently dated documentation observations. No vendor executable, commercial trial or
paid service was run for this comparison (BayesFusion, LLC, 2024g,j; Bayesia, 2026d).
   GeNIe exposes both a PC route and a Bayesian Search route. The PC manual documents
a default significance level of 0.05, maximum adjacency of eight, and no time limit by
default. It allows continuous data under a multivariate Gaussian assumption and returns a
pattern for further inspection. The manual’s suggested histogram and scatterplot checks are
useful exploratory diagnostics; they cannot establish the full joint distribution or the causal
assumptions. Its Bayesian Search discussion instead describes score-guided hill climbing with
restarts, a BDeu-related sample-size parameter and a structural prior. A seed of zero uses
the processor clock, so it is not a fixed reproducibility seed. These are different procedures to
configure and compare, even though they share a graphical application (BayesFusion, LLC,
2024f,d).
   The pattern editor is a particularly important capability. Its documentation explicitly
discusses an equivalence class and permits inspection of unresolved arcs. It also documents a
Create DAG command that randomly chooses directions for unresolved arcs while producing an
acyclic graph. That command can be useful for constructing a representative Bayesian network,
but the choice does not add observational evidence for the selected directions. The proposed
product must preserve the pre-conversion pattern and label any selected representative. It
should not describe GeNIe as necessarily forcing a single graph: the documented pattern
workflow is itself a useful alternative (BayesFusion, LLC, 2024e).
   Consider the funding law in Chapter 34. Its three Gaussian models share the same covariance
but imply effects of either zero or 0.48 thousand currency units per percentage point. A proposed
interface that keeps only a randomly chosen representative could report either zero or 120
currency units per customer for the 25 basis point action. The difference would come from the
extra orientation choice. This is a project counterexample to a lossy integration design; it is
not an observed GeNIe estimate or a claim that the vendor’s documented interface conceals
that choice. A matched trial should inspect the pattern, the conversion and the returned effect
separately (Causal banking research project, 2026g).
