Chapter 90

Calibrating funding inference and
accounting for its information cost

Increasing a study’s size is useful only when its additional information addresses the error that
matters. This chapter derives the shift induced by a fixed independent pilot, contrasts it with
a joint fixed-regression construction, and identifies the assumptions behind a finite covariance
horizon. Fresh comparisons retain the failures of both routes; the final implementation study
asks which memory gains apply to which procedure. The cost of new history and audit
information therefore enters alongside numerical efficiency.

90.4     What the fresh comparisons support
The first fresh comparison uses 600 requests in each of seven laws. Its joint-regression horizon
interval covers 550/600 at 720 dates, with Monte Carlo interval [.8916, .9375]. This is a retained
calibration failure. At 1200 and 4800 dates coverage is .935 and .94; the persistent ρ = .96
case gives .9467. Changing the covariance does not remove all finite-regression error. In the
weak-audit law only 127/600 requests yield intervals, of which 121 cover; conditional coverage
is about .953, but all-request coverage is .202. Reporting only the conditional fraction would
hide the information shortfall. Omitting the quadratic action-mean term gives .2633 coverage;
correlating the action innovation with the structural disturbance gives zero. These are false
scientific declarations that a numerical API cannot detect.
   The exact pilot calculation suggests another route: retain an independent pilot but let
M = 4T . This satisfies the selected sufficient rate and avoids claiming that the small joint-
regression failure has been repaired. A separately frozen fresh study compares the inherited
pilot, a fourfold pilot with Bartlett covariance and a fourfold pilot with horizon covariance.
For the latter, the fitted score converges in mean square to the true-nuisance score, so each of
its fixed-H lag moments converges to the moment in (90.4). The vanishing pilot remainder
supplies the required centering at the desired target. An imperfect pilot held fixed need not
have zero score covariance beyond H; the growing-pilot argument is asymptotic. The last

the same-draw inherited baseline, the coverage changes are 4/600, 0/600 and 1/600; descriptive

90.5     Fitting and covariance without retaining all scores
The joint-regression route permits a stronger memory construction than merely reducing the
fitting matrix. Form each stratum’s block of [x, A, Y ], take its QR factor and combine the
small retained triangular factor with the next block. The final factor supplies the action
projection, full regression coefficients and residual-action sum of squares. A second pass forms
the monetary score directly. It need not materialize the horizon score matrix or retain all T
scalar scores.
   For lag k, store the raw cross-product sum Rk , total score S, count T , and first and last H
scores. If ψ̄ = S/T , the centered lag sum is

                         Rk − ψ̄{2S − Sfirst,k − Slast,k } + (T − k)ψ̄ 2 .

Keeping the final H scores of one block supplies its cross-boundary pairs with the next. Inde-
pendent dense SVD and direct centered lag calculations agree for awkward block sizes, including
blocks shorter than H. The working state is bounded by block size, fixed dictionary/outcome
width and H; raw input storage and resident mapped pages remain real costs.
   Complete fresh-process requests at 120,000 and 1,200,000 dates include imports, immutable-
input checks, fitting, prediction, inference, serialization and read-back. At the larger size,
chunked peak memory is .434–.435 of dense memory, and elapsed time is .831–.841 of dense
time. Maximum relative output differences are below 6.0 10−15 . At the smaller size time ratios
straddle one. These measurements concern the joint-regression procedure whose small-sample
calibration failure remains visible. The growing-pilot implementation still stores its arrays;
it does not inherit these memory gains. Neither procedure has demonstrated bank-platform
service levels or analyst labor savings.


90.6     From a qualified request to an institutional decision
The public request names the procedure, financial target, covariance rule, audit counts and
scientific declarations. Its identity binds evaluation and pilot bytes as well as those choices.
Two local consumer examples preserve the status, qualifications, monetary units and interval
through serialization. Unavailable results withhold monetary estimates and intervals rather
than becoming a confident zero. Interrupted unfinished fits can be retried from immutable
inputs; no partial-fit checkpoint or distributed recovery is implied.
   Independent review must reconstruct the nuisance-bias and filtration arguments and chal-
lenge the retained small-sample failure. Bank validation requires authentic assignment, source-
clock and audit evidence, an independently invariant pilot when that route is used, and
a decision-relevant precision target. Consumer maintainers must accept the real interface;
platform operators must measure complete workloads under intended storage and faults.
Workflow evaluation must price pilot/audit acquisition, active labor and rework at matched
output quality. Correct calculations and useful local memory ratios leave these institutional
requirements to be tested with the relevant owners and operating conditions. The comparison
supports a conditional procedure choice rather than a universal repair: growing the independent
pilot addresses its selected remainder, joint regression changes how the nuisance is fitted, and
streaming changes storage for the procedure actually implemented. The following precision
study asks how these choices behave when useful financial width, small audits and complete
workflow cost are considered together.

Chapter 91

Pilot information, calibration
precision and complete streaming
inference

The funding desk’s question remains the value of a one-month price intervention over a
fixed balance-response horizon. Chapter 90 shows why a fixed imperfect pilot can move the
target of a residual regression. Increasing pilot information and reducing working memory
address different obstacles. More independent history can reduce nuisance error, but does not
create identity truth or remove finite-sample covariance error. Streaming makes a specified
calculation feasible with less working storage, but leaves its statistical assumptions intact.
This chapter gives an equivalent streaming construction and compares fixed procedures with
enough repetitions to resolve larger calibration shortfalls. The banking consequences include
the extra history required, the financial width retained, and the cases in which inference
remains unavailable.

91.4      Information and adoption boundaries
The extra pilot must be independent of evaluation under an invariant relevant law. A bank
cannot replace that condition with arbitrary historical volume; regime changes or overlapping
histories require a different separation and influence argument. Likewise, more nuisance history
cannot create representative identity truth. Near-singular audit information can still require
refusal, and false scientific declarations can yield precise but invalid financial answers. Earlier
omitted-feature and endogenous-map counterexamples remain relevant.
   The selected implementation supplies a bounded scientific operation. It does not identify a
general nonlinear private-state model, retain the true class in arbitrary latent-graph discovery,
or establish rates for an arbitrary learner. Independent review, authentic bank assignment and
source clocks, audit truth, target storage and concurrency, adopted interfaces and measured
analyst costs remain distinct inputs to product qualification. These distinctions determine
which evidence the funding desk can act on and which claims still need testing. The comparisons
separate three investments: pilot history addresses the specified nuisance error, representative
audits inform the assignment inverse, and streaming reduces working storage for the same
calculation. None substitutes for the others. The next chapter replaces a near-rank refusal
with a projected uncertainty set and asks whether its financial width is useful.

      The regular funding interval cannot support inference when a representative identity audit
      cannot distinguish two candidate portfolio assignments. An alternative reporting design
      uses simultaneous bands for the four observable candidate monetary responses, exact
      binomial audit intervals, and projection through the assignment inverse without dividing
      by a nearly zero estimated rank. In 3,000 fresh paired synthetic requests, the resulting
      set has high all-request coverage but markedly worse financial precision. At the near-rank
      design, 546 of 600 sets equal the whole real line. Exact audit inversion does not make the
      response bands finite-sample exact, verify causal exogeneity, or provide a bank decision
      threshold. These findings concern constructed funding studies; their usefulness for a bank
      depends on the validity of the assumptions and the precision it needs.
