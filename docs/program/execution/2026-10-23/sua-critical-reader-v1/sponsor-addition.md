# A stability score cannot certify a causal answer

A genuine 24-page preprint proposes a combined sensitivity and entropy score
for abstention and training. Its perturbation diagnostic is relevant to
business-question translation, but its general risk guarantees do not survive
explicit mathematical checks. The source's expected sensitivity is below its
worst-case sensitivity; replacing the latter in an upper bound requires
additional justification. Its entropy subtraction and conditional-selection
steps also fail in admissible examples. \citep{exec23_sua_original}

A constant fair binary model has self-risk one-half, while the source's claimed
bound can equal one-half minus log two, a negative number. A separate bounded
constant-loss example contradicts the earlier bound. Selecting only a stable
but hard tenth of inputs gives conditional self-risk one-half against an
unconditional one-twentieth. These are authored mathematical counterexamples,
not model trials. Equal entropy and output stability can also coexist with
true classification error nine-tenths. \citep{exec23_sua_counterexamples}

certification as stated. Reported empirical tables remain unverified until
model, data, split, sample and run identities are supplied. The original is
retained with adverse findings; no allegation of fabricated experiments or
