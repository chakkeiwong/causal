Chapter 1

What a public experiment can tell a
bank

A useful prototype should take an intervention question through to a decision that an economist
can defend. Public data permits that exercise before a bank commits staff time, customer
records and money to a pilot. Its value depends on which question it answers. A public
experiment can test whether an analysis preserves the population, intervention and uncertainty.
It cannot establish that the same intervention works for a bank’s customers.
   The National Supported Work demonstration provides a concrete example. In the retained
experimental subset, the estimated increase in 1978 earnings is about $1,794, with an ap-
proximate 95% interval from $479 to $3,109. These are the archive’s earnings units, without
conversion to today’s purchasing power. The result supports a positive average contrast under
the stated design assumptions. It does not establish that the gain exceeds an illustrative
$1,000 cost. That distinction is exactly the distinction a banking proposal must preserve when
discussing a profitable offer or servicing intervention.


1.1    The population is part of the question
The author archive supplies separate files for 185 treated and 260 control observations with
earnings in 1974, 1975 and 1978. The original paper explains that the subset is drawn from
male experimental participants for whom 1974 earnings can be recovered. Selection uses pre-
intervention information, including assignment month and employment history. The authors
explicitly condition the retained experiment’s interpretation on the original randomisation
being independent of those pre-intervention covariates. (Dehejia and Wahba, 1999; Dehejia,
2026)
   The distinction matters. These 445 people are neither the full demonstration nor a sample of
bank customers. The public files do not contain the original assignment logs or a complete site-
by-month randomisation history. We retain the paper’s selection argument as a substantive
premise. We do not infer it from balance tests. Treatment assignment, rather than an
independently identified effect of treatment receipt, defines the contrast. Consistency, absence
of interference and preservation of the observed cohort are additional premises.
  For participant i, let Yi (1) and Yi (0) denote 1978 earnings under assignment to the pro-
gramme and control. The finite-population target is

1.3       Precision, influence and the spending threshold
About 30.8% of observed 1978 earnings are zero; the largest is about $60,308. The largest
leverage is 0.346 for the interacted fit. Its HC3 standard error is about 717, compared with 695
under HC2. Deleting one observation at a time moves the unadjusted point estimate between
about 1,501 and 1,929. This sensitivity is reported without selecting a preferred sample or
substituting a trimmed estimand.

1.4     What software agreement establishes
The same rows and specifications were fitted independently with NumPy least squares and
R 4.1.2’s stats::lm. R uses its QR-based fitting route; the HC2 covariance was constructed
explicitly rather than silently using a default homoskedastic standard error. Point estimates,
variances and standard errors agree to within 10−6 in absolute units. The unadjusted covariance
also agrees with direct group-variance arithmetic, and the interacted coefficient agrees with
separate-arm standardisation. (R Core Team, 2021)
   This result supports reusing existing linear-model machinery for this specified calculation.
It does not establish agreement with every causal package, validate an R adapter, or qualify
missingness, survey weights, dependent records or other models. The proposal’s integration
should preserve the choice of target and covariance explicitly. A language boundary is a
manageable engineering problem; an unrecorded change of statistical meaning can invalidate
the economic conclusion.
  The fitted estimates and covariance summaries were then passed through the existing library,
command-line interface and local MCP service. Each returned the same interval. In this
construction the numerator is the estimated effect, the denominator is the fixed value one, and
the supplied covariance is diag(nVb , 0). The service’s Gaussian residual inversion consequently
reduces to the stated normal interval. This is an executed path from public data to fitted
summaries to consistent inference and a local decision report. Raw-data estimation and report
generation are still local scripts, not newly implemented service endpoints.
   Marking the assignment premise contradicted produces a refusal. Keeping it unverified
preserves conditional inference rather than certifying truth. These behaviours make the worked
example useful to an economist: uncertainty and missing evidence survive the change of
interface. They do not qualify a bank platform or a sibling repository.
Chapter 2

Repeated decisions and customers
who disappear

A servicing policy changes what happens next. A reminder, payment holiday or collection
action affects both the customer’s state and the information the bank subsequently observes.
Estimating a policy from customers who remain in the records can therefore answer a different
question from the one the bank faces. The relevant value averages over the initial eligible
population, including customers whose later outcomes are missing.
   An explicitly generated study makes this distinction measurable. With a correct observation
model, weighting recovers the chosen policy’s value on average, but repeated actions and
dropout leave little effective information. When dropout depends on an unobserved future
shock, the same calculation becomes biased and its nominal 95% interval covers the numerical
reference in only 80% of replications. Analysing complete observed trajectories also overstates
value. These are findings under the stated constructed law, not estimates of a bank’s servicing
policy.

2.3     A usable interval requires enough information
For the fixed threshold 0.5 policy, the reference value is 20.250 with Monte Carlo standard
error 0.030. Never acting has value 19.493; their paired reference difference is 0.756 with
Monte Carlo standard error 0.014. In the baseline, weighting has estimated bias -0.035 with
Monte Carlo standard error 0.142. The complete-case calculation overstates value by 1.915
(Monte Carlo standard error 0.083). This distortion comes from selection, not from a language
or interface difference.
  Baseline weighted coverage is 0.950, with a Monte Carlo standard error of 0.015 and a
Wilson interval approximately [0.910, 0.973]. This is compatible with the nominal rate, not a
precise calibration certificate. Average effective sample sizes fall from about 320 at the first
reward to 97 and 29 at the later rewards. The average interval width is 8.047, far larger
than the policy improvement of 0.756. A positive lower bound for the paired improvement
appears in only 3.5% of baseline replications. Correct weighting therefore leaves an information
problem even in this favourable construction.

2.4     What the next prototype must learn
The bank needs to know why a customer’s record ends, what was available at each action, and
whether all relevant actions had a reasonable chance of being taken. More complete outcome
follow-up or an audit sample can address missing information. More representative action
variation can improve support. A better state model can help prediction, but it cannot by
itself recover outcomes selected by an unobserved future shock.
   The software implication is concrete. Preserve the initial denominator, reward-specific
weights, information dates and policy-selection split; show effective information and sensitivity
alongside the effect. A commercial or open-source implementation should be compared on
these same meanings. A successfully executed policy-value calculation does not settle the
scientific conditions that make it useful. Here the next experiment should test those conditions
before seeking a costly bank pilot.
Chapter 3

A narrower policy interval can be
more confidently wrong

The servicing example leaves a natural question. If a fitted transition model uses the observed
histories more efficiently than weighting, can it give an informative interval for the gain from
intervention? The answer depends on why outcomes are missing and whether the state equation
is adequate. Resampling quantifies variation in an estimated model; it does not establish the
model’s economic assumptions.

policy on 2,000 training customers and evaluates that fixed choice on 1,000 independent
customers. Each of 199 bootstrap draws resamples whole evaluation customers, including their
observation histories, and refits both transition coefficients and innovation variance.

3.2                                     Precision under a maintained assumption
For the separately selected policy, the baseline interval for improvement has empirical coverage
94%, with Monte Carlo standard error 2.4 percentage points. Its average width is 0.711 reward
units. The matched weighting interval has coverage 98% and width 6.905. The difference in
width is descriptive evidence of what the parametric restrictions buy; the sample of replications
does not establish exact calibration or general superiority. Three bootstrap coverage decisions
lie near the separate reference’s numerical uncertainty band.
   A positive lower bound for improvement occurs in 79% of baseline fitted analyses and 2%
of the weighted analyses. This is a decision frequency, not by itself a measure of safety. The
selected list includes an unprofitable threshold in seven of the 100 training samples. Evaluation
must preserve the selected target and the possibility of a poor selection.

Chapter 4

When a bank in the data ceases to
be the same bank

A funding study follows institutions through time. Its economic interpretation requires the
deposit outcome and the exposure to refer to the same institution. A fixed effect removes a
persistent level difference; it cannot repair an outcome assigned to the wrong institution. This
distinction matters before a bank spends money on a more elaborate covariance estimator.


4.1     A controlled comparison
The generated study follows 60 institutions for 20 dates. Exposure follows xi,t = 0.5xi,t−1 + vi,t ,
starting from zero, and the outcome is

                             yi,t = αi + 0.2 sin(t/3) + 0.4xi,t + ui,t .

The institution effects, exposure innovations and outcome disturbances are independent
standard normal draws. The target slope is therefore 0.4 in this specified economy. It is
an illustrative response, not an estimated bank elasticity. One alternative adds a common
innovation with an institution-specific loading. Another circularly reassigns the outcomes of
five institutions after the tenth date while leaving their exposures unchanged. The latter is an
artificial record error, not an authentic merger history.
   There are 300 independent panels in each of the three cases. We compare a pooled
regression with a regression that removes institution and date means. For the latter, the
uncertainty calculations use observation-level HC2, institution-clustered covariance, or the
sum of institution and date cluster covariances less the observation intersection. Reported
intervals use the normal critical value 1.96. These are finite experiments with 20 dates, not a
general justification for that reference distribution.


4.2     What changed the conclusion
In clean panels, the pooled HC2 interval covers the target in 82.7% of requests. Within-
institution and date adjustment gives 94.7% with HC2 and 94.3% with institution clustering.
Monte Carlo standard errors for these coverage fractions are about 1.3–2.2 percentage points.
Removing a nuisance level and repairing a sampling calculation are different operations.
   With reassigned outcomes, the within estimate has bias −0.0170, with Monte Carlo standard
error 0.00169. Coverage is 90.7% for HC2, 93.7% for institution clustering and 91.3% for

two-way clustering. The evaluator’s correct mapping gives bias 0.00102 and institution-cluster
coverage 95.0%. The point is the remaining bias: a wider interval can sometimes cover a target

  Under heterogeneous common shocks, the three within-regression coverage fractions are
96.3%, 96.0% and 94.0%. This construction does not show that two-way clustering is universally
preferable. One two-way variance is nonpositive and is reported unavailable; it is included as a
noncoverage in the denominator of 300 requests. It is never clipped to manufacture an interval.


4.3     The information worth obtaining
An institution-history audit has a different value from another standard-error option. A
supplied warning identifies all 300 deliberately corrupted panels and prompts refusal; this says
nothing about a system’s ability to discover an unknown break. The next useful evidence
would join exposures, outcomes and institution histories using externally checked identifiers,
then test whether the estimated response survives the corrected economic unit.
   The calculation was checked against an explicit regression with institution and date indicator
variables. Coefficients and all three covariance formulas agree to better than 10−10 on the
independent small-panel check. This supports the reported arithmetic. It does not validate an
unobserved bank’s history, guarantee finite-panel coverage, or establish a preferred estimator
outside the specified cases.

5.1     What matching can and cannot establish
The simplest analysis accepts a unique exact name-and-date match. A fuzzy alternative
permits a one-character name difference at the same date and uses date candidates when
the name is missing. Both abstain when a candidate is ambiguous or the same outcome is
proposed for several people. Neither uses the true identity or the intervention outcome to
choose a match.
   With outcome-dependent corruption at 30%, exact matching retains 65.6% of people and
assigns a wrong outcome to 1.56% of the original population. Its effect estimate has bias
−0.560 (Monte Carlo standard error 0.071); its normal interval covers the population effect in
93.5% of replications. The fuzzy rule retains 62.8%, with wrong matches for 0.477% of the
initial population. Its bias is −0.071 (Monte Carlo standard error 0.079) and coverage is 94.5%.

6.2                                     An evaluation should test the proposed repair
This calculation gives a reason to measure errors against an independent reference rather
than to count agreement between prompts. A validation sample could compare a structured
dictionary, a single extraction, repeated extractions and retrieval-assisted extraction using the
same permitted notes and decision date. Each method should record omitted information,
timing violations and error dependence across cases. An outcome observed after the action
must not quietly enter the pretreatment measurement.

at an acceptable cost. Lower text-classification error alone is insufficient if the remaining
error tracks the action or outcome. Conversely, the shared-error limit suggests a concrete
remedy: obtain an independent measurement or better source information, rather than simply
requesting more readings of the same incomplete account. A second model is not automatically
an independent measurement. Independence requires evidence.

competing diagrams, but the relevant evidence must concern the bank’s assignment and
information process. A formal method can evaluate an intervention under a supplied graph; it
cannot establish the graph’s missing arrows from fluent prose. The next prototype comparison
should therefore count consequential formulation and graph errors, alongside time spent
correcting them. It should keep a questionnaire plus ordinary search as an alternative and
preserve the formal method’s unsupported conclusions.

no live model benefit, does not close the unread literature census and supplies no universal
measurement-error correction. Its practical contribution is to identify which additional
information could make repeated assistance useful, and which repetition cannot supply.
Chapter 7

What a causal reasoning test
measures

A banking analyst asks several different questions of an assistant. Can it translate a request for
higher deposits into an intervention and a measurable target? Can it distinguish information
known before an offer from information created by the offer? Can it explain which arrows in a
diagram are assumptions, and recognise when several diagrams fit the same observations? A
correct answer to a narrative puzzle is useful evidence about some of these abilities, but it
does not establish all of them.


7.1     Explanations deserve their own test
Wang and Shen examine three constructed scenarios: coin operations, interrupted file downloads
and a hypothetical vaccination calculation. Four variants of each scenario and three prompting
approaches give 36 prompt conditions. Their scoring distinguishes a correct result and
explanation, a flawed explanation, and an incorrect result. This is valuable because a correct
number can accompany a mistaken economic argument. The study reports mixed changes
from examples and guided reasoning, rather than a uniformly helpful prompting intervention.
(Wang and Shen, 2024)
   The terms used in a benchmark also need scrutiny. The paper calls reasoning from an
observed effect to a cause actual causality. That task description does not, by itself, specify a
formal criterion for an individual’s actual cause. Its smallpox case asks which source produces
more deaths in a stipulated population. With the given numbers, vaccination reactions
produce 990,000(0.01)(0.01) = 99 deaths, while disease among unvaccinated people produces
10,000(0.02)(0.20) = 40. Those population counts differ from the per-person risks, 0.0001
and 0.004. Confusing a total with a risk is an estimand error; the arithmetic alone does not
establish a general Simpson-reversal mechanism. These are deductions from the paper’s stated
example, not new medical evidence.
   The banking analogue is direct. A large customer group can contribute more defaults
while offering a smaller default risk per account. A deposit offer can increase gross balances
without increasing net contribution. An assistant should state the denominator and economic
target before presenting the answer. The paper’s prompts provide useful stress cases for that
distinction. The small scenario set and model configurations do not support a current ranking
of assistants for banking work. We have inspected the published methods and results; we have
not reproduced its model calls or inspected every supplementary response.

7.2     Scale does not remove uncertainty about the answer key
MDC-Bench broadens the comparison to 8,422 questions across seven disciplines, including
business, economics and management. Its tasks distinguish reasoning, intervention, decision
and counterfactual questions. Questions are constructed from causal narratives extracted from
preprints, with model-generated alternatives and a manual sampling check. The published
protocol identifies model versions, prompt templates, temperature and output limits. (Wang
et al., 2026, Sections 2–3 and Appendix A)
   Its harder condition requires two ranked answers instead of one. For example, the reported
Claude thinking configuration has average accuracy 84.56% in the basic condition and 59.25% in
the harder condition. These are different response requirements on the same benchmark; their
difference is not a measured deterioration in one fixed banking task. The benchmark is useful
for comparing errors by causal structure and question type, provided its answer-generation
assumptions remain visible.
   The detailed prompts reveal an important distinction. Reverse reasoning may use necessity
and sufficiency, or otherwise select an abductive explanation judged probable from background
knowledge. Intervention questions construct an altered causal narrative; the answer alternatives
are ranked by their judged plausibility. These procedures do not supply a known structural
equation system, a randomised treatment effect or an identified posterior over causal diagrams.
Appendix A’s sampling review helps detect poor questions but does not turn model-generated
causal statements into independent economic truth. The authors also discuss weak long-chain
relations and distractors that may be too easy. (Wang et al., 2026, Limitations; Tables 28–29
and 39–40)


7.3     An agent architecture still needs an identified effect
Chen combines language models, diffusion models and interacting agents in a study of climate-
policy sentiment. Section 6 describes 43,943 tweets and comparisons with topic, language
and diffusion baselines. It reports F1, AUC, causal-chain precision, counterfactual error and
simulated intervention outcomes. Table 4 removes individual components to examine their
contribution. These are reported results of the paper; the model was not reproduced locally.
(Chen, 2025, Sections 5.6 and 6; Tables 1–4)
   The causal interpretation needs more than those comparisons. Equation (16) factorises a
joint distribution according to a graph. Equation (17) defines a conditional treatment contrast.
Neither statement alone identifies that contrast from the observed tweets. Equation (19)
compares predicted and reference counterfactual outcomes, but its accompanying text allows
the reference to come from annotations or simulated baselines. Section 6.1 does not supply
enough detail to reconstruct an independently identified counterfactual for each tweet. F1 and
AUC assess labelled prediction; they do not repair that missing identification argument.
   The useful proposal lesson is to separate an architecture from the evidence for a decision. A
bank may test whether a language model helps extract a policy variable or whether a simulator
represents a declared mechanism. It still needs a defensible treatment assignment, observation
model and counterfactual comparison before attributing a financial gain to the policy. The
reported architecture is a research lead. Its stated improvements are not evidence that the
proposed banking service already delivers causal or commercial benefits.

7.4     A useful banking comparison
The proposal should use such benchmarks to diagnose formulation and reasoning failures, then
add tasks whose consequences can be checked independently. A compact questionnaire and an
executable calculation are necessary comparators. For a deposit question, all methods should
receive the same decision date, eligibility rule, alternative offer and contribution definition.
For servicing, all should see the same observed history and reasons for dropout. For a diagram,
an admissible set should remain admissible when the observations cannot orient an edge.
   The resulting assessment has distinct outcomes: whether the target is correct, whether a
proposed adjustment is justified, whether an unknown effect remains unknown, whether the
numerical calculation is correct, and how much analyst time the assistance saves. Independent
adjudication is needed for open-ended economic formulations. Generated cases with known
mechanisms can test specific errors before an expensive bank exercise. The public experimental,
servicing and linkage studies in this volume provide numerical cases of that kind, but no live
assistant has yet been evaluated on them.
  The inspected papers therefore sharpen the proposed experiment rather than establishing
a benefit from the proposed service. Repeated agreement among prompts is especially weak
evidence when their errors share a source, as the repeated-measurement calculation shows.
The useful next comparison measures consequential mistakes and time on held-out tasks, with
human adjudication and a fixed cost allowance.

8.1     Panel estimation is already a commercial capability
The official MATLAB documentation retrieved on 29 October 2026 describes one-way fixed-

  The documented treatment of missing identifiers is consequential: a record with an absent

the economic unit is known or that discarded observations are ignorable. The panel study in
Chapter 4 shows why that distinction matters.

possible way to obtain time effects or two-way covariance in the wider MATLAB estate. There
