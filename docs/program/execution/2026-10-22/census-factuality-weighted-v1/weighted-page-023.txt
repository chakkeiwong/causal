inference guarantees for one given w, while the former extends such guarantees to every λ ∈ Λ.
Relative to conventional coverage, the price of uniform coverage is (i) a higher error level from
using η̂1−β to infer heterogeneity and (ii) longer intervals to obtain coverage that is robust to
alternative weights λ ∈ Λ.

Interpretation. Intuitively, if λ ∈ Λ indexes the preferred weights of different readers, then
the robust CIw∗ provides valid coverage for each reader’s estimand τλ (θ). For example, suppose
the baseline CIw excludes zero, leading the researcher to reject the w-null of no average effect
H0,w : τw (θ) = 0 at significance level α. If zero remains excluded from the robust CIw∗ , then a
reader with alternative weights λ ∈ Λ can reject the λ-null of no average effect H0,λ : τλ (θ) = 0
at significance level α+β. In this sense, CIw∗ facilitates robust inference.

Additional Coverage Properties. In Appendix C.1, I show that CIw∗ also provides a form
of simultaneous coverage—but at a lower confidence level in the two-sided case. I compare this
to the uniform coverage in (20). In comparing the two coverage notions, I draw connections
to the literature on inference for partially identified parameters (Molinari, 2020). In Appendix
C.2, I derive an upper bound on the coverage rate of CIw∗ . The bound is strictly less than one
for any λ ∈ Λ under any degree of heterogeneity. This implies that CIw∗ has nontrivial coverage
for τλ (θ) across all values of λ ∈ Λ and θ.
