\section{Synth in R: a comparison reference with explicit preparation and solver contracts}
\label{sec:exec24-synth-reuse}

A regional banking intervention can leave only one treated portfolio and a
small donor pool. The selected R \texttt{Synth} implementation offers a
concrete way to choose donor weights, but a fitted synthetic trajectory is
useful only if the donor population, information clock and counterfactual
restrictions suit the decision. The appropriate proposal choice is to retain
this implementation as a comparison reference while deferring direct adoption
or a port until its preparation, solver and inference contracts have been
qualified for a justified public prototype.
\citep{exec24_synth_choice}

The inspected CRAN-mirror revision declares version 1.1-10, package date
28 April 2026 and publication on 29 April. Its declared license is
GPL at least version two, and its imported packages are \texttt{kernlab},
\texttt{optimx} and \texttt{rgenoud}. No compilation requirement in the
package description does not imply that installed dependencies contain
no native code. These declarations identify the selected source; they do
not establish complete distribution rights, an installed binary or support.
The inherited official manual remains preserved with its original identity.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,v12_sw_synth}

\subsection{The donor fit and its causal interpretation}
Let $x_1$ be the treated portfolio's predictor vector, $X_0$ the matrix of
donor predictors, and $V$ a nonnegative diagonal predictor-weight matrix.
For fixed $V$, the intended donor problem is
\[
 \min_w (x_1-X_0w)'V(x_1-X_0w),
 \qquad w_j\geq0,\quad \mathbf{1}'w=1.
\]
The selected code constructs this constrained quadratic problem, then
chooses predictor weights using outcome fit over the supplied optimization
period. It requires one treated column, at least two donor columns and
compatible predictor and outcome dimensions. Predictor rows without donor
variation are rejected. Scaling uses predictor variance across the treated
unit together with donors, a transformation that a comparison must preserve.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,v12_sw_synth}

In an authored one-predictor example, donor values zero and two match a
treated value one at weights $(1/2,1/2)$. Post-intervention donor outcomes
of 80 and 120 thousand USD give a synthetic value 100. An observed treated
value 120 gives a gap 20 thousand USD. It is a causal effect only under
the stipulated stable counterfactual relationship, eligible untreated
donors and absence of relevant spillovers or other violations. If the
second donor is contaminated and becomes 160, the apparent gap becomes
zero. Choosing the second donor solely after observing the treated outcome
also makes the gap zero. Neither change demonstrates a failed optimizer;
both change the scientific comparison.
\citep{exec24_synth_choice}

The preparatory function validates supplied dates for type, duplicates
and membership in observed time values. Its inspected interface does not
carry an intervention date against which to enforce the information
boundary. The caller must therefore verify that predictor and
weight-selection periods satisfy the predeclared information clock.
The manual's description of pre-intervention matrices does not itself
enforce that condition on arbitrary supplied data.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,v12_sw_synth}

\subsection{Panel balance does not establish a common predictor definition}
The preparation path checks for one observation per selected unit-time
pair. It nevertheless allows individual predictor values to be missing,
reports their removal and rejects all-missing predictor histories.
Structural panel balance therefore does not establish a common measurement
window for every predictor. The result should expose retained periods and
counts by unit and predictor, alongside the requested aggregation operator.
\citep{exec24_synth_source}

A specific source discrepancy concerns ordinary predictor aggregation.
For the treated unit, the code dispatches the requested
\texttt{predictors.op}; for donors, the inspected path explicitly uses
the mean. With the requested median, treated history $(0,0,9)$ becomes
zero, while donor history $(0,0,12)$ becomes four under the donor mean.
Its median would be zero. The special-predictor helper, in contrast,
dispatches the specified operator on both sides. This conditional mismatch
is a static finding with an authored witness, not an observed native
result. Until repaired and qualified, a proposed adapter must restrict
the ordinary route to a matched mean contract or explicitly refuse the
unmet request. It must not silently advertise the caller's median option.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,exec24_synth_choice}

\subsection{Predictor normalization and solver status need separate checks}
Optimized predictor weights are normalized as absolute values divided by
their absolute sum. The supplied custom-weight branch instead divides
absolute values by the signed sum after checking type and length.
For $(-1,2)$ the two formulas yield $(1/3,2/3)$ and $(1,2)$ respectively.
For $(-2,-1)$ the custom formula produces negative diagonal values, and
for $(-1,1)$ the denominator vanishes. These examples establish why
the adapter contract needs finite, nonnegative custom values with a
strictly positive sum. They do not claim that the delegated solver
successfully returns for these invalid inputs. The one-predictor branch
sets the custom weight to one separately.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,exec24_synth_choice}

The outer optimization tries selected methods and starting values,
with local KKT and start tests disabled in the supplied options. Its
collector sorts candidate rows by objective value and selects the first
without a local convergence-code filter. The quadratic wrapper extracts
the delegated primal solution and computes losses; it does not expose a
complete status or KKT certificate in the returned object. These are
wrapper observations. They do not establish that \texttt{ipop} has no
internal checks, or that every selected solution is wrong. Full delegated
solver inspection and native numerical qualification remain explicit tasks.
\citep{exec24_synth_source}

Feasibility and optimality differ even in a small public oracle. For
$\min_{0\leq w\leq1}(w-1/2)^2$, candidate $w=3/4$ is feasible but has
loss $1/16$ and interior gradient $1/2$. A successful process return or
weight-sum check alone would not certify the optimum. A prototype should
retain objective values, constraint residuals, stationarity or an appropriate
numerical certificate, termination reason, iterations and requested
tolerances. The manual's argument prose says seven significant figures
while its usage and inspected function default specify five; resolved
defaults belong in the result rather than being inferred from one prose field.
\citep{exec24_synth_source,exec24_synth_choice}

\subsection{Reference choice, alternatives and public qualification}
Retaining this source as a reference preserves its recognizable constrained
donor construction and inspectable preparation path. Direct adoption is
deferred because no complete environment, solver qualification, dependency
rights or native failure study has been established. A whole-library port
is also deferred: it would require reproducing predictor transformations,
the nested objective, constraints and diagnostics, and no demonstrated
benefit yet justifies that duplication. An independent small quadratic
oracle is valuable for a restricted problem; it does not replace causal
identification or full uncertainty. Broader synthetic-control, augmentation
and factor alternatives need their own target and source reviews.
\citep{exec24_synth_choice}

The scientific role specifies the donor population, intervention clock,
spillover exclusions and uncertainty claim. The adapter role owns matched
operators, missingness, input identities and refusal. The platform and
rights role pins complete dependencies and distribution terms, while the
evaluation role preregisters public or generated oracles and full costs.
These roles do not invent bank owners or independent approval. A selected
manual's general mention of placebo inference cannot qualify every
permutation law, donor filter or postselection procedure.
\citep{exec24_synth_choice,v12_sw_synth}

Public qualification can begin with these exact examples and then
prespecified misspecification, missing predictors, donor contamination,
operator mismatches, custom-weight boundaries and solver termination cases.
All attempts, failures and refusals count. A stipulated total of
2,400 USD over 120 requests, 100 conformant outputs and 80 useful outputs
gives 20, 24 and 30 USD per respective denominator. These are planning
values, not package prices or measured bank returns. Unknown untreated
trajectories and valid donor exchangeability still need justified
restrictions or new information. The method is a reference candidate; execution, reuse rights and the validity of uncertainty estimates still require investigation.
\citep{exec24_synth_choice}
