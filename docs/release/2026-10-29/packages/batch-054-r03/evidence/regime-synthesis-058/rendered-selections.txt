Chapter 48

Longitudinal regimes, heterogeneous
effects and continuous doses

The detailed comparisons distinguish complete policy evaluation from components that estimate
heterogeneity or a dose curve. This chapter brings those choices back to a small set of servicing
and incentive requests. Its finite examples show why the same data field or output name
cannot determine which component the bank needs. The preceding source studies supply the
implementation limits; the task here is to preserve them when composing the workflow.


48.1      A sequence of decisions changes the software contract
Servicing involves repeated decisions. A customer may receive a reminder, a repayment plan
and later a restructuring offer, with delinquency and contact history changing between actions.
An operation for a single binary treatment does not become a longitudinal estimator by adding
a time column. The target must state which histories the policy observes, when actions occur,
what ends follow-up and how default or account closure changes the outcome.
   Let Lt be information available before action At , and let Ht = (L̄t , Āt−1 ) denote the observed
history. A deterministic regime d supplies At = dt (Ht ). For a terminal outcome Y , its value
is V (d) = E(Y d ). Under the required consistency, sequential exchangeability and positivity
conditions, a g-computation construction can integrate an outcome regression against the
covariate transition laws induced by the intervention. Schematically,

The notation suppresses censoring and competing events, which must be specified in an actual

  The software comparison should therefore examine covariate transitions, intervention call-
backs, history updates, horizon conventions, simulation error and sampling uncertainty. It
should also compare parametric g-computation with weighting and longitudinal targeted esti-
mation on the same policy and population. Disagreement over a default horizon or competing
event can dominate any difference in optimization speed.

48.2     pygformula exposes the history and event choices
The inspected ParametricGformula class accepts individual and time identifiers, time-varying
covariate names/types/models, custom histories, custom fit/prediction functions and inter-
vention descriptions. Its outcome options include survival, continuous end-of-follow-up and
binary end-of-follow-up. Separate arguments address competing events, whether to treat them
as censoring, and a censoring model used for natural-course comparisons. (pygformula project,
2026b)
   For servicing, default and account closure should not be casually interchanged. If default
prevents later repayment, a default-free survival probability and a cumulative incidence of
repayment are different targets. If closing an account merely hides future payment information,
a censoring argument is needed. Asking software to treat a competing event as censoring
changes the scientific experiment; the decision requires substantive justification and must
survive the library, CLI and agent interfaces.
   The class documentation sets the number of bootstrap samples to zero by default. The
selected fit path invokes separate bootstrap work when samples are requested. Thus the
presence of bootstrap support does not imply that a particular invocation produced sampling
intervals. The helper review in Section 47.20 establishes whole-subject history resampling
and documents the failed-replicate handling discrepancy. That source finding does not qualify
arbitrary calendar dependence or the fitted intervals. The adapter must retain the actual
sampling unit and complete failure counts. (pygformula project, 2026b)
   Simulation size and bootstrap size address different uncertainties. Conditional on fitted
transition laws, additional simulated trajectories reduce numerical integration error. They
do not provide new bank observations or reduce the uncertainty in estimated transition
parameters. A report should distinguish the number of observed independent sampling units,
simulated histories and bootstrap replications. Treating simulated histories as additional
observed customers would greatly overstate precision.
   The first useful adapter contract therefore preserves customer identity, event and availability
clocks, action timing, history definitions, horizon, competing events and intervention semantics.
It records whether uncertainty was requested and actually obtained. Natural-course reproduc-
tion is a diagnostic: matching factual outcomes does not prove that intervention predictions
are valid. Unsupported actions, missing positivity and impossible history combinations should
remain explicit, even if simulation can generate a numeric outcome.
   The inspected MIT license supports considering reuse. Adoption still needs the complete
simulation and bootstrap paths, environment and dependency terms, and matched comparisons
with R implementations and other longitudinal estimators. The retained source studies resolve
selected simulation and bootstrap questions; they do not replace those native qualification
requirements. Evaluating supplied regimes also does not establish that an optimal regime has
been learned. Policy selection and its evaluation are additional operations.

48.4      A generalized propensity score is a density component
CausalGPS’s inspected estimate_gps function accepts continuous exposure and covariates, a
formula, normal or kernel density mode and a SuperLearner library selection. In its normal

residuals. (CausalGPS project, 2026b)

  The observed-density function alone does not provide a dose-response estimator. To

parametric density is not evidence of practical overlap. Reporting density values together
with observed range, local support and model sensitivity is more informative than presenting
a successful fit as permission to extrapolate. The observed package declaration is GPL-3;
complete dependency and redistribution choices remain part of adoption review.

48.6     A common servicing and incentive benchmark
These components should be compared through a small set of declared banking queries. One
query fixes a two-period servicing regime and a terminal repayment outcome. Another fixes a
binary offer and asks for heterogeneity. A third fixes two continuous incentive levels and asks
for their net financial difference. Each query has its own sampling unit, support requirements,
feasible actions and reference functional. A tool may be appropriate for one and unsupported
for another; that is useful information for architecture.
   The comparison should retain a model that fits factual outcomes but fails an intervention
assumption, a weak-support population, a changed event definition and a changed treatment
unit. It should separately measure statistical error, simulation error, numerical failure and
computation. For longitudinal work, the complete cost includes fitting transitions and histo-
ries, simulating interventions, obtaining uncertainty and reporting diagnostics. Timing one
prediction call omits most of the workflow the bank would operate.
  An eventual selection can combine components: symbolic identification, a longitudinal
engine, a heterogeneous-effect estimator and a dose-response component can coexist behind
one semantic contract. The common contract must preserve their distinct assumptions and
outputs. It should make unsupported requests explicit rather than coercing every query into
the nearest available estimator.

Chapter 49

Longitudinal treatment, monitoring
and mediation

Policy choice also determines what the bank observes. Monitoring can change record availability,
and a recorded engagement measure may become a mediator rather than a baseline control.
The compact example here connects these roles to the detailed longitudinal and mediation
comparisons, so that an interface preserves the question before routing it to an R estimator.


49.1     The history is part of the intervention
The servicing problem is naturally sequential. An initial action A0 , such as an early contact,
can change a later financial condition L1 . A second action A1 is then chosen using that
condition, and a later account cost Y is observed. The variable L1 can be both a consequence
of the first action and a determinant of the second. Treating it as an ordinary baseline control
erases that temporal structure. Treating both actions as one binary indicator can erase the
policy being evaluated.
  Consider a finite project mechanism in which A0 , L1 , A1 are binary and

             Pr(L1 = 1 | do(A0 = 0)) = 0.8,               Pr(L1 = 1 | do(A0 = 1)) = 0.4.

Conditional mean cost, in CNY per account over a fixed horizon, is specified as

                          m(a0 , l, a1 ) = 100l − 50a1 l + 20a0 + 10a1 .

The initial action costs 20 and reduces the chance of the adverse condition. The later action
costs 10 and reduces adverse-condition cost by 50 when that condition is present. These
numbers define the example; they are not estimates of actual bank effects.

49.2      Point treatment and sequential software have different
          contracts
The selected tmle interface concerns binary point-treatment effects with missingness and
subject-ID options. It is a candidate for a suitable static effect question; that does not make
it an arbitrary longitudinal policy engine. The versioned documentation also contains a
treatment-missingness wording difference between DESCRIPTION and the selected Delta
argument. Section 43.17 resolves the selected implementation’s contract: missing treatment
and baseline covariates are rejected, while the no-variation route estimates an outcome mean.
Section 43.26 retains the additional interval and resampling restrictions. A convenient argument
name cannot extend those admitted meanings. (tmle contributors, 2025)
   stremr’s selected sequential g-computation and TMLE documentation is closer to the
servicing problem. It accepts time points, sequential outcome-model formulas, treatment
intervention probabilities, monitoring intervention probabilities and related weighting choices.
Treatment and monitoring are separate intervention components. A bank that increases
contact may also increase the chance that financial stress is recorded; holding monitoring fixed
and changing it are different policy questions. The interface should name which of these is
being evaluated. (stremr contributors, 2017)
   The same documentation makes several operational qualifications explicit. The docu-
mentation labels Qstratify nonfunctional and an error source, while the source review in
Section 47.19 finds a corresponding branch. That discrepancy remains a qualification question;
it is not evidence of failure in every native call. The proposed profile withholds that option
pending qualification. Weight truncation and stabilization are choices, and the reviewed
stopping loops can differ from the requested cap. A wrapper should report the effective weights,
actual updates, final residuals and warnings after execution. A truncated weight or stopped
numerical iteration is not evidence that positivity or statistical accuracy has been established.
It is part of the approximation and diagnostic record.
   survtmle addresses another closely related but distinct problem: right censoring, competing
failures and cumulative incidence. Its selected interface supports two treatment values. A
competing failure must not be recoded as ordinary censoring simply to fit a common input

49.3      Mediation changes the target again
A servicing team may want to decompose an action’s effect into a part operating through
customer engagement and a part operating through other channels. This is a different question
from the total effect of an adaptive policy. The mediator’s definition and observation time
matter, and a missing engagement record may itself be affected by contact intensity.
   medflex supplies a natural-effect-model interface based on expanded data. Its selected
documentation explains that the original mediator is excluded from the final natural-effect
formula and that a full bootstrap refits working models. Both details affect integration.
Expanded records must retain their link to the original account, and a bootstrap operating
on those records independently would not generally reproduce a bootstrap of the original
subjects and fitting procedure. Reusing a fixed first-stage fit would also change the documented
full-bootstrap operation. (medflex contributors, 2025)
   The proposed adapter should therefore export an expansion identity, the original sampling
unit, the effect scale, the mediator definition and the models refitted in each resample.
Section 46.3 traces the expansion, full refitting and original-subject covariance, including the
cross-derivative contribution. That review does not make expanded rows new independent
information. The adapter should not market data expansion as a solution to unmeasured
mediator–outcome confounding. Sensitivity summaries can accompany the decomposition, but
they remain conditional analyses of assumptions. A total-effect engine and a mediation engine
can both be useful without their outputs being interchangeable.


49.4      What an R boundary must preserve
An R process boundary is a plausible first integration route because it permits evaluation
of the existing estimator with its native dependencies. It is not yet a selected or qualified
deployment. The input should contain a stable account identifier, the information and action
clock, event/availability times, the observation convention, the intervention specification and
the target’s units. Serialization must distinguish absent values from absent records and preserve
factor levels, censoring codes and repeated-subject structure.

   The output needs more than an estimate. It must identify the exact target, weights and
truncations, nuisance-model choices, fitted sample, uncertainty procedure and unavailable
quantities. A bridge may fail before the estimator runs, during fitting, or while translating
a result. These failures require different remedies. The first remedy is an environment or
transport repair; the second may require numerical diagnostics or revised assumptions; the
third requires correction of the adapter. None is a causal conclusion about the underlying
servicing policy.
   Porting is an alternative only after the complete method boundary is clear. Reimplementing
a final regression while omitting its expansion, sequential weighting, model-refitting bootstrap
or stopping conditions would create a different procedure. The advantage of a native port
must be demonstrated on the full workflow, including data transfer, model fitting, uncertainty
and failure handling. A faster final matrix multiplication is not enough.
