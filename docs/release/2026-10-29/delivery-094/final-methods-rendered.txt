Chapter 97

Servicing policies with estimated
action and observation probabilities

A servicing team wants to compare an assistance rule with ordinary collection. Its historical
action probabilities may not have been retained, and some customers disappear from the
analytical records before their next repayment is measured. These are two estimation problems
only after a more basic scientific question has been answered: did the recorded history include
enough information to remove action and observation selection? A highly accurate model of
recorded actions cannot answer that question on its own.
  This chapter develops a restricted route with estimated probabilities, a policy selected
on separate training histories, and an explicit finite-sample confidence statement. It also
constructs two observationally indistinguishable worlds in which that route answers different
causal questions. The confidence construction and finite examples are project derivations.
The longitudinal treatment/observation identification principle is inherited from Hernán and
Robins; the importance of independent policy evaluation and cumulative, rather than current-
action, ratios is also explicit in Jiang and Li. Their results do not themselves prove the
composition below. (Hernán and Robins, 2020; Jiang and Li, 2016; Causal banking research
project, 2026ad,ae)

         tion
Fix a finite horizon T . An independently sampled customer has a complete potential history
with pre-action information Ht , binary assistance At , and subsequent net contribution Rt ,
for t = 0, . . . , T − 1. History contains every recorded past variable used by the rule or
the assignment mechanism, including earlier rewards when relevant. A finite table, or an
explicitly sufficient finite summary, indexes the probability models. Arbitrary discretization of
a continuous history does not establish sufficiency.

   The stipulated observation law has four substantive restrictions. First, consistency connects

97.6       Exact small references and the cost of conservatism
A two-decision example uses observed stress St ∈ {0, 1}, with initial probability 1/2. Historical
assistance has probability .3 + .4St . Next stress has probability .2 + .5St − .15At , retention
probability is .9−.1St −.1At , and dated contribution is 2−3St+1 −.25At . These are hypothetical
utility units; their coefficient signs express an assistance cost and an improvement in future
stress. Every path probability is rational. Retention uses a draw independent of the next
stress innovation given current state and action.
   No assistance, always assist, and assist only when stressed form a fixed finite policy
class. Exhaustive path sums under interventions are compared with a separate backward
value recursion. Summation over observed prefixes reproduces the same values with true
denominators. Perturbing action-one probabilities by +.04 and retention probabilities by −.03
verifies the fitted-law bias expression and its conservative envelope. A deliberately incorrect
current-action/retention shortcut is kept as a negative reference. The program enumerates
all paths; it draws no final evaluation sample and measures no empirical coverage. (Causal
banking research project, 2026ae)

   Even with Bt = 2, ce = .25, cg = .65, T = 2, n = 1000 and αE = .025, the evaluation radius
alone exceeds eight utility units. The entire stipulated value range is only eight units wide. A
mathematically valid bound can therefore be unsuitable for choosing assistance. Increasing
the nominal confidence level or adding more rare history cells makes this tradeoff harder. This
is a reason to seek shorter questions, stronger information, sharper variance-sensitive bounds
or a reviewed sequential regression construction; it is not a reason to remove the error budget.
   Sequential doubly robust scores offer a substantive alternative when continuation values
are useful. They require a separate analysis of fitted action/retention models, value-function
errors and sampling dependence. The known-logger variance reduction in Jiang and Li does
not automatically establish estimated-logger finite coverage. The earlier synthetic collection
results already show that mean protection and normal-interval reliability differ. (Jiang and Li,
2016)

97.7      Observational equivalence prevents a hidden-state repair
          by prediction
Consider a single decision with no censoring. In world E, action is a fair independent coin
and Y = A with probability .8, otherwise Y = 1 − A. The intervention values are VE (1) = .8,
VE (0) = .2. In world H, private hardship U is a fair coin, Y = U , and Pr(A = 1 | U ) = .2 + .6U .
Here VH (1) = VH (0) = .5.
  Both worlds have exactly the same observed joint probabilities:

                       Observed pair (A, Y ) (0, 0) (0, 1) (1, 0) (1, 1)
                       Either world                .4      .1     .1     .4


   Even the private-state assignment in world H has overlap: its probabilities are .2 and .8.
Infinite recorded data estimate Pr(A = 1) = .5 and E[Y | A = 1] = .8 perfectly in both worlds.
Weighting by that observed probability returns .8, while the hidden world’s value is .5. No
fitted logger, extra evaluation data or language-model interpretation of these same records can
distinguish the worlds. The effect is .6 in one and zero in the other.
   Observation selection has a separate obstruction. Suppose half the cohort is observed and
half of those observed have Y = 1. One full-data world assigns zero to every missing outcome;
another assigns one. Both yield the same observed records and retention probability .5, but
their cohort means are .25 and .75. Accurately estimating the retention probability does not
recover the unobserved outcome law. The complete-case mean .5 answers neither world’s
population question.
   An additional randomized action design, a truthful relevant-state audit, or a validated prox-
y/instrument model can distinguish some such worlds. Each adds information or restrictions.
The existing proximal and partial-observation treatments explain specific routes and their rank,
exclusion and policy-information requirements; they do not authorize arbitrary hidden-state
inference. (Bennett and Kallus, 2023; Tennenholtz et al., 2020)


97.8      What the proposal can recommend after this derivation
The preserved selected17 study used truthful representative state audits and supplied action
probabilities. It passed its selected valid cells, while wrong probabilities covered only one of
512 requests and policy leakage returned no finite outputs. The present finite-table route
makes estimated-probability uncertainty explicit after restricting to sufficient observed histories.
It removes no hidden-state requirement from that predecessor and claims no new repeated-
sampling qualification. (Causal banking research project, 2026af)
   The separate proposed continuous-state servicing experiment has Gaussian innovations and
a quadratic reward. Its rewards are unbounded and its history table is not finite. Consequently
it cannot use this bounded-table theorem as written. A prospectively declared bounded utility
would define a different target; alternatively, a valid tail/moment bound and a continuous-
model estimation argument are needed. Quietly capping realized losses after evaluating the
results would answer a changed question.
  For the proposal, the executable next comparison is a declared finite-state generated
demonstration with known evaluator truth, estimated training probabilities, independent
evaluation and adverse hidden/censoring cases. Its useful-width screen must be chosen in utility
units before final draws. A broad interval or correct refusal is an informative demonstration

justified dependence law, and an unknown optimal policy remains a separate research question.

98.1      The banking question and the observation law
A bank can observe a stable association between an offer and a subsequent outcome yet remain
unable to price the effect of changing the offer. The missing information can persist even
with an exactly known observational distribution. This chapter develops a restricted temporal
confidence construction and shows what an additional direct intervention would contribute.
The mathematical construction and exact calculations are project derivations, not a fitted
bank result. (Causal banking research project, 2026ah,aj)
   In the preserved temporal graph study, all valid cells covered and retained their declared
true graph in 512 of 512 requests. Weak relevance and unrestricted latent observation each
produced 512 whole-line sets. The persistent cell produced 507 finite sets and five whole-line
sets. Under false exclusion, the true graph was retained zero times despite numerical effect
coverage. These findings distinguish numerical coverage from informative effects and from a
valid causal restriction. (Causal banking research project, 2026ak)

Consider an encouragement to take a bank offer. The bank observes information X before
the encouragement Z, the eventual action A, and its subsequent value Y . Flexible prediction
is useful because accounts differ in their pre-existing circumstances. It also creates a second
estimation problem: removing predictable variation imperfectly changes the instrument moment.
When the residual response to the encouragement is small, that change can be large relative
to the information identifying the effect. A narrow interval computed after fitting a good
predictor need not describe the uncertainty in the bank’s causal decision.
   This chapter develops a restricted alternative. Learn on an independent pilot, obtain
an explicit bound on each prediction error, and carry that bound into a joint region for
two observable moments. Evaluate those moments on a bounded sequence with a known
dependence range. Project the region into the effect, retaining disconnected and unbounded
answers. The construction is a project derivation with exact numerical references, rather than
an implemented general learner or a new repeated-sampling qualification (Causal banking
research project, 2026c). Its restrictions are substantial; explaining them is part of deciding
whether the proposed product merits a larger experiment. After the derivation, a design
analysis asks whether the bound can support a decision at plausible information levels. The
final comparison considers geometric and hierarchical causal models as substantive alternatives:
they change the dependence restrictions or intervention target and require their own estimation
and uncertainty arguments.

99.10      The proposed next experiment and its limits
A useful bounded prototype would compare a small fixed dictionary, the certified sparse pilot,
and the independent calibration route on the same declared target. Its full resource record must
include feature preparation, training, tuning, certificate construction, calibration, dependent
evaluation, set projection and retained refusals. A faster optimizer is of little value if the
required pilot or confidence width makes the decision uneconomical.
   Before final draws, choose a bounded mechanism with an explicit dependence range and a
separate pilot, freeze the dictionary and approximation bounds, review the cone and numerical
certificates, and profile complete costs. Then freeze sample sizes, error allocations, adverse
cases, alternatives, fresh draws, stopping rule and useful-width criterion. The old failed
outcomes cannot become the new evaluation data. No final successor study is registered by
the argument in this chapter.
   The route is now explicit for this restricted family. General unbounded dependent learning,
unknown approximation error, unverified instruments, arbitrary learners and useful weak-IV
precision remain open. Where the certificate is too costly or too wide, narrowing the question
or changing the information design is a substantive product recommendation.

99.12      Structured causal models: restrictions, computation and
           the target
A universal bank observes accounts within branches, repeated decisions within customers and
exposures connected through markets or payment networks. The observations are neither
an arbitrary collection of independent rows nor automatically interchangeable. Two recent
primary contributions clarify different parts of this problem. Geometric causal models use
specified symmetries to organize dependence and identification. A hierarchical causal pipeline
translates distributional interventions into symbolic expressions and fitted numerical factors.
1816                                                                    Causal analysis for a universal bank


Both provide useful ingredients for a proposed product, but neither permits an agent to infer
valid causal meaning merely from a table schema or a successful program execution. (Weinstein
and Blei, 2026; Aiad et al., 2026)


99.12.1     A symmetry is a scientific restriction

In Weinstein and Blei’s geometric causal model, an endogenous variable xv is a structured
collection (xvω )ω∈Ω , where Ω indexes locations, times, nodes or other positions. A group G acts
on these collections. A causal mechanism f v is equivariant when applying a transformation
before evaluating the mechanism is equivalent to transforming its output:
                                                                   
                      f v (ϕ(xpa(v) ), ϕ(ϵv )) = ϕ f v (xpa(v) , ϵv ) ,       ϕ ∈ G.                (99.24)

Here pa(v) denotes the parents of variable v in a specified directed acyclic graph and ϵv
is exogenous noise. The model uses equivariant mechanisms to create dependence among
endogenous positions from the stipulated noise structure. A hard intervention replaces the
selected mechanism with a supplied value. Permutation, spatial-shift and array symmetries
express different models; they are not different software optimizations of one unrestricted
model. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)
   For a banking application, translation of a branch-level model across locations would
assert that the relevant mechanism survives that transformation after the declared context is
represented. A policy change, regulatory boundary or market break may make that assertion
implausible. The proposal should therefore ask which transformations preserve the decision
mechanism, what variables encode exceptions, and what evidence could falsify the proposed
symmetry. These are proposed elicitation questions. The paper assumes the causal graph
and symmetry group are known and leaves their discovery to future work. An LLM may help
formulate candidate restrictions and collect supporting documents; this paper supplies no
experiment establishing that an LLM can validate them. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)


99.12.2     Identification and consistency retain their assumptions

The paper separates learning an observational law from one structured realization, identifying
an intervention from that law, and estimating it from finitely observed coordinates. Its mixing-
action condition requires countably infinite index and transformation sets, with transformations
that eventually move any finite set away from itself. Under the stated model, ergodicity then
makes learning from an expanding observation domain possible. The do-calculus soundness
argument uses the graph’s Markov decomposition. For groups consisting of index transforma-
tions, the completeness argument embeds conventional unrestricted causal models into the
geometric class. Stronger symmetries can add restrictions: the orthogonal-group instrumental
variable example invokes linear-Gaussian structure, a nonconstant relevant instrument and
finite interference. These conclusions have different premises and should not be compressed
into a claim that symmetry identifies any latent graph. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)
   The finite-data consistency result is likewise conditional. It assumes finite interference, local
information under the transformations, an amenable group with a tempered Følner sequence,
finite state spaces and positivity of the required finite observational configurations. Informally,
averages over the permitted transformations estimate finite marginal laws; the identified
intervention functional is then evaluated on those estimates. The proof uses a finite set of
coordinates relevant to the queried outcome and a continuous-mapping step. The statement is
a consistency route under these conditions. It does not deliver a finite-bank error bound or a
generally valid confidence interval for arbitrary persistent maps, shared calendar shocks and
99.12. Structured causal models: restrictions, computation and the target                   1817


learned representations. Rate and central-limit conditions must be established for the actual
proposed estimator. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026)
   Two apparent slips in the selected paper’s extracted proof text also deserve a bounded
response. The example following its amenability assumption prints an eventually empty
intersection for a finite permutation subgroup translated by one of its own elements. Algebra
instead gives ϕGn = Gn , so |ϕGn ∩ Gn | = |Gn | and the required ratio is one. Enumerating the
six permutations of three objects reproduces that identity. Separately, the instrumental-variable
proof concludes that a covariance denominator is positive from relevance and nonzero instrument
variance. Relevance warrants nonzero, not positive, covariance. With first-stage coefficient
−2, instrument variance 4 and outcome coefficient 3, the covariance ratio is (−24)/(−8) = 3.
Both layout and raw text extraction preserve the printed passages. These local corrections
preserve the intended algebraic routes; they do not independently refute or fully verify the
theorems. Exact typeset-symbol inspection and the cited proof dependencies remain separate
review obligations. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026cl)
   The practical evidence has its own limits. The array example explicitly reports poor
absolute uncertainty calibration despite improvement relative to its comparators, with a
mean-field approximation suggested as a cause. In the genomic semisynthetic table, adding
reverse-complement equivariance changes the R-learner MSE from 1.637 to 2.509 in scenario
A, whereas in scenario B an outcome-model variant has MSE 0.084 compared with 0.104 for
the R-learner without that symmetry. These are author-reported experiments, with arithmetic
differences 0.872 and −0.020 respectively. They support the paper’s qualified discussion of
interactions among model restrictions, misspecification and estimation strategy, not a universal
ranking or a bank calibration result. (Weinstein and Blei, 2026; Causal banking proposal
project, 2026cl)

99.12.4     An auditable expression tree must preserve the evaluated functional

The hierarchical pipeline’s abstract syntax tree records products, marginalizations and fitted
density keys. This can expose which conditional models are required and which computations
can be evaluated separately. That is useful for a library/CLI/MCP interface: the declared
estimand, identified symbolic expression, selected numerical factors and resulting value can
remain separately inspectable. The paper reports parametric, mixture, categorical and
nonparametric fitting options and parallel evaluation. Those descriptions are primary paper
claims. The linked repository, dependency versions, factor fallbacks and implementation have
not been newly inspected or executed in this review. (Aiad et al., 2026)
   Crucially, the paper explicitly acknowledges that normalizing unstable multiplicative factors
changes the numerical functional. A constant representation change that cancels exactly is
different from a nonconstant alteration of relative weights. To see the latter in a transparent
construction, take weights p = (1/4, 3/4) and effects τ = (20, −4). Their weighted contrast is
USD 2. Multiplying the two weights by (2, 1) and renormalizing gives (2/5, 3/5), with contrast
USD 28/5 = 5.60. The nominal dependency graph did not change, but the functional did.
This constructed modification is not a reconstruction of the paper’s normalization code. It
shows why the exact transformation must be declared and either proved invariant or reported
as a different functional with its own uncertainty argument. (Aiad et al., 2026; Causal banking
proposal project, 2026cl)
   The STAR application makes the distinction concrete without providing a matched nor-
malization experiment. Its reported mathematics row has 2402.75 − 2365.95 = 36.80 points.
The normalized reading diagnostic has 1788.10 − 1766.47 = 21.63 points. These rows concern
different outcomes; their difference cannot isolate the effect of factor normalization. The
selected ExactBIC graph is described as plausible, with a substantive orientation and added
latent class context, rather than uniquely recovered. The paper also distinguishes student-level
OLS, an IV class-size coefficient and the distributional class intervention. A larger number
across these targets is not, by itself, evidence of a more accurate causal method. (Aiad et al.,
2026; Causal banking proposal project, 2026cl)
   The stated analysis uses 322 classes with ten sampled students per class, and identifies multi-
parent factor estimation as a bottleneck. Its conclusion leaves deeper hierarchies, uncertainty
for stabilized functionals and analysis across plausible graphs open. The reported 3.11-second
sequential and 1.9-second four-process evaluations imply a ratio of about 1.64, but this is a
check of the paper’s arithmetic, not a local timing result or a bank service-level claim. These
limits should remain part of the proposed product comparison. (Aiad et al., 2026; Causal
banking proposal project, 2026cl)


99.12.5     A scoped route for the proposal

The two contributions suggest an orderly prototype route. First specify the banking unit,
target, information clock and intervention law. Then justify a particular dependence restriction
or hierarchical representation, retain the assumed graph or plausible graph family, and derive
the identified functional. Preserve every numerical factor and any alteration introduced for
99.12. Structured causal models: restrictions, computation and the target                    1819


stability. Compare exact finite references before using public or synthetic data to study finite
behavior, uncertainty and cost. An LLM can propose candidates, explain assumptions and
help inspect traces, but each such role needs its own evaluation and refusal rules.
   For software reuse, a typed interface to a symbolic expression and its numerical factors
is worth investigating before porting an entire implementation. That investigation must
inspect the actual code, licenses, dependencies and supported regimes. For research, the
geometric restrictions provide possible scoped assumptions; they do not replace the unresolved
joint-law and calibration arguments in the broader banking program. Accordingly these papers
strengthen the set of explicit alternatives while leaving general discovery, arbitrary dependence
and automatically valid stabilized uncertainty as open obligations. Together these comparisons
locate the next investment: a better certificate, a justified structural restriction, or genuinely
new information. The economic chapters now ask whether such a change can improve a
banking decision enough to cover acquisition, computation and review, including the cost of
an unresolved answer.
