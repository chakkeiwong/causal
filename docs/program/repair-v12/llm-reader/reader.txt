Chapter 1

Translating a banking decision into a
causal question

1.1     A business sentence can contain several questions
“Should we increase the deposit offer?” sounds like a single question, but it leaves several
choices unresolved. The intended action could be a change in the quoted rate, the eligibility
rule, the message shown to a customer or the allocation of a fixed promotional budget. The
outcome could be a new deposit, retained balances, funding cost or total relationship value.
The population could be existing customers, applicants who received an offer or everyone
eligible under a proposed rule. Each choice defines a different causal problem.
    A language assistant can expose these alternatives and organize the evidence needed to
resolve them. It should not choose the easiest target to estimate and silently treat that target
as the owner’s decision. In the project funding case, the response of balances to a 25-basis-point
increase and the change in net funding cost over thirty days are retained as separate candidates.
Their business motivation is shared; their outcomes and decision consequences are not identical.
The unresolved choice is useful information, not a failure that must be hidden by returning a
number.
    The co-pilot roadmap discussed by Alaa and colleagues motivates this progression from a
scientific question through assumptions, statistical targets and interpretation. Their illustrative
medical examples attend to eligibility, baseline time, treatment and follow-up. The inspected
paper also reports that generic uncued answers can omit relevant design concerns. Applying
those principles to banking is a project proposal; the medical examples do not validate banking
clocks or interventions.[16]


1.2     A question as a collection of commitments
The project represents a question as

                                Q = (U, P, A, π, π0 , Y, H, ν, ℓ, I).                        (1.1)

Here U is the unit of analysis; P is the target population; A is the feasible action set; π and
π0 are the policy and comparator; Y is the outcome; H is the horizon; ν is the measure used
to average over the population; ℓ is the loss, including its units; and I specifies information
available at each action. This notation defines the project’s declaration contract. It is not an
identification theorem.
    The unit needs particular care. An account can be an observation while a customer is the
decision unit and a household is the unit within which actions interact. An assistant can ask
which role each identifier plays, but a column called customer ID does not certify ownership or

                                                 5
stable linkage. If several accounts share a budget or pricing rule, an account-level action may
also be an infeasible description of the intervention. The feasible action set should reflect what
the responsible institution can actually change.
    The comparator must be as explicit as the intervention. “No treatment” might mean no
new contact, continuation of the current contact policy, no rate increase, or a different offer
under a fixed budget. A dynamic policy can include future actions conditional on history, so
its comparator is also a history-dependent rule. An effect relative to one comparator cannot
be reused for another solely because the same treatment variable appears in the dataset.
    The outcome and loss should also remain separate. A change in balances has units of
currency held over a horizon; funding expense combines balances, rates and time. A larger
deposit balance can have a positive acquisition interpretation and an unfavorable net-cost
implication. If g(a) is a specified gross benefit and c(a) is a specified cost, the decision may
depend on E[g(a) − c(a)], not on E[g(a)] alone. The definitions and valuation assumptions
must be supplied before that subtraction is meaningful. The assistant should make missing
costs visible rather than invent prices.

excluded population, the new averaging rule and the implications for the decision.


1.4     Information is governed by an availability clock
The date on which an event occurred differs from the date on which an analyst or policy could
observe it. For a recorded variable V , write eV for event time and aV for availability time.

                                                6
The finite project fixtures require eV ≤ aV , and a policy acting at time t can use the value
only if aV ≤ t. A derived feature becomes available no earlier than its required inputs. These
rules prevent an apparently historical feature from leaking future information into a decision.
     In the servicing fixture, an account condition occurs at day ten but becomes available
at day eleven. A rule acting on day eleven may use it; a rule acting on day ten may not.
The distinction matters even when both values later appear in the same warehouse table. A
language assistant should request the recording and availability convention, rather than infer
it from a field name or a sorted timestamp.
     Advance announcements and forecasts require different variable definitions. A forecast
observed today of next month’s condition is a current forecast variable, not a measurement of a
future realized condition with an impossible availability time. Revised historical data similarly
require a vintage or revision convention. The project’s finite validator does not implement
every revision protocol. It checks the stated recorded-value contract and leaves more complex
clocks as explicit extensions.
     Equal timestamps do not determine direction. Two columns aggregated to the same day
may hide a finer action sequence or a common cause. The assistant can propose a finer clock
and identify the evidence needed to support it. It cannot establish temporal precedence by
placing one variable first in a diagram. When the granularity is insufficient, the ambiguity
should survive into the model class.


1.5     An available covariate is not automatically an adjustment
        variable
In a sequential servicing model, A0 changes an intermediate condition L1 , which affects both
a later action A1 and the outcome Y . The same L1 is a mediator for the first action and a
common cause of the second action–outcome relation. Its appropriate use depends on the
target. Including every available column in one regression does not preserve all possible total,
direct and policy effects.
    The project declaration therefore distinguishes a policy-history purpose from an ordinary
adjustment purpose. A simple directed-path diagnostic flags a post-treatment descendant
proposed as an ordinary total-effect control. It does not declare all post-treatment conditioning
invalid. Controlled direct effects and longitudinal procedures need their own question and
formal analysis. A warning from a declaration validator is not a replacement for an adjustment
criterion or an identification algorithm.
    Observation processes can alter the target in a similar way. Suppose only successfully
linked records enter an analysis and both action and repayment affect successful linkage. The
selection indicator is part of the causal problem, and an effect among linked records may differ
from an effect in the original population. A record-link score is an observed proxy; it is not
verified ownership. The observation fixture deliberately retains a latent ownership variable, a
selection node and an unresolved population choice.
    CausalDS provides a useful literature distinction here: its conceptual SCM variables and
its separate observation model are not interchangeable. The paper’s narrated synthetic scenes
can test how an agent handles that distinction, but they do not supply authentic banking
semantic or assignment truth.[24]


1.6     The handoff to formal analysis
The assistant’s useful output is a reviewable declaration and a record of what remains unresolved.
A selected declaration contains one explicit target, its population measure, the intervention
and comparator, clocks, feasible actions, outcome and loss. An ambiguous declaration retains

                                                7
candidate targets without covertly selecting one. A contradicted assumption cannot be left as
an inconsequential note while the calculation proceeds as if it held.
    The local project validator exercises these distinctions with four synthetic banking cases and
adverse contract tests. It checks declared identities, types, references and selected clock/graph
conditions. It does not authenticate an institution, certify a reviewer, determine whether a
causal assumption is true, or implement every MAG/PAG/CPDAG validity condition. A
declaration ready for formal analysis is ready for the next conditional operation, not a qualified
bank recommendation.
    This boundary also explains the limited scope of the current executable prototype. A
declaration can describe a dynamic regime while the selected operation supports only a
static binary population ATE. The correct handoff then reports unsupported target mapping.
Translating that request into a static regression would produce a result at the cost of changing
the user’s question. The appropriate remedy is to qualify a longitudinal operation or obtain
an explicit new target decision, with the original question preserved.

Chapter 2

Variables, diagrams and justified
disagreement

2.1     From vocabulary to measured variables
A useful causal diagram begins with variables whose meanings can survive contact with data.
A business glossary may contain “risk,” “relationship” and “engagement,” while a dataset
contains a risk-score vintage, a set of linked accounts and an observed click. Those are related
concepts, but they are not interchangeable causal variables. An assistant can propose mappings,
detect inconsistent names and assemble definitions. Each proposed mapping still needs an
operational meaning, a unit and an information clock.
    OntoLearner offers relevant modular ideas for ontology loading, typing, taxonomy and
relation-learning tasks. The inspected paper reports domain-dependent errors and retrieval
failures. Its relation-learning capability is not a guarantee that a relation is causal, or that a
concept has a valid measured proxy in the bank. Ontology alignment should therefore precede
and inform graph construction without being silently promoted into causal identification.[23]
    For example, “customer owns account” may describe a legal relation, “records share an
identifier” an administrative relation, and “records have a high linkage score” a model output.
If ownership affects both offer eligibility and repayment behavior, substituting the linkage score
for ownership leaves a measurement problem. Adding an arrow from the score to repayment
does not resolve it. The diagram should distinguish latent ownership, observed records, the
linkage procedure and sample selection.
    Likewise, an ordinal description such as “high influence” cannot be used as an effect size
without a scale. An assistant’s rating of a proposed edge may express a preference among
textual explanations. It is not automatically a probability that the edge is present, a posterior
over graphs, or a confidence level. A typed diagram can retain such a rating as explicitly
uncalibrated metadata while refusing to use it as scientific evidence.


2.2     What an edge asserts
An edge proposal should state more than two variable names. The endpoint marks identify the
proposed graphical object. A source and inspected anchor state what supports the proposal.
A scope describes the population, period and intervention to which that evidence applies. A
counterevidence record states whether contrary evidence was found, absent in the inspected
material, or not successfully searched. A review disposition records acceptance as a premise,
rejection, or unresolved status.
    These fields serve different purposes. A source can be authentic but inapplicable to the
current banking action. A study can concern a different population or a different definition of

                                                9
treatment. A plausible timing argument can constrain direction without establishing that any
causal connection exists. The assistant should make the supported proposition as narrow as
the evidence warrants and retain the reasons for disagreement.
    CausalChat supplies an example of interactive model development: the inspected paper
combines optional CPDAG discovery, model suggestions, graph editing and structural-equation
coefficient updates. Its prompts use ordinal causal-significance ratings and ask for possible
confounders and mediators. These are useful interaction ideas. The paper’s expert feedback
also raises missing citations and uncertainty about the quality of scientific evidence provided
by the model. A coefficient update after adding an edge cannot establish that the edge itself
is true.[18]
    The proposed banking workflow retains the interaction but changes the scientific handoff.
An accepted premise is labeled as a premise with a responsible review, not as a truth certified
by the model. A model proposal alone cannot remove an observationally compatible graph
alternative. The tool responsible for formal identification receives the complete reviewed graph
object and its restrictions, rather than a picture whose uncertainty has disappeared in editing.


2.3     Graph classes and collections of candidate models
A CPDAG or PAG is a structured representation with its own interpretation. A list of proposed
DAGs is another object. A collection of edges marked “possible” is not automatically an
enumeration of every combination of those edges. Confusing these representations can silently
exclude models or include incompatible ones.
    The project contract preserves a CPDAG or PAG directly when that is the appropriate
object, including circle endpoints. It also permits explicitly listed candidate models. An
edge marked possible within a candidate describes the evidence status of that candidate’s
edge; it does not make all optional edge combinations an exhaustive graph class. Downstream
calculations must know which interpretation applies.
    An exact binary example shows the consequence of retaining alternatives. In model F , a
binary offer A is Bernoulli with probability one half, and response Y equals A with probability
0.9. In model R, Y is Bernoulli with probability one half, and A equals Y with probability
0.9. Both models produce
                                             Y =0 Y =1
                                    A = 0 9/20         1/20 .
                                    A = 1 1/20         9/20
In F , setting A from zero to one changes E[Y ] by 0.8. In R, intervening on the equation for A
leaves Y unchanged. The two compatible constructions therefore imply the effect candidates
{0, 0.8}. A directional suggestion favoring F does not provide new identifying information
unless its underlying premise is independently justified. These finite calculations are project
constructions, not a calibrated confidence set for a learned graph.
    More generally, for a declared graph class G, one relevant downstream object is

    Equation (2.1) also clarifies what a disagreement review should resolve. If two experts

immaterial for the current decision while still mattering for another target. If their graphs

imply different effect signs or feasibility, the conflict is decision-relevant. The assistant can
identify that consequence and prioritize the required evidence. It should not average expert
preferences into an unexplained graph.


2.4     Retrieval failure and scientific disagreement
An evidence assistant can fail to find a passage because the document is unavailable, parsing
failed, the query was poor, the retriever missed it, or the passage is absent from the inspected
document. These cases support different statements. “No passage was retrieved” cannot by
itself become “the authors ignored the assumption,” and neither statement establishes that
the assumption is false in the population.
    The inspected ARGUS paper makes this distinction especially useful. It audits reported
evidence concerning DiD identifying assumptions and does not claim to adjudicate causal
truth. Its synthetic flaw study exhibits a retrieval/abstention tradeoff, and its small human-
label pilot reports an in-sample calibration change that was not tested on unseen papers.
These observations motivate a banking evidence state that is separate from a scientific risk
judgment.[27]
    The proposed record contains the source bytes, the query or retrieval configuration,
inspected anchors and the resulting narrow claim. If evidence is missing, it records the missing
step. If two sources conflict, it records their populations, dates and definitions before treating
the conflict as substantive. The analyst can then determine whether the sources concern
different regimes or whether a genuine contradiction remains.
    This process does not require pretending that every edge has a published study behind it.
Some causal premises depend on institutional facts: which system sets a rate, when a policy
becomes visible, how an audit was selected, or which identifier establishes ownership. Those
facts need an accountable owner and authentic records. Literature retrieval can help formulate
the questions, but it cannot supply an institution’s missing assignment truth.


2.5     A reviewable editing sequence
The proposed interaction starts with a question and variable definitions, then records diagram
alternatives. Each edit changes a declared object: add or remove a variable, refine a clock,
replace a proxy, propose an endpoint, or accept a restriction. The edit retains its predecessor
identity and reason. A reviewer can therefore see whether an apparent improvement in fit
came from a new assumption, a different target, or an actual correction of an error.
    Formal checks can reject a directed cycle in a DAG, impossible declared time order or an
explicitly forbidden direction. They can flag mediator, collider or common-ancestor patterns
in the supported representation. Such checks help maintain a coherent declaration but do not
cover every graph class or establish that an accepted edge is causally true. In particular, a
diagram with no syntax error can still omit an important common cause or misrepresent the
observation process.
    The user should be able to retain an unresolved diagram and ask for analyses conditional
on several alternatives. A refusal to pick one graph can be the scientifically correct output.
The later discovery and identification operations then need to report what changes across
those alternatives and what remains common. This makes uncertainty operational: it directs
the next information request rather than appearing only as a disclaimer after a single confident
answer.

Chapter 3

Language assistance, causal
discovery and uncertainty

3.1     The scientific object behind a graph proposal
A banking analyst may begin with a list of columns and a request to explain why balances move
after a change in deposit pricing. A language model can recognize the vocabulary, propose
a market-rate variable, distinguish a customer offer from a realized rate, and suggest that
customer characteristics affect both assignment and retention. These suggestions are useful
because a statistical algorithm does not recover the meaning of a column from its numerical
values. The scientific question nevertheless remains open. The proposed relation may describe
a mechanism in another population, a historical association, a direct effect, an indirect path, or
simply a familiar phrase. The role given to the suggestion determines the claim the resulting
graph can support.
    The inspected literature offers several roles rather than one method called “LLM causal
discovery.” Pairwise language judgments, breadth-first graph construction, retrieval-assisted
discovery, response-based independence queries, statistical prompting, soft graph priors, multi-
agent debate and numerical experiment design alter different parts of an analysis [5, 9, 11, 14,
10, 13, 15, 26]. They should be compared by what information they consume, what object
they change, and what uncertainty survives that change.
    Let D denote numerical observations, T the retrieved or supplied technical text, and
M the language-model mechanism, including its version, prompts and decoding policy. A
proposal is then a function or random output of (D, T, M ). It is not an additional independent
sample from the banking process. If the model proposes a graph G, the relevant questions
are whether the variables have the intended meaning, whether the graph restrictions are
scientifically warranted, whether the data support the inference procedure, and whether the
target effect is identified under those restrictions. These questions remain distinct even when
one conversational interface presents all of them together.
    The following analysis uses inspected primary methods and evaluations. Reported bench-
mark gains are the authors’ results; no external discovery package or language-model endpoint
is executed here. The finite examples and probability calculations below are project construc-
tions. Their role is to test the logic of proposed interfaces and interpretations, not to claim
bank validation.


3.2     What language-only discovery measures
The broad proof of concept of Kıcıman and colleagues motivates language support for several
causal tasks, including the elicitation of background knowledge and reasoning about causal

                                               12
relationships [5]. A model can answer a question such as whether an increase in a borrowing
rate affects demand without receiving a numerical sample. Such an answer may encode useful
inherited knowledge. It cannot reveal which of several observationally compatible assignment
mechanisms generated a particular bank’s records unless the prompt or the model’s knowledge
provides additional applicable information.
    Feng and colleagues investigate relation occurrences in accessible OLMo and BLOOM
pretraining corpora and report that relation frequency is associated with prediction success [17].
Their controlled additions of synthetic relations and the effects of reversed or negated statements
help separate recall from a claim of general discovery. The scope matters: their pretraining
investigation concerns accessible models and predominantly commonsense relationships. It is
evidence for a contamination concern and a test design, not proof that every model operates
only by memorization.
    A sensible banking evaluation therefore crosses two kinds of novelty. One can change the
variable names while holding the mechanism fixed, or change the mechanism while retaining
familiar labels. The first tests reliance on surface vocabulary. The second asks whether
a familiar story prevents the system from responding to contrary evidence. Neither test
should declare a new label intrinsically uninformative: operational definitions often carry
real information about intervention feasibility and time order. Removing all meaning from a
variable name changes the task as well as its contamination risk.
    Context must also be handled carefully. In the inspected reliability study, positive and
negative contexts alter affirmative response rates, with contexts generated by a model and
assessed by human annotators [17]. Some contextual changes legitimately change a causal
claim. A policy that affects demand in one regulatory regime may have no effect in another. A
robustness test should either hold the target world fixed or declare the changed world and its
new truth. Scoring every changed answer as instability would conflate semantic responsiveness
with error.
    Jiralerspong and colleagues use breadth-first organization to reduce graph construction
calls [9]. This offers an engineering alternative to asking about every pair separately. Call
complexity is not total resource cost. A call that lists many nodes may have a growing prompt
and response, require retries, or depend on a long context. A complete comparison must record
input and output tokens, wall time, cache policy, model cost, failure frequency and the review
time needed to adjudicate proposals. Rejecting a cycle is useful structural validation, but does
not make the remaining acyclic graph true.


3.3     Response tests are not sampling-law independence tests
Cohrs and colleagues investigate language-model judgments in a constraint-based discovery
workflow [14]. This is a different mechanism from applying a conditional-independence test to
observed bank records. For example, the statement X ⊥⊥ Y | Z is a claim about a distribution.
A model’s frequency of saying “YES” to that statement is a property of a prompted response
process. Even a correctly estimated response probability does not establish the distributional
statement.

response decision to a numerical zero or one at the independence-test interface. Those values
need a type that exposes their meaning. Passing them through an API named “pvalue” cannot
confer sampling-law calibration.
    In a funding example, repeated model agreement that customer risk is unrelated to pricing
conditional on branch does not validate the bank’s assignment law. The remedy is to separate
a language-evidence object from a numerical test object. The former can carry source anchors,
answer frequencies and unresolved scientific questions. The latter must identify its observations,
null, sampling assumptions and calibration. Neither object should silently replace the other.


3.4     Retrieval, constraints and soft priors
Retrieval changes the information available to a model. It may supply an operational definition
or an expert restriction that the numerical data cannot identify. It can also supply irrelevant or
contradictory evidence. Zhang and colleagues’ retrieval-assisted method reports mixed results:
in the inspected ASIA setting, documents reduce skeleton F1 from 1 to 0.73, while other
settings benefit [11]. The result argues for keeping retrieval conditions visible in a comparison,
rather than labeling retrieval as a uniform improvement.
    The reported orientation accuracy in that work is evaluated on recovered true-positive
edges. Its denominator excludes missing or false edges. For a reader deciding whether a
graph is useful for intervention, skeleton error and orientation error both matter. A system
must report the metric and its denominator before drawing a conclusion about the full graph.
Downstream effect behavior remains a separate outcome.
    A retrieved statement may concern an ancestral path rather than direct adjacency. Ban
and colleagues elicit ancestral constraints and use soft penalties alongside numerical scores
[7]. In a chain A → M → Y , A is an ancestor of Y even when no direct A → Y edge exists.
If an adapter turns “can affect” into “directly causes,” it changes the scientific input. Path,
adjacency, temporal-order and exclusion constraints therefore need different representations
and conversion tests.

   Hard constraints delete graphs. If a wrong constraint removes the true graph, increasing the
numerical sample does not restore it within the constrained search. Darvariu and colleagues
investigate soft language-derived priors that retain support for possible graphs [13]. In
schematic form, one can write a search objective as

    Positive prior support prevents one particular failure: a graph is not logically excluded
solely because a model dislikes it. A large wrong preference can still dominate weak numerical

a posterior model probability are three different objects. A user interface should not place
them under one unqualified confidence label.
    Statistical prompting also raises a data-reuse issue. If D determines the initial graph
summary and that summary shapes the language restriction, the restriction is a function of D.
A later fit cannot casually treat it as independent external evidence. This does not make every
such procedure invalid; it changes the procedure that must be justified or evaluated. Tracking
data hashes and the full adaptive rule makes the reuse visible. Sample splitting may help a
particular construction, but it neither proves the restriction true nor automatically supplies
valid downstream uncertainty.


3.5     Debate, disagreement and scientific evidence
The multi-agent discovery approach of Le and colleagues assigns different roles to language
agents and uses their interaction to construct a graph [15]. This can organize critique and
expose alternative stories. Its inspected assumptions exclude hidden confounding and cycles,
and the reported canonical benchmark scale is limited. Those conditions should remain
attached to a banking interpretation.
    Several agents need not provide several independent pieces of evidence. They may use the
same model family, encounter overlapping training material, or retrieve the same article. A
majority can therefore amplify a common error. Disagreement is useful as a review signal,
but agreement cannot be converted to a causal confidence interval without a model of the
information and dependence involved.
    For servicing, one agent may propose that a contact causes distress, another that distress
causes contact, and a critic may resolve the conflict by choosing the more plausible story.
A more defensible workflow keeps both candidates, asks which timestamp and assignment
evidence would distinguish them, and identifies the effects under each. A later scientific owner
may supply an applicable restriction. The agent’s role is to formulate the unresolved question
and retrieve the evidence, not to manufacture the answer.


                                              15
   Candidate remedies include comparing one-agent and multi-agent conditions, using the same
numerical evidence and total budget, and clustering evaluation results by shared mechanism
rather than counting paraphrased prompts as independent cases. These are proposed tests.
No multi-agent trial has been executed here, and diversity of models alone does not verify
assumptions.

3.7     Temporal discovery is not longitudinal policy inference
The word “sequential” refers to several distinct problems. Verma and colleagues process
successive batches of contemporaneous observations while querying a language model as a
noisy expert and retaining PAG information [19]. The query budget and answer histograms
concern the information supplied to a discovery procedure. They do not identify the value
of a sequence of treatment decisions. Semantic entropy of model answers does not by itself
establish simultaneous graph coverage.
    RealTCD combines language-derived initialization with numerical optimization over tem-
poral graphs and intervention targets [12]. Its setup assumes causal sufficiency, a known
observational regime and an unrolled finite-lag DAG. The inspected theorem conditions its
population optimization result on model capacity, intervention faithfulness, positivity and
finite entropy. A stochastic optimizer with a thresholded adjacency matrix requires additional
analysis before it can inherit any finite-sample guarantee.
    The real cooling-system study does not have a complete ground-truth DAG. It uses selected
expert physical-location rules to assess edges. That is useful domain evidence but a different
evaluation from full known-truth graph recovery. The method also models abnormal windows
as intervention regimes. In a bank, a sudden spread movement could reflect a common market
shock, a changed selection mechanism or measurement failure. Treating it as an exogenous
intervention requires substantive evidence, not a threshold crossing.
    Kougioulis and colleagues’ Large Causal Model is an adjacent numerical transformer rather
than a language-model wrapper [21]. Its inspected scope includes additive-noise, stationary,
causally sufficient, lagged-only systems. These conditions can be useful for a declared class;
they exclude several concerns in private banking states and common shocks. The external
pretrained weights have not been audited or executed locally. A language interface should not
rename this restricted temporal predictor as a general latent-graph solution.
    For longitudinal servicing, the analyst instead needs a sequence of decisions, available
histories, treatment-confounder feedback, censoring and a value target. A graph of temporal
dependence is only one ingredient. The intermediate account state can be affected by an earlier
action while confounding a later action’s effect. The correct question and inferential law must
be specified before an agent chooses an estimator. None of the inspected temporal discovery
results closes the project’s general latent temporal effect-set problem.


3.8     New interventions, mechanism proposals and information
        value
Lampinen and colleagues show how passive learning from expert demonstrations can support
active causal strategies [6]. Their constructive argument first acquires causal information
through experiments, then exploits it. The evaluated learner can intervene in its test environ-
ment. This is a valuable qualification: passive training is compatible with active information
acquisition, but the new interventions supply information that an analyst restricted to historical
bank records may lack.
    The inspected empirical settings use small finite acyclic systems and constrained observa-
tions and actions. A banking translation must specify which interventions are feasible, who
can authorize them, their costs and their effect on future information. Merely asking a model
another question is not an intervention on the customer or the funding market. The two
actions have different likelihoods, costs and ethical or operational constraints.
    Murphy’s Model Discovery Agent proposes executable mechanisms with an LLM and fits and
compares them numerically [26]. The inspected studies include algebraic mechanisms, latent
ODEs and stochastic latent dynamics. The LLM expands the candidate library; BIC or SMC


                                               17
approximations provide model evidence, and a model-disagreement surrogate approximates
expected information gain for experiment selection.
   This architecture gives each component a clearer role than requesting a final effect directly
from the model. It also exposes new obligations. An adaptive library may omit the true
mechanism. Pruning can discard a mechanism that would become useful later. BIC weights
within a retained pool are conditional model weights, not uniform graph-confidence statements.
Residual checks can reveal some misspecification but cannot certify that no policy-relevant
mechanism is absent.
   The paper explicitly states that its finite held-out prediction tests do not establish causal
identification. The experiments use open-loop policies, and the reported comparisons focus
on sample efficiency rather than matched total compute. The chemistry comparison retains
matched evaluable cells from a larger attempted collection. Those denominators must ac-
company the gains. For a bank, the value of information is the expected improvement in an
actual decision after paying acquisition and implementation costs. Information gain within a
model pool can be a design surrogate, but it is not that monetary quantity without an explicit
connection to the bank’s loss.


3.9     An auditable discovery interface
Zheng and colleagues describe causal-learn+ with agents assisting data inspection, assumption
elicitation, tool choice and interpretation around a formal discovery core [22]. They emphasize
separate objects for suggestions, user decisions, algorithm settings and graph outputs. The
design is a useful default for a banking library because it makes changes to scientific input
visible. It is not a universal impossibility result for hybrid discovery; a hybrid can be evaluated
as its own fully specified method.
     CausalAgent supplies another conversational architecture, including MCP tool interfaces
[20]. Static source review finds that an inspected OLC tool calls a function not imported
or defined in that module, and a parser can return an empty dictionary after malformed
JSON. These are integration findings about pinned source, not measurements of a running
service. They show why transport success, parse success and scientific success need separate
dispositions. A successful model message is not a valid graph.
     For each request, an auditable interface should retain the business target, variable definitions
and clocks, numerical-data identity, retrieved-source identity, model and prompt version,
proposal, applied restrictions, selected algorithm and returned graph class. A reviewer should
be able to distinguish an expert’s asserted exclusion from an algorithmic orientation and from
a model’s suggestion. Changed restrictions create a new analysis identity; they should not
overwrite the prior result invisibly.
     Failure recovery must preserve scientific meaning as well as computation. If a job stops
after producing a partial graph, replay must not publish it as a complete answer. If a source
or data hash changes, an earlier identification claim cannot simply be reused. A report should
expose a refused or unknown effect when that is the formal outcome. The completed local
operation contract provides a bounded implementation surface; new discovery adapters still
need their own source, semantic and execution qualification.


3.10      What would establish useful progress
A meaningful comparison begins with a fixed question and information set. The numerical
baseline, language-only baseline, retrieval condition, hybrid and agent-assisted workflow should
receive explicitly matched information or have their information advantage declared. Accuracy
on familiar graphs is only one outcome. The study also needs identification correctness,

                                                 18
retention of compatible alternatives, valid abstention, effect-set coverage where a guarantee is
claimed, interval or set width, decision loss and full cost.
    Every request belongs in the denominator. Failed parsing, unavailable models, timeouts,
nonidentification and legitimate refusals have different meanings and should be recorded
separately. A graph metric conditional on successful requests can be informative, but it must
accompany the success probability and the selection rule. A narrow accurate set emitted only
in easy cases is not the same product as one that delivers useful valid output reliably.
    The proposed remedies are correspondingly bounded. Novel labels test one shortcut;
counterevidence tests retrieval dependence; correctly typed constraints prevent a specific
semantic conversion; fixed prompts and budgets make an experiment reproducible; and
preserved graph classes prevent one form of overstatement. None of these repairs creates
missing bank assignment truth, resolves observational equivalence by itself, or proves arbitrary
dependent-sample calibration.
    The immediate adoption position is therefore to use language assistance for explicit proposals
and reviewable workflow support, while qualifying each hybrid method separately. The original
unresolved temporal graph and useful precision results remain unresolved. New scientific
information, stronger assumptions justified in a declared regime, or additional experiments
may change those conclusions. More fluent explanations alone cannot.

4.1     A decision contract before an agent architecture
A banking assistant becomes useful when it helps an analyst preserve the meaning of a decision
across several transformations. A treasury question becomes a population and an intervention;
account records become measurements of that population; a diagram becomes an identification
problem; an identified functional becomes a numerical request; and the result becomes a
monetary comparison. Each transformation can change the question without producing a
software error. Fluent explanations are particularly dangerous when they make these changes
hard to notice. The appropriate object of automation is therefore a sequence of accountable
transformations, with explicit unresolved states.
    Consider a request to “reduce the funding cost of the customers likely to leave.” An analyst
must establish whether the intervention is a deposit-rate offer, a service action, or a portfolio
allocation; whether the unit is an account, customer, household, or business group; whether
likely departure is a baseline score or a future outcome; and whether cost includes the offer paid
to customers who would have stayed anyway. An assistant can list these alternatives and attach
relevant contract passages. It cannot establish which alternative the bank intends merely by
selecting the most plausible sentence. A structured interview, conducted without an LLM, is
the comparison baseline. The relevant quality measure is the accuracy and completeness of
the resulting target under expert review, together with analyst time and unresolved-question
rates.
    We represent the decision contract schematically as

                               Q = (P, U, A, g0 , g1 , Y, τ, ν, H, A),

where P is the population, U the unit, A the feasible action, g0 , g1 the comparison regimes, Y
the outcome, τ the horizon, ν the target measure, H the available histories and A the declared
assumptions. An ordinary population mean and a propensity-overlap mean have different ν.
A policy responding at day 11 and one acting at day 10 may have different H, even when they
reference the same event. This tuple is a project specification, not a theorem that every target
is identified. Its purpose is to make semantic changes observable.
    The literature supplies several useful components but no generally qualified substitute for
this contract. Causal discovery assistants can separate user decisions from formal algorithm
outputs; retrieval systems can attach passages; benchmark environments can require structured
identification and estimation answers. These roles have different failure modes and should not
receive a single undifferentiated “agent accuracy” score [22, 11, 30].


                                                 20
4.2    Evidence retrieval and the construction of observations
Retrieval has two distinct products: a discovered source and a supported claim. A search
result supplies the first. The second requires reading primary text, checking its version and
population, and showing why its assumptions apply to the present query. A banking process
manual may establish when a contact occurs without establishing why customers receive it. A
statistical paper may justify an estimator conditional on exchangeability without establishing
that exchangeability holds in the bank. A source-to-claim relation must therefore record both
the supported statement and the applicability judgment.
    The retrieval baseline is a curated collection reviewed by an analyst. A useful assistant
can broaden candidate discovery, locate conflicting passages and produce an evidence table
for review. Evaluation should include relevant, irrelevant and misleading documents, missing
sources, obsolete versions and sources that contradict each other. Retrieval failure must
remain distinguishable from evidence that a proposed relation is false. The LACR results
discussed in the preceding chapter are a concrete reason not to assume that more retrieved
text monotonically improves discovery [11].
    Observation construction is equally substantive. A field called owner_id may identify an
operational account holder rather than the economic owner relevant to exposure aggregation. A
link score is a measurement about a proposed relation, not the relation itself. The observation
journey must retain candidate mappings, inclusion rules, missed records, joint dependence
and the origin of adjudicated labels. Ontology tools can help align terminology, but class
membership and linguistic relations do not identify causal arrows. The inspected OntoLearner
download path also illustrates a reproducibility problem: the code revision alone does not
pin ontology bytes when its hub download does not specify a revision [36]. The design uses
explicit local ontology identities and keeps conceptual variables separate from their proxies. A
real ownership audit remains additional information.
    For time-dependent decisions, every relevant measurement has an event time te and an
availability time ta . An event observed on day 10 but delivered on day 11 cannot be used by a
day-10 operational policy. Retrospective analysis may nevertheless use it under a correctly
stated design; that is a different claim from saying a deployed policy could have acted on it.
Corrections and revisions need their own availability records. A validator checks the declared
ordering. Only a process audit can establish whether those declarations reflect the actual
information system.


4.3    Diagrams, discovery and identification as separate handoffs
Diagram elicitation should preserve competing scientific accounts. A proposal must distinguish
a direct edge from an ancestral path, a latent common cause from a measured covariate, and
a circle endpoint from an arrowhead. Revision history should show which source, reviewer
or new experiment changed a claim. Automatic cycle removal is at most a representation
repair: deleting a scientifically important edge to obtain a DAG does not justify the repaired
mechanism. The ordinary baseline is expert elicitation with a deterministic graph editor and
explicit uncertainty.
    Discovery then asks a statistical question under a specified sampling law. An assistant may
suggest a method, summarize its assumptions, or propose a soft constraint. The numerical
procedure remains responsible for its own output semantics. A response histogram from a
language model is not a conditional independence test under a bank sampling law. A temporal
initialization does not establish that observed anomalies were randomized interventions. A
retained equivalence class should reach the effect-query stage intact. The previous chapter
explains why language-assisted graph methods require separate tests of proposal quality, graph


                                              21
retention and downstream effect uncertainty.
    Identification maps a target and assumptions to a functional or set. It can also establish
nonidentification in a declared model class. A missing backend, unsupported graph type or
failed calculation instead means that the implementation did not answer the question. These
outcomes are not interchangeable. The inspected CausalDS helper preserves an unknown
state and does not count an IV estimand as nonparametric population-ATE identification;
its evaluator still requires care with missing metadata and invalid intervals [30]. A prototype
should preserve that distinction even when a downstream report would be easier to write as a
single yes/no answer.
    PyWhy-LLM is a candidate elicitation component, but the inspected front-door routine
is unfinished and back-door suggestions delegate to a language model [37]. Such output can
propose variables for review; it does not supply a verified adjustment proof. Similarly, the
inspected CLadder IV template requires generator-level scrutiny before its population-ATE
labels can become benchmark truth [31]. The project example with half compliers and half
never-takers has Wald ratio one and population ATE two under randomization, exclusion and
monotonicity. Correct algebra for the ratio does not fix an incorrectly named target.


4.4    Estimation, diagnostics and the temptation to change the
       target
Once identification has supplied a functional, the assistant can prepare a fixed estimator
request, summarize diagnostics and explain the output. It should not choose a more convenient
target after inspecting poor overlap. If trimming changes the population, that change belongs
in a new target or an explicitly reported sensitivity analysis. An estimated propensity score,
nuisance learner, sample split and covariance rule must be versioned together with the result.
Prediction accuracy does not by itself qualify the resulting effect interval, especially with
dependent observations or adaptively selected specifications.
    The inspected OCI example makes the target issue concrete. It averages an AIPW quantity
uniformly over a trimmed sample while using an ATO label [35]. Under the corresponding
assumptions this construction targets the retained population’s average effect, not the usual
overlap-weighted population. In the project’s two equally frequent strata, propensities are 0.2
and 0.5 and effects are 0 and 10. Retaining both strata gives mean effect 5. Weighting by

they identify longitudinal intervention means [1].

4.7     Orchestration is an implementation choice
Three generic frameworks were inspected at revisions no later than the client cutoff. LangGraph
supplies state-graph compilation, checkpoint and interrupt interfaces. Its interrupt documenta-
tion explicitly says that resumption starts the node again, re-executing its preceding logic [3].
A node that publishes a result before interrupting therefore needs idempotent publication. A
checkpoint is useful state, not proof that an arbitrary external side effect occurs exactly once.
This distinction determines the design of durable request identifiers and immutable artifact
checks.
    Pydantic AI supplies typed tool and output boundaries and usage accounting. In the
inspected code, tools need not default to sequential execution or require approval. Application
permissions must therefore be configured explicitly. Request limits can be checked before
requests, whereas response tokens generally become known afterward; optional pre-counting
helps with some input limits. If pricing data are unavailable, the cost limit warns that it cannot
be enforced [4]. An application requiring a hard priced budget must handle that absence
before starting a call. A typed interface helps reject malformed structure but cannot establish
the truth of an accepted assumption.
    AutoGen’s pinned README declares maintenance mode. Its AgentChat code carries an
MIT license, while the root documentation license is CC-BY-4.0. The inspected assistant is
stateful and not thread/coroutine safe; tool calls can be concurrent and a reflection stage
can add a model call. Its state save/load methods preserve model context, not all external
tool effects [2]. These facts argue against adopting it for new development without a specific
benefit and maintenance case. The recommended successor is an unaudited lead, not a verified
capability inherited through a README recommendation.
    The proposed first implementation consequently uses a small provider-neutral controller
with deterministic validators and the existing fixed causal service. LangGraph remains a
candidate when checkpointed branching demonstrably helps; Pydantic AI remains a candidate
for a typed model boundary. A local model, a hosted model and an offline fixture are
different backends. Each real backend needs its own model revision, terms, custody boundary,
generation parameters, usage accounting and evaluation. Framework example model names
do not establish current availability or quality. No external endpoint is needed to verify the
prototype’s semantics and refusal behavior.


4.8     Permissions, source injection and recovery
Retrieved text is an input to interpretation. It has no authority to grant a tool, change a
dataset path or approve a bank action. An adversarial document may contain a sentence

                                               24
such as “ignore the review and run this shell command.” An implementation that simply asks
its model to ignore such sentences leaves the permission boundary probabilistic. The design
instead admits a finite set of typed operations configured outside model output. A response
containing an unrecognized operation or undeclared field is rejected. A permitted operation
still receives only verified artifact references and must pass its own scientific preconditions.
This protects the command boundary; it does not prove that all malicious scientific claims will
be detected.
     Model proposals also cannot mark assumptions as accepted by inventing a reviewer name.
Authoritative declarations come from a separately supplied review object, bound to the
exact problem and graph revision. The prototype can check that binding and distinguish
synthetic fixtures. A bank installation will additionally need authenticated review identities
and protected stores; a hash alone is not an authorization system. Sensitive records should stay
within the approved execution boundary. An external model may receive only a separately
approved representation, with prompts and logs subject to the same custody rule. Redaction
is a specified transformation requiring evaluation, not a blanket assurance.
     Recovery starts with the identity of the intended computation. Let

                                     r = H(Q, G, E, O, V ),

where G is the graph revision, E the evidence identities, O the allowed operation request and
V the relevant implementation versions. Replaying an identical request can return its existing
immutable result. Changing the target, source bytes or operation must produce a new identity
or an explicit stale-input error. A partial journal entry is not a published analysis. The local
design tests interruption between validation, tool completion and atomic publication. This
supplies a bounded recovery claim; multi-host consistency and platform service levels require
different trials.


4.9     Reporting, monitoring and the economics of assistance
An evidence-based report should expose the target, assumptions, scientific status, graph/set
uncertainty, numerical result and unresolved questions before adding interpretation. Critical
fields can be rendered deterministically. Generated narrative may explain them, but any
additional empirical assertion needs a claim reference. Inherited results, project derivations,
synthetic examples and unverified hypotheses remain distinct. A tool error should not be
polished into a finding of no effect, and an unknown commercial price should not become a
zero in a business case.
    Monitoring must detect when the evidence for a claim becomes stale. Changes in assignment,
missingness, ownership mapping, policy feasibility or availability time can invalidate a previous
analysis even if software tests still pass. The assistant may triage an alert and propose an
investigation. Suspension and resumption rules belong to the analysis owner. Retuning prompts
or refitting a learner after inspecting drift creates a new procedure whose old calibration does
not transfer automatically.
    The economic comparison includes model calls, retrieval, numerical computation, review,
failures, rework and ongoing maintenance. If Cm , Cr , Cn , Ch , Cf denote these components, a
proposed net benefit is a change in held-out decision value minus Cm + Cr + Cn + Ch + Cf ,
relative to a specified ordinary workflow. This expression is an accounting design, not an ROI
estimate. The available evidence does not supply authentic bank losses, prices, review times
or accepted decision outcomes. A faster explanation can be valuable, but it must not conceal
a lower rate of scientifically usable answers. The appropriate next trial therefore measures
decision quality, all-request availability and total cost separately, while the implementation
preserves the reasons an answer was ambiguous, refused or unavailable.

An auditable proposal-to-tool
prototype

5.1     The implemented boundary
The prototype implements a local handoff from a structured business proposal to fixed causal
operations. It accepts an authored fixture or a recorded model output in a provider-neutral
envelope. It does not contact a model endpoint. The envelope contains the question and its
alternative targets, candidate diagrams, proposed operation names and a provenance record.
That record names the provider, model, revision and generation parameters, retains the exact
prompt, and checks the prompt’s SHA-256 identity. The complete proposal receives its own
content identity. A recorded label is provenance supplied by the caller; it is not independent
proof that a particular model generated the bytes.
    This boundary is deliberately useful before a provider is selected. It permits the same
semantic checks to be applied to different proposed backends without making a framework’s
response format the definition of a causal question. It also separates two evaluations. One
asks whether the controller preserves a given declaration and result. The other asks whether a
model produces good declarations from unfamiliar business questions. The executed examples
answer the first. They supply no empirical answer to the second.
    A proposal can request graph review, graphical identification, discovery or a moment-based
effect calculation. These names do not imply that every operation is implemented for every
input. The fixed service implements syntax review, a supplied-DAG back-door attempt, and
a declared Gaussian moment-inversion calculation. Discovery remains unsupported in the
present service. An unsupported operation returns that status; it does not invent an empty
graph or an effect of zero. There is no operation for sending a customer communication,
altering an account or approving a bank action.


5.2     The three identities of a proposed analysis
Three identities control the handoff. The proposal identity describes the complete proposed
content and model record. The declaration identity covers the problem and diagram together,
including every target alternative, evidence reference, assumption, clock and graph revision.
Finally, the execution identity covers prepared requests, the caller’s grant, source code, schemas
and numerical environment. Changing a population while keeping the same diagram changes
the declaration. Changing an implementation while keeping the same question changes the
execution. Neither change can silently reuse an old result as if nothing had changed.
    The declaration validator checks structure and selected semantic constraints. These include
whether referenced evidence exists in the declaration, whether graph endpoints refer to declared

                                               26
variables, whether a DAG has a directed cycle, whether a proposed history includes a future
decision, and whether a policy uses information before its declared availability. It also flags
unresolved targets, contradicted assumptions, post-treatment adjustment for a proposed total
effect and conditioning a population on treatment descendants. It does not prove that an
asserted DAG is true or fully recognize every MAG, PAG or CPDAG. A successful declaration
review means that a stated problem can proceed to an appropriately scoped formal analysis.
     Evidence identities receive an additional byte check. Source records reference bounded
local files under the documentation tree; symlink escapes and paths outside that boundary are
refused. The actual bytes must match the declared hash. This catches a changed source or
stale citation record. It does not check whether a source’s scientific assertion is true. The full
source record, declaration and proposal are retained as immutable artifacts so that a result
can be traced back to both the claim and the bytes that supported its review.
     The external grant is a trusted local caller object bound to the declaration identity. It
lists permitted operations, names the reviewer and supplies the sampling declaration and any
numerical inputs. Putting a reviewer’s name into the model response does not create this object.
Requesting a new operation does not extend its permissions. The grant is an application
boundary, not a digital signature or a bank identity service. Institutional authentication and
approval stores remain requirements for a later deployment.


5.3     A narrow projection with explicit refusals
The implemented translator supports an unconditional population ATE for one static binary
action, with intervention one and comparator zero. Its horizon starts at the declared origin.
It retains all variables and requires the graph to account for them. Outcome and target-loss
units must agree. It does not silently convert a dynamic regime, an overlap target, a selected
population or a utility transformation into this static ATE.
    For a supported target, the translator builds the fixed operation’s scientific context and
places the complete original declaration in its source evidence. The smaller operation schema
carries the treatment, outcome, population, units, horizon, assumptions and graph. The
retained declaration carries the detailed measurement lineage and numeric decision/availability
clocks. Thus a consumer can reconstruct the original problem even where the narrow operation
schema has no field for every detail. No synthetic or analyst assertion is upgraded to externally
verified truth during this projection.
    Candidate graphs are processed individually. For the synthetic offer question, the input
includes an undirected CPDAG and its two directed alternatives. The controller preserves
all three candidate identities. Syntax review returns the CPDAG’s tail endpoints intact. A
supported directed graph can receive a conditional back-door result. Another candidate may
be outside the implemented identification method. The overall record stays ambiguous, and
every candidate’s response remains visible. It does not average graph outputs or select the
most convenient result. These alternatives describe possible worlds; the prototype does not
assert their simultaneous truth or estimate probabilities for them.
    The distinction between nonidentification and an unsuccessful implementation attempt
is essential here. Failure of the supplied back-door procedure does not prove that no other
valid identification argument exists. Likewise, refusal to translate the servicing policy does not
prove that its value is unknowable. It records that this controller has no qualified mapping
from that richer target into its fixed service. The full policy, clocks and assumptions remain
available for a future longitudinal adapter.

5.4     Representative executed paths
Six synthetic local paths were executed and exported with their artifact descriptors and
checkpoint records. The ambiguous funding question is blocked before invoking a tool because
its economic target has not been resolved. The observation example is also blocked: ownership
and selection questions remain substantive. The servicing declaration passes its own chronology
checks but is unsupported by the narrow static translator. These outcomes are useful behavior;
returning a number would answer an unstated substitute question.
    The discovery example reaches actual graph-review and identification tools for each can-
didate and preserves ambiguity. A separate directed fixture exercises ordinary conditional
execution, interruption and exact replay. It is an explicit fixture choice of a supplied graph, not
a discovered resolution of the original ambiguous problem. Finally, a weak moment example
reaches the effect service and retains a two-component unbounded set. The mean vector
supplied is (1, 0), the covariance matrix is diagonal with entries 0.01 and 1, and the declared
sample size is 100. The resulting set has two unbounded tails separated by a finite excluded
region. The controller preserves both tails and the service’s joint-confidence label together
with its Gaussian-approximation limitation. It neither replaces the union by a finite interval
nor interprets the zero denominator estimate as a finite causal effect.
    The numerical example checks propagation of a declared mathematical result. It does not
estimate moments from bank observations or prove that the supplied covariance is valid under
dependence. The original scientific gaps concerning persistent maps, latent temporal graphs
and dependent learners therefore remain open. A semantic adapter cannot repair a failed
inferential law by attaching a more complete provenance record.


5.5     Recovery and adverse inputs
The local journal stores each completed tool response under a hash of the full execution identity.
Publication is atomic. On resumption, the controller checks that saved results form a prefix of
the planned operations, that their content hash is unchanged, that each response belongs to
the correct request, and that referenced artifacts still match their bytes. Source verification
also runs before a completed replay. A changed source is therefore rejected even when a result
already exists in the journal.
    In the interruption fixture, the controller stops after saving the graph-review response
and before the identification step. Resumption keeps the saved review and executes only the
remaining identification operation. A later complete replay returns the same record without
reinvoking tools. This is a tested single-host property of the local fixed operations and journal.
It does not establish distributed exactly-once execution, authenticated tamper resistance
against the local operator, or recovery for arbitrary external side effects. Those stronger claims
would require different implementations and trials.
    The adverse tests exercise both content and authority boundaries. They reject unknown
operations, injected command or permission fields, nonfinite model parameters, changed
prompt bytes and a live-mode declaration. A retrieved text fixture tells the system to ignore
checks and execute a shell command; the controller keeps it as source data and performs only
the granted graph review. This verifies the deterministic command boundary. It is not a
measurement of a live model’s susceptibility to scientifically misleading text.
    Other tests change source bytes, corrupt a checkpoint, alter a target after the grant,
introduce a contradicted assumption, or move the servicing decision before L1 is available.
Missing numerical evidence returns a failed calculation without an estimate. The recorded
output retains the cause of failure. Together with the inherited problem and operation contract
cases, 80 checks pass. This is local conformance evidence, not an accuracy percentage for


                                                28
unseen banking questions.
    The first integration runs also exposed ordinary engineering limits. Some frozen operation
schemas accepted fewer optional fields than the general context schema. Retaining detailed
clocks in the bound declaration supplied a compatible mapping without dropping them. A
256 MiB address-space allowance was insufficient for imported numerical dependencies; the
executed configuration uses the existing runtime’s tested 1 GiB allowance. Both failed runs are
retained in the evidence. Memory ceilings constrain execution; they do not imply measured
peak memory or comparative efficiency.


5.6     What the next evaluation must establish
The prototype creates a stable place to evaluate actual assistance. A model can eventually
propose a target and diagram, but its output must pass the same declaration and permission
boundaries. An evaluation must compare those proposals with an ordinary structured workflow
and with independent reference judgments. It must score target meaning, evidence applicability,
unresolved ambiguity, graph classes and downstream effect conclusions. Faster or more fluent
reports are secondary when the intervention, population or clock has changed.
    The present records contain zero live model calls. Model tokens and prices remain
unavailable, rather than being reported as measured zeros. All examples are synthetic, all
graph resolutions are explicit fixture assumptions, and no customer action is authorized. A
real comparison still needs exact backend versions, approved data custody, original benchmark
identities, experts for business and diagram judgments, and a frozen analysis of every requested
case. The controller supplies traceable inputs and outputs for that work; the missing scientific
and institutional evidence cannot be inferred from its successful execution.

Evaluating causal assistance without
changing the question

6.1     The benchmark unit is a causal problem
An agent can produce a plausible report, execute valid code and still answer the wrong causal
question. Evaluation must follow the entire chain from business meaning through assumptions
and identification to uncertainty, availability and decision loss. A benchmark of text fluency,
graph edit distance or successful tool calls measures only part of that chain.
    CLadder demonstrates the value of formal questions with varied verbalizations, including
commonsense and deliberately unfamiliar or conflicting stories. The inspected paper also
identifies language-to-formal-tool formulation as a continuing challenge. Its historical binary-
answer accuracy is evidence about those tasks and models; it is not a measurement of effect
coverage, decision loss or banking readiness.[8]
    CausalDS expands the evaluation object to narrated synthetic mechanisms, data and a
separate observation model. Its inspected results show why symbolic success, uncertainty
quality, abstention and cost should be reported separately. Its author-reported exam and
repeated runs are useful design evidence, not model evaluations conducted in this project. A
generated world with known truth also remains different from authentic bank data whose
semantic and assignment mechanisms must be established externally. [24]
    The proposed experimental unit is therefore an independently generated or independently
adjudicated causal mechanism and question. Several phrasings, label conditions or stochastic
model calls can be nested within that unit. They do not create new independent worlds.
Repeating one problem fifty times can measure response variation for that problem; it does
not provide fifty independent demonstrations of general causal competence.


6.2     Matched information and meaningful alternatives
The prospective design compares a fixed numerical workflow, a language-only workflow, retrieval
assistance, a hybrid of numerical tools and typed model proposals, and structured expert
workflows with and without assistance. Each arm needs a declared information budget. If one
receives the true causal graph and another must discover it, their effect-estimation scores do
not isolate the value of language assistance. If retrieval adds a valid institutional fact, that
information gain should be distinguished from a better inference algorithm.
    Eight strata are proposed: independent sufficient DAGs; latent-variable graph classes;
temporal data with known clocks; treatment-confounder feedback; panel or quasi-experimental
assignment; proxies, linkage and selection; weak or nonidentifying effects; and ambiguous
business targets or monetary loss. Label and evidence conditions cross ordinary descriptions,

                                              30
unfamiliar labels, misleading or irrelevant material, and conflict between numerical evidence
and text. These strata test different failure mechanisms rather than treating all causal questions
as interchangeable.
    The current planning allocation is 400 mechanisms, with 50 per stratum and three stochastic
repetitions nested within each mechanism. This is an allocation for developing the design,
not a frozen final sample size or a power calculation. For orientation, the worst-case normal
approximation
    p           for one Bernoulli proportion at 400 independent observations has half-width
1.96 0.25/400, approximately 0.049. Paired differences, stratified outcomes and rare severe
errors require their own precision design. A development pilot would inform that design and
must be excluded from the final test.
    Both natural-workflow and matched-budget comparisons can be useful. The first asks
what an analyst can achieve with a realistic workflow, including review. The second asks what
changes when information or computation is controlled. Neither should hide extra expert time,
repeated retrieval or retry costs. The comparison must state which question it answers.


6.3     A target error can survive perfect arithmetic
An exact project IV construction illustrates a benchmark-label hazard. Let a randomized
binary instrument Z affect treatment for half the population, the compliers; the other half
never receives treatment. Set Y (0) = 0 for everyone, Y (1) = 1 for compliers and Y (1) = 3 for
never-takers. Treatment equals Z for compliers and zero otherwise. Randomization, exclusion
and monotonicity hold in this construction.
    The observed outcome difference across instrument values is 0.5 and the treatment-
probability difference is 0.5, so the Wald ratio is one. The population ATE is two. Changing
the never-takers’ unobserved treated outcome changes the population ATE without changing
the observed law. A scorer that calls the unrestricted Wald ratio the population ATE would
reward a target substitution. This is a finite counterexample supporting review of generator
assumptions, not a claim that every IV question in a named benchmark uses this construction.
    The same concern applies to trimming, overlap weighting and selected populations. A
language answer can reproduce the right formula for a different target. Evaluation should
therefore compare the population, measure, action, comparator, horizon and units before
comparing the numeric answer. Critical semantic errors deserve their own count, even when a
numeric discrepancy happens to be small in the test mechanism.


6.4     Graphs, sets and refusals need separate scores
Graph evaluation should compare adjacency and endpoint statements against the appropriate
identifiable class. A justified circle or undirected edge should not be penalized merely because
a simulator internally selected one fully directed graph. Orientation informativeness can
be reported alongside validity. This makes a method that preserves genuine ambiguity
distinguishable from one that returns little information and from one that confidently overorients
the graph.
     Identification outcomes also need separate labels. Scientific nonidentification, unsupported
implementation, malformed output, explicit abstention, timeout and missing input are not the
same event. A well-designed agent can correctly identify a missing premise without successfully
computing an effect. A backend can be unable to implement a valid functional. Combining
these into one “incorrect” or “no answer” category would obscure the remedy.
     For identified scalar targets, bias and error can be meaningful. For reported sets, evaluate
coverage, topology, finite availability and size. Whole-line answers can have high coverage and
little decision value. A method that returns short intervals on a selected easy subset needs an

                                               31
all-request availability denominator. Unbounded and disconnected sets must remain in the
evaluation rather than being replaced by finite plotting limits.
    For an eligible finite interval [l, u], one prospective score at nominal level 1 − α is
                                   2                  2
                       (u − l) +     (l − θ)1{θ < l} + (θ − u)1{θ > u}.
                                   α                  α
The formula balances length with penalties for missing the target. It is not an instruction to
compress a union of intervals into one interval. For unions, coverage and total Lebesgue length
can be reported while retaining infinite lengths. Raw losses should not be capped simply to
make a plot or ranking convenient. The eligible target type must be stated before the score is
selected.


6.5     Contamination, adaptation and selective reporting
An exposed demonstration cannot become a held-out evaluation merely because its variables
are renamed. The local problem fixtures and replay examples have already been used to
develop and debug the prototype. They can continue to test conformance, but they cannot
provide independent estimates of a language model’s causal accuracy.
    The final design needs a frozen development/test split, mechanism identities, prompts,
model/provider revisions, retrieval corpus and ranking rules, numerical baselines, stopping
rules and analysis code. Mechanism families should be separated where family reuse would
reveal the answer. Label novelty and mechanism novelty should be varied independently. An
unfamiliar name attached to a memorized mechanism is not the same test as an unfamiliar
mechanism.
    Test outcomes cannot be used to tune prompt templates, choose the best retriever,
strengthen selected priors or discard difficult cells while retaining a final-test interpreta-
tion. A changed method requires a new reviewed evaluation revision. Failed and unavailable
requests remain in the record. Reporting only the critic-approved subset would confound
answer quality with selection by the critic.
    The inspected OCI paper provides a useful refusal example: its critic declines many
replications when overlap is poor. That can be preferable to an unsupported estimate, but it
makes the denominator essential. The reported successful answers, refused requests, review
effort and remaining target population must all be visible. The external results are author
reports, not a validation of this project’s critic. [25]


6.6     Decision value and cost
The ultimate question is whether assistance improves feasible decisions at an acceptable total
cost. In a known synthetic mechanism, a policy’s expected loss can be calculated under the
mechanism and compared with the chosen baseline. In a bank, the feasible policy, valuation
model, risk limits, operating costs and outcome truth need authentic inputs. Synthetic decision
loss does not establish institutional return on investment.
    The prospective cost record includes model calls, input/output/cache tokens, retrieval,
numerical CPU time, wall time, memory, retries and human review and rework. Actual dated
prices are needed to turn these quantities into a currency total. Unknown prices remain
missing; zero live calls in an offline prototype do not imply a free live workflow. A fast answer
that requires substantial correction can have a worse total cost than a slower transparent
analysis.
    No final experiment has been registered or run here. Five prerequisite classes remain:
an exact live backend and custody terms; actual prices and a bounded budget; independent

                                               32
held-out mechanisms and original benchmark bytes; named business/diagram adjudicators and
a rubric; and qualified numerical baselines. These are missing program inputs. No credentials
were read and no provider endpoint was probed to infer that a service is unavailable. The
design provides a route to evaluation once those inputs are supplied; it provides no current
empirical model-performance claim.
