8.1    Four different learning problems
Suppose Chapter 6 has justified adjustment by X. The observed data for an independent
household are O = (X, A, Y ), with binary A, propensity e(x) = P (A = 1 | X = x) and
response surfaces ma (x) = E[Y | A = a, X = x]. The scientific target is the population
average θ = E[m1 (X) − m0 (X)], or the conditional contrast τ (x) = m1 (x) − m0 (x). These are
causal targets only under the identification assumptions. The same formulas remain statistical
association functionals when those assumptions fail.
    Machine learning can estimate nuisance functions (e, m0 , m1 ), estimate the effect function
τ , optimize a policy using the estimated effects, or propose a causal representation or graph.
Each has its own loss and generalization question. An outcome predictor minimizes error for
observed Y under the historical action distribution. A policy learner values outcomes under
changed actions. The two losses are not interchangeable, and a random train/test split cannot
by itself bridge them.

8.4     Learning heterogeneous effects: compare the losses
The statistical CATE is a difference of response surfaces, but that does not determine the
best finite-sample estimation strategy. Several methods can target the same τ while exploiting
different structure.


8.4.1    Response models and imputed contrasts

A T-learner fits m0 , m1 separately. An S-learner fits a common regression m(a, x) and evaluates
its difference. Sharing parameters may help when arms are similar, but regularization can
suppress action interactions even when the factual prediction loss barely changes. A T-learner
can waste information when the arms share a complicated baseline but the effect is simple.

8.6     Representations do not manufacture an adjustment set
Suppose X suffices for exchangeability and a learned representation B = b(X) is proposed as a
replacement. A useful sufficient condition is that it also balances action: A ⊥ X | B. Indeed,
                              Z                                   Z
         P {Y (a) | A, B} =       P {Y (a) | x} dP (x | A, B) =       P {Y (a) | x} dP (x | B).

The first equality uses exchangeability given X and the fact that B is a function of X; the
second uses balance. Without that extra property a lossy representation can destroy the
identification route. The true propensity e(X) balances binary treatment because conditioning
on e(X) = b makes the probability of treatment the same b for every remaining X stratum.
An approximate learned score needs an estimation and overlap analysis.
   In the confounded two-world example of Chapter 6, use X = U as an actually observed
covariate. It permits adjustment. Mapping all X to a constant loses the distinction and
recovers a spurious contrast of 0.36. Adding an excellent predictor of a different component of Y
cannot restore the discarded confounder. Conversely, balancing representations cannot remove

8.7     Implications for estimation in the bank
The product should accept an identified functional or moment, an explicit target measure,
nuisance roles and a sampling model. It can then choose compatible learners, splitting schemes
and uncertainty methods. The output must identify whether it is an ATE, an overlap-weighted
projection, a CATE curve, a complier curve or a policy value. A response predictor can be
shared across applications without transferring the causal validity of any particular application.
   Diagnostics should correspond to the mathematics: nuisance residuals and support, score
stability, fold leakage, local moment rank, regularization sensitivity and policy-selection rules.
Predictive validation is useful for nuisance fit; it cannot certify exchangeability, instrument
exclusion, causal representation or transport to another bank. These distinctions also determine
what automated data organization is allowed to discard or aggregate. The next chapter develops
inverse learning with endogeneity and proxies. Chapter 10 then makes the information set
dynamic and shows why sequence prediction is still only one part of the causal problem.

9.1     Which economic quantity must be recovered?
The invitation experiment may identify a linear coefficient or a complier mean. A bank
asking how funding contribution changes over a continuum of deposit prices poses a different
question. Prices can respond to latent liquidity demand, so a flexible regression of contribution
on price learns the historical conditional mean, not automatically the structural response.
Equation (7.16) instead uses an exclusion and conditional moment to turn that response into
an inverse problem. The same mathematical issue reappears when historical actions use an
unmeasured condition for which the bank has imperfect proxies.
  The analysis proceeds in three steps. First it asks whether the requested average is identified
even when the entire price-response curve is not. It then shows how regularization trades
unstable information against bias. Finally it compares instruments with proxy measurements,
whose identifying restrictions differ even though both lead to conditional-moment equations.
   Suppress observed covariates temporarily. Let HA = L2 (PA ), HZ = L2 (PZ ) and T : HA →
HZ be (T h)(z) = E[h(A) | Z = z]. Conditional expectation is a bounded linear contraction.

                            ⟨T h, q⟩PZ = E[h(A)q(Z)] = ⟨h, T ∗ q⟩PA .

weight stabilization would conceal the changed estimating equation and potentially change a
cost-based decision.

9.5     Learning the bridge is a conditional-moment problem
For one action stratum let rh (Z, X) = E[Y − h(W, a, X) | Z, A = a, X]. In the unrestricted
square-integrable test-function class,

  sup E[1{A = a}{2f (Z, X)(Y − h(W, a, X)) − f (Z, X)2 }] = E[1{A = a}rh (Z, X)2 ]. (9.11)
   f

Complete the square conditionally to prove the identity: the maximizer is f = rh . Minimizing
the left side over h targets the conditional moment rather than individual response prediction.
Restricting f to an RKHS or neural class changes which moment violations are detected;
regularizing h selects among unstable approximate solutions. Ghassami et al. (2022, Sections
3 and 5) develops RKHS minimax constructions and rate conditions for this class of problems.
   In contrast, least-squares regression of Y on (W, A, X) learns E[Y | W, A, X], which is
generally not an outcome bridge. In the exact example, posterior U given W, A differs
from the linear bridge correction in Equation (9.7). A learner can therefore have excellent
factual prediction and fail the observable bridge moments. Validation needs held-out moment
violations, regularization sensitivity and target uncertainty in addition to factual loss. Even
zero observed violations cannot verify the unobserved exclusions; they only test implications
within the chosen function class.
   For large banking data, a finite-basis conditional-moment model leads to cross-products
of instrument and response bases, an ill-conditioned linear system, and a regularized solve.
Dense kernel methods instead form matrices whose memory is quadratic in sample size.
Approximations using finite features or blocks change the numerical moment problem; their
residual error must be propagated into the target remainder. An architecture decision follows
from these mathematical objects, not from declaring every inverse learner interchangeable
with a supervised regression adapter.
   The proposed product should return the target functional, operator and proxy roles, rank/-
completeness assumptions, fitted moment residuals, regularization path, and the exact statisti-
cal conditions claimed. It should distinguish a nonunique structural response with an identified
average from an unidentified average, and distinguish both from an identified but statistically
unstable target. These distinctions also prepare the partially observed longitudinal problem: a
hidden-state likelihood, a proxy bridge and a causal value solve different recovery problems
even when they share the same observations.
