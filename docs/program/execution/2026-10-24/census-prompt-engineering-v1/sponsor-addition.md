## Prompt optimization needs an evidence and evaluation contract

The inspected Chen and colleagues survey is the 58-page May 2025 arXiv revision
6. Its 25-page initial version and journal identity remain separate. The survey
organizes prompting, decomposition, optimization, evaluation and security; it
does not qualify the packages or reproduce the studies it cites. More detailed
answers can introduce missing premises, and supplying worked solutions changes
what an evaluation demonstrates. Computational task graphs must remain distinct
from causal graphs.\citep{exec24_promptengineering_original}

Exact authored examples quantify the proposal risks. With a default-probability
reduction between negative and positive ten percentage points, USD 1,000 loss
and USD 20 action cost imply net values from negative USD 120 to positive USD 80.
Inserting an assumed five-point reduction produces USD 30 without identifying it.
Twenty equally accurate prompts tested on four tasks yield an expected best
development score of about 93.11%, although fresh expected accuracy stays 50%
under the stated independent null model. Five stages each correct 95% of the
time give about 77.38% complete correctness if independent; their marginals
alone allow 75% to 95%. These are illustrative probability laws, not observed
model results.\citep{exec24_promptengineering_reference}

The proposal compares deterministic forms, templates, retrieval, bounded
decomposition and adaptive optimization using held-out task families, complete
attempt logs, versioned evidence and full costs. Tool authority should come from
trusted requests and enforced policy, not retrieved prompt text. An eight-case
authored authorization truth table is a logical specification, not security
qualification. A stipulated USD 30 for ten attempts and three accepted outputs
means USD 10 per accepted output; actual prices and bank benefits remain open.
\citep{exec24_promptengineering_reference}
