\chapter{Using shared information to narrow uncertainty}
\label{integrated:weak_audit_sharpening}

\begin{quote}\small
Separate response bands retain uncertainty about customer identity but can
produce broad funding-value sets. Shared information in the two observed
candidate responses can narrow those sets by forming average and difference
scores before covariance estimation. A repaired 3,600-request study finds narrower transformed sets in
all 2,937 paired finite cases of six selected designs, without a general
dominance theorem. Weak audit information remains the limiting factor: 558/600
near-rank outputs are whole-line; even with eight times as many audit labels,
105/600 are whole-line and the finite transformed median width is about
USD 15.4 million. A pathwise observational-equivalence construction shows that
false exogeneity cannot be solved by interval algebra alone. The study uses synthetic populations; performance for a particular bank
requires evidence about its assignment and observation processes.
\end{quote}

\section{A sharper primitive region, with the same target}

The financial target, independent fourfold nuisance pilot, fixed monetary
weights, exact independent-binomial audit inversion and two-candidate origin
mixture are those in Chapter~\ref{integrated:weak_audit_projection}. Write the two observable
candidate monetary responses in stratum $s$ as $\mu_{s1},\mu_{s2}$, the
correct-assignment probability as $p_s$, and the focal/companion responses as
$\theta_s,\delta_s$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{integrated:weak_audit_sharpening:eq:transform}
 A_s=\tfrac12(\mu_{s1}+\mu_{s2})=\tfrac12(\theta_s+\delta_s),\quad
 D_s=\tfrac12(\mu_{s1}-\mu_{s2})=
     \tfrac12(2p_s-1)(\theta_s-\delta_s),\quad
 \theta_s=A_s+\frac{D_s}{2p_s-1}.
\end{equation}
The value remains $V=(\theta_0+\theta_1)/2-C$. At $p_s=1/2$, $D_s=0$ in
the observational law but the focal response is not identified separately
from its companion. Projecting a region for $(A_s,D_s,p_s)$ must retain this
structural ambiguity; no transformation creates audit truth.

The separate-band method constructed four bands for the candidate
responses. Those bands can discard shared candidate noise. The alternative transforms the two financially weighted residual outcomes to their half-sum
and half-difference \emph{before} estimating the four corresponding action
slopes and fixed-horizon calendar score variances. Equivalently, if
$\widehat\phi_{s1},\widehat\phi_{s2}$ are the observable influence scores,
the new scores are $(\widehat\phi_{s1}\pm\widehat\phi_{s2})/2$. Their marginal
variances therefore include the candidate cross-covariance. Four two-sided
normal bands share response error .025 by Bonferroni; two exact equal-tail
binomial audit intervals share audit error .025. The reported set is the exact
image of the resulting $(A_s,D_s,p_s)$ rectangles under
\eqref{integrated:weak_audit_sharpening:eq:transform}, combined with fixed half-stratum weights and direct
repricing cost. A valid joint primitive region still contains $V$ after
projection, but the new rectangle is not nested in the old candidate
rectangle. Neither finite-sample response coverage nor pointwise width
dominance is a theorem.

The covariance gain and its limit can be seen directly. If the candidate
slope covariance in one stratum has diagonal entries $v_1,v_2$ and
off-diagonal entry $c$, the transformed variances are
\begin{equation}\label{rel80:transformed-covariance}
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat A_s)=\frac{v_1+v_2+2c}{4},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat D_s)=\frac{v_1+v_2-2c}{4}.
\end{equation}
Positive shared noise therefore increases uncertainty about the average but
reduces uncertainty about the difference. Near rank zero the inverse magnifies
the difference, so that reduction can matter greatly. Negative covariance
reverses this allocation. Separate marginal bands do not retain all the
information in a joint confidence region even after transformation.

A dimensionless covariance illustration makes the lack of width dominance
explicit. Suppose the two candidate variances are one, their correlation is
$c=0.9$, and the audit probability is known to be $p_s=3/4$. These are
stipulated values for an algebraic comparison, not estimates from the funding
experiment. With the same normal critical value $z$, candidate-coordinate
bands give a projected half-width $2z$. Transformed bands give
$z(\sqrt{0.95}+2\sqrt{0.05})\simeq1.422z$, since the focal response is the
average plus twice the difference. If the correlation is instead $c=-0.9$,
the transformed half-width is
$z(\sqrt{0.05}+2\sqrt{0.95})\simeq2.173z$, wider than the candidate result.
Both covariance matrices are positive definite. The observed narrowing in
the registered study is consequently a property of those paired requests,
not a consequence of the coordinate change alone. Audit uncertainty adds
another projection step and cannot restore a universal width ordering.

The normal/dependent-calendar bands inherit the conditional growing-pilot
rate and score assumptions from the preserved monograph. The inspected
primary DML paper establishes the orthogonal-score/rate premise in its own
iid setting, not this project's dependent result \cite{dml2018}. The
inspected Newey--West 1986 working paper supports a Bartlett covariance
alternative, not finite-sample validity of this untapered fixed-horizon
scalar score \cite{newey1986}. Exactness applies only to the binomial audit
component. Numerical qualification refuses nonpositive/nonfinite response
variance and a difference-score variance at most
$(8\epsilon_{\rm machine})^2$ times its positive average-score variance.
That last guard prevents floating-point roundoff from turning a deterministic
zero difference into a falsely precise normal band.

\section{Registration, preserved failure and fresh comparison}

The original set's large widths motivated this separate plan, which was
registered before the follow-up's final draws. Its first 3,600 requests are
retained as \emph{exploratory and not qualified}. An adversarial fixture with
identical candidate outcomes exposed an inadequate zero-variance guard: the
fitted difference-score variance was numerically positive, roughly
$2\times10^{-33}$ of the average-score variance. The initial check had
exercised an exactly zero accumulator but failed to require refusal for this
fitted fixture. The complete initial code, specification, check receipt,
journal and summary were archived and read back before repair. The scale-aware
guard and a strengthened refusal check were registered before a new 3,600-draw
comparison with a fresh seed. The initial results were not promoted or
quietly hash-replaced.

The qualified comparison holds each evaluation history, 4,800-date independent
pilot and audit counts fixed across two methods: the candidate-response
rectangle and the transformed average/difference rectangle. Five 1,200-date
stress laws are inherited; a sixth keeps the near-rank law but uses 12,800
instead of 1,600 representative audit labels \emph{per stratum}. All have
600 fresh requests, no early stopping, the same fourfold pilot training and
the same error allocation. Numerical checks compare transformed slopes and
lag covariance with an independent dense four-coordinate calculation; its
maximum scaled variance difference was $1.65\times10^{-15}$. Whole-line,
disconnected and empty rank-one geometry, the repaired degenerate fixture and
a pathwise causal counterexample were checked separately. A read-only journal
reconstruction verifies all 3,600 request identities, both primitive bands,
audit intervals, financial sets, coverage counts and paired finite widths.

Across the six selected designs, the transformed set was narrower in all
2,937 requests where \emph{both} sets were finite. This is a paired descriptive
result under the registered laws, not a width theorem or an economic
qualification. Baseline median finite width fell from about USD 1.229 million
to USD 1.096 million; small-audit width fell from USD 1.410 million to
USD 1.178 million. Both still exceed the approximately USD 375,000 synthetic
baseline target by a wide margin. Under persistent responses, median width
fell from USD 3.387 million to USD 2.889 million. All four selected valid-law
cells and the higher-audit cell showed 600/600 synthetic coverage for both
sets; six-cell simultaneous Monte Carlo intervals and individual counts are
saved. This is evidence about fixed simulators, not a bank coverage guarantee.

At the original near-rank audit size, each method returned 558/600 whole-line
sets. Only 42 were finite; their respective median widths were roughly
USD 52.0 million and USD 16.2 million. Increasing audits eightfold raised
finite availability to 495/600, but 105/600 sets still spanned the whole line.
Among the 495 paired finite cases, transformed median width was about
USD 15.4 million, versus USD 47.8 million for the candidate rectangle. Audit
information can improve availability without producing an actionable bank
interval; its acquisition cost and decision threshold are unknown.

Under false exogeneity, candidate and transformed sets covered respectively
597/600 and 561/600 of the original causal target. Tightening lost coverage
in 36 paired requests and gained none. The second fraction is 0.935, but no
coverage theorem applies when the action innovation restriction is false.
The high first fraction is equally incapable of verifying that restriction.

\section{Why exogeneity is an information problem}

The failure is not merely an interval formula. Let
$m(W_t)=.5W_t+.2(W_t^2-1)$, $A_t=m(W_t)+e_t$, and let $g(W_t)$ be an
otherwise unknown baseline balance response. Consider the structural law
\begin{equation}\label{integrated:weak_audit_sharpening:eq:confounded}
 B_t=\rho B_{t-1}+\beta A_t+g(W_t)
      +\sigma\{c e_t+\sqrt{1-c^2}\,z_t\}+\xi_t,
\end{equation}
with independent centered $e_t,z_t,\xi_t$ before the displayed shared term.
Define a second law with independent structural error but
\begin{equation}
 \beta'=\beta+\sigma c,\qquad
 g'(W)=g(W)-\sigma c\,m(W),\qquad
 B'_t=\rho B'_{t-1}+\beta'A_t+g'(W_t)
       +\sigma\sqrt{1-c^2}\,z_t+\xi_t.
\end{equation}
Because $A_t-m(W_t)=e_t$, the two observed balance recursions are
identical path by path for the same initial balance and exogenous draws.
The candidate swaps, independent audit labels and any statistic of the
observed histories are therefore identical as well. Yet under a one-month
intervention on $A_t$, the first law has response $\beta\rho^h$ and the
second $(\beta+\sigma c)\rho^h$. At the registered false-law values
$\sigma=2$, $c=.5$, their one-unit response coefficients differ by 1 and
the fixed financial target differs by about USD 387,575. A direct coupled
numerical witness reproduced identical observed paths to floating precision.

This is an identification counterexample for a class permitting the adjusted
nuisance mean $g'$. An externally known $g$, randomized action variation,
valid instrument or other justified scientific restriction could separate
the laws. More audit labels about candidate identity or a narrower confidence
box alone cannot. Numeric declarations of exogeneity are assertions to be
validated by assignment and source owners, not tests performed by the API.

\section{Decision and delivery boundaries}

The transformed region is a locally reproducible way to use covariance that
the first box omitted. Its precision may still be inadequate for a financial decision: whole-line
sets and multi-million-dollar finite widths remain under weak audit rank,
even in the higher-audit cell. The observational counterexample isolates a separate identification limit that requires new causal information. Any next
statistical refinement must be derived, preregistered and tested on fresh
adverse laws, reporting all-request coverage and finite decision precision.

The bank has not supplied an acceptable value-width threshold, representative
history/audit prices, authentic assignment truth, independent reviewers,
target-platform service evidence or an adopted interface. Bank use still requires independent review and evidence that the
method applies to the institution and decision being considered. The next chapter changes the observation itself: selected audits reveal the focal outcome history rather than only whether a candidate assignment is correct. This can resolve the identity ambiguity, while the exogeneity counterexample continues to apply. Its benefit must therefore be compared with both the new label requirements and the remaining uncertainty.
