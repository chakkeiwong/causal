where w ∈ W is a vector of weights, W is the set of all weights, and 1 is the vector of ones. I refer
to τw (θ) as a weighted estimand. I allow the weights w to be negative unless stated otherwise.
For the case of nonnegative (i.e., convex) weights, I define the simplex W+ = {w ∈ W : w ≥ 0}.

Example (Event Studies). Given units i and time periods t ∈ {0,1,...,T }, where T ≥ 2, the
researcher observes outcomes Yit ∈ R and treatment indicators Dit ∈ {0,1}. A unit i’s cohort
group Gi = min{t : Dit = 1} is the time period when first treated—if never treated, then Gi = ∞.
There are no treated units in the base period (i.e., Gi > 0). Moreover, treatment is “absorbing”
in the sense that a treated unit remains treated (i.e., t ≥ Gi implies Dit = 1).7 Let Yit = Yit (Gi ),
where Yit (g) denotes the potential outcome for unit i when assigned to cohort g. Consider the
group-time average treatment effect on the treated (ATT):

                               ATTg,t = E[Yit (g)−Yit (∞)|Gi = g], t ≥ g.                                    (1)

This parameter gives the period t causal effect of being treated in cohort g versus being never-
treated, averaged among the units i in cohort Gi = g. Under assumptions of parallel trends and
no anticipation, ATTg,t is identified for different group-time pairs (Callaway and Sant’Anna,
2021; Roth et al., 2023). Let k index such pairs (gk ,tk ). For parameters θk = ATTk = ATTgk ,tk
and weights w ∈ W, the corresponding weighted estimand is
