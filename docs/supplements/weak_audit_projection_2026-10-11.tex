{\LARGE\bfseries Weak-audit projection for funding value\par}
\vspace{5mm}
{\large A bounded continuation of the V10 causal-banking monograph\par}
\vspace{3mm}
11 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai)\par
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}
The V10 funding interval refuses inference when a representative identity
audit cannot distinguish two candidate portfolio assignments. This supplement
tests a different reporting design: simultaneous bands for the four observable
candidate monetary responses, exact binomial audit intervals, and projection
through the assignment inverse without dividing by a nearly zero estimated
rank. In 3,000 fresh paired synthetic requests, the resulting set has high
all-request coverage but markedly worse financial precision. At the near-rank
design, 546 of 600 sets equal the whole real line. Exact audit inversion does
not make the response bands finite-sample exact, verify causal exogeneity, or
provide a bank decision threshold. The frozen V10 monograph is unchanged;
this is an author-reviewed research supplement, not an implemented product.
\end{abstract}

\section{The bank's target and the rank-one obstruction}

The target remains the value of a one-month 10-basis-point price intervention
over a fixed balance-stock response horizon $H=6$, summed across 48 branches:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 V=\sum_{h=0}^{H}w_h\beta\rho^h-C.
\end{equation}
The monetary weights $w_h$ include branch scale, margin, discounting and the
first-month repricing interaction; $C$ is the direct repricing cost. The
intervention, weights and horizon were fixed before this experiment. A funding
desk needs both an interval/set that covers under defensible assumptions and
a width small enough to distinguish a worthwhile price change. These are
separate requirements.

For identity-audit stratum $s\in\{0,1\}$, let $\theta_s$ be the focal
portfolio's weighted response before $C$, $\delta_s$ the companion's weighted
response, and $p_s$ the probability that the first candidate is correctly
assigned. Noninformative whole-horizon swaps give observable candidate
responses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
 \mu_{s1}=p_s\theta_s+(1-p_s)\delta_s,\qquad
 \mu_{s2}=(1-p_s)\theta_s+p_s\delta_s.
\end{equation}
For $p_s\ne 1/2$, inversion yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 \theta_s=\frac{p_s\mu_{s1}-(1-p_s)\mu_{s2}}{2p_s-1},\qquad
 V=\tfrac12(\theta_0+\theta_1)-C.
\end{equation}
At $p_s=1/2$, the two observable responses both equal
$(\theta_s+\delta_s)/2$. If the companion response is unrestricted,
$\theta_s$ is then unrestricted. A finite interval obtained by substituting
$\widehat p_s\approx1/2$ would conceal this identification failure. The
preserved V10 procedure instead uses a Wilson rank guard and returns
``unavailable'' when its audit interval crosses $1/2$. The new design reports
the full set of values compatible with primitive response and audit bands.

\section{Primitive inference before inversion}

The nuisance pilot is independent of evaluation and has $M=4T$ dates. It
retains the fixed six-column dictionary, chronological half-split ridge
selection and full-pilot refit of V10. Dependence within the pilot is allowed;
independence from evaluation and a stable relevant law are required. For each
evaluation date, let $v_t$ be the residualized action and $u_{tj}$ the
residualized, financially weighted candidate outcome for $j=1,2$. Write
\begin{equation}
 Q_s=\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}v_t^2,\qquad
 \widehat\mu_{sj}=Q_s^{-1}\sum_t1\{S_t=s\}v_tu_{tj}.
\end{equation}
Crucially, these are \emph{observable mixed-candidate} responses. Neither
an estimated audit probability nor the unstable inverse enters their score.
The scalar influence estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
 \widehat\phi_{tsj}=\frac{T1\{S_t=s\}v_t
 (u_{tj}-v_t\widehat\mu_{sj})}{Q_s}.
\end{equation}
For each of four coordinates separately, center the score over all $T$ dates
and sum lag products through the prespecified response horizon $H$. The
variance of the sample mean is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hac}
 \widehat V_{sj}=T^{-2}\left\{\sum_t\widetilde\phi_{tsj}^2+
 2\sum_{k=1}^{H}\sum_{t=k+1}^{T}
 \widetilde\phi_{tsj}\widetilde\phi_{t-k,sj}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Only positive finite estimates qualify. This untapered fixed-horizon formula
uses the V9 innovation-filtration argument under correct action-mean and
exogeneity premises. Newey and West's positive-semidefinite Bartlett
construction is a distinct inherited alternative, not a proof that
\eqref{eq:hac} is finite-sample positive or correctly calibrated under a
different law \cite{newey1986}. The orthogonal partialling-out score and
nuisance-product logic trace to the inspected primary DML text
\cite{dml2018}; its iid theorem does not supply this dependent-calendar
inference. The fixed-dictionary $M=4T$ centering rate is a project derivation
in the preserved V9 chapter.

Allocate $\alpha_R=.025$ among four two-sided normal response bands:
\begin{equation}
 B_{sj}=\left[\widehat\mu_{sj}\pm
 z_{1-\alpha_R/8}\sqrt{\widehat V_{sj}}\right].
\end{equation}
For independent representative binomial audits $(K_s,n_s)$, allocate
$\alpha_A=.025$ among two exact equal-tail binomial inversions $P_s$. Each
audit interval has error allocation $\alpha_A/2=.0125$ and tails of .00625.
The implementation computes beta-quantile endpoints rounded outwards; exact
binomial coverage was enumerated for sizes 1--15 on a probability grid and
compared to the existing inversion. This exact statement concerns only the
audit component. The response bands are normal/dependent-calendar
approximations, not exact finite-sample intervals.

Let $f(B_{s1},B_{s2},P_s)$ be the exact image of the rectangle under
\eqref{eq:inverse}, retaining disconnected and infinite components. The
reported financial set is the Minkowski sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:set}
 \mathcal V=\tfrac12 f(B_{01},B_{02},P_0)
 +\tfrac12 f(B_{11},B_{12},P_1)-C.
\end{equation}
On the event that all six primitive intervals cover their truths, $V\in
\mathcal V$ regardless of the rank of the audit matrix. For each fixed law
under the stated nuisance rate and response-score central limit theorem,
Bonferroni therefore gives $\Pr(V\in\mathcal V)\geq .95-o(1)$.
No uniform near-rank, false-law or finite-sample response guarantee is
claimed. At exact rank one, a compatible primitive region may project to the
whole real line; that is honest identification uncertainty, not a precise
answer. An empty projection is recorded as noncoverage, not replaced by an
unbounded set.

\section{Registered comparison and independent numerical challenge}

The design and author challenge were recorded before new draws. Five selected
stress cases each use 600 fresh paired requests: the 1,200-date baseline,
small audit, near-rank audit, persistent response and false exogeneity. The
same evaluation histories, fourfold pilot and audit counts feed both the
inherited regular pilot interval and the new projection. Pilot/evaluation/audit
seeds are disjoint from V10. The final journal records every request and
reconstructs all five summaries exactly. At 600 repetitions, the ordinary
Monte Carlo half-width near .95 is about .0174; the worst-case value is about
.0400. Exact-binomial Monte Carlo intervals and a five-cell Bonferroni family
are retained in the machine result. They describe these fixed synthetic laws,
not bank-population coverage.

Before the final draws, eight numerical challenges passed: small-count audit
coverage enumeration and comparison with the inherited inversion; whole-line
and disconnected rank-one sets; dense candidate-slope and horizon-covariance
arithmetic for baseline and near-rank fixtures; and rejection of invalid
counts, declarations, dimensions and nonfinite histories. These are author
checks, not independent scientific review. The streaming implementation keeps
fixed-dimensional pilot factors and four scalar lag accumulators, without
storing all evaluation scores.

The baseline regular interval covered 567/600 requests; the projection set
covered 600/600. The respective median finite widths were approximately
USD 499,000 and USD 1.231 million. In the small-audit design the figures were
571/600 versus 600/600, with median widths USD 499,000 and USD 1.406 million.
The selected persistent design had 555/600 versus 600/600, at approximately
USD 1.346 million and USD 3.426 million median widths. These are wide
selected-law sets, not evidence that a bank would accept their precision.

Near rank, the regular procedure returned a finite interval in only 132/600
requests and covered on 130/600 all requests. The projection produced 546/600
whole-line sets; only 54 were finite. Their conditional median finite width
was about USD 63.7 million, versus USD 1.91 million among the regular
procedure's 132 available intervals. All 600 projected sets covered, mainly
because most contained every real value. Reporting only ``100\% coverage''
would invert the practical conclusion.

In the false-exogeneity design, regular coverage was 122/600 and projection
coverage was 591/600. The latter had median width about USD 1.367 million,
roughly 2.4 times the regular width. This high selected-law coverage does not
validate the false declaration. The method has no causal guarantee there;
a different violation magnitude or economic threshold can expose the bias.
No method was chosen after observing this cell, and no universal correction
is established.

\section{Decision meaning, alternatives and remaining obligations}

Projection can trade noncoverage or refusal for width. That trade is not
automatically useful. A funding decision based on the sign of $V$ would need
the entire defensible set on one side of the bank's specified decision
threshold. A whole-line set can never meet that rule. Even the baseline median
set width exceeds the synthetic target value (about USD 375,000) by more than
a factor of three. The exercise does not provide a bank threshold, the price
of representative audit labels, or the cost and availability of independent
pilot history. Width conditional on a finite set cannot substitute for
all-request coverage and finite-set availability.

The inherited regular inverse is tighter in selected cases but can refuse
near rank and undercovers under some finite designs. A sharper transformed
average/difference confidence region or candidate-specific covariance
inversion is a substantive alternative; it would need its own simultaneous
dependent-score argument and numerical qualification. More representative
audit labels can shrink $P_s$ but cannot cure a false origin-swap or
exogeneity premise. New outcome-linked information may identify targets that
counts and candidate responses cannot. These are prospective designs, not
results of this supplement.

The next scientific D09 step is a newly derived, prespecified precision
improvement with fresh independent evaluation, retaining small-audit,
near-rank, persistence and false-law challenges. D07/D08/D10 still require
named platform and bank owners, authentic histories and audit truth, independent
review, a bank precision threshold, prices, fault/service evidence and signed
acceptance. The completed local experiment does not establish a deployed
service, adopted interface or measured institutional savings.
