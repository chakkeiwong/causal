# Pre-registration author review

# Training-only sparse nuisance learning with calendar separation

Retain the first R05 attempt unchanged as exploratory: it selected penalties using evaluation outcomes, standardized with all rows, crossed an omitted gap in covariance, and ignored scenario dimension overrides. None of its documented rate/selection claims qualify those requests. This successor uses a fresh seed and new files.

The model is partially linear IV. Let z=m_z(X)+v, a=kz+m_a(X)+eta, y=theta a+g(X)+epsilon, with independent iid innovation v,eta,epsilon and stationary Gaussian AR controls X. Nuisances are E[Y|X],E[A|X],E[Z|X]. With residuals u_y,u_a,u_z, E[u_z(u_y-theta u_a)]=0 and Q=E[u_z u_a]=k>0. Estimation uses the ratio of sample products. Writing nuisance errors delta_j, the moment expectation conditional on an independent training fit changes by E[delta_z(delta_y-theta delta_a)]. This is a product term, not a first-order prediction loss.

Outer training and evaluation blocks are separated by g. Inside training only, split again with a gap, standardize on inner training, fit a finite lasso penalty grid, select by inner validation loss, then refit on the entire outer training block using its own mean and scale. No evaluation values enter standardization, tuning or refit. The objective is squared loss/2n plus lambda times the L1 norm; intercept is unpenalized. Coordinate descent is checked by KKT residuals and independent constrained convex optimization. The penalty grid is c sqrt(log(p+1)/n_train), c in {.25,1,2}; it shrinks with history size.

For fixed p, bounded-away-from-zero population Gram eigenvalues, finite Gaussian moments, fixed rho<1 and a correct linear nuisance, mixing sample Gram and score fluctuations are O_p(n_train^-1/2). The finite shrinking grid has coefficients and prediction errors O_p(n_train^-1/2); taking a finite-grid validation minimum preserves this order even at selection boundaries. The product remainder is O_p(n_train^-1)=o_p(n_eval^-1/2). A growing gap g=C log n with C sufficient for rho^g=o(n^-1/2) removes boundary coupling. This is a fixed-dimensional argument, not a proof for p growing faster than n. Growing p requires restricted eigenvalues and sparse approximation rates with s log p/sqrt(n) ->0, which remain conditional assumptions.

Fitted IV scores are centered within each evaluation fold. Bartlett products are summed within folds only; they never treat either side of an omitted gap as adjacent dates. With b=floor(n_retained^(.25)), b->infinity and b/n->0. Fixed p and moment/mixing conditions give score covariance consistency. Cross-fold nuisance correlations are asymptotically negligible under the stated product rates; finite performance is tested, not assumed. Weak k is outside the strong-IV approximation and no large interval is called useful identification.

Eight cells honor actual p=20 or 60, support 3 or 8, instrument k=.8 or .03, gap=40 or 2, rho=.45 or .9, and nonlinear omitted nuisance. Inadequate separation, nonlinear misspecification and weak IV are adverse. The fixed-dimensional dense linear dictionary uses the same folds and a separate least-squares fit, not a purported algebra equivalent of nonzero-penalty lasso. Freeze 99% intervals and 512 requests/cell; require simultaneous lower coverage >=.95 and finite availability>=.99 for valid cells. Results that fail remain negative; the grid does not imply uniform finite coverage or bank utility.

Independent fixtures ran before final draws. R04 checks symbolic covariance, equal-law witnesses and extended-real JSON. R05 checks independent constrained convex optimization, soft-threshold boundaries, training-only selection and independently reconstructed dense score covariance. This is author review, not independent acceptance. Earlier errors remain preserved.
