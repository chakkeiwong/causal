# Reasoning tests and software test generation

Two inspected author originals clarify a practical public-prototype route. The affordance study tests whether models select functionally suitable alternatives rather than familiar associations. Its repeated-task dependence, changed prompting conditions and unresolved novelty limit generalization. The TitanFuzz study combines generated seed programs and mutations with differential and crash oracles. Its reported historical findings justify investigating the pattern, without proving a banking capability. \citep{exec24_affordance_original,exec24_fuzzing_original}

The cutoff-pinned implementation leaves concrete adoption work: unresolved code rights, unpinned dependencies, a Docker/library mismatch with the paper and a separately hosted seed archive. Its later bug counts remain distinct from the paper. The proposal conditionally adopts the testing idea while requiring exact environment, containment and oracle qualification before native reuse. \citep{exec24_titanfuzz_source}

Exact authored examples connect engineering decisions to banking meaning. A functional-choice fixture rejects familiar but late or ineligible funding options; correlated repetitions reduce effective information. A 5,000 USD backend discrepancy passes an illustrative numerical rule while violating a stipulated 1,000 USD business tolerance. Shared backend errors remain invisible to agreement tests. At 600 USD total fixture cost, 100 attempts, 40 unique runnable programs and six confirmed defects cost 6, 15 and 100 USD per respective unit. These are stipulated denominators, not empirical prices. Proposed CLI/MCP artifacts keep generation, execution, scientific adjudication and full costs distinct. \citep{exec24_affordance_fuzzing_reference}
