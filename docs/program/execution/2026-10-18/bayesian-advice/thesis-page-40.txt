     3.2     Eliciting a Prior Distribution
          Defining the prior distribution over possible causal graphs is a key component of BSL.
     Standard approaches generally rely on either simple, uninformative priors (Eggeling et
     al., 2019) or edge probabilities derived from human specialists; however, the former lacks
     domain-specific insight, while the latter is frequently inaccessible. To address this, we
     propose to derive such prior knowledge from LLMs. Specifically, our approach involves
     extracting the prior probabilities for individual edges directly from the LLM.
        We define the smallest instance of our problem as:

        Given a pair of random variables (𝑉𝑖 , 𝑉𝑗 ) from a causal graph 𝒢 , we define the
        set 𝑂𝑢𝑡 ≔ {𝑃(𝑉𝑖 → 𝑉𝑗 ), 𝑃(𝑉𝑖 ← 𝑉𝑗 ), 𝑃(𝑉𝑖 and 𝑉𝑗 not causally related)} and output
        𝑎𝑟𝑔𝑚𝑎𝑥(𝑂𝑢𝑡) and 𝑚𝑎𝑥(𝑂𝑢𝑡).

         As discussed in Section 2.2, there is an ongoing debate in the literature about how to
     calculate the uncertainty over an LLM’s response, which in this case would be the direc-
     tion or nonexistence of an edge between two variables. Then, our goal is to approximate:
     𝑃(output ∣ prompt, output ∈ {‵ →′ ,‵ ←′ ,‵ 𝑛𝑜 ′ }), where output is the LLM output.
        Thus, we may consider each prompting iteration represents one expert and, therefore,
     we use the most frequent answer with its frequency as the uncertainty. We work with a
     Monte Carlo estimate, in which we use 10 sampled LLM generations for each edge. The
     number of samples was limited because of budget constraints.

     3.2.1    Prompting
         In order to define the exact text to use and manner of elicitation, we experimented
     with variations of the prompts used by (Kiciman et al., 2024a). After doing a long pro-
     cess of prompt engineering, through a process of trial and error, we decided to work
     with the following prompt: (Kiciman et al., 2024b; Bazaluk et al., 2025) You are an
     expert on <DATASET_CONTEXT>, who responds only in numbers. You are an expert
     on <DATASET_CONTEXT>. You are investigating the cause-and-effect relationships be-
     tween a set of <VARIABLES_CONTEXT>: <VARIABLES_LIST>. Your task is to determine
     the causal relationship between the variables <VAR_X> and <VAR_Y>. Answer ”0” if
     <VAR_X> and <VAR_Y> are not statistically associated. Answer ”1” if <VAR_X> directly
     causes <VAR_Y>. Answer ”2” if <VAR_Y> directly causes <VAR_X>.
