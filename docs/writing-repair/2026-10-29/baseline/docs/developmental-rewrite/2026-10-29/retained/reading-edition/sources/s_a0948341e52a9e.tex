\section[R dynamic panels and structural shocks]{R dynamic panels, volatility restrictions and nonlinear shock experiments}
\label{sec:exec25-r-dynamic}

A bank comparing branch interventions with aggregate funding shocks needs a common account of time, intervention and uncertainty before it chooses an R interface. Dynamic GMM, a recursively ordered VAR, identification from volatility changes and a nonlinear impulse experiment answer different questions. This comparison develops five concrete alternatives: plm, vars, svars, panelvar and tsDyn. Their selected dated originals establish implementation choices; they do not establish the truth of instruments, stable structural shocks or a bank's assignment mechanism. The Python comparison and all earlier panel arguments remain relevant when an R service is considered as a numerical reference or an isolated adapter.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_plm,exec25_rdyn_vars,exec25_rdyn_svars,exec25_rdyn_panelvar,exec25_rdyn_tsdyn,exec25_rdyn_choice}

\subsection{A panel index must preserve the moment's calendar}

Consider $y_{it}=a_i+\alpha y_{i,t-1}+x_{it}'\beta+u_{it}$ for branch $i$ in reporting period $t$. A proposed moment such as $E[y_{i,t-2}(u_{it}-u_{i,t-1})]=0$ requires both a substantive instrument restriction and an unambiguous meaning for a period. For observations at $(2000,2002,2003)$ with values $(10,20,30)$, a row lag returns $(\mathrm{NA},10,20)$, while a one-year lag returns $(\mathrm{NA},\mathrm{NA},20)$. Balancing across the union of observed labels cannot insert a year absent from every branch. An analyst must supply the intended grid, retain missingness and decide which moments survive. These are exact authored indexing examples, not results from running the packages.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_choice}

The selected plm estimator first obtains a panel index. Its unbalanced-data branch expands the observed individual and time labels. The GMM builder then selects instrument entries by positions and differences extracted matrices by adjacent rows. In the same package, the ordinary pseries lag API distinguishes time shifts from row shifts; its inspected time-based helper matches numerical time offsets, with a separate fallback for nonnumeric levels and an optional faster delegate. These facts must be read together. The existence of a calendar-aware public lag does not by itself qualify every internal GMM transformation on globally missing periods. The proposed adapter therefore records both the declared grid and the realized estimator rows, and tests the complete formula-to-instrument path. It should not simply relabel sorted rows as consecutive months.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_plm}

The selected panelvar preparation similarly constructs the Cartesian product of observed categories and period labels, merges the data, sorts it and converts the period to a factor. Its actual panel lag helper shifts rows; the difference helper differences adjacent rows. Its forward orthogonal-deviation helper instead subtracts the mean of available future observations and uses a factor depending on how many future entries remain. That transformation has a different availability contract from a simple first difference. Missing periods, duplicate category-period rows, short histories and the minimum number of future observations belong in the public test design. The selected source paths alone do not settle every input validation or covariance property.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_panelvar}

Numerical fallbacks do not create valid moments. Plm checks the smallest eigenvalue of selected first- and second-step weight matrices against $10^{-9}$ and warns before using a generalized inverse; its coefficient matrix still uses a direct solve. Its robust covariance routine has its own inverse decision and an additional two-step correction expression. Panelvar also uses inverse fallbacks, a generalized inverse for selected coefficient calculations and direct solves in a variance path. The public tolerance is used in an eigenvalue predicate, while the inspected calls to MASS::ginv do not explicitly pass that tolerance. Consequently the adapter must retain the actual solver, scale and tolerance at each stage, rather than treat one user parameter as a guarantee about all inversions. Neither a finite coefficient nor a corrected covariance formula establishes exclusion, valid cross-branch dependence or useful precision.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_plm,exec25_rdyn_panelvar}

\subsection{Restricted shocks and optimizer custody}

For a reduced-form innovation $u_t$, a structural representation $Au_t=B\varepsilon_t$ implies $\Sigma_u=A^{-1}BB'A^{-T}$ under unit structural covariance. The selected vars direct objective uses $\log\{\det(B)^2\}$, and retains its optim object in the returned estimate. This differs materially from discarding the optimizer diagnostics. For a two-dimensional matrix with unit diagonal and off-diagonal entries $1/2$, the squared determinant is $9/16$; this supplies an exact cross-language objective witness without asserting numerical parity. The ordinary VAR orthogonalization uses a Cholesky factor. A bank must still justify the ordering or other restrictions that label a funding shock.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_vars,exec25_rdyn_choice}

The vars scoring branch warns when its iteration bound is reached and then continues to construct an estimate; that branch returns a null optimizer object. Warnings and the stopping state must therefore cross an API or MCP boundary with the estimate. The inspected routine also normalizes selected signs and reports Sigma.U multiplied by 100. Reconstructing $A^{-1}BB'A^{-T}$ from the returned matrices and explicitly declaring units is a proposed conformance check. It would be unsafe to compare a field named covariance across implementations solely by its name. The source review identifies the required checks; it does not claim that an installed package failed them.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_vars}

Identification from changing volatility adds a different restriction. In the schematic system $\Sigma_1=BB'$ and $\Sigma_2=B\Lambda B'$, the diagonal regime matrix $\Lambda$ must help distinguish shock directions, while the impact matrix remains stable across the declared break. If $\Sigma_1=I_2$ and $\Sigma_2=2I_2$, both $B=I_2$ and the rotation with rows $(3/5,-4/5)$ and $(4/5,3/5)$ reproduce both covariances. Thus a volatility change alone need not name the intervention. This is an authored algebraic counterexample, not an imported identification theorem. Unknown break selection, changing transmission and weakly separated variances require separate treatment.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_choice}

The selected svars id.cv frontend rejects a restricted input varest model, while separately accepting a matrix of impact restrictions. Those are distinct restrictions and should not be conflated in the service contract. The frontend passes a fractional default crit of 0.001 into a registered native bridge that declares an integer argument; both the generated bridge and the C++ implementation retain that integer type. The exact installed conversion remains a native qualification item. An authored conditional witness illustrates why this matters: an improvement of $1/2000$ is below $1/1000$ but above an integer surrogate of zero. A transport test must verify the received value and stopping predicate, not assume that the displayed R argument survives unchanged.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_svars,exec25_rdyn_choice}

The inspected volatility backend calls an inner optimizer with an iteration limit, runs a bounded outer loop and returns the chosen matrices, likelihood and iteration count. Its selected return does not retain the inner optimizer's full convergence object. It also flips whole columns of an inverse-Hessian matrix when their diagonal entry is negative before taking square roots. The inspected non-Gaussian path contains a similar column operation. For the authored symmetric matrix with rows $(-1,1/2)$ and $(1/2,2)$, flipping its first column gives rows $(1,1/2)$ and $(-1/2,2)$, which are no longer symmetric. This algebra demonstrates why positive displayed diagonal entries cannot certify a valid covariance. It is a diagnostic contract and proposed test, not a package-wide rejection or a native failure report.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_svars,exec25_rdyn_choice}

\subsection{A resampling distribution must retain failures and shock meaning}

The selected svars moving-block bootstrap constructs overlapping residual blocks, draws blocks and refits a declared structural method. Some method-specific errors are converted to null results; failed results are subsequently removed before surviving estimates form the covariance summary. Other branches have different error handling. A service must retain requested, attempted, failed and retained counts, the block length, design and failure causes. A successful conditional summary is not automatically an unconditional statement about every requested fit. Sign and permutation alignment, shared calendar shocks and weak identification remain substantive qualification questions.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_svars}

TsDyn provides a complementary nonlinear experiment. Its inspected GIRF path conditions on histories and shocks, then averages paired simulated paths. The low-level helper with shock\_both set to false replaces the first innovation in one path with the requested shock; with shock\_both true, it adds the shock to that path's baseline innovation. The averaging helper used by the public GIRF path defaults to the additive setting. Both paths share the history and subsequent innovations. If the baseline innovation is one and the specified shock is two, replacement gives an immediate difference of one, whereas addition gives two. These are different experimental targets. A natural-language request for a two-unit funding shock must resolve that distinction before an agent constructs a call.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_tsdyn,exec25_rdyn_choice}

The selected routine samples future residual innovations without replacement and samples histories and shocks without replacement when they are not supplied. A horizon longer than the available residual pool therefore needs an explicit input policy and fixture. The public routine passes the supplied seed separately for each history--shock combination. Its averaging helper resets that seed once per combination and then repeats the low-level calls without passing a seed, allowing the random stream to advance within that combination. This can couple innovation draws across combinations; it does not establish that all repetitions within a combination are identical. The joint simulation law and Monte Carlo uncertainty must retain that distinction. The TVAR delegate preserves the supplied starting matrix and innovations, chooses a threshold regime and rounds selected threshold comparisons and paths. Threshold proximity, history selection, seed ownership and paired-path construction all belong in the result provenance. This source reading does not quantify Monte Carlo error for an installed build.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_tsdyn}

History dependence also changes the economic question. In an authored recursion with drift $f(x)=x/2$ for $x\leq0$ and $f(x)=3x/2$ for $x>0$, adding one to the first innovation produces a first-period response of one from either initial state $-1$ or $1$. With zero subsequent innovations, the next response is respectively one or $3/2$. Pooling histories therefore changes the estimand even when paired innovations are controlled. The bank should report which stress or ordinary histories are weighted, distinguish a residual perturbation from an identified policy intervention, and evaluate uncertainty under the selected fitting and simulation law.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_choice}

\subsection{Conditional adoption and a public qualification route}

Plm and panelvar are conditional references for declared panel calendars and moments; vars is a candidate restricted structural reference with explicit optimizer and unit custody. Svars is a candidate for justified volatility or distributional restrictions only after bridge, curvature, identification and resampling diagnostics are qualified. TsDyn supplies conditional nonlinear simulation contrasts, not an automatic business-to-intervention translation. The inspected declarations are GPL version 2 or later for plm, vars, panelvar and tsDyn; svars declares MIT plus a named license file whose genuine holder notice is retained. These are provenance facts, not complete legal clearance. Installed artifacts, native dependencies and any intended distribution or port still require a separate rights decision. No blanket adoption of the ecosystem follows.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_plm,exec25_rdyn_vars,exec25_rdyn_svars,exec25_rdyn_panelvar,exec25_rdyn_tsdyn}

The proposed public evaluation freezes the package artifacts and data schema, constructs known-calendar panels, contrasts valid and violated moments, varies structural restrictions and volatility separation, and records every numerical and refusal state. It includes duplicate and globally missing periods, covariance-unit reconstruction, fractional tolerance transport, indefinite curvature, rejected resamples, threshold histories and seeded replication. The comparison must count all requests and full workflow cost. An illustrative budget of 18,000 USD over 600 requests costs 30 USD per request; 400 completed fits imply 45 USD per completion, and 300 scientifically accepted outputs imply 60 USD per accepted output. These are exact hypothetical denominators, not observed ROI. Bounded discovery queries and selected delegates leave further candidates, rights and native qualification open. The packages have not been validated by execution in this comparison.~\citep{exec25_rdyn_choice}
