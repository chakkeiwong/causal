\section[JVM temporal models and inference]{JVM temporal models: lag identity, forecast contracts and intervention meaning}
\label{sec:exec25-java-dynamic}

A funding team may need a Java service to discover temporal relations, forecast deposit balances or propagate a conditional state distribution. These are distinct tasks. The selected comparison covers Tetrad temporal utilities, Smile AR/ARMA/ARIMA, ScalaTion VAR/VARX and JDemetra+ state-space and regression-ARIMA paths. Each has useful computational structure, but a name such as structural VAR or exogenous predictor does not itself identify a bank intervention. The comparison therefore follows the actual lag transformation, solver, forecast return and failure boundary before proposing integration. Dated source revisions are retained separately from installed binary equivalence or current supported commercial editions.\citep{exec25_jdyn_tetrad,exec25_jdyn_smile,exec25_jdyn_scalation,exec25_jdyn_jdplus,exec25_jdyn_choice}

\subsection{Temporal discovery needs stable variable identity}

Tetrad's inspected lag constructor rejects colons in original names, uses the original name at lag zero and appends a colon and lag number at positive lags. It creates knowledge tiers and copies observations using row offsets. For data at years $(2000,2002,2003)$ with values $(10,20,30)$ and one lag, the authored reconstruction gives rows $(20,10)$ and $(30,20)$ in current/lagged order. The first pair spans two calendar years. A calling service must declare a regular observation grid or an appropriate irregular-time model before interpreting these row shifts as one-period causal parents. The helper's name validation is a real safeguard, but it does not supply the calendar.\citep{exec25_jdyn_tetrad,exec25_jdyn_choice}

The selected structuralVar utility calls that constructor, forbids edges within each tier and chooses a discrete score or a continuous covariance-based score before running FGES. It then collapses the learned graph and regresses contemporaneous targets on selected parents. Its collapse step extracts the base name by searching for a period, whereas the inspected constructor creates colon-delimited names. For plain names funding and funding:1, an authored character-index check returns minus one for the period search. A graph edge whose endpoint enters that parsing path therefore needs a native naming fixture before this method is exposed. This is a specific source-path incompatibility; it does not imply that every Tetrad temporal algorithm uses the same utility or fails.\citep{exec25_jdyn_tetrad,exec25_jdyn_choice}

The selected regression delegate forms a normal-equation inverse and residuals. The continuous score can return NaN on a caught singularity or nonfinite score. Such numerical states, graph endpoints and tier restrictions must survive a CLI or MCP response. A collapsed graph plus residuals is not automatically a structural impact matrix or a calibrated policy-effect interval. Latent confounding, score assumptions, dependent sampling and post-discovery uncertainty remain separate scientific obligations. Conditional adoption should start with the lag/knowledge representation and named utility tests while retaining the earlier Tetrad comparisons.\citep{exec25_jdyn_tetrad}

\subsection{Point forecasts and uncertainty are different output contracts}

The inspected Smile ARMA fit rejects inadmissible orders and short series, estimates an auxiliary autoregression and fits lagged observations and residuals by SVD. Its SVD delegate uses an effective-rank threshold and solves on retained singular vectors. The ARMA routine subsequently forms a Cholesky factor of the design Gram matrix for coefficient standard errors. These are different numerical requirements: a finite rank-truncated coefficient calculation need not imply that the later covariance calculation completes. A proposed test must retain both outcomes and the installed tensor/LAPACK delegate, rather than infer a universal failure or success from the initial solve.\citep{exec25_jdyn_smile}

The selected multistep forecast copies recent observations and residuals, recursively produces future values and leaves future residual entries at zero. It returns a point-forecast array. Its residual variance and coefficient tests are separate objects; the selected return is not a horizon-specific prediction band. In an authored AR(1) example with coefficient $1/2$ and unit innovation variance, conditional forecast-error variances at horizons one and two are $1$ and $1+1/4=5/4$ even with known parameters. A single residual-variance field cannot be silently reused as both. Estimated parameters, model selection and dependent innovations would add their own qualification requirements.\citep{exec25_jdyn_smile,exec25_jdyn_choice}

Smile's inspected ARIMA fit differences by adjacent observations and selects the ARMA, pure AR or pure MA route. Forecasts are restored to the original scale one differencing layer at a time using saved terminal values. With observed levels $(10,13,17)$ and stipulated second-difference forecasts $(1,1)$, the first-difference forecasts are $(5,6)$ and the level forecasts are $(22,28)$. This exact authored calculation gives a useful conformance target. It does not establish stationarity, valid uncertainty or the intervention meaning of a deposit forecast. The information clock must also distinguish genuinely future external inputs from values learned after the prediction origin.\citep{exec25_jdyn_smile,exec25_jdyn_choice}

\subsection{A vector autoregression interface must preserve its regressor layout}

ScalaTion's selected VAR delegates to a multivariate regression forecaster and forges future regressor vectors from actual lags and earlier predictions. Its VARX builder concatenates trend columns, endogenous lags and exogenous lags. The inspected forging routine allocates columns from the number of endogenous response columns and their lag order, without appending the separately constructed exogenous block. In an authored layout with two endogenous series, two endogenous lags, one exogenous series, three exogenous lags and no trend, the constructor expects seven columns while the selected forge allocation has four. That width contract needs a native fixture and an explicit future-exogenous scenario before the VARX name becomes an integration promise.\citep{exec25_jdyn_scalation,exec25_jdyn_choice}

The inspected common forecaster has an unfinished forecast-at-origin entry point, while a separate forecastAt route has an implemented body using forged vectors and recursive predictions. It is therefore inaccurate both to advertise every entry point as complete and to reject all forecasting code. The regression delegate selects among QR, SVD and normal-equation factorizations; those choices and their dependencies need qualification. The lag builder clamps unavailable early lag positions to the start of the series. Its exogenous helper also backfills a zero prefix using a weighted backcast. The backcast delegate uses a slice of supplied observations and a configurable weighting rule. A real zero and a missing historical value are not interchangeable in a banking record. The proposed adapter must declare the treatment of initial lags, zeros and future information, with fixtures for each path.\citep{exec25_jdyn_scalation}

\subsection{State propagation through missing observations}

JDemetra+ provides a distinct conditional state-space reference. Its inspected observation wrapper treats nonfinite entries as missing. The ordinary filter skips the measurement update for such a row but still advances the state. The concrete state delegate applies the transition to the mean and covariance and adds the process-noise covariance at every advance. Under a stipulated scalar transition of one, initial variance one and process variance one, two missing steps therefore produce variances two and three. That is an exact authored model calculation, not a benchmark result or proof that a filtered state is a causal variable. The state law and observation clock must be part of the API contract.\citep{exec25_jdyn_jdplus,exec25_jdyn_choice}

The ordinary filter uses numerical variance thresholds and raises an inconsistency exception for a nonzero innovation with effectively zero variance. The selected diffuse initializer has a separate lifecycle and throws if diffuse initialization cannot be completed within the available data. These are informative refusal states, not interchangeable missing forecasts. An agent should preserve the initialization choice, effective observations, constraints and final-state timing, then explain whether failure arose from the model, information or numerics.\citep{exec25_jdyn_jdplus}

The regression-ARIMA forecast path extends the series with missing future observations and runs the diffuse/ordinary filtering route. It returns forecasts and standard deviations, scales the state variances and, when regressors are supplied, adds a quadratic term using their coefficient covariance. In an authored scalar example with baseline variance one, adjusted future regressor two and coefficient variance $1/4$, that term adds one and the total becomes two. The selected calculation conditions on the supplied ARIMA model and regressor scenario; it does not by itself establish uncertainty from every model-selection or policy-design step. Its public horizon convention also interprets a negative request in multiples of the annual frequency, unlike a generic invalid-horizon refusal. The separate exact-forecast helper has an unsupported numeric-mean overload. These details must be resolved at the wrapper boundary.\citep{exec25_jdyn_jdplus,exec25_jdyn_choice}

\subsection{Reuse decisions and matched public qualification}

The candidate decisions are deliberately distinct. Tetrad is a conditional temporal-discovery and lag-knowledge reference, with the selected collapse utility deferred pending identity tests. Smile is a conditional point-forecast baseline with separately qualified uncertainty and numerical artifacts. ScalaTion is an inspectable regression/forecasting alternative whose entry-point and exogenous-layout gaps preclude blanket admission. JDemetra+ is a conditional state-space and regression-ARIMA reference, with scientific causality supplied by an independently justified model. A forecast package can support a causal workflow without being the identification engine.\citep{exec25_jdyn_choice}

The source licenses also differ. The selected Tetrad root contains GPL version 3; Smile's pinned declaration separates core-library GPL/commercial terms from serving and studio terms; JDemetra+ retains EUPL 1.2. ScalaTion has both a root MIT notice and an additional historical holder notice in its HTML license file, both retained. These are source declarations, not a legal opinion or a signed commercial entitlement. The proposal must name the exact component, artifact, dependencies and distribution plan before an adoption or port decision. Source availability alone supplies neither a bank support contract nor a price.\citep{exec25_jdyn_tetrad,exec25_jdyn_smile,exec25_jdyn_scalation,exec25_jdyn_jdplus}

Public qualification should freeze the artifacts and compare calendar gaps, naming conventions, singular designs, forecast horizons, differencing inversion, exogenous widths and missing-state propagation against independent oracles. Holdout periods and feature availability must be fixed before evaluation. Both scientifically accepted outputs and all failures count. A hypothetical 21,000 USD study over 700 requests costs 30 USD per request; 500 completions cost 42 USD each; 350 accepted outputs cost 60 USD each. These exact denominators are not measured economics. Bounded repository searches leave many irrelevant or uninspected leads, and zero hits from a narrow query are not evidence of absence. Dynamic-panel instruments, structural intervention identification, complete delegates and native uncertainty remain open. The packages have not been validated by execution in this comparison.\citep{exec25_jdyn_choice}
