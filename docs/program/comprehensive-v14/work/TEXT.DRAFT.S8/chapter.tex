\chapter{Commercial alternatives, acquisition boundaries and the competitive capability gap}
\label{exec17:s8}

\section{The procurement question is a scientific question}
\label{exec17:s8-question}

A bank does not purchase ``causal inference'' in the abstract. It purchases a
workflow for a named decision, under a named information clock, with a named
population, data owner and loss function. A lending committee might ask whether
an eligibility threshold changes repayment, a treasury desk might ask whether a
rate change changes retained balances, and a servicing team might ask whether a
sequence of contacts is worth its cost. A commercial product can be excellent
for one of these questions and irrelevant to another. A common product proposal
therefore needs a comparison at the level of targets and assumptions before it
compares commands, languages or license units.

This chapter develops the acquisition part of that comparison. It uses the inspected GeNIe,
SMILE, BayesiaLab, Bayes Server, Stata, SAS, JMP, SPSS, Mplus, Amazon and
historical Azure material already audited in the working reader. GeNIe 5.0.R2
and SMILE 2.2.4.R1 identify their manual builds; several other official pages
are mutable or were retrieved after the 7 October 2026 cutoff. Those facts are
source limitations, not evidence that an uninspected edition lacks a feature.
No paid engine was run and no price below is a vendor quotation
\citep{exec13_genie_readme,exec13_smile_intro,exec13_bayesia_api,
exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality,exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro}.

The chapter has four contributions. First, it maps the 17 software areas to
documented commercial alternatives and to the present local capability. Second,
it identifies the semantic information that would be lost at an adapter
boundary. Third, it turns the documented license and deployment distinctions
into an executable acquisition design. Fourth, it gives exact hypothetical
cost, regret and information-value references. The monetary values are an
authored planning model. They are useful for deciding what a prototype should
measure; they are not evidence of a price, performance, or return on investment.

The rows are not claims that every named vendor implements every operation in
the row. They are a checklist for a matched comparison. For example, GeNIe's
pattern editor preserves an equivalence class and its documented Create DAG
command randomly orients unresolved arcs. BayesiaLab EQ searches equivalence
classes and gives fixed, forbidden and temporal restrictions different
semantics. A proposed adapter that maps both products to a single
\texttt{required\_edges} field has already changed the scientific input
\citep{exec13_genie_pattern,exec13_genie_bs,exec13_bayesia_eq}.

The local implementation column is deliberately narrower. The capability
manifest implements graph syntax review, a supplied-DAG back-door operation,
a supplied-moment residual inversion, deterministic sensitivity and a strict
observation clock. It does not implement general discovery, policy evaluation,
survival, report construction or job lifecycle. A completed ``unsupported''
response is an implementation boundary, not a negative result about a vendor
or about the scientific possibility of the method
\citep{exec16_commercial_runtime,exec16_commercial_matrix}.

\section{Four journeys define four different purchases}
\label{exec17:s8-journeys}

The buying unit can be written as $(q,e,d,o)$: a causal question $q$, a
specific edition and update $e$, a deployment arrangement $d$, and an output
contract $o$. Let $\mathcal A_q$ contain the units whose assumptions, operations,
rights and outputs are adequate for that question. Optimization of cost takes
place only over $\mathcal A_q$. Unknown membership is a due-diligence item;
it is not coded as either ``cheap'' or ``incapable.'' The same family can
supply an admissible analyst tool for one target and an unresolved production
service for another. The following comparisons apply that distinction to
banking decisions rather than award one aggregate vendor score
\citep{exec17_s8_paths}.

\subsection{Structural funding: what exactly changes when a rate changes?}

For treasury, distinguish a change in posted rate from a change in customer
mix and a change in the bank's pricing rule. JMP's binary-treatment report
separates ATE from ATET, while its continuous-treatment report offers a
quadratic marginal structural model and a generalized-propensity-score route.
The inspected page explicitly warns that a potential-outcome density plot
does not directly supply a causal effect. These are concrete capabilities
against which the proposal should be assessed. They can support a supplied
static comparison under their assumptions; their existence does not establish
an effect of a new dynamic pricing rule in a dependent balance system
\citep{exec13_ch_jmp_causal}.

An acquisition request for this journey should specify a monetary contrast at
two stated rates, a customer population and a balance horizon. If the business
question concerns retained balances for all eligible customers, returning an
ATET for customers who actually received the high rate changes the population.
If the rate is continuous, a binary high/low recoding changes the intervention.
A prospective adapter must retain the two evaluated doses, centering and model
form; a quadratic coefficient alone is not the requested contrast. Stata's
static treatment-effect routes offer another comparator, but their inspected
i.i.d., conditional-independence and overlap conditions cannot be silently
replaced by the bank's shared-calendar sampling law
\citep{exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro,exec13_ch_jmp_causal}.

The proposal's current supplied-moment inverse problem is a different service.
Its narrow reproducible calculation can preserve an ambiguity set, while a
commercial report may provide a developed estimation and diagnostic workflow
for a different supported model. Buying an analyst tool could therefore be
appropriate before building a general estimation interface. The proposed
common layer would have to demonstrate a further benefit: that the rate,
population, information law, uncertainty and profit mapping survive a handoff.
It cannot claim estimator superiority from returning a richer envelope around
an otherwise much narrower calculation.

The inexpensive admission case should contain two rate levels with unequal
assignment probabilities and two customer strata with unequal effects. Demand
separate ATE and treated-population outputs; change the target population
without changing the fitted outcome model and check that the aggregation
changes. Add a shared-calendar version to establish the boundary of an i.i.d.
procedure. A refusal or explicitly restricted interpretation is the intended
answer to the latter unless a justified dependence-aware route is supplied.
This is a proposed comparison of semantic behavior, not a newly executed
vendor study.

\subsection{Sequential servicing: a seat for analysis or an engine for decisions?}

Servicing combines time to resolution, competing exits, repeated actions and
cost. Stata's inspected survival treatment command excludes delayed entry and
multiple-record or time-varying-covariate data. SAS's inspected competing-risk
route has a different risk set and ignores specified options when EVENTCODE
is active. A purchase justified by a survival feature name can therefore fail
the actual request even if both products are working as documented. An
analyst may legitimately simplify a question to a fixed treatment at baseline;
the interface must make that new question explicit rather than transform
sequential records into one row without a scientific decision
\citep{exec14_survival_stata,exec14_survival_sas}.

The buying unit for a fixed horizon should name the event of interest, competing
events, censoring, entry rule, action history and net utility. If repayment and
write-off have different monetary consequences, a survival probability for
either exit is inadequate. If eligibility is learned after the first contact,
the policy cannot use it at the first contact. SMILE's influence-diagram route
is a candidate for supplied-model decision analysis, while Stata's
prespecified-policy evaluation is a candidate for its documented static
setting. They answer different questions unless the history and causal model
have deliberately been restricted to make them coincide
\citep{exec16_cp_bayesfusion,exec13_ch_stata}.

A graphical analyst seat can be useful for reviewing a supplied mechanism and
utilities without authorizing a server embedding of the same engine. Conversely,
a production license can permit an operational route whose scientific model
has not been justified. The acquisition dossier must therefore contain both
the accepted analysis and the permitted deployment. A generated two-contact
case with an explicit history clock can test feasibility before a costly
experiment. It cannot establish truthful logging probabilities in the bank's
historical records. The present dispatcher still refuses the proposed
sequential-policy identifiers; a licensed alternative is a capability to
investigate, not functionality already added by this chapter.

\subsection{Discovery to intervention: ownership of the unresolved graph}

Discovery procurement has two deliverables: an analyst's model-building
experience and the mathematical object consumed by an intervention query.
GeNIe documents a pattern editor and random completion to a DAG. BayesiaLab EQ
documents equivalence-class search and different treatment of fixed versus
forbidden arcs. These features make commercial editors credible alternatives
to building a new graphical front end. They also show why ``export graph''
cannot be the entire acceptance criterion. The exported object must retain
what the search did not resolve and what the analyst supplied
\citep{exec13_genie_pattern,exec13_bayesia_eq}.

In a deposit-rate example, the procurement packet should distinguish an edge
oriented by a time restriction, an edge implied by a conditional-independence
procedure, an edge asserted by an analyst, and an edge chosen only to produce
a usable DAG. A downstream supplied-model inference engine may need a DAG;
that computational need is not evidence selecting one member of a learned
class. The proposed layer can retain the class and evaluate a declared
representative as a scenario, or refuse an unqualified class-wide effect. Its
competitive value depends on making this distinction inspectable without
preventing the analyst from using a productive commercial editor.

The buying unit also changes between development and repeated scoring.
Bayesia's public API overview distinguishes modeling and learning from
inference configurations. The flattened feature table does not justify an
exact edition-by-operation parity claim. A development demonstration of a
learning command cannot by itself qualify a production inference entitlement
to relearn a graph after drift. Separate the learning component, retained
model, update authorization and inference component in the proposed deployment.
Request an exact feature/rights confirmation only for the narrow configuration
that survives public semantic tests \citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

\subsection{Trustworthy observations: who constructs the variables?}

The fourth journey concerns the objects delivered to every engine: rows,
identities, fields, states and times. A node called ``arrears'' can denote a
balance known before contact, a revised accounting figure published later, or
a post-contact outcome. Matching a column name to a network node does not
establish any of those meanings. A commercial inference engine can correctly
process the wrong time slice. Buying more sophisticated inference therefore
does not resolve the observation problem.

The proposed common service's strict clock is a useful narrow boundary, but
it accepts declared times and cannot authenticate them. Similarly, record
linkage and missingness analysis from the preceding chapter require their own
sampling and truth assumptions. This journey's purchasing unit includes a
conversion specification: customer or account identity, row inclusion rule,
state mapping, measurement units, decision time and source publication time.
The commercial feature census has not established a general engine that
reconstructs those meanings from banking extracts. That is a recorded scope
gap, not an inference that no vendor offers data preparation.

For a public/generated admission case, permute node order while preserving
labels, then permute labels while preserving numeric positions. An adapter
should accept a valid explicit permutation and reject an ambiguous one; it
should not reject all reordered data. Include a retrospectively revised field
with a publication time after the action. The same values should lead to a
different eligibility disposition when their information clock changes.
These are observable and affordable semantic tests. Later owner evidence is
needed to decide whether the recorded clock and identity are truthful.

\section{Graph discovery and model construction}
\label{exec17:s8-discovery}

\subsection{Equivalence is a deliverable}

GeNIe's PC documentation returns a pattern and describes a significance
threshold, maximum adjacency and a time-limit setting. Its Bayesian Search
route is a score-guided hill-climbing procedure with restarts and a structural
prior. The pattern editor identifies directed, unresolved and possible
hidden-common-cause arcs. Create DAG chooses directions randomly when a fully
directed network is required. These are useful capabilities, but the final
random representative is not new observational evidence. The proposed product
must store both the pattern and any selected representative, with the selection
seed and reason visible to the consumer.

SMILE makes the boundary concrete in code. Its PC call returns a
\texttt{DSL\_pattern}, can be interrupted by a progress callback, and reports
an interruption code. A time limit is checked after an iteration, so it is not
the same as a hard external wall-time guarantee. Pattern coordinates follow
the data-set variable order. A wrapper must validate an explicit permutation
and reject an ambiguous or inconsistent matrix-to-name mapping
\citep{exec13_smile_pc,exec13_smile_pattern}.

BayesiaLab's EQ description supplies a different comparison. It searches
equivalence classes, treats fixed arcs as normal arcs, and takes forbidden arcs
and temporal indices into account. Thus two fields with similar names can have
opposite effects on the search. The integration contract should carry the
restriction kind, time index and class representation separately. A license or
REST call that returns a network is not evidence that the restriction was
interpreted as intended.

The banking consequence is immediate. Suppose a deposit-rate graph has an
unresolved rate--balance orientation. One representative says that the rate
changes balances; another says that a balance shock changes the quoted rate.
Both can fit the same observational law. The proposed service should return an
equivalence class or a refusal to answer an intervention query. A commercial
editor may be the best analyst-facing component, while the shared library owns
the typed class, provenance and refusal semantics. Building another graphical
editor would need a separate business case; porting SMILE is not an
open-source option because its manual describes proprietary development and
deployment licenses
\citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license}.

\subsection{The latent and supplied-model distinction}

Bayes Server documents causal optimization, an evidence-to-abduction-to-
intervention counterfactual workflow, and criteria such as back-door, front-door
and instrumental-variable routes. Its guide says that some helper functions
are available from version 10 and that the worked calculation can be performed
from 9.4. This is strong evidence of a documented abduction workflow. It is
not a complete domain theorem for every graph, nor an execution of the local
service. The request must state whether the output is a population intervention
or an individual structural counterfactual, and which exogenous-variable model
couples the factual and alternative worlds
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality}.

BayesiaLab supplies graphical surgery, likelihood matching and contribution
analysis in the inspected pages. Stata supplies treatment effects and a
developed one-mediator workflow. These are valuable alternatives for specific
targets. Their assumptions remain part of the output: Stata's manual names
i.i.d. sampling, conditional independence and overlap; its mediation manual
addresses treatment--mediator and mediator--outcome confounding restrictions.
An adapter that returns a coefficient while dropping those conditions is a
semantic failure even if the numerical call succeeds
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesia_surgery,exec13_ci_bayesia_matching,
exec13_ci_stata_causal_intro,exec13_ci_stata_mediate}.

The buying route is therefore conditional. Reuse a commercial engine for a
supplied model when its edition exposes the required intervention or abduction
operation and its data movement and deployment rights are acceptable. Integrate
an open method when its source, license, numerical representation and target
semantics are compatible. Build the missing graph-class and assumption ledger
in the common library. Reject a direct port when the license, endpoint meaning
or hidden-state contract cannot be preserved. These are distinct decisions;
``use the package'' is not one of them.

\section{Effects, IV/RD and the limits of a command menu}
\label{exec17:s8-effects}

Stata's treatment-effect reference documents ATE, ATET and potential-outcome
means using regression adjustment, inverse-probability weighting, augmented
weighting and matching. Its caveats explain why i.i.d. sampling, conditional
independence and overlap matter. The \texttt{cate} postestimation path
evaluates a prespecified treatment-assignment policy. JMP's causal-treatment
documentation similarly defines positivity, consistency and conditional
exchangeability, while its uplift and treatment personalities address distinct
estimands. These capabilities set a high baseline for estimation. A new common
service must justify its cost by preserving target identity, evidence, clocks,
uncertainty and cross-tool failure semantics, not by adding another estimator
name \citep{exec13_ch_stata,exec13_ch_jmp_causal,exec13_ch_jmp_uplift}.

The IV/RD row is especially important for procurement. Stata's weak-instrument
postestimation describes confidence sets that can be finite, disconnected,
unbounded, whole-line or empty; its grid and simulation controls are exposed.
SAS/ETS SYSLIN supplies 2SLS, LIML, K-class, SUR and 3SLS system alternatives.
SPSS and JMP provide narrower equation or model-implied instrument paths. A
bridge that serializes only a point estimate loses exactly the result that is
most decision-relevant when the instrument is weak. The bridge should preserve
the set components, search bounds, grid, seed, covariance choice and whether an
empty set means model rejection or numerical noncoverage
\citep{exec13_ivrd_stata,exec13_ivrd_sas}.

For a credit threshold, the business request may be a local complier effect,
an all-applicant effect, or a reduced-form jump. The same columns and cutoff do
not identify all three. The proposed acquisition comparison should execute a
small public/generated fixture in which exclusion holds, then a negative control
where eligibility has a direct outcome effect. A commercial command can be the
reference implementation for the first fixture; it cannot make the second
restriction true. The cost of a licensed command is justified only if the
workflow preserves that distinction and reports the negative control.

\section{Time, panels and survival}
\label{exec17:s8-temporal}

GeNIe documents dynamic Bayesian networks and parameter learning for an existing
dynamic structure. Its parameter-learning page says that the structure is not
learned by that procedure and explains the node-and-time-slice column mapping.
SMILE's dynamic tutorial distinguishes initial from transition tables, lag order,
slice count and temporal evidence indices. SAS/ETS and Stata provide VAR,
SVAR, VARMAX and Granger-style operations, while SPSS TCM uses ``causal'' in
the Granger-predictive sense. Mplus provides intensive longitudinal and latent
dynamic models. These are substantial tools, but a predictive lag, a supplied
transition model and an intervention effect are different targets
\citep{exec13_ct_genie_dbn,exec13_ct_genie_learning,exec13_ct_smile_tutorial,
exec13_ct_stata_granger,exec13_ct_sas_causal,exec13_ct_ibm_tcm}.

The adapter must preserve sampling interval, time origin, state ordering,
calendar shocks, contemporaneous restrictions and the intervention regime. A
state array with swapped categories can pass type checks and reverse a
servicing action. A VAR test on shared monthly observations can be a useful
diagnostic while failing to identify a rate intervention. The proposal should
buy or reuse supplied-model temporal inference when it meets those fields and
build a common refusal when the question asks for an unsupported change of
regime.

Survival tools provide another concrete baseline. Stata's inspected
\texttt{stteffects} documentation excludes delayed entry, time-varying
covariates and multiple-record data in that command. SAS PHREG documents
counting-process records and competing-event output, but some options are
ignored under its EVENTCODE route. SPSS, Statistica, Minitab, JMP, NCSS and
Mplus each provide useful narrower conversion or reporting functions. The
local reference dispatcher returned completed unsupported responses for the
three authored fixed-treatment, competing-incidence and sequential-policy
identifiers. This establishes a present implementation gap and a route to a
matched public/generated bridge; it does not rank vendors or prove their
absence of other methods \citep{exec14_survival_stata,exec14_survival_sas,
exec14_survival_jmp,exec16_commercial_matrix,exec16_commercial_runtime}.

\section{Policy value, uplift and decision analysis}
\label{exec17:s8-policy}

Stata's \texttt{estat policyeval} evaluates a prespecified treatment-assignment
probability. GeNIe and SMILE influence diagrams compute expected utility for a
supplied model with decision nodes, information and costs. JMP uplift emphasizes
treatment differences in partitioning. Amazon Personalize documents train/test
recommendation metrics, including average rewards at rank. These capabilities
should be compared on aligned targets, not on the shared word ``policy''.

For a binary action, a prespecified static policy has value
\begin{equation}
 V(\pi)=\E\{\pi(X)Y(1)+[1-\pi(X)]Y(0)\}.
 \label{exec17:s8-static-value}
\end{equation}
For a sequential policy with history $H_t$, logging probability $b_t$ and
target policy $\pi_t$, the trajectory weight is
\begin{equation}
 W_T=\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\pi_t(A_t\mid H_t)}{b_t(A_t\mid H_t)}.
 \label{exec17:s8-sequential-value}
\end{equation}
The second object requires action histories, positivity and an outcome clock.
It cannot be obtained by placing the last action in a static treatment column.
An influence diagram answers a supplied-mechanism utility question; a
recommendation metric evaluates a predictive ranking. The service must retain
which object was requested.

Azure Personalizer is a historical comparator. Its archived documentation
describes Rank and Reward calls, contextual and action features, delayed reward,
and a service that does not maintain individual user histories. It also records
that new resources stopped in 2023 and the service retired on 25 August 2026.
The migration recommendation to an open project does not establish estimator
or feature parity. AWS metrics and a retired hosted policy service cannot be
treated as evidence of a causal-policy guarantee
\citep{exec16_cp_azure,exec16_cp_aws}.

The proposed route is to keep a static policy adapter, a sequential off-policy
research module and a supplied influence-diagram adapter separate. First use a
public or generated trajectory fixture to test clocks, logging probabilities,
costs, action support, failure denominators and independent evaluation. Only
then consider a licensed engine trial. A model that refuses a sequential target
is preferable to a static score silently substituted for it.

\subsection{Information parents can change the affordable policy}
\label{exec17:s8-information}

The SMILE influence-diagram manual supplies an unusually concrete acquisition
requirement. After inference, the returned matrices may be indexed by
unobserved decision nodes and by chance nodes that should have been observed
before a decision. It provides \texttt{GetIndexingParents} to recover those
indices. Chance-node entries are then posterior probabilities for each policy;
decision entries are expected utilities for outcomes and policies; value-node
entries are expected utilities for policies. An integration that buys access
to the matrix but exports only a scalar has discarded part of the result's
meaning \citep{exec16_cp_bayesfusion}.

Consider a generated servicing decision with equally probable histories
$h_0,h_1$ and actions 0 (no contact) and 1 (contact). Stipulate net utilities
in USD per customer:
\begin{equation}
 u(h_0,0)=u(h_1,0)=0,\qquad u(h_0,1)=10,\qquad u(h_1,1)=-30.
 \label{exec17:s8-utility-table}
\end{equation}
The histories can represent eligible and ineligible contact contexts; the
amounts already include contact costs. If the history is available before
acting, a deterministic policy is the pair $(a_0,a_1)$, with value
\begin{equation}
 V(a_0,a_1)=\tfrac12u(h_0,a_0)+\tfrac12u(h_1,a_1).
 \label{exec17:s8-indexed-value}
\end{equation}
The four values for $(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)$ are respectively 0, $-15$,
5 and $-10$. The best feasible history-dependent policy contacts only at
$h_0$ and has value 5. If history is unavailable until after the decision,
only the two constant policies are feasible, with best value 0. Randomizing
contact independently of history with probability $r$ has value $-10r$ and
cannot improve that value. Taking the largest matrix entry, 10, is neither
the best population value nor a feasible unconditional policy value. The
exact four-policy enumeration is a project derivation, not a SMILE run
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

For this stipulated utility model, purchasing a perfectly timely history
feed has gross value at most USD~5 per customer before feed, review and
deployment expenses. Buying a faster decision engine does not create this
information. A feed that becomes available after contact has zero policy
value here, even when it has the same data contents. With noisy history,
the posterior contact benefit must be positive; if $p$ is the conditional
probability of $h_0$, that benefit is $10p-30(1-p)$, so contact requires
$p>3/4$. The purchased signal needs an information clock and calibrated
meaning for $p$, not merely an accuracy percentage.

This example defines a specific acceptance case for a commercial adapter.
Export parent identifiers, state order, observed/unobserved status, decision
ordering and policy indices with the utility matrix. Reconstruct all four
policy values and the restricted two-policy value outside the engine from
that export. Then intentionally withhold the history and require the feasible
policy set to shrink. If the wrapper reports value 5 for a decision that
cannot observe history, the correct response is to reject that integration
for the requested deployment. The experiment tests the preservation of
documented information semantics; it does not rank SMILE's optimizer against
other optimizers or validate the stipulated utilities.

An alternative acquisition is a static prespecified-policy tool when the bank
can truly observe the relevant covariates before the one decision. Stata's
documented policy probability supplies that narrower comparison. A supplied
influence diagram remains useful when the bank wants to inspect mechanisms
and decisions together. A future sequential estimator is needed when the
question concerns a learned policy under repeated, uncertain assignment.
Those alternatives should compete only within their supported target and
information class. Their different purchase costs cannot compensate for a
missing predecision variable \citep{exec13_ch_stata,exec16_cp_bayesfusion}.

\section{Language, deployment and semantic loss}
\label{exec17:s8-boundary}

SMILE's C++ engine and its documented wrappers for Java, Python, R, .NET and
COM are access paths to one proprietary engine. The licensing page separates
academic and commercial development, server deployment and end-user
redistribution. Bayesia's API page separates Java modeling and inference from
REST inference, describes customer-hosted model files for one configuration and
hosted models for another, and prices production by simultaneously loaded model
instances. The phrase ``Java API'' or ``Bayesia API'' therefore does not name a
single acquisition unit \citep{exec13_smile_intro,exec13_smile_license,
exec13_bayesia_api}.

The request contract should include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a target and population with unit, horizon, currency and sign convention;
\item a graph or supplied model with endpoint marks, restriction types and
      unresolved-class representation;
\item treatment or action identity, assignment/logging law and observation clock;
\item estimator, uncertainty object, numerical controls, seed and refusal state;
\item artifact hashes, dependency versions, license entitlement, data location,
      interruption status and recovery identity.
\end{enumerate}

The consumer should see whether the result is documented, locally observed,
project-derived or proposed. A wrapper should preserve an interrupted SMILE
learning call as incomplete, preserve Bayes Server's intervention rather than
ordinary evidence, and preserve Stata's disconnected confidence set. It should
not coerce a vendor error or an unsupported target into a null estimate.

The recommended integration pattern is a narrow adapter plus a native semantic
ledger. For a proprietary engine, the ledger records the license, model
location, wrapper version, serialization and permitted deployment. For an
open-source package, it records the commit, license, dependency graph, source
anchors and whether the package can be adopted, wrapped, ported or rejected.
The common CLI and MCP surfaces expose the ledger and request envelope; they do
not make proprietary code open or transform a predictive metric into a causal
estimand.

\subsection{Entitlements determine the architecture that can be quoted}
\label{exec17:s8-editions}

For SMILE, the inspected license page separates development from deployment,
and server deployment from distribution of end-user programs. This creates
at least three different cost inquiries for a proposed banking product:
analysts building a prototype, a centrally hosted calculation service, and
applications distributed to consumers. The existence of a Python or R wrapper
does not collapse these distinctions. An academic development license is not
the planning assumption for a commercial bank; a server integration needs
its own applicable entitlement. No clause inspected here authorizes porting
the proprietary engine. The feasible proposed route is a licensed adapter,
with an open-source alternative considered separately
\citep{exec13_smile_license}.

Bayesia's overview describes another architecture. It distinguishes Java
deployment with model files on customer servers from a hosted REST
configuration whose model files reside on Bayesia servers in France. It also
states that the Java route validates credentials with a license server and
requires a permanent Internet connection. Those documented dependencies must
enter a deployment comparison even when all model files stay local. Model
residency and license connectivity are different properties. The public table's
extraction loses visual feature markers, so the exact edition-function matrix
and contractual guarantees remain unresolved. The explicit prose does support
inquiring about model residency, network dependence, loaded-instance capacity
and learning versus inference \citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

Stata illustrates why concurrent users and concurrent engines must not share
one field. Its license-options page defines an identified single user and a
network license for a specified number of concurrent users within a single
site. It describes annual and perpetual arrangements. This is evidence about
the published user-license categories, not confirmation that unattended
service accounts, remote MCP clients, geographic replication or redistribution
are covered. The proposal should use a licensed analyst workflow as a
candidate reference and keep an unattended service route pending the relevant
terms. Counting API workers as ``users'' would be an unsupported legal and
operational assumption \citep{exec13_ivrd_license}.

These distinctions change the quote request. It should state the legal
deployment entity, exact edition/update, development users, runtime users or
instances as applicable, sites, model residency, permitted data movement,
offline needs, recovery replicas, consumer access and expected model churn.
An edition list without those quantities does not price the workflow. The
public originals establish which questions matter; they do not provide binding
permission or numeric prices. This is why the financial examples below use
transparent hypothetical terms rather than convert an undocumented seat price
into a server cost.

\subsection{Version replacement is a scientific change}

An upgrade can add a needed helper without preserving an operational contract.
Bayes Server's inspected example says that its counterfactual calculation is
possible from version 9.4, while the illustrated abduction helpers arrive in
version 10. A prototype that manually constructs the older calculation and a
workflow that invokes the later helper have different reproducibility and
review costs, even if both answer the same supplied structural question.
The acquisition dossier should preserve the structural coupling, evidence
removal and intervention steps, then test whether a replacement produces the
same declared result. Matching a button name is insufficient
\citep{exec13_ci_bayesserver_causality}.

Retirement has a similar consequence at the service boundary. The inspected
Azure Personalizer pages record retirement on 25 August 2026, before the
literature cutoff. They describe Rank/Reward and explicitly say that the service
does not maintain individual user information or history. A migration candidate
must therefore reconstruct whatever external history the old workflow used,
its reward delay and assignment logging. A replacement recommendation engine
can preserve a ranking interface while changing the evaluation problem. Treat
the old service as a historical design comparator, and price migration,
requalification and export retention separately from a recurring engine fee
\citep{exec16_cp_azure}.

For SAS EVENTCODE, the ignored-option list makes the upgrade/translation test
particularly observable: parse the actual effective procedure and covariance
settings, then compare them with the request. If an integration asserts that
an ignored RANDOM statement supplied a random-effects fit, the problem lies
in the integration's claim. Preserving the original request alongside the
effective invocation enables a reviewer to see that mismatch. A successful
numeric result does not authorize relabeling the effective model
\citep{exec14_survival_sas}.

\section{Scheduling a licensed engine}
\label{exec17:s8-scheduling}

Bayesia's production description makes simultaneous loaded model instances a
license quantity. A simple planning model is useful before a quote arrives.
Let job $i$ have supplied load-and-inference duration $d_i$, and let $m$ be the
number of permitted instances. For independent jobs and a nonpreemptive
schedule, the makespan is
\begin{equation}
 T_m=\min_{\text{assignment to }m\text{ instances}}
       \max_{j\leq m}\sum_{i\in j}d_i.
 \label{exec17:s8-makespan}
\end{equation}
This is a workload assumption, not a measured vendor rate. Three planning jobs
with durations 7, 5 and 4 take 16 units on one instance and 9 units on two
instances by placing the 5 and 4 jobs together. The calculation tells the
prototype to measure model-load time, queueing and failure recovery. It does
not establish that a production license permits that schedule or that the
engine scales linearly.

There is a simple lower-bound check on this reference: every schedule needs
at least the longest job time, 7, and at least the average assigned load,
$16/m$. For two instances, a makespan below 9 would require each instance to
carry exactly 8 units because the durations are integers and total 16. No
subset of $\{7,5,4\}$ sums to 8. The partition $\{7\},\{5,4\}$ attains 9,
proving optimality for this three-job instance. Three instances attain 7.
A deadline of 8 therefore requires three under these assumptions; a deadline
of 9 needs two. This is a useful prospective quote distinction even though
none of these durations is an observed product timing
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

The vendor's loaded-model language also exposes a limitation of the simple
job formula. A warm model that is idle between requests can still consume a
licensed instance. Let $I_k(t)$ indicate that model instance $k$ is loaded at
time $t$. The proposed entitlement constraint is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_k I_k(t)\leq m\quad\hbox{for every }t.
 \label{exec17:s8-occupancy}
\end{equation}
This counts simultaneously loaded instances, not daily request volume or
physical servers. In the simple three-job reference each job loads, computes
and unloads within its stated duration. If models are retained between jobs,
their occupancy intervals must be extended. If loading and unloading are
costly, the policy can trade repeated startup against scarce instance slots.
The correct evidence is a trace of load, readiness, request start, completion
and unload events, with license failures retained. A thread count alone cannot
recover this trace. Exact concurrent-query behavior within an instance remains
an edition/runtime question; it is not assumed by the model
\citep{exec13_bayesia_api}.

For example, retaining one model throughout a quiet period can leave only
$m-1$ slots for the other models even while that first model performs no
inference. A benchmark that reports only CPU utilization can miss this binding
constraint. Conversely, sequential loading may satisfy a one-instance license
while violating the decision deadline. A bank choosing between those routes
should measure the requested deadline and refusal/fallback outcome together,
then ask what capacity is needed for the same supported workload. A larger
license has no demonstrated value merely because more cores are available.

SMILE's time-limit semantics introduce a different scheduling issue. The
manual checks elapsed search time after an iteration completes. Without a
bound on the remaining iteration, setting the nominal limit to the bank's
deadline does not prove that the result arrives by that deadline. An external
wrapper may enforce a wall-time stop, but it must mark the learning result
as interrupted and retain the last valid artifact separately from a completed
graph. Killing the process cannot convert a partial search into an accepted
scientific answer. The prototype should include a deadline at an iteration
boundary and one during an iteration; the latter is precisely the case a
simple elapsed-time setting can fail to resolve \citep{exec13_smile_pc}.

The same ledger must include model transfer, data residency, license-server
availability, retries, operator review and retirement. A cheaper per-instance
license can be dominated by a long load time or by the cost of converting a
pattern, state ordering or uncertainty object. Those terms belong in the
workflow cost, not in an unqualified engine benchmark.

\section{Exact hypothetical cost and route choice}
\label{exec17:s8-cost}

To make the acquisition decision reviewable, consider three routes for $n$
assigned requests in one planning year. The values below are deliberately
round and hypothetical:
\begin{align}
 C_B(n)&=420000+1.8n, &\text{build the common service},\\
 C_L(n)&=190000+5.6n, &\text{licensed adapter and conversion},\\
 C_H(n)&=285000+3n, &\text{hybrid: common ledger plus licensed engine}.
 \label{exec17:s8-costs}
\end{align}
The fixed terms include engineering, review and deployment preparation. The
variable terms include request preparation and operation. They do not include
an invented vendor quote. The exact pairwise crossings are
\begin{equation}
 n_{LB}=\frac{230000}{3.8}=\frac{1150000}{19},\qquad
 n_{LH}=\frac{95000}{2.6}=\frac{475000}{13},\qquad
 n_{HB}=\frac{135000}{1.2}=112500.
 \label{exec17:s8-crossings}
\end{equation}
Thus the licensed route is cheapest at low volume under this model, the
hybrid route is cheapest in the middle, and building the common service is
cheapest at sufficiently high volume. The ordering is a consequence of the
assumed costs, not a recommendation to purchase any named product.

At volumes 10,000, 50,000 and 150,000, the exact costs in USD are respectively
$(246000,315000,438000)$ for licensed, hybrid and build at the first volume;
$(470000,435000,510000)$ at the second; and $(1030000,735000,690000)$ at the
third. The route minimizing maximum regret over these three scenarios is hybrid:
its regret is USD~69,000, compared with USD~340,000 for licensed and USD~192,000 for
build. This is a minimax decision over a declared volume set, not an expected
bank cost. Minimizing the largest absolute cost instead selects build: its
largest cost is USD~690,000, against USD~735,000 for hybrid and USD~1,030,000
for licensed. A committee must choose its objective before using the table.
The exact enumeration is reproduced in the accompanying S8 calculation record
\citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

Semantic admissibility comes first. If a licensed route cannot preserve a
disconnected IV set, a graph class, or a sequential policy's logging law, its
cost is not comparable to a build route that can. The route is then rejected
for that target regardless of its nominal price. The prototype should measure
the fraction of requests that require conversion, refusal or human review and
replace the planning terms with observed quantities.

\subsection{A quote ceiling for a genuinely comparable configuration}

Suppose the hypothetical hybrid envelope excludes a new annual deployment
charge $z\geq0$, with all other expenses held fixed. It remains competitive
with both alternatives exactly when
\begin{equation}
 z\leq z_{\max}(n)=\min\{135000-1.2n,\,-95000+2.6n\}.
 \label{exec17:s8-quote-ceiling}
\end{equation}
At 50,000 requests the admissible increment is at most USD~35,000. Across
continuous nonnegative volume, the largest such ceiling occurs where the two
terms agree, $n=1150000/19$, and equals USD~$1185000/19$, approximately
62,368.42. Outside the interval from $475000/13$ to 112500 requests, hybrid
is already more costly than another route even with $z=0$. At integer request
counts the continuous maximum is an upper bound; it need not be attained.
These are affordable-price ceilings under assumed equal service, not
estimates of a vendor's willingness to sell \citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

A quote for an analyst seat cannot be inserted as $z$ for a server deployment.
Nor can an instance fee be multiplied by customer count when the contract
counts loaded models. First choose the configuration and feasible schedule,
then enter its charge in the correct fixed or occupancy-dependent term.
If the quote changes the route's permitted geographic use, history retention
or model-learning operations, reassess admissibility before comparing cost.
The cost surface makes this negotiation specific: it identifies the required
entitlement and the largest incremental price consistent with the stated
volume and alternative routes.

Shared costs need the same discipline. If the bank already operates a source
and assumption ledger required by every route, its sunk acquisition cost
does not become a saving unique to hybrid. If building that ledger is still
necessary in every route, include its incremental cost in every relevant
envelope. Likewise, a manual fallback needed after an unsupported request
must be charged to the assigned population. Earlier cost accounting develops
these principles generally; here their role is to prevent a favorable
commercial comparison obtained by assigning common scientific review to only
one alternative. The proposal needs observed workflow accounts before any
of the displayed cost inequalities can support a procurement decision.

\section{The value of a compatibility signal}
\label{exec17:s8-signal}

The uncertain acquisition fact may be compatibility with the required
information and uncertainty contract. At 50,000 assigned requests, suppose
that a common licensed engine has a good or bad compatibility state. Both
licensed routes share that state. Their annual costs, in USD, are
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|rr}
 & \text{good} & \text{bad}\\ \hline
 B&510000&510000\\
 L&470000&720000\\
 H&435000&535000
\end{array}.
\label{exec17:s8-signal-cost-table}
\end{equation}
The bad-state additions pay for conversion repair, replacement or fallback
that restores the same scientific acceptance. A route with an uncorrected
scientific error is inadmissible; these are not prices for tolerating wrong
causal answers. Build is compatible in both states by assumption. This is an
acquisition scenario, not a measured advantage of our present prototype.

Let the prior good-state probability be $3/5$. Expected costs are USD~510,000,
570,000 and 475,000 for $B,L,H$ respectively, so the no-test choice is hybrid.
A proposed fixture has sensitivity $19/20$ and false-positive probability
$1/20$ for the common compatibility state. The four joint probabilities for
good-positive, bad-positive, good-negative and bad-negative are .57, .02, .03
and .38. Bayes' rule therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \Prob(+)=\frac{59}{100},\quad
 \Prob(\mathrm{good}\mid +)=\frac{57}{59},\quad
 \Prob(\mathrm{good}\mid -)=\frac{3}{41}.
 \label{exec17:s8-signal-posterior}
\end{equation}
For a posterior probability $p$, the route costs are
$510000$, $720000-250000p$ and $535000-100000p$.
Licensed is dominated by hybrid throughout $0\leq p\leq1$, since their
difference is $185000-150000p>0$. Hybrid beats build exactly when $p>1/4$.
Thus a positive signal retains hybrid and a negative signal selects build.
Using the joint probabilities avoids rounding the conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{signal}}=.57(435000)+.02(535000)+.41(510000)=467750.
 \label{exec17:s8-signal-value}
\end{equation}
Gross information value is USD~7,250. A USD~5,000 test has net value
USD~2,250; a USD~15,000 test has net value minus USD~7,750. Perfect state
information would select hybrid in the good state and build in the bad state,
for expected cost USD~465,000 and value USD~10,000. This upper bound rules out
any USD~15,000 experiment justified only by this one annual decision. All nine
signal-contingent pairs of routes give the same optimum under separate
enumeration \citep{exec17_s8_examples}.

This example connects the commercial architecture to experiment design. Testing
the R and Python wrappers of the same SMILE core does not supply two independent
observations of a core semantic defect. Wrapper-specific serialization defects
need separate states or conditional error laws. Similarly, a public fixture
with known answers can reveal whether graph marks or utility indices survive
conversion, while it cannot authenticate deployment rights or estimate a
bank's future mixture of requests. The probabilities above are stipulated
properties of a signal about one acquisition state. They are not a claim that
95\% sensitivity on generated examples transfers to a bank.

A prospective measurement must distinguish failure at the engine, wrapper,
licensing, data-conversion and human-review levels. Count every assigned
fixture, including unavailable and unsupported cases. Before increasing the
budget, recompute the choice with every feasible alternative, including doing
the narrow task with an existing analyst tool. The experiment is valuable only
through the decision it can change, at the scale and time horizon to which its
information applies.

\section{Adoption decisions and remaining competitive gaps}
\label{exec17:s8-decision}

The comparison yields six concrete decisions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reuse a commercial editor} for analyst graph construction when
      the bank accepts the edition, licensing and model-export boundary. Keep
      the unresolved class and source evidence in the common ledger.
\item \textbf{Integrate a licensed engine} only for a named supplied-model,
      intervention or inference profile whose wrapper, error and deployment
      behavior pass a matched public/generated fixture.
\item \textbf{Adopt open-source components} when the original source, license,
      dependency rights, coordinate convention and uncertainty object can be
      preserved. A package's presence in a catalog is not a runtime result.
\item \textbf{Port selectively} only for a narrow algorithm whose semantics,
      numerical tolerances and adverse cases can be reproduced. Proprietary
      SMILE or hosted vendor implementations are not porting candidates.
\item \textbf{Build the semantic ledger and refusal layer} because it spans
      graph classes, target clocks, uncertainty, artifacts and licenses rather
      than one estimator. This is the proposed product's defensible common
      capability, still unqualified at bank scale.
\item \textbf{Reject a route for a target} when it changes the estimand,
      drops unresolved structure, cannot preserve an uncertainty set or lacks
      a lawful deployment path. Rejection is a successful proposal outcome.
\end{enumerate}

The gaps remain substantial. The local service has no general discovery or
sequential-policy runtime; no commercial engine has been executed here; exact
edition parity, paid prices, embedding rights and support terms remain unknown
for many families; and arbitrary latent graphs, dependent learners, authentic
assignment truth and bank economics remain open. A complete census across all
17 areas requires additional primary manuals and owner inputs. The present
chapter narrows what should be measured and purchased; it does not claim that
the competitive survey is closed.

\section{A prototype-to-purchase protocol}
\label{exec17:s8-protocol}

The next public/generated comparison should freeze one target from each of four
journeys: a graph-class intervention query, a weak-IV set, a competing-event
row conversion and a fixed-history policy value. For each target, freeze the
input schema, unit, clock, graph/model, method, uncertainty object, license
mode, seed, resource budget and stopping rule. Run the common ledger, a selected
open-source reference and a documented commercial route when accessible. Report
semantic equivalence, refusal/unsupported counts, numerical error, uncertainty
shape, preparation and review time, memory, queueing, recovery and full cost.

The study must include negative controls: a randomly oriented graph, a
transposed node matrix, an unsupported delayed-entry survival record, a
nonpositive logging probability, a direct-effect IV violation and a recommendation
metric presented as though it were a causal value. A system passes only when it
preserves the refusal or identifies the changed target. The full denominator
includes unavailable originals, failed conversions and manual reviews.

An accepted public/generated result would justify a narrow paid trial. It would
not justify a bank deployment. The paid trial would bind the exact edition,
entitlement, data residency, service account, operating platform and publication
rights, then repeat the frozen matched protocol. Authentic bank assignment,
audit truth, accepted loss and institutional economics remain later inputs.
The prospective cases and their source-specific acceptance conditions are
recorded separately from the exact arithmetic \citep{exec17_s8_design}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{exec17:s8-end}

Commercial alternatives already provide meaningful graph, model, estimator,
survival, policy and deployment capabilities. The proposal's gap is not a lack
of commands; it is the lack of a common contract that keeps target meaning,
ambiguity, uncertainty, numerical state, data clock, rights and refusal visible
across those commands. A hybrid acquisition route is a useful planning default
under the displayed hypothetical costs, while a cheap compatibility signal can
change a route only under a declared signal law. The immediate work is therefore
the public/generated matched protocol and the semantic ledger. Prices, runtime
parity, bank truth, independent acceptance and ROI remain open evidence, as the
proposal must say plainly.
